>

> **反 Whig 核心**: 「启蒙」(Enlightenment / Lumières / Aufklärung) 是 19 世纪后设概念 — 18 世纪人自称「哲学家 (philosophes)」或「百科全书派 (encyclopédistes)」, 不自称「启蒙运动」。选 Voltaire 不选 Locke, 是因为 Voltaire 同时持奴贸股份 + 写反犹辞典 — 他是这场运动矛盾的人格化。选 Toussaint 是因为他用启蒙语言推翻了启蒙语言所包庇的奴隶制 — 这是整个 Topic 最大的讽刺。

## §1 前史知识图景：「理性」从哪里来？（1455-1689）

「理性时代」(Age of Reason) 这个标签让人以为 18 世纪某一天, 欧洲人突然从黑暗里醒来, 发现了「思考」这件事。这是错的。「理性」不是从天上掉下来的 — 它是 200 年印刷机 + 宗教战争 + 科学院 + 一场限制国王权力的革命的积累。在理解 Voltaire 之前, 要先看这五层地基。

> The "Age of Reason" label makes it sound like Europeans woke up one morning in 1700 and discovered thinking. They didn't. Reason, as the philosophes deployed it, took 200 years of printing presses, religious wars, scientific societies, and one constitutional revolution to build. Before Voltaire, five things had to happen first.

**第一层: 印刷机与审查速度竞赛 (1455)**

1455 年 Gutenberg 活字印刷术 (回挂 Renaissance Topic), 不只是「书变便宜了」。它根本改变了**信息传播速度与审查速度的竞赛格局**。手抄本时代, 一部危险的书要流通, 至少需要几个月和几个抄写员 — 审查机构总有时间拦截。印刷机之后, 一个异端文本一夜之间能出 500 本, 分散到五个城市。等教会拿到样本、开会决议查禁、通知各地主教, 这批书已经在私人书架上了。Voltaire 1734 年《哲学书简》(Lettres philosophiques) 被巴黎查禁这件事 (§4 详写), 本身就是这个竞赛格局的活教材: 书先流通, 命令再来, 命令追不上书。

> After Gutenberg (1455), censorship became a race it could never win. A hand-copied dangerous text took months to circulate — slow enough to catch. A printed one could be 500 copies in five cities overnight. By the time a bishop's office identified the problem, convened a hearing, and sent orders to every bookshop, the copies were on private shelves. Voltaire's *Lettres philosophiques* banned in Paris (1734, see §4) is a case study: the book circulated first, the ban came second, the ban lost.

**第二层: 宗教战争的疲倦 + 威斯特伐利亚和约 1648**

1618-1648 年三十年战争 (Thirty Years' War): 神圣罗马帝国领土上, 天主教 vs 新教诸侯 + 法国 + 瑞典 + 丹麦, 算上饥荒和瘟疫, 中欧某些地区人口损失 25-40%。战争结束时签的 1648《威斯特伐利亚和约》(Peace of Westphalia) 有一句核心安排: **各诸侯在自己领土上决定信仰, 别国不得以宗教理由干涉内政**。这是「国家主权」概念的条约形式。它没有直接催生「言论自由」, 但它断掉了一根链条: 宗教不再是合法的国际战争理由。欧洲知识分子到 17 世纪末开始问: 如果宗教差异可以不打仗, 那宗教到底凭什么管人用哪种历法、用什么医学、读什么书?

> The Thirty Years' War (1618-1648) killed, by famine and plague, perhaps a third of Central Europe. When it ended, the 1648 Peace of Westphalia encoded a principle: each ruler controls religion in their own territory; no foreign power intervenes on religious grounds. This was sovereignty as a treaty concept. It didn't grant free speech. But it cut one chain: religion was no longer a legal justification for international war. By the 1690s, thinkers were asking: if religious difference doesn't justify war between states, why should it justify censorship within them?

**第三层: 科学革命的制度化 1543-1687 (SciRev cross-ref)**

科学革命 (Scientific Revolution) Topic 详写了从 Copernicus 1543 到 Newton 1687 这段历程。此处只标几个「制度」锚点: 1660 年英国 Royal Society (皇家学会) 成立 (查理二世特许状), 1666 年法国 Académie des sciences (法国科学院) 成立 (Louis XIV 资助)。这两个机构的关键意义不是「科学家第一次聚到一起」, 而是「**理性实验**这个认识论方法被国家机构授权, 获得了制度合法性」。一个由国王特许的机构说「我们靠实验验证知识」, 这跟一个孤独哲学家说同样的话, 社会重量天差地别。Royal Society 的格言 Nullius in verba (「不凭任何人之言」) — 任何主张都要靠实验证据, 不靠权威引用 — 是 18 世纪 philosophes 批判「迷信」时手里最顺手的武器 (SciRev Mersenne cross-ref: Mersenne 的书信网络是前体, Royal Society 是制度化版本)。

> The Scientific Revolution (covered in the SciRev Topic, Copernicus 1543 → Newton 1687) matters here for its *institutional* endpoints. The Royal Society got a royal charter in 1660; the French Académie des sciences opened in 1666 with Louis XIV's funding. Their significance: **experimental reason** as a method of knowing got state-level authorization. A king-chartered body saying "we verify claims by experiment, not by citing authorities" carries social weight no lone philosopher can match. The Royal Society's motto — *Nullius in verba* ("take nobody's word for it") — handed the philosophes their sharpest tool before they even coined the word philosophe.

**第四层: 1688-1689 英国光荣革命**

1688 年秋, 英国议会邀请荷兰执政 William of Orange (威廉三世) 渡海入英, 取代天主教国王 James II (詹姆斯二世)。James II 基本没抵抗就流亡法国。这场政权更迭后来被称为「光荣革命」(Glorious Revolution) — 「光荣」的意思不是壮烈, 而是**几乎没流血就完成了**。1689 年《权利法案》(Bill of Rights) 规定: 国王不能暂停议会立法, 不能设常备军不经议会批准, 天主教徒不能继承英国王位, 议员在议会里的言论受豁免。这不是民主 — 投票资格仍限于有产者, 女性无参政权, 爱尔兰天主教徒被排除。但它是一个关键的**概念示范**: 一个国家可以用文件限制君主, 君主可以受法律约束而政体不崩溃。Voltaire 1726-1729 在英国流放期间亲眼看到的就是这个体制在运转 (§4 详写)。

> In 1688, the English Parliament invited Dutch Stadtholder **William of Orange** to replace Catholic King James II. James fled to France with almost no fighting. The 1689 **Bill of Rights** followed: Parliament controls taxation; the King cannot maintain a standing army without Parliament; Catholic succession is barred; MPs have speech immunity in Parliament. This was not democracy — voters were propertied men, women were excluded, Irish Catholics were out. But it was a conceptual proof of concept: a country could put a monarch under law and not collapse. Voltaire saw it working (§4). He used it as a mirror held up to France.

**第五层: Grade 7 钩子 — 五层地基压缩**

五件事合在一起: ①印刷机让禁书令永远追不上书; ②三十年战争的尸体让欧洲人怀疑宗教战争有任何意义; ③Royal Society 让「实验理性」获得国家背书; ④威斯特伐利亚让主权概念与宗教脱钩; ⑤英国 1689 让「国王受法律约束」从理论变成运行中的现实。18 世纪初坐在巴黎咖啡馆里讨论政治的人, 继承的是这五笔遗产。他们不是在发明新思想 — 他们是在把这五笔遗产组合成新的政治要求。

> Grade 7 hook: Five things handed the philosophes their toolkit. Printing made censorship a losing race. The Thirty Years' War made religious killing seem pointless. The Royal Society made experimental reason respectable. Westphalia made sovereignty a secular concept. The English Bill of Rights made limited monarchy a real country, not a theory. The coffee-house talkers of 1700 Paris didn't invent new ideas — they assembled five inheritances into new demands.

## §2 1689-1720 启蒙前夜：咖啡馆 + 书信网 + Locke + Deism

**公共空间的诞生: 咖啡馆**

1650 年代起, London + Amsterdam + Paris 陆续出现咖啡馆 (coffee house / café). 到 17 世纪末, London 估计有 2000 家以上。咖啡馆的社会意义不是咖啡: 它是**第一批不需要贵族出身就能进入的公共知识空间**。一个商人、一个印刷工人、一个小律师, 花一便士买一杯咖啡, 可以坐一整天, 拿到当天的报纸 (1702 年第一份英国日报《Daily Courant》创刊), 跟陌生人辩论政治和宗教。这在旧制度 (Ancien Régime) 法国是危险的 — 法国警察实际上安插线人在巴黎咖啡馆监听 — 但危险本身说明这个空间的重要性。Jürgen Habermas 在《公共领域的结构转型》(Strukturwandel der Öffentlichkeit, 1962) 里用咖啡馆作为「资产阶级公共领域」(bourgeois public sphere) 概念的历史证据 — 这是一个有争议的框架, 它忽略了女性和工人阶级被排除在大多数咖啡馆讨论之外这一事实, 但它抓住了一个真实变化: 政治讨论第一次在贵族院墙和教会之外有了稳定空间。

> From the 1650s, coffeehouses spread: London had perhaps 2,000 by the 1690s. The drink was not the point. For one penny you got a cup of coffee and the right to sit all day, read newspapers, and argue politics with strangers — regardless of birth rank. This was new. French police actually planted informers in Paris cafés — a sign of how threatening this space was. Habermas's *Structural Transformation of the Public Sphere* (1962) uses coffeehouses as evidence for the "bourgeois public sphere." His framework is contested — women and workers were largely excluded from these discussions — but it captures one real change: political argument now had a stable home outside noble courts and Church buildings.

**Locke 1689: 经验主义 + 自然权利**

约翰 Locke (John Locke, 1632-1704) 在 1689 年同时出版了两部奠基文本, 时间不是巧合 — 他 1683-1689 年在荷兰流亡 (因为卷入反 Charles II 的阴谋), 光荣革命后回国, 带着写好的书稿。《政府论两篇》(Two Treatises of Government): 第一篇反驳 Robert Filmer 的「君权神授」论; 第二篇建立新框架 — **人在自然状态下拥有生命 (life)、自由 (liberty)、财产 (property) 三项自然权利**, 政府存在是为了保护这三项权利, 如果政府违反, 人民有权革命。《人类理解论》(An Essay Concerning Human Understanding, 1690): 人生而无先天观念 (against innate ideas), 心灵是**白板 (tabula rasa)**, 所有知识来自感官经验 + 反思。这两套思想加在一起给了 18 世纪 philosophes 两件武器: 政治上的「权利」语言, 认识论上的「经验主义」(empiricism) — 用经验检验一切, 包括传统宗教权威。

> John Locke (1632-1704) published two foundational texts in 1689, returning from Dutch exile after the Glorious Revolution. *Two Treatises of Government*: government exists to protect natural rights — **life, liberty, and property** — and loses legitimacy if it violates them. *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1690): the mind begins as a **tabula rasa** (blank slate); all knowledge comes from sense experience. Together, these gave the philosophes two weapons: a political language of rights, and an epistemological license to test everything — including Church authority — against experience.

**Spinoza + Leibniz: 理性主义 vs 经验主义的分歧**

荷兰犹太哲学家 Baruch Spinoza (斯宾诺莎, 1632-1677) 的《伦理学》(Ethica, 1677, 死后出版) 是 17 世纪最激进的文本之一: 上帝即自然 (Deus sive Natura), 没有独立于自然的人格神, 没有奇迹, 没有灵魂不朽。Spinoza 被阿姆斯特丹犹太社区逐出 (cherem, 驱逐令), 被基督教圈子视为无神论者。德国哲学家 Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (莱布尼茨, 1646-1716) 1714 年的《单子论》(Monadologie) 走另一条路: 宇宙由无数不可分的精神单元「单子 (Monad)」构成, 上帝是最高单子, 这是一个理性主义 (rationalism) 体系 — 从第一原则用逻辑推导整个宇宙。Jonathan Israel 在《激进启蒙》(Radical Enlightenment, 2001) 里做了一个重要区分: **激进启蒙 (Radical Enlightenment)** — 以 Spinoza/Bayle 为代表, 拒绝上帝、奇迹、等级制; **温和启蒙 (Moderate Enlightenment)** — 以 Locke/Newton 为代表, 保留上帝 (理神论形式) + 现有政治秩序。这个区分重要, 因为 18 世纪 philosophes 大多属于温和派 — 包括 Voltaire — 而激进派往往在地下流通。

> Dutch Jewish philosopher **Baruch Spinoza** (1632-1677): God equals Nature (*Deus sive Natura*), no personal deity, no miracles, no afterlife. He was expelled from the Amsterdam Jewish community and called an atheist by Christians. German **Leibniz** (1646-1716) went the opposite direction: pure rationalism, deriving the whole universe from first principles, God as the supreme Monad. Jonathan Israel's *Radical Enlightenment* (2001) draws a crucial line: **Radical Enlightenment** (Spinoza, Bayle) rejected God and hierarchy altogether; **Moderate Enlightenment** (Locke, Newton) kept a designer God plus existing social order. Most philosophes, including Voltaire, were moderate. The radical stuff circulated underground.

**Deism (自然神论) 1710s-1720s**

光荣革命后的英国知识圈, 在 Newton 《Principia》(1687, SciRev cross-ref) 发表之后, 出现了一种宗教立场叫**自然神论 (Deism)**: 上帝创造了宇宙, 给它设定了自然规律 (引力、运动定律), 然后**不再干涉**。宇宙像一个精密钟表, 上帝是钟表匠, 钟做好之后不需要他每天来拧发条。这意味着: 祈祷没用, 奇迹不存在, 启示宗教 (基督教、伊斯兰教的「上帝直接说话」) 没有认识论基础, 天堂地狱是人类恐惧的发明。英国 Deist 的代表人物有 John Toland (《无奥秘的基督教》Christianity Not Mysterious, 1696) + Matthew Tindal (《创世以来的基督教》Christianity as Old as the Creation, 1730)。Voltaire 是一个终身 Deist: 他不是无神论者 — 他认为宇宙的规律性证明有造物主 — 但他恨一切有组织的宗教 (organised religion), 尤其是 Catholic Church (天主教会)。这区别很重要: Voltaire 的口号「Écrasez l'infâme!」(消灭那个臭名昭著者!) 里的「臭名昭著者」不是上帝, 是教会。

> Newton's *Principia* (1687) inspired a religious position called **Deism**: God set up the universe with natural laws, then stepped back. The universe runs like a clockwork mechanism; God is the clockmaker who built it but does not rewind it. This ruled out miracles, prayer, and revelation — no God-spoke-directly-to-prophet moments. English Deists included John Toland and Matthew Tindal. Voltaire was a lifelong Deist: he believed the regularity of the universe proved a creator, but he despised organised religion. His famous battle cry — **"Écrasez l'infâme!"** ("Crush the infamous thing!") — was aimed at the Church, not at God. The distinction matters.

**Pierre Bayle: 怀疑主义的武器库**

法国 Protestant 学者 Pierre Bayle (皮埃尔·贝勒, 1647-1706) 1685 年 Edict of Fontainebleau 撤销 Edict of Nantes 后流亡荷兰 (Louis XIV 撤销了胡格诺教徒的宗教保护)。1697 年出版《历史与批判词典》(Dictionnaire historique et critique) — 这本书是 18 世纪 philosophes 的标准工具书。Bayle 的方法是: 在词条里先引用传统权威的说法, 然后在注脚里用逻辑把它拆解。圣经人物、历史奇迹、神学教条, 一一用同一把理性刀切开。这本词典直接启发了 Diderot + d'Alembert 1751 年的《百科全书》(Encyclopédie) — 启蒙运动最重要的集体项目 (Part 2 详写)。Bayle 的核心立场: 一个无神论者 (athée) 可以是道德上良好的人 — 这在 1690s 是极端危险的主张, 因为它断掉了「没有地狱就没有道德」这条锁链。

> **Pierre Bayle** (1647-1706), a French Protestant scholar exiled to Holland after Louis XIV revoked Protestant toleration in 1685, published the *Historical and Critical Dictionary* (*Dictionnaire historique et critique*, 1697). His method: present the traditional authority's view, then demolish it in the footnotes with logic. Biblical figures, historical miracles, theological dogmas — he cut them all with the same rational knife. The *Dictionnaire* was the philosophes' standard reference and directly inspired Diderot's *Encyclopédie* (1751, covered in Part 2). Bayle's most explosive claim: an **atheist can be morally good** — which snapped the chain that said "without Hell, no morality."

**Grade 7 钩子: 书信网络 = 「互联网 before 互联网」**

Erasmus 时代 (Reformation Topic cross-ref) 的书信网络是单人中心 — 学者们写信给 Erasmus, 他转发消息。17 世纪末的书信网络已经变成多节点的分布式系统: Locke 跟荷兰学者通信, Bayle 从荷兰给英国 + 法国的 Protestant 圈子发文章, Leibniz 给至少 1000 个通信人写了信 (档案显示约 15000 封)。1680s-1720s 的这张知识网络, 是互联网出现之前人类有过的最有效的思想传播基础设施之一。Voltaire 后来成为这张网的核心节点之一, 他一生写了约 20000 封信。

> Grade 7 hook: Erasmus (Reformation Topic) had a letter-network centred on one person — everyone wrote to him, he relayed news. By the 1690s the network was distributed: Locke wrote to Dutch scholars, Bayle mailed pamphlets to Protestant circles in England and France, Leibniz wrote roughly 15,000 archived letters to over 1,000 contacts. This web was the closest thing to the internet that pre-digital humanity built. Voltaire later became one of its central nodes — he wrote approximately 20,000 letters in his lifetime.

## §3 Voltaire 1694-1726：巴黎文学青年 + 两次 Bastille

**1694.11.21: François-Marie Arouet 出生**

Voltaire 1694 年 11 月 21 日生于巴黎 (Paris), 原名 François-Marie Arouet。父亲 François Arouet 是 minor notary (小公证员) — 法律服务阶层, 有职业训练但没有贵族身份。母亲 Marie Marguerite Daumard 1701 年 Voltaire 7 岁时去世。这个出身背景很重要: Voltaire 终其一生在**两个世界的夹缝里**生存 — 他出身不够高, 进不了贵族圈的顶层; 但他的财富和名声最终超过了大多数贵族。这个夹缝是他性格矛盾的结构性根源: 他批判贵族特权, 同时费尽心机跻身贵族圈; 他攻击「出身」决定命运, 同时为自己购置庄园、争取封号。

> Voltaire was born November 21, 1694, in Paris, as François-Marie Arouet. His father was a minor notary — a trained professional, but not noble. His mother died when he was seven. This origin placed him permanently between worlds: not noble enough to belong to the top, but eventually wealthy and famous enough to surpass most nobles. The gap between these two positions — the man who attacked aristocratic privilege while desperately seeking it — is the structural root of his personality's contradictions.

**Collège Louis-le-Grand: 耶稣会学校的矛盾**

父亲要他学法律, 他要写诗。妥协点是 Collège Louis-le-Grand — 巴黎最好的耶稣会 (Jesuit) 学校, 1704-1711 年 Voltaire 在这里度过了七年。耶稣会的教育方法是 17 世纪欧洲最系统的: 拉丁文读写、修辞学辩论、希腊文选读、话剧表演 (Jesuit 学校用演戏练辩论技巧)。Voltaire 后来以拉丁文精准著称, 以辩论术犀利出名 — 这些工具全来自耶稣会教育。**这是他最深的矛盾之一**: 他终身反耶稣会, 反天主教「迷信」, 但他用来反耶稣会的修辞学武器是耶稣会教会他的 (Ian Davidson, 《Voltaire: A Life》, 2010, p.9-15: Davidson 明确指出 Voltaire 对耶稣会老师的个人情感是复杂的, 他恨这个机构但对某些老师保持终身敬意)。

> Voltaire spent 1704-1711 at the Collège Louis-le-Grand, Paris's finest Jesuit school. The Jesuit curriculum was the most systematic in 17th-century Europe: Latin, rhetoric, Greek, drama (they used theatrical performance to train debating skills). Voltaire's later sharpness — his precision in Latin, his flair for rhetorical devastation — came from this school. **Here is his deepest contradiction**: he spent his life attacking the Jesuits and Catholic superstition, using exactly the rhetorical tools the Jesuits gave him. Ian Davidson's *Voltaire: A Life* (2010) notes that his feelings about individual Jesuit teachers were complex — he hated the institution but maintained lifelong respect for some of the men.

**旧制度 (Ancien Régime) 审查体制: 为什么写一首诗会让你进监狱?**

Grade 7 钩子: 为什么写一首诗会让一个人被关进巴士底狱 (Bastille)?

法国旧制度 (Ancien Régime) 的政治体制不是「独裁者说不准写诗」这么简单。关键机制叫 **lettre de cachet** (国王密令): 国王可以签发一张没有具体罪名、没有审判程序的命令, 把任何人关进 Bastille 或流放。这张命令不需要法院、不需要陪审团、不需要公开理由。Bastille 本来是一座中世纪城堡要塞, 17 世纪起被用作关押「对王权有威胁」的人的政治监狱 — 关的不一定是罪犯, 而是让权贵不舒服的人。被关进 Bastille 最常见的理由是: 写了冒犯国王、皇室或大主教的诗歌或小册子; 散布「危险言论」; 或者有钱的家族花钱买一张 lettre de cachet 把一个麻烦的亲戚关进去。对 12 岁的读者说: 想象你的国家有一条规定, 只要权贵觉得你说的话让他不爽, 他花点钱就能让你消失一年, 不需要任何证明你做错了什么的程序。这就是 Voltaire 生活的法国。

> Grade 7: Why did writing a poem land someone in jail? The key mechanism was the ***lettre de cachet*** — a royal sealed letter that could imprison anyone, for any duration, with no stated charges, no trial, no jury. Bastille was a medieval fortress repurposed as a political holding facility: not for criminals, but for anyone who made powerful people uncomfortable. Common grounds: a poem mocking the King or a bishop; spreading "dangerous ideas"; or a wealthy family paying to lock away an inconvenient relative. Imagine a rule that said: if someone powerful finds your words offensive, they can make you disappear for a year without proving you did anything wrong. That was Voltaire's France.

**1717-1718: 巴士底狱第一次**

1713-1717 年, Voltaire 在巴黎沙龙 (salon) 圈子里以写讽刺诗出名。当时法国的摄政王是 Philippe d'Orléans (奥尔良公爵, 国王 Louis XV 尚年幼)。Voltaire 写了一批诗嘲讽摄政王跟其女儿有乱伦关系 (这是当时在巴黎圈子里流传的流言, Voltaire 把它写成了诗)。1717 年 5 月, 一张 lettre de cachet 到了 — Voltaire 被关进 Bastille, 关了 11 个月。这是他第一次被 Bastille 关押。

牢里的生活并不是最糟糕的: Voltaire 在 Bastille 里写了他第一部悲剧《Œdipe》(俄狄浦斯, Oedipus) 的大部分。1718 年出狱后, 《Œdipe》在 Comédie-Française (法兰西喜剧院) 上演, 连演 45 场, 大获成功。Voltaire 进入巴黎上流沙龙圈。他同时改名: 原名 Arouet 这时开始不用, 采用笔名 **Voltaire** — 最常见的字谜说法是 Arouet le jeune (Arouet 小 = A.L.J.) 的字母换位 (anagram), 但这个说法在文字上对不上 (Davidson 2010 p.24-26: Davidson 指出 Voltaire 改名的真实动机更可能是贵族化 — 新名字听起来像贵族领地名, 与他 notary 家庭的平民姓氏彻底切割)。

> In May 1717, after poems mocking Regent Philippe d'Orléans's alleged incestuous relationship with his daughter circulated Paris salons, a *lettre de cachet* arrived. Voltaire spent 11 months in the Bastille. He used the time well: he drafted most of his first tragedy, *Œdipe* (Oedipus), while imprisoned. On release in 1718, the play ran 45 performances at the Comédie-Française — a major success. He also dropped the name Arouet. The new name **Voltaire** has been explained as an anagram of "Arouet l.j." (Arouet le jeune), but Davidson's biography (2010) argues the real motive was social: the new name sounded like a noble estate, cutting him off from his notary-class origins. He wanted to sound aristocratic.

**1726: 第二次 Bastille + 流放英国**

1726 年初, Voltaire — 现在已经是巴黎知名作家 — 跟贵族 Chevalier de Rohan-Chabot 在剧院发生口角 (起因是 Rohan 嘲笑 Voltaire 的出身, Voltaire 还口, Rohan 怒)。几天后, 一群 Rohan 的仆人在街上截住 Voltaire 把他打了一顿。Voltaire 要求决斗 — 贵族与平民之间的决斗是贵族的权利, Voltaire 没有这个权利, 请求被拒绝。他被再次关进 Bastille。这一次他主动申请: 与其坐牢, 不如流放英国。申请被批准。

这件事的结构值得在课上多停一秒。Voltaire 对「平等」的政治信念, 他对贵族世袭特权的批判 — 这些是他后来写的。但 1726 年, 他的第一反应是要求**决斗权** — 这是贵族阶级的专属权利, 是他批判的那个等级制度的一部分。他不是在要求废除阶级, 他是在要求被纳入上层阶级。Grade 7 钩子: 「Voltaire 恨权贵, 但他一辈子都想成为他们 — 这是他最大的矛盾。」

> In early 1726, Voltaire — now a famous Parisian writer — had a verbal clash with the Chevalier de Rohan-Chabot at the theatre. Rohan mocked his lowly origins; Voltaire answered back sharply. Days later, Rohan's servants ambushed and beat him in the street. Voltaire demanded a duel. But **the right to duel was an aristocratic privilege** — a commoner could not demand it. Request refused. Voltaire went back to the Bastille. This time he asked to be exiled to England instead of imprisoned. Permission granted.
>
> Grade 7 hook: Stop here for a moment. Voltaire's later writings would attack aristocratic privilege and demand equality. But in 1726, his first response to being beaten was to demand a *duel right* — a right that existed **because** of the very class system he attacked. He wasn't asking to abolish classes. He was asking to be admitted to the upper one. He hated the powerful. He wanted to become them. This is his largest contradiction.

## §4 Voltaire 英国流放 1726-1729：「发现英国」

**到达英国: Newton 葬礼的神话**

1726 年 5 月, Voltaire 到达英国 (London 附近, 之后在 Twickenham 住了一段时间)。关于他到达英国的时间, 有一个著名的 Voltaire 自我神话 (self-mythology): 他声称他亲眼目睹了 Isaac Newton 的葬礼, 深受感动, 觉得英国是一个尊重知识的国家 (Newton 1727 年 3 月 20 日死亡, 4 月 4 日在 Westminster Abbey 国葬)。**careful framing**: 历史记录显示 Voltaire 1726 年 5 月才到英国 — Newton 去世时他很可能还在法国或旅途上 (Davidson 2010, p.56-58: Davidson 指出 Voltaire 的英国旅行文件显示他 5 月到达, Newton 已经死了近两个月; Voltaire 可能是从别人那里听说葬礼、后来写进自己的叙述里变成了亲历)。这个细节不是为了贬低 Voltaire, 而是提醒一件事: Voltaire 是一个精心构建自己形象的人 — 他的回忆录和信件里的「亲历叙述」要小心辨别。

> Voltaire arrived in England in May 1726. One of his most-quoted memories: he witnessed Newton's funeral, was moved to see a nation honor its thinkers with a state burial at Westminster Abbey. **Careful framing**: Newton died March 20, 1727, buried April 4. Documents place Voltaire in England from May 1726 — which means he arrived nearly a year before Newton died. Davidson's biography (2010) notes that Voltaire's account of "witnessing" the funeral may be a later self-mythologizing, absorbed from reports and retold as personal memory. This matters not to diminish Voltaire but to flag a habit: he was a meticulous self-image builder, and his "eyewitness" accounts in memoirs and letters require careful checking.

**在英国的三年: 见谁, 看到什么**

Voltaire 在英国 1726-1729 年的三年里干了几件事:

1. 学了一口流利英语 — 他到英国几个月后就能用英语写信和写作
2. 读了 Newton《Principia》和《Opticks》(光学) — 他的法语没有能力直接读《Principia》的数学部分, 他读的更多是第二、三版里的「查询」(Queries) 和 Newton 的推论性文字 (这点重要, §5 的 Émilie 教他 Newton 故事的背景在这里埋下)
3. 见了 Jonathan Swift (《格列佛游记》Gulliver's Travels, 1726) — 两个讽刺作家互相欣赏, 都把讽刺当政治武器
4. 见了 Alexander Pope (《论人》Essay on Man, 《蠢才录》The Dunciad, 1728) — Pope 的 Deism + 讽刺风格跟 Voltaire 高度共鸣
5. 进入 Freemason (共济会) 圈子 — 这是当时英法知识精英的另一个网络节点

**关键发现: 英国是什么?**

Voltaire 在英国看到三件事让他震惊: ①宗教宽容 — 英国允许多种 Protestant 教派 (Quakers 贵格会, Presbyterians 长老会, Anglicans 圣公会) 合法存在, 天主教徒虽然受限但不被烧死; ②议会制度 — 国王受法律约束 (1689 Bill of Rights 回挂 §1); ③Newton 的科学被社会接受 — 当时法国大学里笛卡尔 (Descartes) 的涡旋宇宙论 (vortex theory) 还是主流, 英国已经全面接受 Newton 引力理论 (SciRev cross-ref)。

**careful framing**: Voltaire 的「英国」是一个**理想化构建**, 不是完整图景。他的英国书信里看不到: 英国圈地运动 (enclosure movement) 把几十万农民赶离土地; 英国童工; 英国奴隶贸易 (Britain was at this point the world's largest slave trading nation — Voltaire 后来自己持有 Compagnie des Indes 的股份 Part 2 会详写, 而他在英国期间对英国奴贸没有批评); 爱尔兰天主教徒的系统性歧视。John Robertson 在《The Case for the Enlightenment》(2005) 里指出: philosophes 的「英国」和「荷兰」是示范模型, 不是田野调查报告 — 他们用的是欧洲大陆政治论争的镜子, 不是英国现实。

> Voltaire found three things in England that stunned him: ①**Religious toleration** — multiple Protestant denominations coexisted legally; ②**Parliamentary monarchy** — the king was under law, as he had seen in the 1689 Bill of Rights; ③**Newtonian science accepted** — while French universities still taught Descartes's vortex cosmology, England had moved on to Newton's gravity.
>
> **Careful framing**: Voltaire's "England" was an idealized construction, not a complete picture. His English letters contain no mention of: the enclosure movement displacing hundreds of thousands of rural poor; child labor; England's position as the world's largest slave-trading nation at this period; the systematic oppression of Irish Catholics. John Robertson's *The Case for the Enlightenment* (2005) argues that the philosophes' "England" and "Holland" functioned as rhetorical mirrors for critiquing Continental politics — not as field reports on English reality. Voltaire used England to attack France. He was not reporting England.

**1734《哲学书简》(Lettres philosophiques)**

回到法国后 (1729 年), Voltaire 整理他的英国观察, 1733 年先以英文出版 (Letters Concerning the English Nation), 1734 年法文版在法国出版——立刻被查禁。法文版的副标题在法国的政治背景下是炸弹: 书里有专章讲 Quakers 的简单平等精神 (对比法国天主教复杂繁文), 有专章讲 Newton 和 Locke 优于 Descartes (间接攻击法国学院), 有专章讲英国宗教宽容 (隐含攻击法国对 Huguenot 的迫害)。最后一章直接讲 Pascal——攻击 Pascal《沉思录》里的悲观人性论, 批评它宣扬「人生而有罪、等待神恩救赎」这种观念。

书在法国被当局判为「对宗教和政治有危险倾向」, 同一天下令全部销毁。Voltaire 再度逃离巴黎。

> Back in France from 1729, Voltaire shaped his observations into the *Letters Concerning the English Nation* (English, 1733) / *Lettres philosophiques* (French, 1734). In France, it was immediately banned and ordered burned. Chapters on Quaker simplicity implicitly attacked Catholic ceremony; chapters on Newton and Locke implicitly dismissed French Cartesianism; chapters on English religious toleration implicitly accused France of persecuting Huguenots (Protestant French expelled by Louis XIV's 1685 revocation). The final chapter attacked Pascal's *Pensées* — condemning Pascal's claim that humans are born sinful and helpless without divine grace. The Paris authorities ruled the book "dangerous to religion and the state." Ordered destroyed. Voltaire fled Paris again.

**Grade 7 钩子: 为什么一本书能让全国害怕?**

对 12 岁读者: 一本书有什么可怕的? 《哲学书简》真正危险的不是它里面写了什么新鲜事 — 英国议会、Newton、Locke, 这些法国知识分子都知道。危险的是它把这些事实**组合成一个论证**: 「你们看, 英国有宗教宽容 + 议会 + Newton 科学, 英国比法国更先进。你们法国的问题是天主教会 + 专制国王。」这个论证的危险之处是: 它给每个在旧制度下感到压抑的法国人一个**比较框架**。一旦你能说「别的地方可以这样, 我们为什么不能?」, 现存制度的合法性就开始松动。统治者最害怕的从来不是异端神学, 而是**有具体比较对象的不满**。

> Grade 7: What made a book so dangerous? The *Lettres philosophiques* wasn't revealing new facts — educated French people knew about the English Parliament, Newton, and Locke. Its danger was combining them into one argument: "Look — England has religious tolerance + Parliament + Newtonian science, and England is more advanced than France. France's problem is the Catholic Church + absolute monarchy." That argument handed everyone frustrated with the *Ancien Régime* a **comparison frame**. Once you can say "other places do it differently, why can't we?" — the legitimacy of the current system starts to crack. Rulers have never feared heretical theology as much as they fear **dissatisfaction with a specific alternative in hand**.

## §5 1734-1749 Cirey + Émilie du Châtelet

**逃往 Cirey**

《哲学书简》被查禁后, Voltaire 在 1734 年匆忙离开巴黎。他去了哪里? 去找了 Émilie du Châtelet (埃米莉·杜沙特莱, 1706-1749), 一位他早已认识的贵族女性, 她把自己在 Champagne (香槟省) 的 Cirey-sur-Blaise 庄园提供给他住。接下来 15 年, Voltaire 和 Émilie 在 Cirey 同居 — 两个人都有各自的其他关系, 这不是秘密, 18 世纪上流社会对此有默契的容忍 — 共同从事哲学和科学研究。

> After the *Lettres philosophiques* ban in 1734, Voltaire fled Paris to join Émilie du Châtelet (1706-1749), a noblewoman who offered him refuge at her estate, Cirey-sur-Blaise in Champagne. The next 15 years were intellectually the most productive of his life. Their relationship was a recognized partnership — both had other attachments, which 18th-century high society tolerated with a knowing silence.

**Émilie du Châtelet: 谁在教谁?**

Gabrielle Émilie Le Tonnelier de Breteuil (婚后姓 du Châtelet, 因此称 Marquise du Châtelet, 侯爵夫人) 1706 年生于巴黎贵族家庭, 父亲是 Louis XIV 宫廷的大礼官 (Chief of Protocol)。Émilie 从小接受了异常充分的数学和科学教育 (对一个贵族女性来说极不寻常): 她少年时就能读拉丁文 + 意大利文 + 英文, 并在父亲的朋友圈里接触到了数学家 Maupertuis 等人的指导。

她在 Cirey 的科学工作不是「Voltaire 的助手」。她写了《物理学要点》(Institutions de physique, 1740), 这是一部系统介绍当时自然哲学 (含 Leibniz 的活力 vis viva 概念) 的著作; 更重要的是, 她翻译并注释了 Newton《自然哲学的数学原理》(Principia Mathematica) 的法文版 — 这个译本包含了她自己对 Newton 力学的分析和 Leibniz 能量概念的整合。**这个译本今天仍然是《Principia》的标准法文版本**。

Voltaire 1738 年出版《牛顿哲学原理》(Éléments de la philosophie de Newton) — 这是 18 世纪最成功的 Newton 科学普及书之一, 让牛顿引力理论的法文阐述深入法国知识圈。**careful framing + 反 Whig**: Voltaire 对 Newton 力学的理解, 在很大程度上来自 Émilie 的教导和纠正。他 1730 年代初到英国读《Principia》时, 读懂的主要是 Newton 的通俗表述部分, 他并不能处理《Principia》的数学核心 (拉丁文微积分式的几何证明)。是 Émilie 把真正的 Newton 力学教给他的。Voltaire 自己在书的前言里感谢了她, 但前言之外, 是他的名字在封面上, 是他的声誉从这本书里获益。

这是「SciRev Maria Winkelmann Kirch → Newton 彗星发现」的 18 世纪回声 (SciRev cross-ref): 女性的知识劳动 — 无论是翻译 + 教导, 还是观测 + 计算 — 被记在男性名下, 或者被降格为「帮助」。Londa Schiebinger 在《The Mind Has No Sex?》(1989) 系统梳理了这个结构性模式。Émilie du Châtelet 在这个模式里是一个典型的, 但比大多数人幸运的案例 — 她的《Principia》法译本最终以她自己的名字出版 (1759, 死后出版), 她的学术贡献今天被历史学界承认, 尽管在 18 世纪主要以「Voltaire 的朋友」的身份进入公众视野。

> **Émilie du Châtelet** (1706-1749) was not Voltaire's assistant. At Cirey she wrote the *Institutions de physique* (1740), a systematic natural philosophy incorporating Leibnizian *vis viva* energy concepts. She also produced a full French translation and commentary of Newton's *Principia Mathematica* — still the standard French edition today, published posthumously in 1759. It included her own mathematical analyses and a synthesis of Newtonian and Leibnizian mechanics.
>
> Voltaire published *Éléments de la philosophie de Newton* in 1738 — one of the century's most successful Newton popularizations. **Careful framing and anti-Whig**: Voltaire's understanding of Newtonian mechanics was substantially taught to him by Émilie. He had read *Principia* in England but could not handle its mathematical core (geometrical proofs in Latin calculus notation). She corrected his misunderstandings and supplied the physics. He thanked her in the preface — but his name was on the cover, and his reputation grew from the book. This is the 18th-century echo of Maria Winkelmann Kirch (SciRev Topic): women's intellectual labor — translating, teaching, calculating, observing — credited to men or demoted to "assistance." Londa Schiebinger's *The Mind Has No Sex?* (1989) documents this structural pattern. Du Châtelet was a typical case, and luckier than most — her *Principia* translation was eventually published under her name.

**Grade 7 钩子: Voltaire 把 Newton 介绍给法国——但 Newton 是 Émilie 教他的**

对 12 岁读者: 如果你的数学作业是同学帮你做的, 然后老师只把你的名字写上去, 同学的名字连提都没提 — 你觉得公平吗? 18 世纪的学术世界对女性的系统性遮蔽, 跟这个道理差不多。不同的是, 这不是一次事故, 而是整个知识体系运作的默认规则。Émilie du Châtelet 的《Principia》法译本, 是法国 18 世纪最重要的科学文献之一。她的名字在 1738 年时不在封面上——Voltaire 的名字在。

> Grade 7 hook: Imagine a classmate does most of the real math in your homework, explains the hard parts to you, corrects your mistakes — and then the teacher puts only your name on the board when the assignment is praised. That is approximately what happened with Voltaire's Newton book and Émilie du Châtelet. This wasn't an accident or one bad actor. It was the default operating rule of the 18th-century knowledge system. Du Châtelet's *Principia* translation was one of the most important scientific documents in 18th-century France. In 1738, her name was not on the cover of the book explaining Newton to France. Voltaire's was.

**1749: Cirey 时代的终结**

1749 年 9 月, Émilie du Châtelet 在 Lunéville 宫廷 (洛林公爵的宫廷) 生产后难产去世, 年仅 43 岁。Voltaire 在写给朋友的信里描述她的死时, 措辞既有真实的悲痛, 也有一种他特有的表演性修辞 — 两者在 Voltaire 的写作里很难完全分开。她死后第二年 (1750), Voltaire 应 Frederick the Great (腓特烈大帝, 普鲁士国王) 的邀请去了柏林宫廷 — 这是他人生的下一章 (Part 2 详写)。

> In September 1749, Émilie du Châtelet died from complications of childbirth at the court of Lorraine, aged 43. Voltaire's letters on her death contained genuine grief alongside his characteristic rhetorical self-performance — the two are difficult to fully separate in his writing. The following year, 1750, he accepted Frederick the Great's invitation to Berlin. That chapter comes in Part 2.

## §6 百科全书 1751-1772 + Voltaire 的战斗口号 + Calas 案

### 《百科全书》的计划野心

1747 年, 一个法国书商找到 Denis Diderot (狄德罗), 想要他翻译一本英国百科全书。Diderot 接下这个活, 然后把它变成了欧洲史上最大规模的知识整理工程。他找来数学家 Jean le Rond d'Alembert (达朗贝尔) 做共同主编, 开始约稿。

> In 1747 a bookseller hired Denis Diderot to translate an English encyclopedia. Diderot accepted — then transformed the project into the largest organized knowledge effort Europe had seen. He brought in mathematician **Jean le Rond d'Alembert** as co-editor and began commissioning entries.

1751 年第一卷出版。最终成品是 **17 卷正文加 11 卷图版, 共 28 卷**, 1772 年全部完成。光是图版卷里的铜版插图——制铜匠的锤法、织布机的齿轮、外科手术器械的剖面——就已经是对「手艺知识值得被知识界记录」这件事的一个政治声明。在《百科全书》之前, 大学课程里没有「工匠是知识的贡献者」这种位置。

> The first volume appeared in 1751. The final product: **17 text volumes plus 11 plate volumes, 28 volumes in all**, completed 1772. The engraved plates alone — a coppersmith's hammer technique, a loom's gears, cross-sections of surgical instruments — made a political point before any word was read: craft knowledge deserved to be recorded alongside theology and philosophy. Universities had never assigned artisans that status.

供稿者名单是启蒙欧洲的一份人名录。Voltaire 贡献了大约 40-45 条目 (Frank Kafker 1994《The Encyclopédists as a Group》考证, Kafker 逐条核对笔名、手稿、Voltaire 通信, 确认这个数字)。他的条目包括「品味」(Goût)、「历史」(Histoire)、「才智」(Esprit) 等——写得像他的其他一切东西: 尖刻, 有观点, 嘲讽的对象就在句子里。Rousseau (卢梭) 写了音乐相关条目, d'Alembert 写数学, Montesquieu (孟德斯鸠) 贡献了政治条目。

> The contributor list reads like a directory of Enlightenment Europe. Voltaire contributed roughly 40–45 entries — confirmed by Frank Kafker's *The Encyclopédists as a Group* (1994), which cross-checked pen names, manuscripts, and Voltaire's correspondence. His entries included "Taste" (*Goût*), "History" (*Histoire*), "Wit" (*Esprit*) — written like everything he wrote: pointed, opinionated, with a target already in the sentence. Rousseau handled music entries, d'Alembert mathematics, Montesquieu contributed to political theory.

**但这不是「自由思想的胜利游行」。**

1759 年, 巴黎高等法院 (Paris Parlement) 下令查禁《百科全书》。理由是: 传播「危险原则」, 破坏宗教和王权权威。耶稣会 (Jesuits) 是查禁的主要推动力量——他们控制着法国大部分精英学校, 深度介入王室审查机制。出版商被逼吊销销售许可。

> **But this was not a triumph march for free thought.**
>
> In 1759, the Paris *Parlement* banned the *Encyclopédie*. The charge: spreading "dangerous principles," undermining religion and royal authority. The Jesuits were the primary pressure group — they ran most of France's elite schools and had deep reach into royal censorship mechanisms. Publishers lost their sales licenses.

Diderot 的回应是: 秘密继续印。他在当局默许的灰色地带操作——部分法院官员睁一只眼闭一只眼, 部分是因为他们自己订了——一边假装停刊, 一边把印刷分包到巴黎周边。1765 年 (查禁后 6 年) 最后几卷悄悄完成。

这件事的 careful framing: 1773 年耶稣会被教皇克莱门特十四世解散 (cross-ref: Reformation Topic 的宗教改革长尾效应)。Jesuit 对《百科全书》的阻击失败了, 但这个失败不是因为「启蒙终于赢了」——而是因为欧洲天主教内部的权力博弈, Jesuit 被其他教会派系挤出去了, 启蒙获得的是夹缝里的喘息空间, 不是胜利。

> Diderot's response: keep printing secretly. He operated in a gray zone where some court officials looked away — partly because they had subscriptions themselves. Final volumes quietly finished in 1765, six years after the ban.
>
> A careful framing note: the Jesuits who led the crackdown were themselves dissolved in 1773, when Pope Clement XIV suppressed the order (cross-ref: Reformation Topic). The *Encyclopédie* survived not because "Enlightenment won" — but because Jesuit power collapsed due to internal Catholic politics. The philosophes got breathing room from someone else's fight.

### Voltaire 的战斗口号

Voltaire 的通信口号是「**Écrasez l'infâme!**」(消灭卑劣之物！), 缩写 Écr. l'inf., 他在信件末尾重复它成百上千次。

infâme (卑劣之物) 到底指什么？Voltaire 给了很多版本, 但核心是三件事叠在一起: 天主教迷信 (他认为是对理性的最大伤害), 宗教不宽容 (不同信仰的人被追杀、处死), 和专制王权 (法院不需要证据就能以宗教罪名处决人)。

> Voltaire's letters close with a battle cry: **"Écrasez l'infâme!"** — "Crush the infamous thing!" — repeated hundreds or thousands of times across his correspondence.
>
> *L'infâme* had several targets but three main ones: **Catholic superstition** (what he saw as the greatest enemy of reason), **religious intolerance** (the persecution and execution of people for the wrong beliefs), and **despotic law** (courts that could execute people on religious charges without proper evidence).

这个口号是抽象的。1762 年 Calas 案让它变得具体。

### Calas 案 1762: 用文字打赢的法庭战

1761 年 10 月, 法国南部 Toulouse (图卢兹), 一个叫 Marc-Antoine Calas 的年轻人死在自家店铺里。他的父亲 Jean Calas 是 Protestant (新教徒) 布料商人。天主教邻居传言: Jean Calas 杀了儿子, 因为儿子要改信天主教。Toulouse 的天主教法庭在没有物证的情况下, 1762 年 3 月以谋杀罪判 Jean Calas 极刑: **车裂** (roue) ——先折断四肢, 再勒死, 再烧尸。

> In October 1761, in Toulouse, a young man named Marc-Antoine Calas was found dead in his family's shop. His father Jean Calas was a Protestant cloth merchant. Catholic neighbors spread a rumor: Jean had killed his son to prevent him from converting to Catholicism. Toulouse's Catholic court, without physical evidence, convicted Jean Calas of murder in March 1762. The sentence: **the wheel** (*roue*) — limbs broken on a rack, then strangling, then burning of the corpse.

Voltaire 在 Ferney (他的庄园, 在法瑞边境, 此时他 67 岁) 读到这个判决。他花了三个月时间核实信息来源——他怀疑的不是 Calas 无罪, 他怀疑的是这个程序本身是否有任何证据基础。核实之后他确信: Jean Calas 是无辜的, 法庭是用宗教仇恨替代了司法程序。

他的回应是发动一场横跨欧洲的舆论战: 给腓特烈大帝写信, 给凯瑟琳大帝写信, 给英国知识圈写信, 在《哲学辞典》(Dictionnaire philosophique) 里写相关条目, 写《关于宽容的论文》(*Traité sur la tolérance*, 1763)。David Bien 在《The Calas Affair》(1960) 里把这场运动定位为欧洲近代历史上最早的「媒体舆论翻案」: 不是律师翻案, 而是知识界通过出版物制造压力迫使王权重审。

> Voltaire, 67 years old at his estate Ferney (on the French-Swiss border), read the verdict. He spent three months verifying sources — his suspicion was not just "the man is innocent" but "was there any evidentiary basis at all?" His conclusion: Jean Calas was innocent; the court had substituted religious hatred for legal procedure.
>
> He launched a pan-European press campaign: letters to Frederick the Great of Prussia, to Catherine the Great of Russia, to English intellectual circles, entries in his *Dictionnaire philosophique*, and the full treatise *Traité sur la tolérance* (Treatise on Tolerance, 1763). David Bien's *The Calas Affair* (1960) positions this as one of Europe's earliest "media-driven legal reversals" — not lawyers winning a retrial, but the intellectual press creating enough pressure on royal power to force one.

1765 年 3 月, Jean Calas 被正式平反——3 年后。他本人已死, 被平反的是他的名字和他的家庭。

**Grade 7 钩子**: 「Voltaire 在 67 岁的时候拿起笔, 打了一场他永远见不到结果的法庭战——Jean Calas 1762 年就死了, 平反是 1765 年。这比他写的所有书加起来更能说明一件事: 文字是什么武器。」

> **A note for young readers:** Voltaire was 67 when he picked up his pen for Jean Calas. Jean was already dead by 1762 — the exoneration in 1765 cleared a name, not a living person. That Voltaire fought anyway, for three years, across the courts of three countries, tells you more about what words can do than any of his philosophy books.

## §7 Voltaire 的矛盾: 奴贸投资 + 反犹辞典 (perpetrator-actor 核心)

### 他的财富从哪里来

Voltaire 不靠稿费生活。他是 18 世纪欧洲最精明的金融投机者之一。他的财富主要来源有三个: lottery 操作 (他发现某些国家彩票的数学漏洞, 组织财团系统性地购票套利), 贵族放贷 (向法国贵族提供短期借贷, 收高息), 以及公司股份——其中最重要的是 **Compagnie des Indes** (法国东印度公司) 的股份。

Nicholas Cronk 在《The Cambridge Companion to Voltaire》(2009, p.20) 引用 Voltaire 的财务记录: 他在 1720 年代到 1750 年代持有 Compagnie des Indes 的股份, 这是他投资组合的重要组成部分。Voltaire 死时的遗产折合约 200 万法郎 (Livres tournois) ——相当于今天至少数千万美元的购买力——在 18 世纪作家里是异常巨额的数字。

> Voltaire did not live off royalties. He was among the 18th century's most skilled financial speculators. His wealth came from three main sources: **lottery exploitation** (he identified mathematical flaws in certain state lotteries and organized syndicates to systematically arbitrage them), **aristocratic lending** (high-interest loans to French nobles), and **company shares** — most importantly, shares in the **Compagnie des Indes** (French East India Company).
>
> Nicholas Cronk's *The Cambridge Companion to Voltaire* (2009, p.20) cites Voltaire's financial records: he held Compagnie des Indes shares across the 1720s–1750s, a significant portion of his portfolio. He died worth roughly 2 million *livres tournois* — at least tens of millions in modern purchasing power — extraordinary for an 18th-century writer.

### Compagnie des Indes 是一台什么机器

Compagnie des Indes 的业务范围包括: 与印度的纺织品贸易, 与中国的茶叶贸易, 向加勒比种植园运输物资——以及通过 **asiento** 分成模式 (与奴隶贸易代理商的利润分成合同) 与西非奴隶贸易的关联。它是一台殖民地贸易机器, 利润来自整个帝国体系, 而这个体系的底层是种植园劳动力, 种植园劳动力的底层是大西洋奴隶贸易。

**这里需要一个 careful framing**: Voltaire 投资 Compagnie des Indes 是有历史记录的事实。「他是否清楚地知道他持有的股份中有多大比例的利润直接来自奴隶贸易」——Cronk hedge 了这个问题: Voltaire 知道自己投资的是一家殖民地贸易公司, 但他是否追踪过每一项业务与奴贸的具体关联, 没有直接证据。

历史学家的标准表述 (Laurent Dubois 2004《Avengers of the New World》):「Voltaire 的财富来自他投资的公司, 这些公司的利润来自殖民地贸易——他不是奴隶船的直接经营者, 但他是殖民地经济的受益者。」

**〔补充更新研究〕** 这里要补一句：更新的研究 Giovannetti-Singh 2022（*Racial Capitalism in Voltaire's Enlightenment*, History Workshop Journal）给出了更硬的事实——Voltaire 曾在 **1752 年直接出资一次贩奴航程**（资助 San Jorge 号）。所以「他不是奴隶船的直接经营者」更准确的说法是「他不是奴隶船的**日常**经营者」——「从不直接沾手贩奴」这种绝对说法不能成立。

这两者之间的区别不是开脱, 而是精确: **知情 (knowing) 与受益 (benefiting) 可以同时成立, 即使知情程度是不完整的。**

**时间线精确 framing（Cronk 2009 p.20）**：Voltaire 的 Compagnie des Indes 持股高峰在 **1720s-1750s**，而《Candide》第 19 章里那段苏里南黑奴描写写于 **1759 年**——他的持股高峰期当时已经过去。这个时间顺序值得学生注意：他在持股期间没有任何公开文字直接批评奴贸；对奴隶制最直接的描写，出现在他大规模持股的高峰之后。历史学家无法确认这是偶然时间差还是有意回避，Cronk 2009 明确注明「证据不足以确认」。（**careful framing**：更新的研究 Giovannetti-Singh 2022 认为 Voltaire 其实**终身持股**，因此「写《Candide》时持股已基本清盘」这一时间线不能下死结论——持股与批奴文字的时间关系，史学界有不同解读。）

> The Compagnie des Indes operated across: Indian textile trade, Chinese tea trade, Caribbean plantation supply chains — and connections to the West African slave trade via **asiento** profit-sharing contracts with slave trade intermediaries. It was a colonial trade machine whose profits flowed from the entire imperial system, and that system's foundation was plantation labor, and plantation labor's foundation was the Atlantic slave trade.
>
> **A careful framing is needed here.** That Voltaire held Compagnie des Indes shares is documented fact. Whether he tracked exactly what percentage of his dividend profits flowed directly from slave-trade revenues — that Cronk hedges: Voltaire knew he was investing in a colonial trade company, but whether he traced each business line to its specific labor source is not documented.
>
> The historian's standard formulation (Laurent Dubois, *Avengers of the New World*, 2004): "Voltaire's wealth came from companies whose profits came from colonial trade — he was not a direct slave ship operator, but he was a beneficiary of the colonial economy." The distinction is not an acquittal. It is precision: **knowing (even partially) and benefiting can coexist**.
>
> **A note on more recent research:** Giovannetti-Singh 2022 (*Racial Capitalism in Voltaire's Enlightenment*, History Workshop Journal) establishes harder facts: Voltaire **directly financed a slave-trading voyage in 1752** (the *San Jorge*) and appears to have held his colonial shares for life. So "not a direct slave ship operator" is more accurately "not a *day-to-day* slave ship operator" — and the older claim that his shareholding "had essentially been liquidated by the time he wrote *Candide*" cannot be treated as settled. The timing relationship between his shareholding and his anti-slavery writing is read differently by different historians.

### 《各民族风俗论》1756: 种族 hierarchy

1756 年 Voltaire 出版《各民族风俗论》(*Essai sur les mœurs et l'esprit des nations*), 这是他最重要的历史著作, 雄心勃勃地想写一部「全人类的历史」——不只是欧洲史, 而是从中国、印度、伊斯兰世界开始写。这个出发点在 18 世纪是开创性的。

但这本书里的「黑人民族」描述——Voltaire 用了当时欧洲知识界通行的种族 hierarchy 框架: 不同「民族」或「种族」在智力、道德能力上有等级差异, 非洲人被放在这个 hierarchy 的底层。

Careful framing: 这套种族 hierarchy 表述符合 18 世纪欧洲知识界主流——Buffon、Hume、Kant 都有类似的种族论述。这不是对 Voltaire 的开脱, 而是一个更令人不安的事实: **启蒙运动的「所有人的理性」, 从一开始就有一个注脚——「但哪些人算人？这个问题, 启蒙运动主流没有认真回答」**。

> In 1756 Voltaire published *Essai sur les mœurs et l'esprit des nations* (Essay on the Customs and Spirit of Nations), his most ambitious historical work — a global history starting from China, India, and the Islamic world, not just Europe. The ambition was genuinely novel for the 18th century.
>
> But the book's treatment of "Black nations" employs the racial hierarchy framework standard in European intellectual circles at the time: different "peoples" or "races" possess different gradations of intellectual and moral capacity, with Africans placed at the bottom of this hierarchy.
>
> A careful framing: this racial hierarchy language was mainstream in 18th-century European thought — Buffon, Hume, and Kant wrote similar frameworks. That is not a defense of Voltaire. It is a more disturbing fact: **Enlightenment's "reason for all" had a footnote from the beginning — "but who counts as a person capable of reason?" The Enlightenment mainstream never seriously answered that question.**

### 《哲学辞典》1764: 反犹内容

1764 年 Voltaire 出版《哲学辞典》(*Dictionnaire philosophique*), 这是他最广泛流传的作品之一, 按字母排列, 条目短而辛辣, 是启蒙「理性批判」的工具书。

其中「Juifs」(犹太人) 条目: Arthur Hertzberg 在《The French Enlightenment and the Jews》(1968) 里做了详细的文本考证。Voltaire 的条目对犹太人的描述混合了宗教批判和文化 stereotype: 对犹太教起源的嘲讽、对犹太人商业行为的贬称、对犹太文化的傲慢评价。他的框架是「理性批判一切宗教」——他攻击基督教, 也攻击犹太教的历史起源。

Hertzberg 1968 的关键论断:「法国启蒙运动对现代反犹主义的形成有贡献——不是宗教反犹 (*religious antisemitism*), 而是世俗/族裔反犹 (*secular/ethnic antisemitism*) 的早期形态。」传统的宗教反犹说「犹太人杀了基督」; 启蒙式反犹说「犹太人的文化和思维方式是落后的」——后者的危险在于它戴着「理性」的面具。

> Voltaire's 1764 *Dictionnaire philosophique* contains the entry **"Juifs"** (Jews). Arthur Hertzberg's *The French Enlightenment and the Jews* (1968) provides the detailed textual analysis: Voltaire's entry mixes religious critique with cultural stereotyping — mockery of Jewish religious origins, derogatory characterization of Jewish commercial behavior, condescension toward Jewish culture. His framing: "rational criticism of all religions," attacking Christianity and Judaism alike.
>
> Hertzberg's key argument: "The French Enlightenment contributed to the formation of modern antisemitism — not *religious* antisemitism, but an early form of *secular/ethnic* antisemitism." Traditional religious antisemitism said "Jews killed Christ." Enlightenment-inflected antisemitism said "Jewish culture and modes of thought are backward" — the latter's danger was that it wore the mask of **reason**.

**Grade 7 表述**: 「Voltaire 批判基督教的武器是『理性』。他用同一把武器攻击犹太人——他告诉自己这是理性批判宗教, 但他的靶子里有很多不是宗教, 是偏见。有时候最危险的偏见就是那些穿着理性衣服的。」

> **For young readers:** Voltaire's weapon against the Church was "reason." He aimed the same weapon at Jewish people — telling himself it was rational religious criticism, but many of his targets were not religion. They were stereotypes. Sometimes the most dangerous prejudice is the kind dressed up in the language of rational analysis.

### perpetrator-actor 总结

「Voltaire 是启蒙矛盾的人格化: 他用笔为 Jean Calas 平冤, 同时他的钱在另一台压迫机器里运转。他写文章反对宗教不宽容, 同时他的《哲学辞典》给了世俗反犹主义一个洗练的模板。他谴责欧洲王权, 同时他是这个王权支撑的殖民地经济的股东。」

这不是说 Voltaire 是伪君子。伪善要求当事人自知其矛盾而继续。Voltaire 的情况更复杂: 他的矛盾是 18 世纪欧洲启蒙主义的**结构性矛盾**, 不是个人道德的失败——宣称「所有人的理性」, 同时从没有问过「所有人」具体包括谁。

> Voltaire is the human face of Enlightenment contradiction: he used his pen to exonerate Jean Calas, while his money ran inside another machine of oppression. He wrote against religious intolerance while his *Dictionnaire* gave secular antisemitism a polished template. He condemned royal despotism while holding shares in the colonial economy that despotism protected.
>
> This is not to call him a hypocrite. Hypocrisy requires knowing your contradiction and continuing anyway. Voltaire's situation is more troubling: his contradictions were the **structural contradictions of 18th-century Enlightenment itself** — claiming "reason for all" while never seriously asking who "all" included.

Saint-Domingue 的甘蔗田里, Toussaint Louverture 在 1750 年代还是种植园里的一个孩子 (他约 1743 年生)。Voltaire 的《百科全书》条目在 Paris 的沙龙里被朗读。这两个事实同时存在, 互不干扰, 直到 1791 年才开始碰撞。

> In the sugarcane fields of Saint-Domingue, Toussaint Louverture — born around 1743 — was a child on a plantation in the 1750s. Voltaire's *Encyclopédie* entries were being read aloud in Paris salons. These two facts coexisted simultaneously, undisturbed, until 1791.

## §8 Voltaire 晚年: Ferney + 《Candide》+ 1778 回 Paris

### 《Candide》1759: 反乐观主义的小说

1755 年 11 月 1 日, 里斯本发生大地震, 加上地震引发的火灾和海啸, 死亡人数在 17,000 至 50,000 人之间 (careful framing: 当时的统计极不可靠, 现代估计范围很宽, 见 T.D. Kendrick 1956《The Lisbon Earthquake》)。这一天是天主节 (All Saints' Day), 大量死亡发生在教堂里做弥撒的人当中。

这个灾难在欧洲知识界引发了一场关于「上帝是否善意」的哲学大辩论。Leibniz (莱布尼茨, 已死于 1716) 有一个著名的论点: 上帝创造了「所有可能世界中最好的那个世界」(*the best of all possible worlds*)。如果最好的世界里会有 17,000 人在教堂里被压死, 这个论点还能成立吗？

> On November 1, 1755, Lisbon was struck by an earthquake followed by fire and tsunami. Death toll estimates range from 17,000 to over 50,000 — careful framing: contemporary records were unreliable, and modern estimates span a wide range (T.D. Kendrick, *The Lisbon Earthquake*, 1956). The date was All Saints' Day; many deaths occurred among people attending Mass inside churches.
>
> The disaster triggered a philosophical argument across European intellectual circles. Leibniz (died 1716) had famously argued that God created "the best of all possible worlds." If the best possible world contained 17,000 people crushed in churches, could the argument hold?

Voltaire 的回答是 1759 年的《Candide, ou l'Optimisme》(*Candide, or Optimism*)——一本讽刺小说, 主角 Candide 从小被他的家庭教师 Pangloss 教导「一切皆是最好的安排」, 然后他经历了战争、强奸、宗教裁判所的火刑、里斯本大地震、海难, 直到小说末尾满目疮痍地得出结论:「我们必须耕种我们的花园」(*il faut cultiver notre jardin*)。

**《Candide》第 19 章: 苏里南的黑奴**

《Candide》里有一处对奴隶制的直接描写, 发生在第 19 章, 在苏里南 (南美 Dutch 殖民地)。Candide 遇到一个躺在地上的黑人, 少了左腿和右手。黑人解释: 在糖厂里, 机器轧掉了手指时, 监工会砍去整只手; 如果试图逃跑, 会被砍去腿。「这就是欧洲人吃糖的代价。」

然后 Candide 继续他的旅程。

这一段在整本书里大约两段的篇幅。Laurent Dubois 在《Avengers of the New World》(2004) 里引用这一段作为一个重要的节点: Voltaire 知道奴隶制的存在, 他把它写进了他最重要的文学作品里——作为一个悲剧性的道德事实。但他的主角看了, 感慨了, 继续走了。

**Grade 7 careful framing**: 「Voltaire 知道奴隶存在。他在《Candide》里给了奴隶两段篇幅, 让他说出了真相。然后他的主角离开了, 去找自己的花园。这种『知道——记录——继续走』, 是 18 世纪大多数欧洲知识分子对奴隶制的标准姿态。把它写进书里, 和真的停下来, 是两件不同的事。」

> *Candide*, Chapter 19, Surinam (a Dutch South American colony): Candide encounters a Black man lying on the ground, missing his left leg and right hand. The man explains: in the sugar mills, when the grinding machines take a finger, the overseer cuts the hand; when someone tries to escape, they cut the leg. "This is the price Europeans pay for their sugar."
>
> Then Candide continues his journey.
>
> This passage is roughly two paragraphs in the full novel. Laurent Dubois (*Avengers of the New World*, 2004) cites it as a significant moment: Voltaire knew slavery existed, and he put it into his most important literary work — as a tragic moral fact. His protagonist saw it, felt the weight of it, and kept walking.
>
> **For young readers:** Voltaire knew enslaved people existed. He gave one of them two paragraphs in his most famous book, and let him speak the truth. Then his hero left to find his own garden. That "knowing — recording — moving on" was the standard posture of most 18th-century European intellectuals on slavery. Writing it into a book, and actually stopping, are two different things.

### Ferney 王国 1759-1778

1759 年 (《Candide》出版同年), Voltaire 买下 Ferney 庄园, 定居在法国和瑞士边境。选址是经过计算的: 如果法国当局来抓他, 他步行几分钟可以越过边境到瑞士; 如果日内瓦当局来, 他也可以往法国这边退。他在两个主权之间给自己保了一个永久的逃跑选项。

> In 1759 — the same year he published *Candide* — Voltaire bought the Ferney estate on the French-Swiss border. The location was calculated: if French authorities came for him, he could walk into Switzerland within minutes; if Geneva moved against him, he could retreat into France. He had permanently insured himself a sovereign escape route between two sovereigns.

在 Ferney 的将近 20 年, Voltaire 建立了他的小「王国」: 一个钟表工坊 (雇用了约 300 名工匠), 一个制丝工厂, 一个私人剧院, 一个科学实验花园。他接待来访的欧洲知识界——Boswell (约翰逊的传记作者) 来访记录了一次著名对话; 俄国凯瑟琳大帝与他通信 25 年, 她后来买下他的全部图书馆 (今藏圣彼得堡俄罗斯国家图书馆)。

他同时继续写——每天写信、写文章、写戏剧——他的通信量在欧洲是传奇级别的: 现存信件约 20,000 封。这是 18 世纪没有电话、没有电报、没有印刷机之外的任何通讯技术的情况下, 一个人用手写字创造的信息网络。

> In nearly 20 years at Ferney, Voltaire built a small "kingdom": a watchmaking workshop (employing roughly 300 artisans), a silk factory, a private theater, and a scientific garden. He hosted visiting European intellectuals — Boswell (later Johnson's biographer) recorded a famous conversation there. Catherine the Great of Russia corresponded with him for 25 years and ultimately bought his entire library (now in the Russian National Library, St. Petersburg).
>
> He kept writing — letters, essays, plays, every day. His correspondence is legendary: roughly 20,000 letters survive. This was an information network built entirely by hand, with no technology beyond paper and ink and the postal system.

### 1778 回 Paris: 城市的拥抱

1778 年 2 月, Voltaire 离开 Ferney 回 Paris——他已经 28 年没有踏上这座城市。这是他一生里最奇异的一幕: 他整整一生都在攻击 Paris 的天主教机构、攻击法国王权的专制、攻击 Paris 精英的虚伪——然后他 83 岁回来, 被这座城市当成了英雄。

Comédie-Française (法兰西喜剧院) 演他的戏剧《厄律菲勒》(*Irène*), 他出席首演。观众在剧院里高呼他的名字, 给他戴桂冠。他在 Paris 的公寓每天有数十人来访。他在信里写: 他被人们的热情压垮了, 他需要休息, 他的身体撑不住了。

1778 年 5 月 30 日, Voltaire 死在 Paris。他死的时候, 天主教会以他「没有做临终忏悔」为由, 拒绝给他教会葬礼——他的遗体被秘密送出 Paris, 葬在他侄子管理的修道院里 (Scellières)。

1791 年大革命期间, 他的遗体被移到 Paris 万神殿 (Panthéon), 成为法兰西共和国的「国家英雄」。

**Grade 7 钩子**: 「他一生攻击巴黎的教会和权力, 但他最后死在那里, 被这座城市拥抱——然后被这座城市埋进了它的英雄祠。历史最喜欢这种讽刺: 你一生的对手, 有时候最后会把你变成它自己的神话。」

> In February 1778, Voltaire left Ferney for Paris — a city he had not entered in 28 years, a city he had spent 28 years attacking from a distance. What followed was one of the strangest scenes of his life: the man who had spent decades denouncing Paris's Catholic institutions, royal despotism, and elite hypocrisy walked in at 83 and was treated like a returning emperor.
>
> The Comédie-Française staged his play *Irène*; he attended the premiere. The audience chanted his name and placed a laurel wreath on his head. Dozens of visitors crowded his Paris apartment every day. He wrote in letters that the public's enthusiasm was crushing him, that his body could no longer hold.
>
> Voltaire died in Paris on May 30, 1778. The Catholic Church refused him a church burial — he had not made his final confession, they said — and his body was secretly smuggled out of the city to a monastery in Scellières.
>
> In 1791, during the Revolution, his remains were transferred to the Paris Panthéon, making him a hero of the French Republic.
>
> **For young readers:** He spent his life attacking Paris's church and its power structures. He died there, embraced by the city — and then buried in its hall of national heroes. History has a taste for this kind of irony: sometimes your lifelong adversary ends up turning you into its own mythology.

## §9 Olympe de Gouges (1748-1793): 屠夫女儿 + Paris + 1791 《女性宣言》

### 出生: Montauban, 1748

1748 年 5 月 7 日, 法国南部 Montauban (蒙托邦), 一个叫 Marie Gouze 的女孩出生了。她父亲 Pierre Gouze 是屠夫, 母亲 Anne Olympe Mouisset。

Careful framing: 关于她的出生有一个传说, Olympe 本人在某些时刻似乎相信它——传说中她的「真正父亲」是当地贵族 Marquis Lefranc de Pompignan, 诗人。这个说法至今没有文件证据, Olivier Blanc 在《Olympe de Gouges》(2003) 里做了详细考证后给出 hedge: 可能是 Olympe 的母亲告诉过她这个故事, 可能 Olympe 自己需要这个贵族血统的想象来支撑她后来闯入贵族-知识圈的身份; 但历史记录里这个说法没有实证基础。

> On May 7, 1748, in Montauban, southern France, a girl named **Marie Gouze** was born. Her father, Pierre Gouze, was a butcher. Her mother, Anne Olympe Mouisset.
>
> A careful framing is needed about her birth. A legend surrounds it — one that Olympe herself sometimes seemed to believe: that her real father was a local nobleman, the Marquis Lefranc de Pompignan, a poet. Olivier Blanc's *Olympe de Gouges* (2003) provides detailed investigation of this claim and issues a hedge: the story may have come from her mother, and Olympe may have needed the imagined aristocratic lineage as a kind of credential when she later pushed her way into elite intellectual circles. There is no documentary evidence for the claim.

她的原名不是 Gouges, 是 Gouze。她后来改了。这个改动本身是一个故事。

---

### 婚嫁、逃脱、Paris

约 1765 年, 她 17 岁, 嫁给了一个叫 Louis Aubry 的男人, 他是帽商。她生了一个儿子。Aubry 于 1766 年或 1767 年死——具体年份不确定 (Blanc 2003 考证, 教区记录有空缺)。

Aubry 死后, Olympe 拒绝再嫁。她后来写下:「婚姻是妇女的墓穴」(*le mariage est le tombeau de la confiance et de l'amour*)——婚姻是信任和爱的坟墓。这句话是她在 30 年后写的, 回望的是她 17 岁那年的经历。

> Around 1765, at 17, she married Louis Aubry, a hat merchant. She bore a son. Aubry died in 1766 or 1767 — the exact year is uncertain (Blanc 2003 notes gaps in the parish records).
>
> After Aubry's death, Olympe refused to remarry. She later wrote: "Marriage is the tomb of trust and love" (*le mariage est le tombeau de la confiance et de l'amour*) — a line she put on paper 30 years later, looking back at being 17.

1768 年前后, 她带着儿子到了 Paris。她改了名字——把 Gouze 改成 Gouges (让它读起来更贵族化), 取了 Olympe 这个名字 (来自她母亲的名字 Olympe)。她以「寡妇 Aubry」的身份进入社交圈——在 18 世纪, 寡妇的法律地位比已婚女性高得多: 她可以签合同, 可以独立持有财产, 可以在某些场合代表自己。

> Around 1768 she arrived in Paris with her son. She changed her name — transforming *Gouze* into *Gouges* (to sound more aristocratic) and taking the given name Olympe (borrowed from her mother). She entered social circles as "the widow Aubry" — in 18th-century France, a widow's legal status was considerably higher than a married woman's: she could sign contracts, hold property independently, represent herself in certain legal contexts.

她进入了 Paris 的戏剧圈。她没有受过正式教育——她的识字能力是后来自学补起来的, 她的早期手稿语法错误很多。但她有语言直觉, 有剧场感, 有愿意为她磨稿的合作者。她开始写剧本。

> She entered Paris theater circles. She had no formal education — literacy came through later self-study, and her early manuscripts were grammatically rough. But she had an instinct for language, a sense for the stage, and collaborators willing to polish her drafts. She began writing plays.

---

### 1780-1789: 巴黎戏剧圈的九年

**缺口 1 补充 (Blanc 2003)**

Olympe de Gouges 在 Paris 戏剧圈站稳脚跟用了将近十年。Olivier Blanc 的研究表明, 她在 1770 年代末到 1780 年代, 主要活动于 Palais-Royal 一带——这是当时 Paris 最密集的文化地带: 咖啡馆、书摊、剧院、沙龙, 以及各种非正式的政治聚会场所, 聚集了记者、剧作家、废奴派人士和将来的革命者。

她没有固定的赞助人 (patron)。这一点与同时代大多数依赖贵族资助的作家不同。Blanc 的考证显示, 她维持生计的方式是多元的: 写作、出版, 以及她在某个时期与一位富商 Jacques Biétrix de Rozières 保持了一段较为稳定的同居关系——她拒绝正式再婚, 但接受了他的经济支持。Blanc 明确指出: 这段关系是她选择的, 不是她被迫的; 她保留了自己作为独立寡妇的法律身份, 以便能够继续签署合同和出版文件。

> **Gap 1 expansion (Blanc 2003)**
>
> Olympe de Gouges spent nearly a decade establishing herself in Paris theater circles. Olivier Blanc's research places her primary activity in the late 1770s through the 1780s around the Palais-Royal — then the densest cultural district in Paris: cafés, booksellers, theaters, salons, and informal political gatherings that drew journalists, playwrights, abolitionist thinkers, and future revolutionaries.
>
> She had no fixed patron. This distinguished her from most writers of her era, who depended on aristocratic sponsorship. Blanc's research shows she supported herself through multiple means: writing, publishing, and a period of domestic partnership with a wealthy merchant, Jacques Biétrix de Rozières — a relationship she chose, and from which she retained her legal identity as an independent widow rather than remarrying. That status mattered: it was what allowed her to sign contracts and publish in her own name.

她写剧本的方式与正统戏剧人不同。她的草稿——Blanc 考证过若干手稿——语法不规范, 句子有时散漫, 用的是她从口语直接转写的节奏, 而不是受过古典训练的作家惯用的那种书面法语。她有合作者帮她修改语言, 但主意和框架是她的。

她出没的圈子里有一些将来成为 Girondins (吉伦特派) 的人物——Girondins 是大革命中的温和共和派, 主张宪政、反对激进专制。Blanc 记录她与 Girondins 人脉的接触是零散的, 没有文件证明她这一时期正式参与过任何政治团体; 但她在政治上的立场倾向在 1789 年之后会越来越清晰地站到立宪派一侧。

> Her method of writing plays differed from trained dramatists. Blanc's examination of surviving manuscripts shows her drafts were grammatically unruly — sentences built from spoken rhythms rather than the written register of classically trained writers. She had collaborators to smooth the language, but the ideas and structure were hers.
>
> Her social circle included figures who would later become Girondins — the moderate republican faction of the Revolution, favoring constitutionalism over radical Jacobin centralism. Blanc's documentation of her contact with future Girondin figures during this period is fragmentary; no documents establish formal political membership. But her political orientation — toward constitutionalism, against extremism — would become explicit after 1789.

这里有一个细节值得停下来想: Palais-Royal 是当时 Paris 唯一一个贵族「治外」的公共广场——Orleans 公爵 (Duc d'Orléans) 为了收入开放了这片产业, 租给咖啡馆、书店、剧场, 因此这里不受警察日常管控。它成了一个言论相对自由的泡泡: 你可以在这里讨论政治, 出版没有审查的小册子, 认识各种背景的人。Gouges 在这个地带度过的九年, 是她思想形成的关键背景。Blanc (2003) 把她大革命前的活动描述为「在 Paris 边缘知识圈缓慢积累声誉」——她不是在中心, 但她在中心旁边, 足够近到看清楚里面的人在说什么、做什么, 又足够边缘到知道那些「在中心」的人把哪些人排除在外了。

> One detail is worth pausing on: the Palais-Royal was the only major public space in Paris that operated outside normal police jurisdiction — the Duc d'Orléans had opened it to commercial tenants, including cafés, booksellers, and theaters, which meant the usual censorship mechanisms did not apply there. It became a relative bubble of free expression: political arguments, uncensored pamphlets, cross-class encounters. The nine years Gouges spent in this district were the formative context for her ideas. Blanc (2003) characterizes her pre-Revolutionary trajectory as "a slow accumulation of reputation in Paris's peripheral intellectual circles" — not at the center, but close enough to observe who was there and what they said, and marginal enough to notice exactly which voices the center had shut out.

**Grade 7 钩子**: 「Gouges 花了九年在 Paris 边缘的圈子里。她不是在中心——但她够近, 近到看得清楚里面的规则。这种位置有时候比在中心更危险: 你看见了别人没有打算让你看见的东西。」

> **For young readers:** Gouges spent nine years in Paris's peripheral circles. She wasn't at the center — but she was close enough to see the rules clearly. That position can be more dangerous than being inside: you see things the center never intended to let you see.

### 1780 年代: 为奴隶制写剧本

在《百科全书》一代知识分子纷纷老去或去世的 1780 年代, Olympe de Gouges 已经在 Paris 的戏剧圈里建立了一个位置——不是中心位置, 而是边缘上的一个声音, 一个说别人不说的事情的声音。

她写了一部戏剧:《奴隶制》(*L'Esclavage des Nègres, ou l'Heureux Naufrage*, 黑人的奴役, 或幸运的海难), 1784 年完成, 1789 年才上演。这是法语戏剧史上最早正面以人性视角描写黑奴生活的剧本之一——不是用黑奴作为异域背景或道德寓言, 而是给他们写台词, 让他们有内心世界。

Comédie-Française (法兰西喜剧院) 拒绝了这部剧。不是一次, 是在 1784 年到 1789 年之间至少三次提交, 三次书面拒绝——Blanc 2003 记录了其中保存下来的拒绝理由, 措辞是: 「题材有损观众体验」(*le sujet nuit à l'agrément du spectateur*)。这个表述本身值得停下来想一下: 剧院不是说这个剧本写得不好, 也不是说它在法律上有问题; 它说的是——奴隶制这个题材会让观众不舒服。而这恰恰证明了 Gouges 的直觉是对的: 人们知道这件事, 人们不想被迫去感受它。

1789 年大革命改变了这个方程式。国民议会开始辩论殖民地与自由的问题, 废奴组织 Société des amis des Noirs (黑人之友协会, 成立 1788 年) 已经公开活动, 关于奴隶制的话语在 Paris 公共场合变得可以说出来了。在这个背景下, Comédie-Française 在压力下同意上演《奴隶制》——1789 年 12 月。演出了几场, 然后撤了。台下的观众反应, 史料记载是混乱的: 有人鼓掌, 有人离场, 有人抗议。

**关于她为什么写这个题材**: 她本人在剧本的前言里解释了一部分——但这个解释需要 careful framing。她写道, 她同情被压迫者 (les opprimés), 她认为奴隶制是对人性的侵犯。但 Joan Scott (1996) 在分析她的文本时指出一个更深的结构: Gouges 作为一个在性别上被 Paris 正式体制排斥的女性, 对「被系统性排斥」这件事有一种来自切身经验的理解——这种理解让她能够看见远在 Saint-Domingue 的黑奴的处境, 不是作为抽象的道德课题, 而是作为她熟悉的那种结构: 有人因为出生就被排除在「人」的定义之外。Scott 的分析没有说 Gouges「理解了」种族压迫的全部; 而是说她找到了一条联结路径, 即使这条路径经过的是她自己的性别困境。

**剧本内容的开创性**: 《L'Esclavage des Nègres》在法语戏剧史里的意义不只是「第一部反奴隶制的戏」——法国此前有过以奴隶为背景的戏剧, 但黑人角色在那些剧里通常是功能性的: 作为异域色彩, 作为白人道德的映衬, 没有属于自己的内心。Gouges 的剧本给主要的黑人角色写了自己的情感逻辑, 写了他们对自由的渴望, 写了他们在奴役中的尊严——不是作为讽喻, 而是作为人物。这在 1784 年的法语舞台上是异常的。

> The Comédie-Française refused the play — not once, but at least three times between 1784 and 1789. Blanc (2003) documents the written rejection language that survives: *le sujet nuit à l'agrément du spectateur* — "the subject matter damages the audience's enjoyment." This formulation is worth pausing on. The theater was not saying the play was badly written. It was not saying the play was legally prohibited. It was saying: *slavery as a subject will make audiences uncomfortable.* Which is precisely the point. People knew about it. They did not want to be made to feel it.
>
> The Revolution changed this equation. By late 1789, the National Assembly had begun debating the question of the colonies and liberty; the Société des amis des Noirs (Society of the Friends of the Blacks, founded 1788) was operating publicly; the language of abolition had become speakable in Paris's public forums. Under this pressure, the Comédie-Française agreed to stage *L'Esclavage des Nègres* — in December 1789. It ran briefly, then was withdrawn. Contemporary accounts of audience reaction are fragmentary and contradictory: applause, walkouts, protests.
>
> **Why did she write about this subject?** Gouges herself explains part of it in the play's preface — though this explanation requires careful framing. She writes of sympathy for *les opprimés* (the oppressed), of seeing slavery as a violation of human nature. Joan Scott (1996), analyzing her texts, identifies a deeper structural link: Gouges, as a woman systematically excluded from Paris's formal institutions by virtue of sex, had a lived understanding of what it meant to be constitutively placed outside the definition of "the human." That understanding gave her access to the situation of enslaved people in Saint-Domingue — not as an abstract moral problem but as a familiar structure: some people are excluded from personhood by birth. Scott does not claim Gouges fully understood racial oppression. She argues that Gouges found a connective path, one that ran through her own gendered exclusion.
>
> **What made the play formally significant:** *L'Esclavage des Nègres* was not simply the first French anti-slavery play. Earlier French theater had used enslaved characters — but as functional elements: exotic background, moral foils for white protagonists, figures without interiority. Gouges gave her Black characters their own emotional logic, their own desire for freedom, their own dignity under bondage — not as allegory but as characterization. In French-language theater in 1784, this was genuinely anomalous.

**跨 Topic 的 careful framing**: Toussaint Louverture 在 Saint-Domingue 的甘蔗田里——他有没有听说过 Gouges 的这部剧？不可能直接听说过。Saint-Domingue 与 Paris 有信件往来, 但一部在 Comédie-Française 短暂上演的戏剧不太可能传到具体的种植园。但 Toussaint 在 1780 年代已经是种植园里的一个自由黑人管理者, 他识字, 他读 Raynal (激进废奴派的《两个印度的历史》); 这个信息流是存在的, 只是路径不是 Gouges → Toussaint, 而是整个废奴思想的网络在同时变厚。

> **A cross-topic careful framing:** Did Toussaint Louverture in Saint-Domingue hear about Gouges's play? Almost certainly not directly. Saint-Domingue maintained postal contact with Paris, but a play that ran briefly at the Comédie-Française would not reliably reach specific plantations. By the 1780s, Toussaint was a free Black overseer who could read — he was reading Raynal, the radical abolitionist *History of the Two Indies*. The information flow existed. But the path was not "Gouges → Toussaint." It was an entire network of abolitionist thought thickening simultaneously, feeding multiple people from multiple directions.

### 1789 大革命开始

1789 年 5 月, 路易十六在财政危机下召开三级会议 (Estates-General)——贵族、教士、平民三个等级的代表第一次在凡尔赛聚集开会。这是整个启蒙时代最大的政治危机节点, 也是它最大的「考场」: 100 年的「自由、理性、人的权利」辩论, 要在这里兑换成真实的政治体制。

1789 年 7 月 14 日, 巴黎民众攻占巴士底狱 (Bastille)——象征性的一枪: 巴士底狱里只有 7 个囚犯, 但它是王权专制的符号。大革命开始了。

1789 年 8 月 26 日, 国民议会通过《人和公民权利宣言》(*Déclaration des droits de l'homme et du citoyen*), 17 条, 开篇:「人生而自由, 且在权利上平等 (Les hommes naissent et demeurent libres et égaux en droits)」。

> In May 1789, Louis XVI — facing fiscal crisis — convened the Estates-General at Versailles: representatives of the nobility, clergy, and commoners in session for the first time. This was the Enlightenment's moment of political reckoning: a century of debate about liberty, reason, and human rights now had to be converted into actual governing structures.
>
> July 14, 1789: Parisians stormed the Bastille. The fortress held only 7 prisoners — but it was the symbol of royal despotism. The Revolution had begun.
>
> August 26, 1789: the National Assembly adopted the *Déclaration des droits de l'homme et du citoyen* (Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen), 17 articles, opening: "Men are born and remain free and equal in rights."

**「人 (l'homme)」是谁？**这是 1789 年 8 月这份文件的裂缝。法语 *homme* 有双重意义: 「人」(generic human being) 和「男人」(male person)。起草这份文件的大多数人——Sieyès, Lafayette, Mirabeau——心里想的「享有这些权利的人」是: 有财产的法国男性公民。女性不在这个圈子里。殖民地的奴隶不在这个圈子里。

Olympe de Gouges 读了这份文件。

> **Who was "*l'homme*"?** This was the fault line in the August 1789 document. The French word *homme* carries double meaning: "human being" (generic) and "man" (male). Most of the document's drafters — Sieyès, Lafayette, Mirabeau — had in mind a specific class of rights-holder: propertied French males. Women were outside this circle. Enslaved people in the colonies were outside this circle.
>
> Olympe de Gouges read the document.

### 1789-1791: 革命中的 Gouges——政治 pamphlet 的两年

**缺口 3 补充 (Blanc 2003; Scott 1996)**

1789 年到 1791 年是 Olympe de Gouges 最密集的政治写作时期。这两年她写了大量政治 pamphlet (小册子)——Blanc (2003) 列举的数量超过二十份, 涵盖了几乎所有当时的热点议题。

她批评 Marat (马拉, 激进派), 认为他煽动仇恨、伤害无辜; 她批评 Robespierre (罗伯斯庇尔), 担忧激进派要把大革命变成新的专制; 她公开发言反对把路易十六处死——不是因为她保皇, 而是因为她认为流血不是共和国的基础, 是对革命原则的背叛。这些立场会在 1793 年要她命。

她的政治位置是: **立宪君主制派 → 温和共和派 → Girondins 周边**。她不是 Girondin 党员, 但她的政治同情明确在 Girondins 一侧——主张宪法约束权力, 反对 Jacobins 的集权和恐怖。Blanc 记录她与 Girondin 人物如 Brissot 有过直接联系; Scott (1996) 分析她的文本立场与 Girondin 纲领在反极端主义这个核心点上高度吻合。

她也写关于女性的 pamphlet。她在 1790 年出版了《爱国主义者的礼物》(*Étrenne patriotique de la mère de l'auteur de la Déclaration des droits de l'homme et du citoyen*) 等文本, 要求女性被纳入国民卫队, 要求女性有权在公共空间发言。这些文件没有引发议会辩论, 但它们是 1791 年《女性宣言》的思想草稿。

> **Gap 3 expansion (Blanc 2003; Scott 1996)**
>
> The period 1789–1791 was Gouges's most intensive phase of political writing. These two years produced more than twenty pamphlets, covering nearly every major public question of the moment (Blanc 2003).
>
> She attacked Marat for inciting hatred against the innocent. She publicly criticized Robespierre, warning that the radical faction was converting the Revolution into a new despotism. She spoke openly against the execution of Louis XVI — not from royalist sentiment, but from the conviction that bloodshed was no foundation for a republic, and that killing a king betrayed the principles the Revolution had claimed. These positions would cost her her life in 1793.
>
> Her political location was: **constitutional monarchism transitioning to moderate republicanism, aligned with the Girondin orbit.** She was not a formal Girondin party member, but her sympathies ran clearly toward their platform — constitutional constraint on power, resistance to Jacobin centralism and the Terror. Blanc documents direct contact with Girondin figures including Brissot. Scott (1996) analyzes her published texts and finds her anti-extremism consistently aligned with Girondin core positions.
>
> She also wrote pamphlets specifically about women — demanding that women be admitted to the National Guard, that women be granted the right to speak in public forums. These texts produced no parliamentary response. But they were the intellectual drafts for what would become the *Declaration of the Rights of Woman* in 1791.

**献词的政治逻辑**: 当 Gouges 把 1791 年的《女性宣言》献给 Marie-Antoinette 时, 这个选择让很多大革命的支持者感到困惑, 甚至愤怒。「献给王后？她是保皇党？」

Joan Scott (1996) 给出了一个不同的分析: 这个献词是孤立无援者对另一个孤立无援者的符号性联结。Marie-Antoinette 在 1791 年是软禁中的王后, 被革命剥夺了政治权力, 被公众舆论妖魔化, 是一个被「所有人」排除在外的女人。Gouges 的《女性宣言》——同样是一份被「所有人」排除在外的文件——把自己递给另一个被同样的「所有人」排除在外的女人。

Scott 的分析明确说: 这不是保皇主义。Gouges 在别处的文本里批评过王室体制。这个献词的逻辑是: 「我们都被排除了, 而这本身就是我要说的问题。」

> **The logic of the dedication:** When Gouges dedicated the *Declaration of the Rights of Woman* to Marie-Antoinette, it confused and angered many Revolutionary supporters. Was she a royalist?
>
> Joan Scott (1996) offers a different reading. The dedication is a symbolic gesture from one excluded person to another. In 1791, Marie-Antoinette was the Queen under house arrest — stripped of political power by the Revolution, demonized by public opinion, a woman excluded from "all men's" rights as surely as any woman in France. Gouges's *Declaration* — itself a document excluded by "all men" — was being handed to a woman who was excluded by the same "all men."
>
> Scott's analysis is explicit: this was not royalism. Gouges criticized the royal system elsewhere in her writing. The dedication's logic was: "We are both outside. That is precisely what I am writing about."

这两年 (1789-1791) 里 Gouges 的处境有一个结构性悖论值得注意: 大革命在表面上是历史上最大规模的「人民发声」——但对于女性来说, 它同时是一次「新的封口」。国民议会的辩论不允许女性旁听席发言; 女性组织被法律上约束不得介入政治领域; 「公民 (citoyen)」这个词有阳性形式和阴性形式, 但在法律上只有阳性才指向完整的政治权利。Gouges 在这两年里写了大量东西, 却没有一份被任何议会程序接收。Scott (1996) 描述这个时期的 Gouges 是「在一个关于她但不包括她的对话里, 持续发言的人」。

> The two years 1789–1791 presented Gouges with a structural paradox worth noting: the Revolution was, on its surface, the largest "people's voice" event in history — but for women, it was simultaneously a new silencing. The National Assembly's debates excluded women from speaking even from the public gallery; women's organizations were legally barred from political matters; the word *citoyen* (citizen) had both masculine and feminine grammatical forms, but in law only the masculine carried full political rights. Gouges wrote prolifically during these years, yet not a single document she produced was taken up by any parliamentary procedure. Scott (1996) describes this-period Gouges as "a person speaking continuously in a conversation that was about her but did not include her."

**Grade 7 钩子**: 「大革命喊的是『人人平等』。但『人人』里没有她。她写了二十份以上的文件, 没有一份进入议会程序。不是因为写得不好——而是因为程序本身没有设计一个接收她的入口。」

> **For young readers:** The Revolution shouted "equality for all." But "all" didn't include her. She wrote more than twenty documents. Not one was taken up by any parliamentary process. Not because the writing was poor — but because the process itself had no mechanism designed to receive her.

### 1791《女性和女公民权利宣言》

1791 年, Olympe de Gouges 出版《女性和女公民权利宣言》(*Déclaration des droits de la femme et de la citoyenne*)。

她的方法是字对字的镜像模仿: 把 1789 年文件的 17 条逐条拿来, 在每一条里加入「女性」。第 1 条原文:「男人生而自由, 且在权利上平等」; 她的版本:「女人生而自由, 且在权利上与男人平等」。

**第 10 条**是她最著名的一句——也是整个 18 世纪女权思想里最锋利的一句:

「**如果女性有权被送上断头台, 她就应该同样有权站上讲台。**」
(*Si la femme a le droit de monter sur l'échafaud, elle doit avoir également celui de monter à la tribune.*)

这句话的逻辑结构是完美的: 国家承认女人有资格被处死 (这已经是法律现实, 大革命的断头台不分性别); 那为什么处死的资格和发言的资格不是同一个标准？

> In 1791, Olympe de Gouges published the *Déclaration des droits de la femme et de la citoyenne* (Declaration of the Rights of Woman and of the Female Citizen).
>
> Her method was exact mirror-image imitation: she took the 17 articles of the 1789 document and rewrote each one, inserting "woman" throughout. Article 1 in the original: "Men are born free and equal in rights." Her version: "Woman is born free and remains equal to man in rights."
>
> **Article 10** is her most famous line — and one of the sharpest sentences in 18th-century feminist thought:
>
> **"If woman has the right to mount the scaffold, she must equally have the right to mount the podium."**
> (*Si la femme a le droit de monter sur l'échafaud, elle doit avoir également celui de monter à la tribune.*)
>
> The logic is airtight: the state already acknowledges that women are eligible to be executed (the Revolution's guillotine did not discriminate by sex). So why does eligibility for death not equal eligibility to speak?

她把这份文件献给 Marie-Antoinette (玛丽-安托瓦内特, 法国王后, 此时已在软禁中)——这个献词本身是一个政治声明: 她不是在向大革命的权贵讨好, 她是在对一个也被「人的权利」排斥在外的女人说: 我们的困境有一个共同的逻辑。

> She dedicated the document to Marie-Antoinette, then under house arrest — the Queen of France. This dedication was itself a political statement: she was not courting the Revolution's powerful men. She was addressing a woman who, like all women, had also been excluded from "the Rights of Man" — saying: our situations share a common logic.

**Careful framing**: Joan Scott 在《Only Paradoxes to Offer》(1996) 里追踪了这份文件的命运:「《女性宣言》在 1791 年几乎完全被忽略——被大革命主流男性政治圈忽略, 没有引发辩论, 没有进入国民议会的任何议程。这份文件直到 20 世纪才被『重发现』, 成为 feminist 运动的历史经典。」

Scott 的核心论点: 女性权利倡导者在 18 世纪面临的不是「等待一个小人物的顿悟时刻」——而是**制度的系统性排斥**。Gouges 写了完美的逻辑, 用了完全合法的革命语言, 遵循了革命的文本格式。制度依然无视她——不是因为逻辑不成立, 而是因为制度从一开始就没有设计一个听这种声音的程序。

> **Careful framing:** Joan Scott's *Only Paradoxes to Offer* (1996) traces the document's fate: "The *Declaration of the Rights of Woman* was almost entirely ignored in 1791 — overlooked by the Revolution's male political mainstream, generating no parliamentary debate, entering no National Assembly agenda. The document was only 'rediscovered' in the 20th century, when it became a canonical text of feminist history."
>
> Scott's central argument: what women's rights advocates faced in the 18th century was not a matter of waiting for an individual moment of insight or breakthrough. It was **the systematic exclusion built into institutions**. Gouges wrote logically airtight arguments using the Revolution's own language and its own textual forms. The institution ignored her — not because the logic failed, but because the institution had never built a mechanism for hearing this kind of voice in the first place.

**Grade 7 表述**: 「Olympe de Gouges 用大革命自己的文件格式, 写了一份它的人都不肯读的宣言。这不是说她写错了——而是说, 一个制度不听一样东西, 有时候不是因为它听了之后觉得不对, 而是因为它根本没有打算听。」

> **For young readers:** Olympe de Gouges used the Revolution's own document format to write a declaration that the Revolution's own people refused to read. This doesn't mean she wrote badly. It means that sometimes an institution ignores something not because it considered the argument and disagreed — but because it never intended to listen in the first place.

### 1793: 断头台

1793 年 11 月 3 日, Olympe de Gouges 被大革命的断头台处死。她被指控为「保皇党」(royalist) 和「联邦主义者」(federalist)——在大革命极端化的 Jacobin 统治下, 这些指控足以致命。她 45 岁。

她的第 10 条关于断头台的那句话在两年后成为了她自己的命运。

> On November 3, 1793, Olympe de Gouges was executed by the Revolution's guillotine. She was charged with royalism and federalism — under the Jacobin Terror, these accusations were enough to kill. She was 45.
>
> Her own Article 10 — the one about the scaffold — had become her biography.

### Part 2 视角汇合: 1791 年的同一时刻

1791 年, 三条线同时在运动:

**Voltaire** 已死 13 年 (1778 年死), 但他的遗体在 1791 年被移进万神殿, 大革命把他封圣成「理性之父」。他的矛盾被遗忘了——不是解决了, 是被「启蒙英雄」的神话覆盖了。

**Olympe de Gouges** 在 1791 年出版《女性宣言》, 被忽略。两年后死在断头台。

**Toussaint Louverture** 在 1791 年 8 月 22 日加入 Saint-Domingue 的奴隶起义 (Bois Caïman 誓言仪式之后)——这场起义将成为人类历史上唯一一次成功的大规模奴隶革命。他在用行动回答那个「所有人的权利」文件里没有回答的问题: 谁是「所有人」？

这三条线在 1791 年 没有见面。但它们指向同一个中心——启蒙运动「普世理性」的外壳裂开了, 裂缝里出来的是它从来没有打算包含的人。

> In 1791, three trajectories moved simultaneously:
>
> **Voltaire**, dead 13 years (died 1778), had his remains moved to the Panthéon in 1791 — the Revolution canonized him as "father of reason." His contradictions were not resolved. They were overwritten by the myth of the Enlightenment hero.
>
> **Olympe de Gouges** published the *Declaration of the Rights of Woman* in 1791. It was ignored. Two years later she died on the guillotine.
>
> **Toussaint Louverture** joined the Saint-Domingue slave uprising on August 22, 1791 — following the Bois Caïman ceremony. This uprising would become the only successful large-scale slave revolution in human history. He was answering by action the question that the "rights of all men" document had left unanswered: who counted as "all"?
>
> These three trajectories never met in 1791. But they converged on the same fault line — the Enlightenment's shell of "universal reason" cracking open, and from the cracks emerging exactly the people it had never intended to include.

## §10 Saint-Domingue 糖业帝国: 500,000 奴隶 + 40% 法国的糖

### 10.1 一个岛，两个世界

**同一块岛屿，两段历史。**

如果你打开加勒比地图，在大西洋和墨西哥湾交界处有一个形状像一只乌龟的岛——Hispaniola（伊斯帕尼奥拉岛）。你在 Pre-Columbian Topic 里见过这个名字：1508 年，Taíno 女酋长 Anacaona 的女儿（我们称她 Anacaona Jr.）在这里目睹了西班牙人的到来，目睹了整个 Taíno 世界的瓦解。

那是 1508 年。

现在是 1743 年。

235 年后，同一块岛屿，Toussaint Louverture 出生了。

Taíno 人已经消失——被天花、被强制劳役、被战争——1520 年代基本上从记录里消失。岛屿西部 1/3 现在是法国殖民地，名字叫 **Saint-Domingue**（圣多明克）。岛屿东部 2/3 是西班牙殖民地，名字叫 Santo Domingo（今多米尼加共和国）。

同一个岛。从 Taíno 人的终结，到另一场革命的开端。

### 10.2 40% 的糖从这里来

**你喝过加糖的咖啡吗？在 1780 年代的法国，那块方糖很可能来自 Saint-Domingue。**

1700 年代，Saint-Domingue 是地球上最富有的殖民地之一。两位历史学家给出了核心数字：

- C.L.R. James《The Black Jacobins》(1938)：Saint-Domingue 在 1780 年代是「世界糖业中心」
- Laurent Dubois《Avengers of the New World》(2004)：到 1789 年，Saint-Domingue 生产了法国消费量约 40% 的蔗糖，约 50% 的咖啡

**〔careful framing〕** C.L.R. James 写这本书是在 1938 年，那是全球反殖民运动的高峰期，他本人是特立尼达出生的马克思主义者。他的《The Black Jacobins》仍然是研究海地革命最重要的叙事著作之一，但要知道它带有政治立场——他选择了什么，强调了什么，都有他的时代背景。Laurent Dubois 2004 年的修正版是目前学术界更审慎的权威参考。两本书都是这段历史的必读文本。

那么这 40% 的糖是怎么生产出来的？

### 10.3 种植园里的世界

**1789 年，Saint-Domingue 的人口构成如下：**

| 群体 | 人数 | 地位 |
|------|------|------|
| Grands blancs（大白人） | 约 10,000 | 种植园主 + 商人，顶层 |
| Petits blancs（小白人） | 约 30,000 | 小商人、工匠、雇佣白人，中层 |
| Gens de couleur（有色自由人） | 约 30,000 | 自由的混血或黑人，法律上低于白人 |
| 奴隶 | 约 500,000 | 种植园劳动力，无权 |

**〔careful framing〕** 这些数字来自 Dubois 2004 的整合。500,000 这个数字是估算，基于殖民地政府的不完整档案。真实数字可能更高，因为殖民地记录本身就不准确。

糖蔗种植是极端高强度劳动：砍甘蔗（sugar cane / sucre）、榨汁、煮糖、装桶——在加勒比炎热湿度下，每道工序都是体力与健康的极限消耗。同样在种植的还有咖啡（café）和棉花（coton）。

**奴隶能在糖业种植园活多久？**

**〔careful framing〕** C.L.R. James 在《The Black Jacobins》里说：「糖业种植园的奴隶平均存活 7 到 10 年。」这个数字在反奴隶制写作中被广泛引用。Laurent Dubois 2004 提出这是史学上的夸张——实际死亡率极高，但 James 的「7-10 年」没有扎实的档案证明，是道德叙事而非精确统计。

我们能确定的是：Saint-Domingue 的奴隶数量从 1680 年的约 2,000 人增长到 1789 年的约 500,000 人。这个增长本身说明了什么——死亡率极高，所以必须不断从非洲输入新的劳动力。用历史学家的话说：「这不是一个奴隶能繁殖的系统，这是一个消耗奴隶的系统。」

---

### 10.4 跨 Topic 链接：同一条线

**〔Cross-ref: AoE Las Casas + Mali Empire〕**

你在 Age of Exploration Topic 里见过 Bartolomé de las Casas（拉斯·卡萨斯）。1542 年，他向西班牙国王递交了《New Laws》，呼吁保护美洲原住民——但他早期曾主张用非洲奴隶替代原住民劳动（后来他改变了立场，晚年深感悔恨）。

结果：非洲奴隶贸易在 16-18 世纪急剧扩大。

你在 Mali Empire Topic 里见过西非的贸易路线、精英文化、跨撒哈拉商路。现在要记住：西非王国的崩溃和战争，有一部分直接输送了被俘虏的人到大西洋奴隶贸易里。

Saint-Domingue 种植园里的 500,000 人——他们来自哪里？塞内冈比亚、黄金海岸、达荷美（今贝宁）、刚果盆地。很多人是在非洲内战中被俘后卖给欧洲商人的。这条链条从 1540 年代的 Las Casas，连到 1743 年 Toussaint Louverture 的父亲。

## §11 Toussaint Louverture (1743-1803): 种植园出生，学会阅读

### 11.1 Bréda 种植园，北部省

**1743 年，Saint-Domingue 北部省，Bréda 种植园。**

一个男婴出生了。他的奴隶名字后来是 Toussaint。他的父亲，据 C.L.R. James 的记录，叫 Gaou Guinou。

关于这个父亲，有一个家族传说：他是西非 Allada 王国（今贝宁共和国境内）被俘贵族的后代——不是普通农民，而是被奴隶贸易从贵族家庭撕走的人。

**〔careful framing〕** 「贵族后代」这个说法主要来自 C.L.R. James 1938 年的叙事，他从口述传统和 Toussaint 家族的后世陈述中整理。Laurent Dubois 2004（p. 10）更审慎：父亲可能来自非洲精英家庭，但「Allada 王国贵族」的具体细节难以证实。这个传说对海地国家记忆很重要，历史学家承认它可能有真实根据，但不能当作确证事实引用。

Toussaint 在 Bréda 种植园长大。这个种植园属于 Bayon de Libertat——一个相对温和的管事主人（对比当时 Saint-Domingue 极端暴力的种植园主标准）。

---

### 11.2 他在种植园里学到的事

Toussaint 的种植园岁月不是普通奴隶的命运。他获得了几件在 Saint-Domingue 极为罕见的东西：

**一，他学会了读写。** 用法语。据传他还懂一些拉丁文。Bayon de Libertat 允许，甚至鼓励了这件事——原因不明，可能是因为 Toussaint 聪慧，可能是因为 Bayon 需要一个识字的管事助手。

**二，他学习了草药医术。** 这让他在种植园里获得了某种非正式的医疗权威。

**三，他管马。** Bréda 种植园的马圈由他负责。这不只是体力活——懂马、能骑马，在后来的军事生涯里价值无量。

然后是那本书。

**〔careful framing〕** C.L.R. James 在《The Black Jacobins》里列出了 Toussaint 读过的书单，最重要的一本是 Abbé Raynal 1770 年的《Histoire des deux Indes》（两印度史，即亚洲和美洲的殖民史）。这本书是 18 世纪最激进的批判殖民主义文本之一，里面有一段预言：「一个伟大的人将出现，带领被压迫者反抗压迫者。」

Toussaint 读过这本书吗？

Laurent Dubois 2004 的回答是：**可能，但不能确定。** Saint-Domingue 1780 年代确实有印刷品流通——通过 affranchis（自由有色人种）的社交网络，书籍可以在殖民地传播。Toussaint 作为识字的自由黑人，接触到 Raynal 的可能性是真实存在的。但「Toussaint 读过 Raynal」是一个高概率的叙事，不是一个铁证事实。

这个区别重要吗？重要。因为整个启蒙传播史的核心问题之一，就是：**「启蒙语言怎么从巴黎的沙龙到了圣多明克的种植园？」** 答案不是「Voltaire 寄给他的」——答案是通过 affranchis 的流通网络，通过识字的自由黑人，书籍缓慢地渗入了殖民地的裂缝。

---

### 11.3 约 1776 年：自由身（manumission）

**约 1776-1779 年间，Bayon de Libertat 给了 Toussaint「自由身」（manumission）。**

这意味着他在法律上成为了 affranchi——「自由黑人」（libre de couleur）。他不再是奴隶。

但在 Saint-Domingue 的三层体制里，他的位置是这样的：

```
白人（grands blancs + petits blancs）—— 顶层
↓
Gens de couleur（自由有色人种，混血 + 自由黑人）—— 中层
↓
奴隶（500,000）—— 底层
```

Toussaint 获得了自由，进入了中层——但中层里他还是「自由黑人」，在 gens de couleur 里也处于更低的位置（混血自由人（mulâtres）的社会地位通常高于自由黑人）。

**1780 年代：他管着一小块租来的土地。**

**〔careful framing〕** Laurent Dubois 2004（p. 91）记录了一个让今天的读者感到不适的事实：在起义前，Toussaint 短暂地持有过少数几个自己的奴隶，用于耕种他租来的土地。这不是要为他辩护或谴责——这是理解 Saint-Domingue 社会复杂性的关键：自由黑人进入了一个系统，而这个系统里连自由黑人也可以成为奴隶主。这个细节在很多通俗叙事里被略去，但历史学家必须面对它。

**Grade 7 钩子：**

1789 年，法国大革命爆发时，Toussaint 47 岁。他是一个在 Saint-Domingue 三层体制中处于第二层的自由黑人。他会读书，会骑马，懂草药，懂法律，懂法语——他拥有的工具，在这个岛上的 500,000 奴隶里，几乎没有人拥有。但他不是白人，不是平等的公民。他在一个裂缝里，看着法国大革命的消息传来。

---

## §12 1789 大革命的语言在 Saint-Domingue: 谁是公民？

### 12.1 《人和公民权利宣言》抵达 Saint-Domingue

**1789 年 8 月 26 日，巴黎：《人和公民权利宣言》通过。**

第一条：「所有人生而自由，在权利上平等。」

这份宣言跨越大西洋，抵达 Saint-Domingue。

然后它引爆了一个问题，这个问题比巴黎的任何哲学家都更爆炸：

**「谁算'人'？谁算'公民'？」**

Saint-Domingue 有四个群体，每个群体对这个问题的回答不同。

---

### 12.2 四个群体，四个答案

**Grands blancs（大白人种植园主）的答案：**「自治。我们不需要巴黎指手画脚。『自由』意味着殖民地自己管自己的经济——尤其是奴隶制不能动。」

**Petits blancs（小白人）的答案：**「白人特权必须维持。我不是大地主，但我比任何有色人种地位高——这个等级不能因为革命而消失。」

**Gens de couleur（自由有色人种）的答案：**「我们也是自由人。我们读了同样的《宣言》。『人和公民』为什么不包括我们？我们要政治权利。」

**500,000 奴隶的答案：**

——他们还不在这个对话里。

---

### 12.3 Ogé 起义 1790：有色自由人的第一次尝试

**Vincent Ogé** 是 Saint-Domingue 的 gens de couleur 领袖之一。他 1789 年在巴黎，亲眼看见了大革命，亲自向国民议会请愿，要求给予有色自由人公民权。

巴黎的 Julien Raimond（同样是 gens de couleur 代表）也在做同样的事。

**1790 年 10 月，Ogé 回到 Saint-Domingue，率领约 300 人起义**——要求实施给予有色自由人的公民权。

关键细节：Ogé **明确拒绝**让奴隶加入他的起义。他不是为了废奴——他是为了 gens de couleur 的政治地位。

起义在两个月内失败。Ogé 被俘，1791 年 2 月在 Cap-Français（今海地角）被处以「车裂」（roue）——四匹马分四个方向撕开身体，在公众面前执行。

这种刑罚在法国本土正在废除（1791 年正式废除）。在 Saint-Domingue，它用来警告任何胆敢要求权利的有色人种。

---

### 12.4 巴黎的矛盾：给了又收回

**1791 年 5 月 15 日，巴黎国民议会通过决议**：给予在 1789 年以前出生于自由父母的有色自由人公民权。

这是一个极其有限的决议——它不涵盖所有 gens de couleur，不涵盖奴隶，只覆盖了估计约 400 人。

结果：Saint-Domingue 的白人殖民地议会**拒绝执行**。他们声称国民议会无权干涉殖民地内部事务。

巴黎说「自由」。Saint-Domingue 的白人说「不关你的事」。

这就是 1791 年 8 月前的 Saint-Domingue：一个「自由」的宣言，四个群体，没有共识，随时爆炸。

---

### 12.5 Raynal 的预言（和这个预言的问题）

回到那本书——Abbé Raynal 1770 年《Histoire des deux Indes》。

第 10 册，第 1 章，有这样一段法语：

> *「Où est ce grand homme que la Nature doit à ses vœux opprimés？ Où est ce nouveau Spartacus？」*
>
> 「自然欠受压迫人们的那个伟大人物在哪里？那个新的斯巴达克斯在哪里？」

C.L.R. James 在《The Black Jacobins》里说：这段文字预言了 Toussaint Louverture。而且 Toussaint 读过这本书。

**〔careful framing〕** 这是一个值得辨析的重要问题。

第一，**「预言」这个框架本身是后见之明（hindsight）**。Raynal 写这段文字的时候，不知道 Toussaint 是谁。他是在做道德呼吁，不是占卜。

第二，**Toussaint 是否真的读过 Raynal，证据链不完整**。Dubois 2004 的立场是：可能读过，但不能确证。

第二点补充：**Raynal 本人是启蒙里的「被本体制排斥者」**，不是主流。《Histoire des deux Indes》在法国 1781 年被查禁，Raynal 本人被迫流亡。如果说 Toussaint 通过 Raynal 学到了「自由」的语言，他学到的是启蒙里**被自己的体制排斥的那部分**，不是被王室 + 巴黎沙龙接受的部分（Dubois 2004, p.21）。这让「启蒙解放了奴隶」这个神话更准确地被击穿：启蒙的主流并没有解放奴隶，是启蒙的边缘——被查禁的书、被流亡的作者——给了 Toussaint 工具。

第三，**更重要的问题**：就算 Toussaint 读过 Raynal——这意味着什么？

这里有一个 Grade 7 必须理解的**反 Whig（anti-Whig）**核心：

「启蒙思想解放了奴隶」——这是一个太简单的、让欧洲哲学家显得很好看的叙事。

更接近历史真相的说法是：**奴隶拿起了启蒙的语言，用来反对那些制造了启蒙语言却不愿意推行它的人。**

Voltaire 写过《Candide》，里面有两段描写奴隶的文字——我们在 Part 1 见过。他知道。他笔下的苏里南奴隶说：「这就是他们告诉我们的代价。」Voltaire 写完了，然后继续他在巴黎和腓特烈大帝宫廷里的社交。

Toussaint 读了（可能）Raynal，然后用接下来 10 年打了一场改变世界的战争。

同一个启蒙。两种选择。

---

## §13 1791.8.22 Bois Caïman + 起义爆发

### 13.1 仪式前夜

**1791 年 8 月 22 日夜晚。**

Saint-Domingue 北部，一片叫 Bois Caïman（鳄鱼树林）的地方。

历史记录说，奴隶起义的领袖们在这里聚集——举行了一场 Vodou 仪式，立下了起义的誓约。仪式的主持者是 **Dutty Boukman**，一个来自牙买加的大个子奴隶，在 Lenormand de Mézy 种植园做马车夫。

这场仪式后来成为海地历史最核心的集体记忆之一：两百年来，在海地的学校、艺术、音乐里，Bois Caïman 是「革命诞生的那一夜」。

**〔careful framing〕** 但历史学家面对 Bois Caïman 必须非常诚实：**原始史料极其稀少，而且来源都不中立。**

我们现在看到的 Bois Caïman 记录，主要来自两类来源：

1. **欧洲殖民地记录**——写 Vodou 仪式的欧洲人，倾向于把它描述成「野蛮仪式」「血腥契约」。这些描述带有强烈的种族偏见和耸人听闻的渲染（sensationalism）。

2. **后世口述传统**——海地建国后，Bois Caïman 被民族记忆塑造和美化，细节在传承中演变。

Laurent Dubois 2004 的立场是：**这个事件发生了**，这一点历史学家基本达成共识——1791 年 8 月 22 日确实有一场集会，确实是起义的组织节点。但具体细节（仪式内容、参加人数、「猪血」「誓约」的描述）来自立场不中立的来源，历史学家无法逐条核实。

这不是说「Bois Caïman 是假的」。这是说：一个事件可以是真实的，而关于它的所有细节记录都是有问题的。这就是研究被压迫者历史时最常见的困境——被压迫者没有权力书写自己的记录。

**〔史料距离 careful framing〕** 现存最早的书面记录是 1797 年 Moreau de Saint-Méry 在《Saint-Domingue 的物理、民族、民事和政治描述》里的叙述——距 Bois Caïman 事件约 6 年。比多数口述历史的记录距离更近，但 Moreau 本人是白人种植园精英立场（Dubois 2004, p.99）。此外 1791 年 8 月的 cap-Français 信件中也有最早的片段性记录。两类史料都是事件当时或事件后数年写下的，但都不中立——这正是 Bois Caïman sourcing 训练的核心价值：不是「哪个来源正确」，而是「为什么所有来源都有立场」。

---

### 13.2 1791 年 8 月 22 日：火光起

**1791 年 8 月 22 日深夜到 23 日凌晨，北部种植园区开始燃烧。**

Dutty Boukman 领导的奴隶队伍袭击了周边种植园。在接下来的数周里，北部平原——Saint-Domingue 最富裕的糖业产区——有超过 1,000 个种植园被烧毁，约 1,000 名白人殖民者被杀，约 10,000 名奴隶死于随后镇压。

这个数字来自 Dubois 2004 的估算，基于殖民地档案，本身也是不完整的。

Cap-Français（今日海地角）的天空，据幸存者描述，被北部平原的火光照红了。糖厂、咖啡种植园、制糖机械——Saint-Domingue 生产法国 40% 糖的那些设施——开始化为灰烬。

---

### 13.3 Toussaint 在哪里？起义第一夜

**〔careful framing〕** 这是 Part 3 里最需要辨析的争议点之一。

**C.L.R. James 的版本**（《The Black Jacobins》1938）：Toussaint 在 Bois Caïman 出席了仪式，是起义组织者之一。他把这个叙事写得非常有英雄感：Toussaint 从那一夜就是这场革命的核心人物。

**Laurent Dubois 的版本**（《Avengers of the New World》2004）：更谨慎。Dubois 提出：关于 Toussaint 在起义第一夜做了什么，证据语焉不详。比较可信的记录是——Toussaint 在 Bréda 种植园，帮助他的旧主人 Bayon de Libertat 安全撤离，护送他离开了 Saint-Domingue。

Bayon 在 1791 年 10 月离开了 Saint-Domingue，去往美国。Toussaint 帮了他一程。

**这是道德选择还是谨慎计算？** 历史无法判断。一种解读是：Toussaint 对曾给他自由身的主人有人情义务。另一种解读是：他在等待，观察形势，计算最佳时机加入。

两种解读都有道理。历史学家选择诚实地说「不知道」。

**Toussaint 加入起义军的时间大约是 1791 年 10 月。** 此后他迅速成为重要的军事指挥官——因为他会读、会写、会骑马、会策略，而这些在起义军里是稀缺资源。

---

## §14 1791-1798 三方博弈: Toussaint 的政治棋局

### 14.1 三面旗帜

**Grade 7 钩子：想象 Saint-Domingue 是一个棋盘，棋盘上有三面旗帜在移动。**

- **蓝白红旗**（法国）：大革命后的法兰西共和国，1791 年后派军来维持秩序——但「秩序」对他们意味着什么，在不断变化
- **黄红旗**（西班牙）：控制 Hispaniola 东半部（Santo Domingo），1793 年决定支持奴隶起义军，因为这能削弱法国
- **米字旗**（英国）：1793 年，趁法国大革命混乱，英国军队登陆 Saint-Domingue——理由是「保护英国侨民」，真实目的是夺取世界上最富有的糖业殖民地

Toussaint 在 1791 年到 1798 年间，和这三面旗帜都打过交道——有时候是敌人，有时候是盟友，取决于哪面旗帜当下对「废奴」的立场更有利。

---

### 14.2 1793 年：Toussaint 选择西班牙

**1793 年初，Toussaint 带领他的部队加入西班牙军队。**

原因是战略计算，不是情感效忠：

1. 西班牙向 Saint-Domingue 的奴隶起义军承诺：加入西班牙军队的将士获得自由，并在西班牙国王保护下作战。
2. 法国共和国在 1793 年的立场仍然摇摆——殖民地白人议会拒绝废奴，而巴黎的立场在革命派系斗争中不清晰。
3. 西班牙给了 Toussaint 军衔、军饷、武器。

1793 年，Toussaint 在西班牙旗帜下打仗，打法国军队。

他在这一年写了一份声明，第一次签上了那个将使他永久被历史记住的名字：

**Toussaint Louverture**

「Louverture」——法语，意思是「开口」「破口」「缺口」。他自己起的，因为他「在敌人阵线上打出了缺口」。

---

### 14.3 1794 年 2 月 4 日：巴黎废奴

**这是 Part 3 的关键转折点。**

**1794 年 2 月 4 日，法国国民公会（Convention nationale）通过决议：废除法国所有殖民地的奴隶制。**

这是人类历史上第一次国家级别的废奴令。

背景：Saint-Domingue 的三位代表（其中两位是有色人种和黑人代表）抵达巴黎，向国民公会陈情。代表团里有一位黑人代表 Jean-Baptiste Belley，他的出现让国民公会的激进派（包括 Abbé Grégoire）当场推动投票。废奴令在当天以鼓掌方式通过，没有反对票（历史学家注意：这种「鼓掌通过」说明它在当时更像是战时政治决定，而非深思熟虑的政策，但它仍然是真实有效的法律）。

消息从巴黎传到 Saint-Domingue 需要数周。

**Toussaint 收到消息时，他正在西班牙阵营。**

**〔careful framing〕** C.L.R. James 把 Toussaint 收到废奴消息后「立刻转投法国」写得非常戏剧性：他瞬间看到了法国才是他真正的盟友，带着部队投奔法国。Dubois 2004 的解读更复杂：Toussaint 的转向不是「一夜之间的灵光乍现」，而是经过了数周的评估——他确认法国 1794 年的废奴令是真实的政策，不是虚词，然后才行动。

无论过程多少天，结果是一样的：

**1794 年，Toussaint 率领他的部队离开西班牙阵营，投向法国共和国。**

他的理由，用他自己后来的话说（记录于 Dubois 2004 引用的文件）：西班牙承诺了自由，但这个承诺只给了加入他们军队的部分人；法国国民公会用法律宣布了所有人的自由。

**战略还是信仰？** 两者都有。这就是 Toussaint：他不是一个纯粹的理想主义者，也不是一个纯粹的机会主义者。他在评估哪一方的「自由」承诺更真实、更可执行。

---

### 14.4 1795-1798：击退英军

**英国军队在 1793 年登陆了 Saint-Domingue。**

他们的公开理由是「保护英国公民的生命财产」。真实原因是：Saint-Domingue 是地球上最富裕的殖民地，法国大革命造成的混乱是夺取它的历史机会。英国当时是奴隶贸易的重要参与者，完全不打算废奴——他们想要的是让 Saint-Domingue 在英国旗帜下继续运营糖业。

**〔anti-fab：英国不是「反奴隶制」的一方〕** 这是一个在通俗历史里容易被混淆的点：英国 1807 年废除奴隶贸易（不是废除奴隶制），1834 年才废除殖民地奴隶制。1793 年入侵 Saint-Domingue 的英国军队，是要**恢复**奴隶制，不是解放奴隶。

Toussaint 领导的军队对英国军队展开了多年的游击战和正面战役。

**但还有一个盟友：黄热病（yellow fever / fièvre jaune）。**

加勒比的热带气候对英国士兵是致命的。黄热病（病因当时未知，1900 年才确认是蚊子传播的病毒）在英军中造成了极高的死亡率。

**〔careful framing〕** Dubois 2004 引用的数字：英国在 1793-1798 年间在 Saint-Domingue 损失了超过 40,000 名士兵——其中绝大多数死于黄热病，而非战斗。这是历史上规模最大的热带疾病军事失败之一。（「40,000+」这个数字本身在历史学界有争议范围，从 20,000 到 60,000 都有不同估算，Dubois 取中间审慎数字。）

Toussaint 的军事才能是真实的——他的机动战术、他的情报系统、他使用山地地形的能力。但黄热病也是他的盟友，一个他没有召唤但充分利用了的战略优势。

**1798 年，英国军队撤出 Saint-Domingue。**

---

### 14.5 1798 年的 Toussaint：一个什么样的人

**Grade 7 钩子，放大版：**

让我们算一下 1798 年的 Toussaint 经历了什么：

| 年份 | 他做了什么 | 旗帜颜色 |
|------|-----------|--------|
| 1791.10 | 加入奴隶起义军 | 无旗帜，或起义军旗 |
| 1793 | 投靠西班牙军队 | 黄红旗 |
| 1794 | 听说法国废奴，转投法国 | 蓝白红旗 |
| 1795-1798 | 击退英国军队 | 对抗米字旗 |
| 1798 | 实际控制 Saint-Domingue 北部 | 法国旗帜下的自治权 |

他不是一个「效忠某个帝国的将军」。他是一个在三个帝国的裂缝中操作的战略家——他的目标从来不是「帮法国赢」或「帮西班牙赢」，他的目标是**让 Saint-Domingue 的奴隶不再是奴隶，让他的人民自由。**

为了这个目标，他用了任何他能用的旗帜。

---

### 14.6 Voltaire vs. Toussaint：一个对比

在 Part 1 和 Part 2，我们见过 Voltaire。

他写了《哲学通信》，写了《哲学辞典》，写了《坎第德》。他是启蒙的符号人物，「理性」「自由」「人的尊严」的代言人。

在《Candide》（1759）里，有两段直接描写奴隶的文字——你在 Part 1 见过那个苏里南奴隶的话。Voltaire 知道奴隶制。他用笔写了，让读者道德震惊了一下，然后继续他的信件、沙龙、与国王和贵族的周旋。

1778 年，Voltaire 在巴黎去世，那一年被称为「启蒙的胜利」。

1743 年，也就是 Voltaire 49 岁的时候，Toussaint 出生在 Saint-Domingue 的 Bréda 种植园。

他们生活在同一个世界里，都在「启蒙」这两个字的笼罩下——一个用笔，一个用身体。

这不是要说 Voltaire 是坏人，Toussaint 是英雄。这是要说：**启蒙的「自由」话语，在它被说出来之后，由谁来承担它的代价，由谁来真正执行它的逻辑——这两者完全不同。**

Toussaint 没有写《哲学辞典》。

他带着他的军队，在 Saint-Domingue 的山里，让「自由」这个词变成了一件真实发生的事情。

---

## Part 3 结语：启蒙在 Saint-Domingue

1798 年，英国军队离开 Saint-Domingue。Toussaint Louverture 是这块土地上最有实力的军事和政治力量。

他还没有赢——海地独立要到 1804 年，他本人不会活到那一天（1803 年在法国监狱里去世）。Napoleon 1801 年会尝试重新建立奴隶制，Toussaint 会被诱捕，会被关押在法国东部阿尔卑斯山的 Fort de Joux，会在那里死于肺炎和营养不良。

那是 Part 4 的故事。

但现在，站在 1798 年看：

一个在 Bréda 种植园出生的奴隶，读过（可能是）Raynal 的书，用西班牙人的武器、法国人的旗帜、英国人无法适应的热带气候——击退了三个帝国中的两个，废除了 Saint-Domingue 的奴隶制。

Raynal 的那句话——「那个伟大的人在哪里？」

他不是巴黎沙龙里的那些人。

他在 Bréda 种植园。他花了 47 年积累他的工具。然后他用了。

## §15 Toussaint 1798-1801: 「Saint-Domingue 的 Washington」

### §15.1 1798 英军撤出——Toussaint 的战略大胜

CN: 1798 年秋, 英军从 Saint-Domingue 撤出。

这不是小事。英国 1793 年出兵 Saint-Domingue, 目的是趁法国大革命乱局, 吞并这块当时世界最富有的糖业殖民地。五年后, 英军损失估计超过 40,000 人——大部分不是战死, 而是死于黄热病 (*fièvre jaune*)。Toussaint Louverture 的战术: 不跟英军硬碰硬, 把他们困在沿海炎热港口, 等黄热病做工。

**careful framing**: 英军撤出的交涉由 Toussaint 亲自跟英国将领 Thomas Maitland 谈判——双方签订了一份秘密协定 (Maitland-Toussaint Agreement, 1798.8)。协定内容: 英国撤军 + 美国 + 英国对 Saint-Domingue 开放贸易。Toussaint 当时已在行事上像一个主权国家的领导人, 而不是法国殖民地的军事长官。Laurent Dubois,《Avengers of the New World》(Harvard 2004), pp. 208-212: 这份协定让 Toussaint 获得经济命脉, 但它对法国是什么性质——Toussaint 从没公开说过。

1798 之后, Toussaint 控制 Saint-Domingue 北部 + 西部。南部由 mulatto (混血) 将领 André Rigaud 控制。

EN: In autumn 1798, British forces withdrew from Saint-Domingue.

This was not a minor event. Britain had intervened in 1793, intending to seize what was then the world's wealthiest sugar colony during France's revolutionary chaos. Five years later, British losses stood at an estimated 40,000 or more—mostly not from battle, but from *fièvre jaune* (yellow fever). Toussaint Louverture's strategy: avoid direct confrontation, pin British forces in the hot coastal ports, and let yellow fever do its work.

**Careful framing**: The British withdrawal was negotiated by Toussaint himself with British General Thomas Maitland—they signed a secret agreement (Maitland-Toussaint Agreement, August 1798). Terms: British withdrawal plus opened trade for Saint-Domingue with Britain and the United States. Toussaint was already acting like the leader of a sovereign state, not a military commander of a French colony. Laurent Dubois, *Avengers of the New World* (Harvard, 2004), pp. 208-212: this deal gave Toussaint an economic lifeline, but its meaning toward France was something Toussaint never stated publicly.

After 1798, Toussaint controlled the north and west of Saint-Domingue. The south remained under mulatto General André Rigaud.

### §15.2 1799-1801 「颜色战争」——革命内部的内战

CN: 1799-1800 年, Toussaint 和 Rigaud 之间爆发全面战争, 史称「La Guerre des couteaux」(刀战)。

表面: 两名革命军将领的权力之争。
深层: 颜色政治。Rigaud 代表 affranchis (自由有色人, 即法律上自由的混血人), 他们 1791 年前就有公民权, 但跟被解放的黑人奴隶不同——他们自己曾拥有奴隶。Toussaint 代表 anciens esclaves (前奴隶), 黑人多数。

**反 Whig**: 不要把「海地革命」当成铁板一块的「黑人自由运动」。它内部有 affranchis vs anciens esclaves 的阶级 + 颜色 + 利益分裂——跟美国独立战争内部也有 Loyalists 一样。Dubois 2004 称这是「启蒙自由观念遭遇种族 + 经济现实的内部断裂」(pp. 230-240)。

1800 年 Toussaint 获胜。Rigaud 流亡。Toussaint 统一 Saint-Domingue。

EN: In 1799-1800, full-scale war broke out between Toussaint and Rigaud—known in history as *La Guerre des couteaux* (the War of Knives).

On the surface: a power struggle between two revolutionary generals. At the deeper level: color politics. Rigaud represented the *affranchis* (free people of color—legally free mixed-race people who had held civic rights before 1791 but had themselves sometimes owned enslaved people). Toussaint represented the *anciens esclaves* (former enslaved people), the Black majority.

**Anti-Whig**: Do not treat the Haitian Revolution as a monolithic "Black freedom movement." It contained real fractures of class, color, and interest—between *affranchis* and *anciens esclaves*—just as the American Revolution had internal Loyalists. Dubois 2004 calls this "the internal fracture where Enlightenment ideals of liberty collided with racial and economic reality" (pp. 230-240).

By 1800, Toussaint won. Rigaud went into exile. Toussaint unified Saint-Domingue.

### §15.3 1801.7.9 Saint-Domingue 宪法——「Saint-Domingue 的 Washington」?

CN: 1801 年 7 月 9 日, Toussaint 颁布《Saint-Domingue 宪法 1801》。

核心条款:
- 废奴永久有效 (slavery abolished permanently)
- Toussaint 任终身总督 (Governor-for-Life)
- 名义上仍是法国殖民地, 实际独立运作
- 宪法由 Saint-Domingue 本地精英起草, 未经巴黎批准, 甚至没送给 Napoleon Bonaparte 审阅就颁布

**careful framing — 「Saint-Domingue 的 Washington」还是「黑人 Napoleon」?**

C.L.R. James 1938《The Black Jacobins》(最早的重要英文学术分析) 曾把 Toussaint 称作「黑人 Napoleon」。但 Laurent Dubois 2004《Avengers of the New World》提出更准确的框架: Toussaint 是「Saint-Domingue 的 Washington」。

逻辑: Washington 1783 年后也是「革命者变成建国领袖」; Washington 在 1780s 的《美国宪法》起草中同样扮演威权性 presiding 角色; Washington 拥有奴隶至死 (Toussaint 废除了奴隶制)。「终身总督」是专制还是务实?

**反 Whig**: Dubois 2004 的 hedge 不可省略——在 1801 年 Napoleon 已经上台 (1799 雾月政变)、法国曾在 1794 废奴但 Napoleon 可能翻案、Saint-Domingue 周边有英国、美国、西班牙虎视眈眈的背景下, 1801 宪法是政治现实主义, 不是单纯的个人独裁欲望。用 21 世纪民主标准直接评判 Toussaint 是时代错置 (anachronism)。

但问题也真实存在: 1801 宪法有劳动管制条款——要求前奴隶留在种植园工作 (不同于奴隶制, 但有强制性)。Toussaint 知道没有出口糖, 经济就垮——而种植园需要劳动力。这是革命领袖的经济约束, 不是道德纯洁。

EN: On July 9, 1801, Toussaint promulgated the *Constitution of Saint-Domingue 1801*.

Key provisions:
- Slavery abolished permanently
- Toussaint appointed Governor-for-Life
- Nominally still a French colony, but operating independently in practice
- Written by local Saint-Domingue elites, approved without Paris, and promulgated without even sending it to Napoleon Bonaparte for review

**Careful framing — "Saint-Domingue's Washington" or "Black Napoleon"?**

C.L.R. James, *The Black Jacobins* (1938)—the first major English-language scholarly analysis—called Toussaint the "Black Napoleon." But Laurent Dubois, *Avengers of the New World* (2004), proposes a more precise frame: Toussaint was "Saint-Domingue's Washington."

The logic: Washington too was "revolutionary leader become founding father." Washington played an authoritative presiding role in drafting the 1780s US Constitution. Washington owned enslaved people until his death (Toussaint abolished slavery). Is "Governor-for-Life" despotism or pragmatism?

**Anti-Whig**: Dubois 2004's hedge must not be dropped. In 1801, Napoleon had already taken power (coup of 18 Brumaire, 1799); France had abolished slavery in 1794 but Napoleon might reverse it; Britain, the United States, and Spain were watching Saint-Domingue from every side. The 1801 Constitution was political realism, not simply a personal lust for dictatorship. Judging Toussaint by 21st-century democratic standards is anachronism.

But the tension is real: the 1801 Constitution included labor-control clauses requiring former enslaved people to remain on plantations (distinct from slavery, but compulsory). Toussaint knew that without sugar exports the economy would collapse—and plantations required labor. This is the economic constraint of a revolutionary leader, not a measure of moral purity.

## §16 1802 Napoleon 远征 + 1803 Toussaint 被捕死亡

### §16.1 Napoleon 1802 的目标

CN: Napoleon Bonaparte (1769-1821) 上台后, 对 Saint-Domingue 有明确意图。

1802 年 1 月, Napoleon 派他的内弟、将领 Charles Leclerc 率约 20,000 名法国士兵远征 Saint-Domingue。同年 5 月 20 日, Napoleon 正式颁令恢复法属加勒比海其他殖民地 (Guadeloupe 等) 的奴隶制——但 Saint-Domingue 法令措辞故意模糊。

**careful framing**: Napoleon 是否明确计划恢复 Saint-Domingue 的奴隶制? C.L.R. James 1938 + Dubois 2004 都认定: 意图明确。Leclerc 远征的目标之一是解除 Toussaint 的武装 + 恢复法国直接控制 + 最终恢复种植园经济。Guadeloupe 1802 恢复奴隶制的先例让 Saint-Domingue 人看清 Napoleon 的方向。Robin Blackburn,《The Overthrow of Colonial Slavery 1776-1848》(Verso, 1988), pp. 241-252: 「Napoleon 在 1802 年的 Saint-Domingue 政策是全面恢复殖民秩序, 废奴只是必须绕过的障碍。」

EN: After taking power, Napoleon Bonaparte (1769-1821) had clear intentions regarding Saint-Domingue.

In January 1802, Napoleon dispatched his brother-in-law General Charles Leclerc with approximately 20,000 French soldiers to Saint-Domingue. On May 20 of the same year, Napoleon formally decreed the restoration of slavery in France's other Caribbean colonies (Guadeloupe, etc.)—but the Saint-Domingue decree was deliberately left vague.

**Careful framing**: Did Napoleon explicitly plan to restore slavery in Saint-Domingue? Both C.L.R. James (1938) and Dubois (2004) conclude: yes, the intention was clear. The Leclerc expedition's goals included disarming Toussaint, restoring direct French control, and ultimately restoring the plantation economy. The precedent of Guadeloupe's 1802 restoration of slavery made Napoleon's direction unmistakable to Saint-Domingue's people. Robin Blackburn, *The Overthrow of Colonial Slavery 1776-1848* (Verso, 1988), pp. 241-252: "Napoleon's 1802 policy toward Saint-Domingue was the full restoration of colonial order, with abolition as merely an obstacle to be circumvented."

### §16.2 1802.6.7 被捕——「谁背叛了谁?」

CN: 1802 年春夏, Toussaint 跟 Leclerc 几度交战, 几度谈判。5 月 Leclerc 提出条件: 如果 Toussaint 同意停战 + 承认法国主权, 可以保留职位和荣誉。Toussaint 谈判接受。

但 1802 年 6 月 7 日, Toussaint 应邀前往将领 Jean-Baptiste Brunet 处会面 (据说是讨论当地治安事务)——到达后被逮捕。

**careful framing — 「谁背叛谁」**: Leclerc + Brunet 显然设下圈套——这次逮捕违反了 5 月停战协议的精神, 是 Leclerc 单方面决定消灭 Toussaint 政治存在的手段。Toussaint 被捕时有没有预感? Dubois 2004, p. 261-265: 「Toussaint 当时对谈判仍有幻想——他认为只要法国不恢复奴隶制, 双方有合作空间。这个幻想在 1802.5 Guadeloupe 恢复奴隶制后本应破灭, 但 Toussaint 低估了 Napoleon 的意图。」

被捕后, Toussaint 被立即装船运往法国。

EN: In the spring and summer of 1802, Toussaint alternated between fighting and negotiating with Leclerc. In May, Leclerc offered terms: if Toussaint accepted a ceasefire and acknowledged French sovereignty, he could keep his position and honors. Toussaint negotiated and accepted.

But on June 7, 1802, Toussaint was invited to meet with General Jean-Baptiste Brunet (ostensibly to discuss local security matters)—and was arrested upon arrival.

**Careful framing—"Who betrayed whom?"**: Leclerc and Brunet clearly set a trap. This arrest violated the spirit of the May ceasefire—it was Leclerc's unilateral decision to eliminate Toussaint's political existence. Did Toussaint have any premonition? Dubois 2004, pp. 261-265: "Toussaint still harbored illusions about negotiation—he believed that as long as France did not restore slavery, there was room for cooperation. That illusion should have shattered when Guadeloupe restored slavery in May 1802, but Toussaint underestimated Napoleon's intentions."

After his arrest, Toussaint was immediately put on a ship bound for France.

### §16.3 Fort de Joux 囚禁 + 1803.4.7 死亡

CN: Toussaint 被送往法国东部 Franche-Comté 地区汝拉山 (Jura Mountains) 中的 Fort de Joux——一座建于 12 世纪、用于关押政治犯的石头要塞。海拔约 1,000 米, 冬天极寒。

他被单独关押。法国当局没有指控他任何罪名, 没有审判。他不断要求见 Napoleon, 要求解释被捕原因——没有回应。

1802 年秋冬, Toussaint 开始衰弱: 营养不足, 囚室漏风潮湿, 拒绝提供足够的木柴取暖。1803 年 4 月 7 日, Toussaint Louverture 死于 Fort de Joux。他的狱卒 (geôlier) 报告记录死因: 肺炎 + 胸膜炎——与囚禁环境直接相关 (C.L.R. James 1938《The Black Jacobins》第 13 章 + Dubois 2004, pp. 277-280 引原始档案)。

他死时 59 岁或 60 岁。

**「传说中的最后遗言」——careful framing 必须在此**:

流传最广的 Toussaint 最后遗言是:

> *「En me renversant, on n'a abattu à Saint-Domingue que le tronc de l'arbre de la liberté ; il repoussera par les racines, car elles sont profondes et nombreuses.」*
> 
> (「打倒我, 只是砍掉了自由之树的主干; 它会从根重生, 因为根又深又多。」)

但这句话的来源需要 hedge: C.L.R. James 1938 引用了这句话, 但他自己的原始文献来源是 19 世纪的传记 + pamphlet 文学——并非 Toussaint 当时留下的书写记录。Dubois 2004 在 p. 280 脚注处明确: 「这句话的文字形式最早见于后世文献, 是否确为 Toussaint 原话无法确证——但它已成为海地革命历史记忆的核心文本。」这不代表这句话是「假的」, 它代表「口述革命传统」(oral revolutionary tradition)——和美国革命中 Patrick Henry 「Give me liberty or give me death」的来源问题一样复杂。

EN: Toussaint was sent to Fort de Joux—a stone fortress built in the 12th century in the Jura Mountains of Franche-Comté in eastern France, used for political prisoners. Elevation approximately 1,000 meters; winters brutally cold.

He was held in solitary confinement. French authorities filed no formal charges and held no trial. He repeatedly requested an audience with Napoleon, demanding an explanation for his arrest—no response came.

Through the autumn and winter of 1802, Toussaint deteriorated: insufficient food, a drafty and damp cell, firewood deliberately withheld. On April 7, 1803, Toussaint Louverture died at Fort de Joux. His jailer's (*geôlier*) report listed the cause of death as pneumonia and pleurisy—directly related to his conditions of confinement (C.L.R. James, *The Black Jacobins*, 1938, ch. 13; Dubois 2004, pp. 277-280, citing original archives).

He was 59 or 60 years old.

**"The legendary last words"—careful framing required here**:

The most widely cited last words attributed to Toussaint are:

> *"En me renversant, on n'a abattu à Saint-Domingue que le tronc de l'arbre de la liberté ; il repoussera par les racines, car elles sont profondes et nombreuses."*
>
> ("In overthrowing me, you have done no more in Saint-Domingue than cut down the trunk of the tree of liberty. It will spring back from the roots, for they are numerous and deep.")

But the source requires a hedge: C.L.R. James (1938) cited these words, but his own original sources were 19th-century biographies and pamphlet literature—not a contemporaneous written record by Toussaint himself. Dubois 2004, in a footnote on p. 280, explicitly states: "The written form of this statement first appears in later documents; whether it represents Toussaint's actual words cannot be confirmed—but it has become a core text in the historical memory of the Haitian Revolution." This does not mean the quotation is "false." It belongs to an *oral revolutionary tradition*—as complex in origin as Patrick Henry's "Give me liberty or give me death" in the American revolutionary context.

## §17 Olympe de Gouges 1793 断头台: 预言的实现

### §17.1 1792 大革命激进化——Gouges 的政治立场

CN: 1789-1792 年, 法国大革命从「改革君主制」走向「共和」。关键转折: 1792.8 革命民众攻打 Tuileries 王宫, 路易十六被废; 1792.9.20 Valmy 炮战之后第一共和国宣告成立。

在这个节点, Gouges 做了一个让她日后送命的政治选择: **她为路易十六辩护**。

1793 年 1 月路易十六即将被处死, Gouges 公开发表文章, 提出应该让法国人民通过全民公投 (referendum) 决定国王的命运, 而不是由国民公会 (Convention) 直接判决。她同情 Girondins (吉伦特派)——温和共和派, 反对 Jacobin 山岳派的暴力激进路线。

**反 Whig**: 不要把 Gouges 的立场简化为「她支持国王」——她反对的是未经人民授权的审判程序, 是对革命暴力逻辑的异议。她自己是共和主义者 + 废奴主义者 + 女性权利倡导者。Joan Scott,《Only Paradoxes to Offer: French Feminists and the Rights of Man》(Harvard, 1996), pp. 30-35: 「Gouges 被处死, 根本原因是她拒绝接受 Jacobin 的政治权威——她用启蒙的普世理性挑战山岳派, 而山岳派把自己的权力建立在同样一套普世理性的名义下。」

EN: Between 1789 and 1792, the French Revolution moved from "reform of the monarchy" toward "republic." The turning point: in August 1792, revolutionary crowds stormed the Tuileries palace; Louis XVI was deposed. On September 20, after the cannonade of Valmy, the First Republic was declared.

At this moment, Gouges made a political choice that would eventually cost her life: **she defended Louis XVI**.

In January 1793, as Louis XVI was about to be executed, Gouges publicly argued that the French people should decide the king's fate through a popular referendum rather than by direct vote of the National Convention. She sympathized with the Girondins—the moderate republican faction, opposed to the Jacobin Mountain's path of violent radicalism.

**Anti-Whig**: Do not simplify Gouges's position as "she supported the king." What she opposed was a trial process lacking authorization from the people—a dissent against the logic of revolutionary violence. She was herself a republican, an abolitionist, and an advocate of women's rights. Joan Scott, *Only Paradoxes to Offer: French Feminists and the Rights of Man* (Harvard, 1996), pp. 30-35: "Gouges was executed because she refused to accept Jacobin political authority—she used Enlightenment universal reason to challenge the Mountain, while the Mountain built its own power on the same invocation of universal reason."

### §17.2 1793.11.3 断头台——「她用自己的死完成了她的逻辑」

CN: 1793 年 7 月, Robespierre 领导的 Jacobin 山岳派彻底清洗吉伦特派。1793.11.3, Olympe de Gouges 在 Paris Place de la Révolution (革命广场, 今 Place de la Concorde) 被断头台处死。

罪名: 「保王党 (royalist) + 反革命」。实际原因: 她支持吉伦特派 + 她反对对国王的不经公投的处死 + 她持续用文章批评 Jacobin 政府的暴力。

**「断头台前的最后一句」——careful framing**:

流传最广的 Gouges 死前遗言是:

> *「一个女人能被推上断头台, 她为什么不能登上讲台?」*

这正是她 1791 年《女性权利和女性公民权利宣言》(*Déclaration des droits de la femme et de la citoyenne*) 第 10 条的文字: 「如果女人有权被推上断头台, 那么她就应该有同等的权利登上讲台。」

她用自己的死, 完成了她自己写下的逻辑。

**careful framing**: 这句话是否确实是她在断头台现场说的? Olivier Blanc,《Olympe de Gouges》(Syros, 2003), p. 198 hedge: 「Gouges 最后遗言的来源是当时的 pamphlet 文学 + 共和派报纸的报道——这类文献有后世建构的可能性。她是否在死前说了这句话, 或者这句话是事后把她的文字作品归纳在她的死亡时刻, 无法从原始档案确证。」但这句话的内容——无论是否确为现场遗言——已成为feminist 史学的经典引文, 因为它的逻辑结构来自她本人的著作是有案可查的。

**反 Whig**: Gouges 不是「因为要求女性权利被杀」——她是因为政治立场 (反 Jacobin + 支持吉伦特派 + 为路易十六辩护) 被杀。但她被杀的方式 (断头台)——与她自己《宣言》第 10 条对应——创造了历史上最具讽刺意味的自我证明。Joan Scott 1996, p. 33: 「Gouges 的死变成了她预言的实现——不是因为革命者设计了这种讽刺, 而是因为她的文字结构本身已经包含了这种可能性。」

EN: In July 1793, the Jacobin Mountain led by Robespierre completed its purge of the Girondins. On November 3, 1793, Olympe de Gouges was executed by guillotine at the Place de la Révolution in Paris (today's Place de la Concorde).

The charge: "royalist and counter-revolutionary." The actual reasons: she had supported the Girondins, opposed the execution of the king without a popular vote, and continued to publish articles criticizing the violence of the Jacobin government.

**"The last sentence at the guillotine"—careful framing**:

The most widely cited last words attributed to Gouges are:

> *"Une femme peut monter à l'échafaud ; elle doit pouvoir monter à la tribune."*
> ("A woman can be sent to the scaffold; she should equally be able to mount the podium.")

This is precisely the language of Article 10 of her 1791 *Déclaration des droits de la femme et de la citoyenne*: "If a woman has the right to mount the scaffold, she must equally have the right to mount the podium."

She completed her own written logic with her own death.

**Careful framing**: Was this actually said at the guillotine? Olivier Blanc, *Olympe de Gouges* (Syros, 2003), p. 198, hedges: "Gouges's last words come from contemporary pamphlet literature and republican newspaper reports—sources that carry the possibility of later construction. Whether she spoke this sentence at the moment of her death, or whether the sentence was retrospectively attached to that moment from her published writings, cannot be confirmed from original archives." But the content of the words—whatever its precise provenance—has become a canonical text in feminist historiography, because its logical structure demonstrably comes from her own documented writing.

**Anti-Whig**: Gouges was not killed for demanding women's rights—she was killed for her political positions (anti-Jacobin, pro-Girondin, defense of Louis XVI's right to a referendum). But the manner of her death (guillotine)—mapped against Article 10 of her own *Déclaration*—created the most ironically self-confirming moment in Enlightenment history. Joan Scott 1996, p. 33: "Gouges's death became the fulfillment of her own prophecy—not because the revolutionaries designed that irony, but because the structure of her writing had already contained that possibility."

### §17.3 跨 lens 连接: 1793 年, Gouges 死的那年

CN: 1793 年 11 月, Olympe de Gouges 在断头台死去。

同一年, 1793 年 8 月, Toussaint Louverture 在大西洋另一端的 Saint-Domingue 宣布转投法国革命政府——带着他的军队对抗西班牙军队, 换取法国 1793.8 废奴令的承认。

**跨 lens 时刻**: 两个被「启蒙普世价值」包含又排斥的人——一个是巴黎的 lonely-mediator 女性剧作家, 用说话的权利换来断头台; 一个是 Saint-Domingue 的 receiving-end 前奴隶, 用军事能力换来短暂的政治生存空间。他们从未见面, 从未通信, 但他们的生命弧线在 1793 年的启蒙矛盾里同时发生。

EN: In November 1793, Olympe de Gouges died at the guillotine.

That same year, in August 1793, Toussaint Louverture—on the other side of the Atlantic in Saint-Domingue—declared his switch to the French revolutionary government, bringing his army to fight against Spanish forces in exchange for recognition under France's August 1793 abolition decree.

**Cross-lens moment**: Two people simultaneously included in and excluded from "Enlightenment universal values"—one a lonely-mediator female playwright in Paris, paying for the right to speak with her life at the guillotine; one a receiving-end formerly enslaved man in Saint-Domingue, trading military capacity for a brief space of political survival. They never met. They never corresponded. But their life arcs unfolded simultaneously inside the contradictions of 1793 Enlightenment.

## §18 1804 海地独立 + 1825「赔偿」

### §18.1 Toussaint 之后——Dessalines + 1803 Vertières

CN: Toussaint 1802 年 6 月被捕后, Saint-Domingue 的革命并没有停止。

领导权转到 Jean-Jacques Dessalines (1758-1806)。Dessalines 曾是 Toussaint 的重要将领, 他的战术比 Toussaint 更激进, 对法国的幻想也早已归零。

1803 年, 两件事同时发生:
1. **Napoleon 放弃 Saint-Domingue**: 黄热病第二次摧毁法军——Leclerc 本人 1802.11 死于黄热病。继任者 Rochambeau 残暴镇压 (包括使用猎犬撕咬被俘革命者), 但无力回天。同时 1803.5 法英重新开战 (Napoleonic Wars), Napoleon 需要资源——他开始谈判以出售 Louisiana Territory 给美国 (Louisiana Purchase, 1803.4, 约 $15 million)。Saint-Domingue 计划实际上已被放弃。

2. **1803.11.18 Vertières 战役**: Dessalines 领导的革命军在 Cap-Français (今 Cap-Haïtien) 附近的 Vertières 击败法军最后主力。法军残部谈判撤退。

**careful framing**: 海地革命军获胜, 黄热病是不可忽视的因素。法国两次远征军 (英国 1793-1798 + 法国 1802-1803) 都被黄热病 + 战场抵抗的组合打败。Dubois 2004, pp. 285-291: 「这不降低革命者的军事能力——Toussaint + Dessalines 的战略是有意识地利用环境优势, 这本身就是战略。」

EN: After Toussaint's arrest in June 1802, the revolution in Saint-Domingue did not stop.

Leadership passed to Jean-Jacques Dessalines (1758-1806), once one of Toussaint's leading generals. His tactics were more radical than Toussaint's; his illusions about France had collapsed far earlier.

In 1803, two things happened simultaneously:
1. **Napoleon abandoned Saint-Domingue**: Yellow fever destroyed the French army a second time—Leclerc himself died of yellow fever in November 1802. His successor Rochambeau resorted to brutal repression (including using dogs to kill captured revolutionaries), but the effort was already lost. Simultaneously, in May 1803, France and Britain went back to war (the Napoleonic Wars); Napoleon needed resources. He was already negotiating the sale of the Louisiana Territory to the United States (Louisiana Purchase, April 1803, approximately $15 million). The Saint-Domingue project had effectively been abandoned.

2. **November 18, 1803, Battle of Vertières**: Revolutionary forces under Dessalines defeated the last major French force near Cap-Français (today Cap-Haïtien). French remnants negotiated a withdrawal.

**Careful framing**: Yellow fever was an unavoidable factor in the revolutionary army's victory. Both foreign expeditions—Britain 1793-1798 and France 1802-1803—were broken by a combination of yellow fever and battlefield resistance. Dubois 2004, pp. 285-291: "This does not diminish the military capability of the revolutionaries—Toussaint's and Dessalines's strategy deliberately exploited environmental advantage, which is itself strategy."

### §18.2 1804.1.1 海地独立

CN: 1804 年 1 月 1 日, Jean-Jacques Dessalines 在 Gonaïves 宣布独立。

**两个「世界第一」**:
- 世界上第一个由奴隶起义建立的独立国家
- 西半球第二个摆脱欧洲殖民的独立国家 (第一是美国, 1776)

**国名「Haïti」的来源**: 来自 Taíno 原住民语言中的「Ayiti」(多山的土地)。这是 1804 年革命领袖有意识的历史选择——拒绝法国殖民名称「Saint-Domingue」, 回到这块土地在欧洲人到来之前的名字。

**跨 Topic**: 「Ayiti」这个词里活着的, 是 1508 年前后 Hispaniola 岛上的 Taíno 记忆 (见 Pre-Columbian Topic, Anacaona Jr. 节)。从 Taíno 的 Hispaniola 到海地的 Haïti, 同一块土地上, 两种抵抗, 隔了约 300 年。

**1804.2-4 屠杀——careful framing 不可省略**:

Dessalines 在独立后下令屠杀岛上大多数留守的白人 (估计 3,000-5,000 人被杀, 2-4 个月内)。幸免者主要是: 波兰士兵 (曾反对参与镇压革命)、德裔工匠、医生、及部分与革命者有个人关系者。

**careful framing**: Dubois 2004, pp. 299-302: 「Dessalines 的屠杀必须放在历史背景下理解——200 年奴隶制暴力、法军两年来在 Rochambeau 指挥下的系统性屠杀 + 活埋 + 猎犬处决——但理解背景不等于道德正当化。」屠杀是真实的历史事实, 对受害者的暴力是真实的, 对 Dessalines 的道德评价不能因为背景而消失——只能变得更复杂, 更诚实。

**Grade 7 careful framing**: 革命里的暴力不是单方向的。这不意味着「双方都一样坏」——而意味着历史里的人在极端条件下会做出我们很难从今天的舒适状态评判的事情。

EN: On January 1, 1804, Jean-Jacques Dessalines declared independence at Gonaïves.

**Two "world firsts"**:
- The first independent state created by an enslaved people's uprising
- The second nation in the Western Hemisphere to break free from European colonialism (the United States was first, 1776)

**The name "Haïti"**: From the Taíno indigenous word "Ayiti" ("land of mountains"). This was a deliberate historical choice by the revolutionary leaders of 1804—rejecting the French colonial name "Saint-Domingue" and returning to the name this land carried before Europeans arrived.

**Cross-Topic**: Living inside the word "Ayiti" is the Taíno memory of Hispaniola Island around 1508 (see Pre-Columbian Topic, Anacaona Jr. section). From the Taíno's Hispaniola to Haiti's Haïti—the same land, two forms of resistance, approximately 300 years apart.

**The 1804 massacres—careful framing cannot be omitted**:

After independence, Dessalines ordered the killing of most white people remaining on the island (estimated 3,000-5,000 killed over 2-4 months). Those spared included: Polish soldiers (who had resisted participating in suppressing the revolution), German craftsmen, physicians, and some individuals with personal relationships to revolutionary leaders.

**Careful framing**: Dubois 2004, pp. 299-302: "Dessalines's massacres must be understood in historical context—200 years of enslaved-system violence, plus two years of systematic killing, burial alive, and dog executions under Rochambeau's command—but understanding context is not the same as moral justification." The massacres are real historical facts; the violence against victims was real; the moral evaluation of Dessalines cannot dissolve because of context—it can only become more complex and more honest.

**Grade 7 note**: Violence in revolution does not flow in only one direction. This does not mean "both sides were equally bad"—it means that people in history, under extreme conditions, do things that are very difficult to evaluate from today's position of comfort.

### §18.3 1825「赔偿」——12 岁必须理解这件事

CN: 1804 年独立后, 没有任何国家承认海地。美国拒绝 (因为美国南方种植园主害怕海地革命「传染」)。法国拒绝。英国拒绝。

海地在孤立中挣扎了 21 年。

1825 年, 法国国王 Charles X 终于「承认」海地独立——条件是: 海地支付 **1.5 亿法郎赔偿** (*l'indemnité*, the indemnity)。赔偿对象是: 因海地革命失去财产 (包括失去奴隶) 的法国种植园主。

**Grade 7 具体比喻** (必须有):

想象: 一个孩子在家里被虐待了 18 年, 终于逃出去了。逃出去 21 年后, 虐待他的那家人说: 「我们承认你是独立的人——但你必须赔偿我们, 因为你逃走让我们损失了一个免费劳动力。」而且这个孩子为了付这笔钱, 借了高利贷——然后用接下来 100 多年的时间在还这笔债。

这就是 1825 年发生的事。

**史实细节**: 1.5 亿法郎对 1825 年的海地是什么量级? 估计相当于海地当年 GDP 的 10 倍以上 (Thomas Piketty 等人 2022 年相关研究: 「La dette de réparations d'Haïti: une catastrophe économique séculaire」)。海地向法国 + 向支持这笔债务的外国银行 (主要是法国 Crédit mobilier 等) 举债。最后的债务支付延续到 20 世纪。

2003 年海地总统 Jean-Bertrand Aristide 要求法国「返还」这 1.5 亿法郎加利息 (总计约 210 亿美元)。法国政府拒绝。

**反 Whig**: 标准欧洲叙事是「海地因为革命后管理不善 + 政治腐败而贫穷」。这个叙事不是完全错的, 但它系统性地省略了 1825 年赔偿债务对海地发展的结构性影响。Piketty 2022 等研究者提出: 这笔债务是海地 19-20 世纪欠发展的重大结构原因之一——不是唯一原因, 但被主流叙事长期忽视。

EN: After 1804 independence, no country recognized Haiti. The United States refused (Southern plantation owners feared revolutionary "contagion"). France refused. Britain refused.

Haiti struggled in isolation for 21 years.

In 1825, French King Charles X finally "recognized" Haitian independence—on condition that Haiti pay **150 million francs in compensation** (*l'indemnité*, the indemnity). The beneficiaries: French plantation owners who had lost property (including their enslaved people) in the Haitian Revolution.

**Grade 7 concrete analogy (required)**:

Imagine: a child was abused in a household for 18 years, and finally escaped. Twenty-one years later, the family that abused him says: "We recognize you as an independent person—but you must compensate us, because your escape cost us a free laborer." And to pay this sum, the child borrowed money at high interest rates—and then spent more than 100 years paying off that debt.

That is what happened in 1825.

**Historical scale**: What did 150 million francs mean for Haiti in 1825? Estimated at more than 10 times Haiti's annual GDP at the time (see Thomas Piketty et al., 2022 related research: *"La dette de réparations d'Haïti: une catastrophe économique séculaire"*). Haiti borrowed from France and from foreign banks supporting the debt (primarily French institutions including Crédit mobilier). The final debt payments extended into the 20th century.

In 2003, Haitian President Jean-Bertrand Aristide demanded France "return" the 150 million francs plus interest (totaling approximately $21 billion). The French government declined.

**Anti-Whig**: The standard European narrative is that "Haiti is poor because of post-revolutionary mismanagement and political corruption." This narrative is not entirely false, but it systematically omits the structural effect of the 1825 indemnity debt on Haiti's development. Piketty et al. (2022) and other researchers argue that this debt was one of the major structural causes of Haiti's 19th- and 20th-century underdevelopment—not the only cause, but one systematically omitted from mainstream accounts.

## §19 三 lens 1780 切片 + Cross-Topic 缝合 + reflection

### §19.1 三 lens 1780 切片

CN: 三个人在同一年: 1780 年。

**Voltaire**: 已经死了两年。1778 年 5 月 30 日, Voltaire 在巴黎死于前列腺疾病, 83 岁。但他的影响比他的身体活得更长——《哲学辞典》(*Dictionnaire philosophique*, 1764)、《candide ou l'optimisme》(1759)、卡拉斯案 (affaire Calas, 1762-1765) 的辩护文章、百科全书 (《Encyclopédie》) 的参与——这些文本 1780 年在整个欧洲流传。启蒙的「perpetrator-actor」已经完成了他的作品, 正在变成一个被引用的符号。

**Gouges**: 1780 年, Olympe de Gouges 32 岁。她已经在巴黎生活多年, 正在写剧本, 活跃在 Palais-Royal 一带的知识沙龙。她还没有写《女性宣言》(1791), 大革命还有 9 年才爆发。她不知道自己会死于断头台。她 1780 年的身份: 一个有话要说但还没找到历史性时机的剧作家。

**Toussaint**: 1780 年, Toussaint Louverture 大约 37 岁。他出生于 Saint-Domingue 北部 Bréda 种植园——他的父亲，据 C.L.R. James 1938 版本叫 Gaou Guinou，据部分海地史料叫 Hyppolite Thésée，两个名字来自不同来源，学界未有定论（careful framing：§11.1 对此已注）。父亲据说是从西非 (Allada 王国, 今 Bénin) 被贩卖来的第二代非洲人。Toussaint 本人的法律身份: 「自由黑人」(affranchi libre)——他在 1776 年前后被 Bréda 种植园主正式释放。1780 年他的职位: 种植园的马夫管事 (*commandeur*)。**〔注：获得自由身后仍在原种植园担任 commandeur，是当时 affranchi 常见的过渡性安排，不等于「自由黑人必须脱离种植园」〕**他在这个时期读书——他接触到 Abbé Raynal 的《两印度史》(*Histoire philosophique et politique des établissements et du commerce des Européens dans les deux Indes*, 1770, 修订 1780)。Raynal 在书中明确预言: 「黑人的 Spartacus 终将出现」。

三个人: 一个已经完成自己的历史角色; 一个正在等待历史; 一个正在准备自己。

EN: Three people in the same year: 1780.

**Voltaire**: Dead for two years. Voltaire died in Paris on May 30, 1778, from a prostate condition, aged 83. But his influence outlived his body—the *Dictionnaire philosophique* (1764), *Candide ou l'optimisme* (1759), the Calas Affair (*affaire Calas*, 1762-1765) pamphlets, and his contributions to the *Encyclopédie*—all of these were circulating across Europe in 1780. The Enlightenment's "perpetrator-actor" had completed his work and was in the process of becoming a symbol to be cited.

**Gouges**: In 1780, Olympe de Gouges was 32 years old. She had lived in Paris for years, was writing plays, and was active in the intellectual salons around the Palais-Royal. She had not yet written the *Déclaration des droits de la femme* (1791); the Revolution was nine years away. She did not know she would die at the guillotine. Her identity in 1780: a playwright with something to say who had not yet found a historical moment.

**Toussaint**: In 1780, Toussaint Louverture was approximately 37 years old. He was born on the Bréda plantation in northern Saint-Domingue—his father Hyppolite Thésée was reportedly a second-generation African, brought from the Kingdom of Allada (today's Bénin) in the slave trade. Toussaint's own legal status: "free Black man" (*affranchi libre*)—formally emancipated by the Bréda plantation owner around 1776. His position in 1780: overseer of the plantation's stables (*commandeur*). During this period he was reading—he had access to Abbé Raynal's *Histoire philosophique et politique des établissements et du commerce des Européens dans les deux Indes* (1770, revised 1780). Raynal in that book explicitly predicted: "A Black Spartacus will eventually appear."

Three people: one who had already completed his historical role; one who was waiting for history; one who was preparing himself.

### §19.2 Cross-Topic 缝合: 五条线

---

**缝合 1: Scientific Revolution Newton → Voltaire 《哲学书简》**

Voltaire 1726-1729 年在英国流亡期间接触了 Newton 的自然哲学。1734 年他出版《哲学书简》(*Lettres philosophiques*, 也叫《英国书简》), 用 Newton 的物理学方法论当武器: 如果自然界服从可被理性推导的定律, 为什么法国社会不能用同样的理性方法审视 + 改革?

这是跨领域迁移的经典案例: Newton 的苹果证明了普遍引力定律——Voltaire 的笔把「普遍」这个词从物理搬进政治。

**跨 Topic**: Scientific Revolution Topic 的「system-builder」Newton 提供了工具; Enlightenment Topic 的「perpetrator-actor」Voltaire 把这个工具武器化。

---

**缝合 2: Reformation 印刷术 → Voltaire + 百科全书**

1517 年 Erasmus 出版希腊语《新约》+ Luther 印《九十五条论纲》(*Ninety-Five Theses*)——这是「印刷武器」(print weapon) 的第一次大规模政治使用。

1751 年 Diderot + d'Alembert 主编《百科全书》(*Encyclopédie, ou dictionnaire raisonné des sciences, des arts et des métiers*, 1751-1772, 17 卷文字 + 11 卷图版)——这是同一条印刷武器的第三代。不同的战场: Reformation 打的是教会对知识的垄断; Enlightenment 打的是王权 + 教会对政治 + 哲学的垄断。武器相同, 目标升级。

**跨 Topic**: Reformation Topic 的「perpetrator-actor」Luther 打开了「印刷 = 颠覆」的先例; Enlightenment 的《百科全书》继承并扩大了这个先例。

---

**缝合 3: Byzantine Theodora → Olympe de Gouges**

532 年, 拜占庭皇后 Theodora (c. 500-548) 在 Nika 暴乱中站起来对皇帝 Justinian 说:「皇冠是最好的裹尸布。」——她用声音稳住了一个摇摇欲坠的王朝。她成功了, 活了下来, 影响了帝国 16 年。

1793 年, Olympe de Gouges 用声音挑战一个走向恐怖统治的革命政权——她失败了, 死了, 但她的文字活了下来。

两个 lonely-mediator 女性——一个在东罗马 6 世纪, 一个在法兰西 18 世纪——都在用自己的声音试图稳住 / 校正权力的方向。她们的结果截然不同: 一个成功存活, 一个死亡留名。但她们使用的工具是同一个: 在男性主导的权力空间里, 用语言创造影响。

**跨 Topic**: Byzantine Topic 的「lonely-mediator」Theodora 的策略是「用声音保住现存秩序」; Enlightenment Topic 的「lonely-mediator」Gouges 的策略是「用声音挑战正在建立的新秩序」。同一个工具, 两种方向, 两种命运。

---

**缝合 4: Pre-Columbian Anacaona Jr. → Toussaint Louverture**

1508 年前后, Hispaniola 岛的 Taíno 少女「Anacaona Jr.」(学者虚构的综合名, 代表 1508 年左右仍在岛上抵抗的最后一代 Taíno 年轻人) 见证了 Taíno 文明在西班牙殖民下的最后消亡——从 1492 到 1542 年, Hispaniola 岛的 Taíno 人口从约 30-60 万下降到不足 500 人 (Las Casas 估计, Noble David Cook 修正)。

1743 年, 在同一块土地 Hispaniola 上——此时叫 Saint-Domingue——Toussaint Louverture 出生在 Bréda 种植园。他是非洲后裔, 不是 Taíno 后裔; 他的到来是奴隶制的产物, 与 Taíno 的消亡有直接历史连接——Taíno 被灭绝后, 空出的劳动力缺口由非洲奴隶贸易填补。

1804 年, 海地革命领袖选择「Haïti」作为国名——Taíno 语中的「Ayiti」。同一块土地, 235 年: 从 Taíno 抵抗到黑人革命, 从 Anacaona Jr. 到 Toussaint 到 Dessalines, 抵抗的形式变了, 但土地没有。

**跨 Topic**: Pre-Columbian Topic 的 Anacaona Jr. 的消亡 + Enlightenment Topic 的 Toussaint 的革命, 是同一块土地上两种殖民暴力的两端——中间的连接是跨大西洋奴隶贸易。

---

**缝合 5: Phase 2 桥接 (Grade 8 钩子)**

1804 年海地独立——世界上第一个「自由、平等、博爱」在所有人 (不论肤色) 身上真正实践的国家——但它立刻被孤立, 被勒索, 被要求付出「赔偿」。

启蒙矛盾没有在 1804 年解决。它被移交给 19 世纪:

- 1848: Karl Marx + Friedrich Engels 《共产党宣言》(*Manifest der Kommunistischen Partei*)——「一个幽灵在欧洲游荡」——这次不是「理性」的普世平等, 而是「阶级」的普世解放
- 1848: 法国第二共和国废除法属殖民地奴隶制 (终于)——比海地 1804 晚了 44 年
- 1863: 美国林肯《解放宣言》(*Emancipation Proclamation*)——比海地 1804 晚了 59 年

启蒙的逻辑: 它生产了「自由」的概念, 但它没有能力 (或者没有意愿) 让「自由」包含所有人。这个矛盾被传递到 19 世纪, 再传递到 20 世纪的去殖民化运动。Voltaire、Gouges、Toussaint——他们三个人各自代表了启蒙矛盾的三个维度: perpetrator (推动启蒙但自身有盲区) / lonely-mediator (在启蒙内部要求其自身逻辑的彻底实践) / receiving-end (用启蒙的语言对抗启蒙的施害者)。

EN: **Stitch 1: Scientific Revolution Newton → Voltaire's *Lettres philosophiques***

During his English exile 1726-1729, Voltaire encountered Newton's natural philosophy. In 1734 he published the *Lettres philosophiques* (also known as the *Letters Concerning the English Nation*), using Newton's method as a weapon: if nature obeys laws that reason can derive, why can't French society be examined and reformed by the same rational method?

This is a classic case of cross-domain transfer: Newton's apple demonstrated a universal law of gravitation—Voltaire's pen moved the word "universal" from physics into politics.

**Cross-Topic**: The Scientific Revolution Topic's "system-builder" Newton provided the tool; the Enlightenment Topic's "perpetrator-actor" Voltaire weaponized it.

---

**Stitch 2: Reformation printing press → Voltaire + the *Encyclopédie***

In 1517, Erasmus published the Greek New Testament and Luther printed the *Ninety-Five Theses*—this was the first large-scale political use of the "print weapon." In 1751, Diderot and d'Alembert edited the *Encyclopédie, ou dictionnaire raisonné des sciences, des arts et des métiers* (1751-1772, 17 text volumes + 11 plate volumes)—this was the third generation of the same print weapon. Different battlefield: the Reformation attacked the Church's monopoly on knowledge; the Enlightenment attacked the monarchy's and Church's monopoly on politics and philosophy. Same weapon, upgraded target.

**Cross-Topic**: The Reformation Topic's "perpetrator-actor" Luther established the precedent that "print = subversion"; the Enlightenment's *Encyclopédie* inherited and expanded that precedent.

---

**Stitch 3: Byzantine Theodora → Olympe de Gouges**

In 532, Byzantine Empress Theodora (c. 500-548) stood up during the Nika Riots and told Emperor Justinian: "The purple is the noblest shroud." She used her voice to stabilize a dynasty on the verge of collapse. She succeeded, survived, and influenced the empire for 16 years.

In 1793, Olympe de Gouges used her voice to challenge a revolutionary government sliding into the Reign of Terror—she failed, died, but her words survived.

Two lonely-mediator women—one in 6th-century Eastern Rome, one in 18th-century France—both used their voices to try to steady or correct the direction of power. Their outcomes were completely different: one survived successfully; one died into legend. But the tool was the same: in male-dominated power spaces, using language to create influence.

**Cross-Topic**: The Byzantine Topic's lonely-mediator Theodora's strategy was "use voice to preserve the existing order"; the Enlightenment Topic's lonely-mediator Gouges's strategy was "use voice to challenge the order being newly established." Same tool, two directions, two fates.

---

**Stitch 4: Pre-Columbian Anacaona Jr. → Toussaint Louverture**

Around 1508, on the island of Hispaniola, Taíno girl "Anacaona Jr." (a composite name used by scholars to represent the last generation of Taíno youth still resisting around 1508) witnessed the final extinction of Taíno civilization under Spanish colonialism—from 1492 to 1542, the Taíno population of Hispaniola collapsed from an estimated 300,000-600,000 to fewer than 500 (Las Casas's estimate, revised by Noble David Cook).

In 1743, on the same land of Hispaniola—now called Saint-Domingue—Toussaint Louverture was born on the Bréda plantation. He was of African descent, not Taíno; his presence was a product of slavery, directly historically connected to Taíno extinction—after the Taíno were destroyed, the resulting labor gap was filled by the transatlantic slave trade.

In 1804, Haiti's revolutionary leaders chose "Haïti" as the nation's name—"Ayiti" in the Taíno language. The same land, 235 years: from Taíno resistance to Black revolution, from Anacaona Jr. to Toussaint to Dessalines, the form of resistance changed, but the land did not.

**Cross-Topic**: Pre-Columbian Topic's Anacaona Jr.'s destruction + Enlightenment Topic's Toussaint's revolution are the two ends of two forms of colonial violence on the same land—the link between them is the transatlantic slave trade.

---

**Stitch 5: Phase 2 bridge (Grade 8 hook)**

1804 Haiti independence—the world's first country where "liberty, equality, fraternity" was genuinely practiced for all people regardless of skin color—was immediately isolated, extorted, and required to pay "compensation."

The Enlightenment contradiction was not resolved in 1804. It was handed to the 19th century:

- 1848: Karl Marx + Friedrich Engels, *Manifest der Kommunistischen Partei* (Communist Manifesto)—"A specter is haunting Europe"—this time not "rational" universal equality, but "class" universal liberation
- 1848: The French Second Republic abolished slavery in French colonies (finally)—44 years after Haiti's 1804
- 1863: Lincoln's *Emancipation Proclamation*—59 years after Haiti's 1804

The Enlightenment's logic: it produced the concept of "liberty," but it lacked the capacity (or willingness) to include everyone in "liberty." That contradiction was passed to the 19th century, and then to the 20th-century decolonization movements. Voltaire, Gouges, Toussaint—the three of them each represent one dimension of the Enlightenment contradiction: perpetrator (drives Enlightenment but has blind spots in his own life) / lonely-mediator (demands from within Enlightenment that its own logic be fully practiced) / receiving-end (uses Enlightenment's language against Enlightenment's perpetrators).
