## §9 历史观四派 / Four Schools of Tang-Song Historiography

### CN

研究 Tang-Song 中国不是单一答案。21 世纪学界至少有四派,各派背后是不同政治 + 哲学立场。读历史书时知道作者在哪派,比记住"正确答案"重要。

**1. 传统 Confucian 史观——道统传承中心**

代表学者:司马光 (1019-1086)《资治通鉴》、朱熹 (1130-1200)《资治通鉴纲目》、20 世纪钱穆 (1895-1990)《国史大纲》。

主张:Tang-Song 是中华文明巅峰。唐诗 + 宋词 + 程朱理学 + 科举制度 = 中国"道统"在这 700 年成形 + 后世延续到清。文天祥 (1236-1283)、岳飞 (1103-1142)、苏轼 (1037-1101) 是道统人物。崖山 1279 后中华文明虽未消失,但巅峰已过。

优点:重视思想 + 文化连续性 + 个体道德选择。

偏见:民族主义滤镜 + 抗拒外来批判 + 倾向把历史写成"圣人传记"而非社会过程。钱穆《国史大纲》1940 年代抗战写就,有强烈民族情绪。

**2. Marxist 史观——封建社会 + 农民起义**

代表学者:翦伯赞 (1898-1968)、范文澜 (1893-1969)、郭沫若 (1892-1978)。50s-80s 中国大陆教科书主导。

主张:Tang-Song 是封建社会一个阶段。推动历史的不是皇帝 + 文人,而是农民起义:黄巢 875-884 (Tang 末)、方腊 1120 (北宋末)、红巾军 1351-1368 (元末)。地主阶级 + 农民阶级矛盾是基本动力。王安石变法 1069-1085 是地主阶级内部"改良派 vs 守旧派"斗争。

优点:首次重视底层社会 + 经济结构 + 起义记录。

偏见:阶级斗争还原论 + 忽视文化精英 + 把所有现象塞进"五种生产方式"框架 (原始 / 奴隶 / 封建 / 资本 / 社会主义)。文学价值常被贬为"统治阶级麻醉工具"。

**3. 西方 modernist 史观——Needham Question**

代表学者:Joseph Needham (1900-1995)《Science and Civilisation in China》多卷本、John K. Fairbank (1907-1991)、Mark Elvin《The Pattern of the Chinese Past》(1973)。

主张:Tang-Song 12-13 世纪在科技 + 经济 + 国家能力上领先世界。北宋 GDP 估占当时世界 30% 左右 (Angus Maddison 估算)。火药 + 印刷 + 罗盘 + 纸钞都在这期间发明。Joseph Needham Question:为什么中国领先 800 年后,科学革命没在中国发生而在 17 世纪欧洲?Mark Elvin 提出"高水平均衡陷阱"假说——中国传统农业太成功,反而锁住了进一步突破的动力。

优点:严谨比较 + 量化证据 + 把中国放进世界史。

偏见:现代化 framework 隐含"欧洲为标准"。Joseph Needham 后期被批太线性 + 太"工业革命中心论"。

**4. 21 世纪 globalist 史观——Eurasian network**

代表学者:Peter Bol《This Culture of Ours》(1992) + 《Neo-Confucianism in History》(2008)、余英时 (1930-2021)《士与中国文化》(2003)、Robert Hymes《Statesmen and Gentlemen》(1986)。

主张:Tang-Song 不是孤立"中国 example",而是 Eurasian 大网络一极。丝绸之路 + 海上贸易 + 元朝 1271-1368 把中国 + 中亚 + 中东 + 欧洲连成一张网。Peter Bol 重视宋代士大夫"localist turn"——精英从依附朝廷转向地方社会。余英时重视宋明理学是"内向超越"——中国哲学的内部突破,不是欧洲外来刺激。

优点:反 essentialism + 重视 cross-cultural mobility + 把中国当世界一部分。

偏见:有时过度解构"中国"概念 + 削弱内部连续性。

**怎么用四派**

读 Tang-Song 史的实用建议:钱穆 / 司马光看思想。翦伯赞 / 范文澜看农民起义。Needham / Elvin 看科技 + 经济。Peter Bol / 余英时看 globalist + 内部哲学。一派的盲点是另一派的视野。

### EN

Studying Tang-Song China has no single answer. There are at least four schools in 21st-century scholarship, each with its own political and philosophical stance. When reading a Tang-Song book, knowing which school the author belongs to matters more than memorizing a "correct answer."

**1. Traditional Confucian — Center of the Way**

Key scholars: Sima Guang (1019-1086) *Comprehensive Mirror in Aid of Governance*, Zhu Xi (1130-1200) *Outline and Details of the Comprehensive Mirror*, and 20th-century Qian Mu (1895-1990) *Outline of National History*.

Claim: Tang-Song is the peak of Chinese civilization. Tang poetry, Song lyrics, Cheng-Zhu Neo-Confucianism, and the imperial examination took shape across 700 years and the orthodox lineage continued through the Qing dynasty. Wen Tianxiang (1236-1283), Yue Fei (1103-1142), and Su Shi (1037-1101) are figures in this lineage. After Yashan in 1279, Chinese civilization did not vanish, but its peak had passed.

Strengths: emphasis on thought, cultural continuity, individual moral choice.

Bias: a nationalist filter, resistance to outside critique, tendency to write history as "biographies of sages" rather than as social process. Qian Mu wrote during the 1940s wartime period and the work carries strong nationalist emotion.

**2. Marxist — Feudal Society and Peasant Uprisings**

Key scholars: Jian Bozan (1898-1968), Fan Wenlan (1893-1969), Guo Moruo (1892-1978). Dominant in mainland Chinese textbooks from the 1950s to the 1980s.

Claim: Tang-Song is a stage of feudal society. The drivers of history are not emperors and literati but peasant uprisings: Huang Chao 875-884 (late Tang), Fang La 1120 (late Northern Song), Red Turban 1351-1368 (late Yuan). The landlord-peasant contradiction is the basic engine. Wang Anshi's reforms 1069-1085 were a struggle within the landlord class between "reformers and conservatives."

Strengths: first to take seriously bottom-of-society conditions, economic structure, and uprising records.

Bias: class-struggle reductionism, neglect of cultural elites, forcing every phenomenon into the "five modes of production" frame (primitive, slave, feudal, capitalist, socialist). Literary value is often dismissed as "anesthesia for the ruling class."

**3. Western modernist — the Needham Question**

Key scholars: Joseph Needham (1900-1995) multi-volume *Science and Civilisation in China*, John K. Fairbank (1907-1991), Mark Elvin *The Pattern of the Chinese Past* (1973).

Claim: in the 12th-13th century, Tang-Song China led the world in technology, economy, and state capacity. Northern Song GDP is estimated at roughly 30 percent of world GDP at the time (Angus Maddison's estimate). Gunpowder, printing, the compass, and paper money were all invented in this period. The Needham Question: after leading for 800 years, why did the scientific revolution not happen in China but in 17th-century Europe? Mark Elvin proposed a "high-level equilibrium trap" hypothesis: Chinese traditional agriculture was so successful that it locked away the incentive for further breakthroughs.

Strengths: rigorous comparison, quantitative evidence, places China inside world history.

Bias: the modernization framework implicitly sets Europe as the standard. Late Needham has been criticized as too linear and too centered on the Industrial Revolution.

**4. 21st-century globalist — Eurasian network**

Key scholars: Peter Bol *This Culture of Ours* (1992) and *Neo-Confucianism in History* (2008), Yu Ying-shih (1930-2021) *Scholar-Officials and Chinese Culture* (2003), Robert Hymes *Statesmen and Gentlemen* (1986).

Claim: Tang-Song is not an isolated "China example" but one node in a Eurasian network. The Silk Road, maritime trade, and the Yuan dynasty 1271-1368 wove China, Central Asia, the Middle East, and Europe into one web. Peter Bol emphasizes a Song-era "localist turn" by literati, who shifted from dependence on the court toward local society. Yu Ying-shih treats Song-Ming Neo-Confucianism as an "inward transcendence," an internal breakthrough in Chinese philosophy rather than a response to a European stimulus.

Strengths: anti-essentialism, attention to cross-cultural mobility, places China as part of the world.

Bias: sometimes over-deconstructs the concept of "China" and weakens internal continuity.

**How to use the four schools**

Practical advice for reading Tang-Song history: read Qian Mu and Sima Guang for thought. Read Jian Bozan and Fan Wenlan for peasant uprisings. Read Needham and Elvin for technology and economy. Read Peter Bol and Yu Ying-shih for the global frame and internal philosophy. One school's blind spot is another school's field of view.

## §10 八条反 Whig 钢筋 / Eight Counter-Whig Reinforcements

### CN

Whig 史观 = "历史是单向进步,过去是为现在铺路"。下面 8 条挑战常见的简化叙事。

**1.「唐朝最强」**

通俗叙事说唐朝 (618-907) 是中国"最强"。证据:唐疆域峰值约 5,000,000 km²、长安百万人口、丝绸之路繁盛。

复杂化:5,000,000 km² 是约 660 年高宗时期峰值,755 安史之乱后唐快速衰退,藩镇林立到 907。如果用 GDP 估算,北宋 (960-1127) 占当时世界 30% (Angus Maddison) 反而是真"最强"——只是版图更小 + 军事不向北扩张。

**2.「宋朝弱」**

通俗叙事说宋朝重文轻武 = 弱。证据:1127 靖康之变 + 1279 崖山。

复杂化:重文轻武 = 内部稳定。两宋 320 年没有大规模内乱,常备军 100 万左右 + 武器技术领先 (火药 + 神臂弓 + 投石机)。宋的战略选择是"买和平"——岁币换边境稳定 + 集中资源发展经济。这不是"弱",是不同的国家战略。把它叫"弱"是用现代民族国家眼光评判 medieval 帝国。

**3.「崖山之后无中华」**

钱穆 1942 抗战时期提出的命题。意思:崖山 1279 宋元交替后,中华文明本质消失,清末民初要"重建"。

复杂化:21 世纪学界主流 (Peter Bol、余英时) 认为这是 essentialism + 民族情绪。中华文明在元 (1271-1368)、明 (1368-1644)、清 (1644-1912) 继续演变 + 改造。元朝杂剧 + 明清小说 + 王阳明心学 + 顾炎武实学,都是"崖山之后"的中华文明。1942 命题是 wartime emotion,不是历史判断。

**4.「四大发明炫耀」**

中国教科书强调火药 + 印刷 + 罗盘 + 造纸 (有时加纸钞) 是中国对世界文明的贡献。

复杂化:四大发明确实在唐宋发明 + 完善。但传到欧洲 + 改变历史 (军事革命 + 大航海 + 科学革命) 主要是 1300-1500 年。中国本身在唐宋发明这些,却没有用它们改变中国 (火药主要做烟花 + 防御武器、印刷主要印佛经 + 科举书,没有引爆类似欧洲宗教改革的传播革命)。这就是 Joseph Needham Question——"为什么发明在中国,革命在欧洲"。

**5.「武则天篡夺」**

传统史观骂武则天 (624-705,在位 690-705) 篡唐 + 杀子 + 用酷吏。

复杂化:21 世纪学界重新评价。武则天是中国历史唯一称帝女性。她推行科举改革 (扩大殿试 + 武举 1102 后追溯制度化)、打击门阀士族 + 提拔寒门、农业政策稳定。"篡夺"是从男性继承制角度看的。21 世纪从制度史 + 性别史角度看,武则天是 Tang 制度建设关键人物。

**6.「王安石变法失败」**

通俗叙事说王安石 (1021-1086) 变法 1069-1085 被反复废除立 + 北宋变法党与守旧党斗争消耗了国力 + 1127 靖康崩盘是变法失败的结果。

复杂化:21 世纪学界 (Peter Bol、漆侠) 认为变法是宋代国家能力建设关键。青苗法 + 募役法 + 保甲法 + 市易法等是宋代向"现代国家"方向的尝试。1127 崩盘的直接原因是徽宗 1100-1126 末期联金抗辽政策错误,不能简单归因于变法。"变法失败论"是司马光《资治通鉴》视角,不是唯一视角。

**7.「岳飞被秦桧害死」**

通俗叙事:岳飞 (1103-1142) 抗金英雄,被秦桧 (1090-1155) 在 1142 风波亭以"莫须有"罪名害死。

复杂化:21 世纪学界 (王曾瑜、邓广铭) 把这复杂化。真决策者是高宗赵构 (1107-1187,在位 1127-1162),秦桧是执行者,不是首谋。高宗的政治判断:岳飞 1140 北伐打到朱仙镇,如果继续打回北宋故都汴京,高宗自己的合法性会被质疑 (因为父亲徽宗 + 哥哥钦宗还在金朝活着,迎回后高宗就要让位)。岳飞坚持北伐 + 拒绝接受"议和"军令,从政治判断角度有自己的问题。这不是为秦桧洗地,但避免把它简化成"忠 vs 奸"的脸谱戏。

**8.「中国一直是中央集权」**

通俗印象:中国从秦 (前 221) 到清 (1912) 一直是中央集权大一统。

复杂化:Tang-Song 期间不是。安史之乱 755 后,Tang 731-907 进入藩镇时代,节度使割据 + 中央失控。907-960 五代十国是分裂期。北宋 960-1127 重建中央集权但仅一段时间。1127 靖康后南北分裂,1127-1279 南宋只控制淮河以南。Tang-Song 700 年里"统一"+ "分裂"交替,不是单一 monolith。

### EN

The Whig view says history is one-directional progress and the past was paving the way for the present. Eight reinforcements below challenge common simplifications.

**1. "Tang was the strongest"**

The popular narrative says Tang (618-907) was China's "strongest" dynasty, citing territory peaking near 5 million km², Chang'an's million-person population, and the busy Silk Road.

Complication: 5 million km² was the peak around 660 under Emperor Gaozong. After the An Lushan Rebellion in 755, Tang declined quickly, with regional military commands fragmenting authority until 907. By GDP estimates, Northern Song (960-1127) at roughly 30 percent of world GDP (Angus Maddison) is the real "strongest" period, with smaller territory but a different strategy.

**2. "Song was weak"**

The popular narrative says Song privileged civil over military and was therefore weak. Evidence cited: the 1127 Jingkang collapse and 1279 Yashan.

Complication: privileging civil over military meant internal stability. Song's 320 years saw no large-scale civil wars. The standing army was around one million with leading weapons technology (gunpowder, *shenbi* crossbow, trebuchets). Song's strategic choice was to buy peace, paying annual tribute for stable borders and concentrating resources on the economy. This is not weakness; it is a different state strategy. Calling it weak applies a modern nation-state lens to a medieval empire.

**3. "After Yashan, no more China"**

A thesis Qian Mu put forward in 1942 during the war: after the 1279 Yashan transition from Song to Yuan, the essence of Chinese civilization vanished, and the late Qing and early Republic period had to "rebuild" it.

Complication: the 21st-century mainstream (Peter Bol, Yu Ying-shih) sees this as essentialism plus nationalist feeling. Chinese civilization continued and transformed through Yuan (1271-1368), Ming (1368-1644), and Qing (1644-1912). Yuan zaju drama, Ming-Qing novels, Wang Yangming's School of Mind, and Gu Yanwu's substantive learning are all post-Yashan Chinese civilization. The 1942 thesis is wartime emotion, not historical judgment.

**4. "The Four Great Inventions" boast**

Chinese textbooks emphasize gunpowder, printing, the compass, and papermaking (sometimes plus paper money) as China's contribution to world civilization.

Complication: the four were indeed invented and refined in Tang-Song. But their transmission to Europe and the historical changes they caused (military revolution, age of exploration, scientific revolution) mostly fell between 1300 and 1500. China itself invented these but did not use them to transform China. Gunpowder was used mainly for fireworks and defensive weapons; printing was used mainly for Buddhist scriptures and exam books, without triggering anything like the European Reformation's print revolution. This is the Needham Question: "Why was the invention in China and the revolution in Europe?"

**5. "Wu Zetian usurped"**

The traditional view condemns Wu Zetian (624-705, ruled 690-705) as a usurper of Tang who killed sons and used cruel officials.

Complication: 21st-century scholarship reassesses her. Wu is the only woman to have declared herself emperor of China. She extended the imperial examination (expanding the palace exam, with the military exam later institutionalized after 1102), curbed aristocratic clans and promoted men of humble origin, and stabilized agricultural policy. The "usurpation" frame comes from the male-succession lens. From institutional and gender history perspectives, Wu is a key figure in Tang state-building.

**6. "Wang Anshi's reforms failed"**

The popular narrative says Wang Anshi's (1021-1086) reforms 1069-1085 were repeatedly abolished and reinstated, and that the reformer-conservative struggle drained Northern Song so the 1127 Jingkang collapse was the result.

Complication: 21st-century scholarship (Peter Bol, Qi Xia) treats the reforms as central to Song state-capacity building. The Green Sprouts policy, hired-service system, *baojia* mutual-responsibility system, and state trade policy were attempts to move toward a "more modern state." The 1127 collapse was directly caused by Emperor Huizong's late-period (1100-1126) failed alliance-with-Jin policy against Liao, not simply by reform. The "failure" reading is Sima Guang's *Comprehensive Mirror* perspective; it is one perspective, not the only one.

**7. "Yue Fei was killed by Qin Hui"**

The popular narrative: Yue Fei (1103-1142), an anti-Jin hero, was killed in 1142 at Wind Wave Pavilion by Qin Hui (1090-1155) under the trumped-up charge of "perhaps so."

Complication: 21st-century scholarship (Wang Zengyu, Deng Guangming) complicates this. The real decision-maker was Emperor Gaozong, Zhao Gou (1107-1187, ruled 1127-1162). Qin Hui was the executor, not the prime mover. Gaozong's political calculation: Yue Fei's 1140 northern campaign reached Zhuxianzhen. If he kept advancing back to the old Northern Song capital Kaifeng, Gaozong's own legitimacy would be questioned, since his father Emperor Huizong and brother Emperor Qinzong were still alive in Jin captivity, and on their return Gaozong would have to abdicate. Yue Fei's insistence on northward war and refusal to accept the peace orders also reflected his own political miscalculation. This is not a defense of Qin Hui, but it avoids reducing the story to a costume drama of "loyal hero versus treacherous minister."

**8. "China was always centralized"**

Popular impression: from Qin (221 BCE) to Qing (1912), China was always a centralized unified empire.

Complication: not during Tang-Song. After the An Lushan Rebellion in 755, Tang from 731 to 907 entered the era of military commissions, with regional military governors in effective autonomy and the center losing control. The 907-960 Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms were a fragmentation period. Northern Song 960-1127 rebuilt central control, but only for one stretch. After 1127 Jingkang, north and south split: Southern Song 1127-1279 controlled only south of the Huai River. Across the 700 years of Tang-Song, "unity" and "fragmentation" alternated. There is no single monolith.

## §11 不下结论思考问题 / Open Discussion Questions

### CN

下面 6 个问题没有标准答案。和 {{userParentWord|爸妈}} 聊一次,记录两个人观点不同的地方,不必达成一致。

**Q1. Needham Question——你的判断**

12-13 世纪 Tang-Song 中国比同代欧洲先进:火药 + 印刷 + 罗盘 + 北宋 GDP 占世界 30%。但 18 世纪后欧洲反超 + 19 世纪鸦片战争。

问题:这是中华文明的内在缺陷 (例如太重稳定 + 科举僵化 + 缺乏宗教改革式的精神突破),还是政治选择 (例如明清海禁 + 闭关锁国是具体决策,不是文明命运)?

跟 {{userParentWord|爸妈}} 聊:如果你是 1700 年的中国皇帝,你会怎么决策?有没有现实的"另一条路"?

**Q2. 王安石 vs 司马光——你站哪边**

王安石 1069-1085 变法主张:国家主动干预经济 + 收编兼并地主 + 扩大国家能力。司马光主张:维护既有秩序 + 减少干预 + 给士大夫 + 民间自由空间。

问题:21 世纪你面对类似选择吗?例子——政府是否应该用 AI 监管市场 + 是否应该限制科技公司 + 福利国家 vs 自由市场。

跟 {{userParentWord|爸妈}} 聊:你们对"国家干预"的本能反应一样吗?哪种问题上你更愿意要"王安石"哪种问题上你更愿意要"司马光"?

**Q3. 「崖山之后无中华」——你信吗**

这个 1942 命题说中华文明在 1279 后本质消失。

问题:你家族里 (移民来美国前 + 后) 有没有保留任何东西是 "Tang-Song 时代的"?例如:一首唐诗 + 一道宋朝菜 + 春节 + 中秋 + 一种处事方式 (中庸 / 含蓄)。

如果有保留,那"无中华"命题就是错的——文明在你身体里继续。如果没有,那命题对你个人来说可能是真的。这不是民族主义问题,是个人文化连续性问题。

跟 {{userParentWord|爸妈}} 聊:你们带到美国 + 想传给孩子的"中国"具体是什么?是 Tang-Song 那条线,还是更近代 (清末民初 + 1949 后) 的版本?

**Q4. 苏轼三度流放——你的 21 世纪 pattern**

苏轼 (1037-1101) 一生三次流放:1080 黄州 + 1094 惠州 + 1097 海南。每次流放,他写出更好的诗词 + 食物发明 (东坡肉) + 更深的人生哲学。

问题:21 世纪你身上有没有这种 pattern?例子——内卷 + 跳槽 + 创业失败 + 移民 + 独立思考者被排挤。被"流放" (失业 / 失恋 / 离家 / 不被理解) 反而成就了你某部分?
