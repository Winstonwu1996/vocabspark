## 0. Open Your Textbook First (Textbook Anchor)

> Your 7th-grade Houghton Mifflin *Medieval & Early Modern Times* —
> **Ch.11 The Renaissance and Reformation** —
> **Lesson 2 The Reformation** — **p.318-323**.
>
> Your textbook will tell you:
> - **In 1517, Martin Luther nailed 95 theses to a church door in Wittenberg**
> - **He was protesting against indulgences and church corruption**
> - **The Reformation split Europe into Catholic and Protestant camps**
> - **King Henry VIII broke England away from Rome over a divorce**
>
> **What your textbook will NOT tell you** (this is why we need this lesson):
> - **Luther never "wanted to split the church"** — **he wanted to fix it** —
>   **He died still calling himself a Catholic monk** — it was **the church that excommunicated him first** —
>   "Reformation = Luther deciding to break away" is a **simplified, wrong** narrative
> - **The printing press + the 95 Theses gave Europe its first viral moment** —
>   **Luther nailed the theses in Wittenberg on October 31, 1517** —
>   **Within 6 weeks**, every German university town had printed copies —
>   **Within 3 months**, scholars across Europe were reading them —
>   **Without the printing press — no Reformation**. The Renaissance handed the Reformation its match
> - **In the 1525 Peasants' War, 100,000 peasants died** — and this is **the most painful event of Luther's whole life** —
>   **Luther himself wrote *Against the Murderous Hordes of the Peasants* — cursing the very peasants who had followed him** —
>   **Lutheranism lost its rural base** — and this is **the most ignored dark chapter** of Reformation history
> - **Calvin's Geneva 1541-1564 was a "new theocracy"** —
>   **weekly church courts + public flogging + exile + executions for heresy (Servetus burned in 1553)** —
>   **Calvin's Geneva was stricter than Catholic Rome** — yet it became **the template** for global Protestantism
> - **Henry VIII's 1534 break with Rome was not a real "Reformation"** — **he just wanted a divorce** —
>   The actual English Reformation was completed **40 years later by his daughter Elizabeth I** —
>   The 1559 Elizabethan Settlement is what real English Protestantism is built on
> - **The Counter-Reformation was NOT just "Catholics fighting back"** —
>   **It was a substantive internal Catholic reform**:
>   The 1545-1563 Council of Trent fixed **corruption + clergy training + theology** —
>   **Catholicism after Trent** became **more disciplined + more intellectual** —
>   **Roughly 80% of how modern Catholicism still operates** comes from Trent
> - **The real "result" of the Reformation isn't** Protestant vs. Catholic — **it's** nation-state sovereignty + religious pluralism + the First Amendment —
>   **Without 130 years of religious wars + rivers of blood — there is no concept of "separation of church and state"** —
>   **The U.S. First Amendment of 1791 is directly inherited** from the lessons of 1517-1648
> - **Contemporary China** — Ming Dynasty, the Wanli reign 1572-1620 + Zhang Juzheng's reforms 1572-1582 —
>   **No Reformation** — but **the same tension between "state and religion/thought" existed** —
>   **The path was completely different**: Wang Yangming's School of Mind + Matteo Ricci arriving in 1583 — **not revolution — quiet infiltration**
>
> **Open your textbook to p.318 and read together** — this lesson uses **the Ming Dynasty you already know** as the comparison
> (why a "Chinese Reformation" never happened — the other side of the Joseph Needham question).

## 0.5 Carrying Over from the Last Lesson — The 4 Gifts the Renaissance Gave the Reformation

**The Reformation didn't appear out of nowhere** —
**It **stood on 100 years of the Renaissance's shoulders**** —
**Without the Renaissance's 4 gifts — the hammer-blow at Wittenberg's church door on October 31, 1517 **would have stopped right there at the door, with nobody hearing it****:

**1. The printing press (Gutenberg c. 1450)** — covered in the Renaissance lesson under «technology» —
   Gutenberg invented movable type in Mainz in 1450 — by 1500 there were over 1,000 print shops in Europe —
   **Without it**: the *95 Theses* would have remained a Latin memo among a handful of scholars at one Wittenberg university;
   **With it**: within 6 weeks **every university town in Germany** had printed copies — within 3 months **scholars across all of Europe were reading it** —
   The printing press is the **detonator** of the Reformation.

**2. Humanism + the return to the classics** — covered in the Renaissance lesson with Pico della Mirandola's 1486 *Oration on the Dignity of Man* —
   Pico wrote: **«Man has free choice of conscience — from the highest to the lowest, he himself decides»** —
   In 1486 that line was a piece of Florentine philosophical debate;
   **35 years later, Luther pushed it into political execution**: at the 1521 *Diet of Worms* Luther told the Emperor **«Here I stand — I can do no other»** —
   That is the same thought evolving from «a person **may**» to «a person **must**» —
   **Without Pico's 1486 right of conscience — Luther's 1521 stand has no prior intellectual scaffolding**.

**3. Vernacular literature — making it conceivable to write «serious things» in your own language** — covered in the Renaissance lesson with Dante's *Divine Comedy* (1320, Italian) and Boccaccio's *Decameron* (1353, Italian) —
   Before them — the European rule was: **«serious matter must be written in Latin; only common matter may be written in the vernacular»** —
   Dante and Boccaccio wrote **the most serious theology + philosophy + moral fiction** in the Italian vernacular —
   **and broke the prejudice that «vernacular is only fit for low matter»** —
   **Exactly that break — made it conceivable for Luther in 1522 to translate the New Testament into common Saxon German** —
   **If Dante had not written the *Divine Comedy* in Italian 200 years earlier — Luther would not have felt «German is also worthy of carrying Scripture»**.

**4. The pamphlet market + a distribution network (from the 1480s on)** — covered in the Renaissance lesson under «commerce»: from the 1480s on, Florence and Venice grew an entire pamphlet industry —
   print shops + intercity merchant trains + university bookstores + bookstalls outside the cathedral doors — **for the first time in Europe, a wholesale-and-retail network for lightweight printed material existed** —
   **The reason Luther's 1517 *95 Theses* spread across Germany in 6 weeks** is **not that it was «important enough» to spread on its own** — **it is that 30 years of distribution infrastructure had already been built for commercial pamphlets** —
   **Luther only had to slip theological content into a delivery network that already existed**.

— **Acknowledging these 4 gifts means the Reformation is not «Luther, a single hero, suddenly erupting»** —
**It is 100 years of slowly accumulated **technology + thought + language + commerce** from the Renaissance, converging on one man in 1517** —
**Luther was the one who **lit the fire**** — **but the wood had been stacked for decades**.

> 💡 **Cross-topic teaching point**: you **studied the Renaissance first** — then the Reformation — that order is not arbitrary —
> **It is the causal chain**: **Renaissance is cause — Reformation is effect** —
> The reverse does not hold (the Reformation could not have produced the Renaissance) —
> **History Topics are not islands — they are continuous layered additions of 65 + 35 + 130 + … years**.

## 1. The One-Line Hook

**October 31, 1517 — All Hallows' Eve — in a forgotten little German town called Wittenberg —
a 33-year-old monk picked up a hammer and nails** —
**He walked to the door of the Schlosskirche (the Castle Church)** —
**He posted a sheet of 95 theses written in Latin** —
**"I oppose the Pope selling indulgences — this betrays the spirit of Christianity"** —
**He thought this was just an invitation to a scholarly debate among university teachers** —

**Six weeks later** — his theses **had spread across Germany on the printing press** —
**Four years later** — he was excommunicated by Rome —
**One hundred and thirty years later** — eight million Europeans were dead in religious wars —

**The monk's name was Martin Luther** —
**He had no idea his single sheet of paper would set off 130 years of bloodshed** —
**He also had no idea his single sheet of paper would change the West forever**:
**nation-state + religious pluralism + the First Amendment + the right of individual conscience + the scientific method**
— **all of it built on top of that hammer-stroke on October 31, 1517**.

## 2. The Stage: Europe in the 1500s

### Geography (L0)

**Europe in 1500 looked very different from Europe in 1095 (the Crusades)** —
**"Europe" was starting to feel like one thing** —
**Five emerging nation-states had begun to take shape**:

| Country | Dynasty | 1500 population | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spain** | Habsburg + Aragon-Castile | 7 million | Reconquista finished 1492 + Columbus 1492 |
| **France** | Valois | 15 million | Largest single European country — strong royal power after the Hundred Years' War |
| **England** | Tudor (from 1485) | 2.5 million | Island kingdom — beginning ocean expansion |
| **Holy Roman Empire** | Habsburg | 17 million (300+ states) | Unified in name, fragmented in fact |
| **Ottoman Empire** | Ottoman | 15 million | Took Byzantium in 1453 — pushing into the Balkans |

**Add up these five powers** — Europe was no longer a heap of fragments; it was **a few rival great powers**.

**The key for the Reformation**: **these five powers had different relationships to the Pope**:
- **Spain**: monarchy and pope worked closely together (Inquisition from 1478)
- **France**: monarchy stronger than the pope — **but still Catholic**
- **England**: broke from Rome from the 1530s on
- **The Holy Roman Empire's 300+ states**: each prince could **choose** Catholic or Lutheran for his territory
- **Italy**: the Papal States themselves — remained Catholic

— This **fragmentation** is the key reason the Reformation succeeded —
**Italian fragmentation → the Renaissance** (the previous Topic) —
**German fragmentation → the Reformation** —
**Fragmentation was the soil for European innovation** —
**No emperor** could **control religious thought across Europe the way a Chinese emperor could**.

### Climate (L1)

**The 1500s were still inside the Little Ice Age** —
**The 1535-1540s were especially cold** — European harvests failed — **hunger and unrest** built up —
**This gave the Reformation its popular base**: **poor + anxious + hungry for new meaning** —
**The 1525 Peasants' War was driven directly by famine pressure**.

### Society (L2)

**1500s European society had changed enormously since 1095 (the Crusades)** —

**Five new social layers** (mostly inherited from the Renaissance):
- **Bankers** (the Fugger family in Augsburg taking over from the Medici)
- **Printers** (1,000+ print shops in Europe by 1500)
- **Scholars** (humanists + classical scholars)
- **Lawyers** (after the revival of Roman law, a legal class grew up)
- **Professional officers** (after the rise of muskets and cannon, the military became professional)

**Old layers**:
- **Royals + high nobility** (still there)
- **Monasteries + clergy** (still there, but **status falling**)
- **Peasants + urban workers** (still there, but **peasants beginning to organize**)

**Key shifts**:
- **The clergy's wealth and moral authority both fell** (the Renaissance had spread news of papal corruption all over Europe)
- **Peasants began reading the Bible** (printing press + vernacular translations)
- **Scholars publicly questioned the church** (Erasmus / Reuchlin / and later Luther)

**This is the social ground that let the Reformation explode** —
**1500 Europe was not 1095 Europe** — **a totally different population + a totally different speed of idea circulation**.

### Institutions (L3) — The Penance System

**To understand the Reformation — you have to understand the "penance system" first** —

**Medieval Catholic theology**:
- **Every person carries original sin + personal sins** — and must **do penance** to enter heaven
- **Methods of penance**:
  - **Confession**: tell your sins to a priest
  - **Penance** (acts): prayer + fasting + pilgrimage
  - **Indulgence**: pay money **or** perform certain acts to remit a portion of punishment
  - **Plenary indulgence**: invented by Urban II in 1095 for the Crusaders — a one-shot remission of **all** punishment

**The key for 1517**:
- **Pope Leo X** (a Medici) was **rebuilding St. Peter's Basilica** — **burning cash + going broke**
- **In 1515 Leo X authorized Albrecht of Brandenburg (a German archbishop) to sell indulgences** — **half the proceeds to St. Peter's, half to Albrecht**
- **Albrecht hired Johann Tetzel as a "professional salesman"** to walk the streets selling indulgences
- **Tetzel's famous line**: "**As soon as the coin in the coffer rings, the soul from purgatory springs!**"
- **This commercialization made some monks furious** —
- **Luther was one of them** — that's how he reacted **on October 31, 1517**.


## 3. The Main Characters (Multiple Perspectives + Across Class)

### The North-German Reform Faction

#### Martin Luther (1483-1546)

##### Origins: A Miner's Family — A Hard Father

**Born November 10, 1483 in Eisleben** (central German Saxony) —
**His father, Hans Luther, was a miner — and later a small copper-mine owner** —
**Strict + hot-tempered** —
**Hans pinned all his hopes for "climbing one rung up"** on Martin —
**He wanted Martin to be a lawyer** — that was the **highest dream** a miner's son could carry in the 1480s.

**Martin was beaten hard as a child** —
**He remembered at 60**: "**My father beat me until I bled — for the deepest fears in my heart**".
**That childhood shadow** shaped a lifetime of **sensitivity to a furious father figure** —
**His later theological obsession with an "angry God"** is very likely connected to that early bond.

##### July 2, 1505 — Lightning Changes Everything

**Luther was 21 — already studying law at Erfurt University** —
**He was on the road home — caught in a storm** —
**A lightning bolt** nearly hit him — he **dropped to his knees screaming**:
> "**Saint Anne save me — I will become a monk!**"

**Fourteen days later** — he had **entered the Augustinian monastery in Erfurt** —
**His father Hans was furious** —
**Hans thought his son had "wasted" his law degree** —
**The two men did not speak for years**.

— Entering the monastery **out of fear** is the "origin point" of Luther's whole life —
**He didn't go in because he loved God** — he **went in because he feared God** —
**For his entire life he was wrestling with one question**: **how can I get this furious God to accept me?**

##### Ten Years in the Monastery — More Discipline, More Despair

**1505-1515 — for ten straight years** — Luther was **the most rigorous monk in his house**:
- **Confessing 6-8 hours a day** —
- **Constant prayer + fasting** —
- **Reading the Bible + the entire major works of the church fathers** —
- **Pilgrimage to Rome** (1510)

**But he grew more desperate the harder he disciplined himself** —
**He always felt he was a deeper sinner** —
**He always felt he had not done enough** —
**He later remembered this dark night of the spirit**: "**I hated God** —
because he demanded what I could not do — he gave me **impossible standards** — and then **punished me for failing them**".

##### 1510-1511 — The Shock of Rome

**Luther was 27 — first time outside Saxony** — **walking to Rome** —
**Rome was the holy city for every Catholic monk** —
**Luther expected to see a sacred place** —

**What he actually saw**:
- **Priests on the street flirting + with prostitutes** —
- **Priests saying Mass in 6 minutes** just to collect the fee
- **Pope Julius II in armor leading wars** (covered in the Renaissance §3) —
- **Indulgence stalls everywhere**

**Luther later wrote**:
> "**Rome made me unable to love Rome ever again** —
> **I saw with my own eyes that Rome was a cesspool** —
> **not a holy place — but the world's headquarters of greed, sex, and money**".

— **But he didn't break right away** —
**He was still a loyal monk** — **he waited 7 more years** — until 1517.

##### 1513-1517 — The Theological Breakthrough

**From 1513 onward — Luther was teaching the Bible at Wittenberg University** —
**He kept rereading Romans + Galatians** —
**He landed on one key sentence**:
> **Romans 1:17 — "The righteous shall live by faith"**

**Luther turned this verse over and over — and suddenly he understood**:
> "**Salvation is not earned through penance + works + pilgrimages** —
> **Salvation comes through faith** —
> **A person who believes in Christ is "counted as righteous" — he is saved** —
> **He doesn't need to do "enough" — he can't anyway** —
> **Salvation is a free gift — not a paycheck**".

— **This is the theological foundation of "Sola Fide" (faith alone) that came later** —
**Luther felt this was the greatest joy of his life** —
**He no longer had to fear the "angry God"** —
**because faith = acceptance**.

**This breakthrough is exactly why he was so furious at Tetzel selling indulgences** —
**Indulgences directly contradicted the truth he had just found** —
**Indulgences said**: "**pay money to save a soul**" —
**Luther said**: "**You're wrong — only faith saves a soul — money does nothing**" —
**This is the root of the 95 Theses in 1517**.

##### October 31, 1517 — The Afternoon That Changed History

**Luther was 33 — a theology professor at Wittenberg University** —
**He wrote 95 debate points in Latin** —
**He wanted to discuss them with other scholars** —
**This was **normal practice** for a medieval university teacher** —
**Posting them on the Castle Church door was also **normal** — that was the school's "bulletin board"**.

**He did not expect it to become a revolution** —
**He just wanted an academic debate**.

**But**:
- **Some student translated the theses into German within days** —
- **Print shops ran 1,000 copies in a few weeks** —
- **Six weeks later** — every German university town had a copy —
- **Three months later** — scholars across Europe were reading them —
- **Luther himself only realized in early 1518 that this had blown up** —
  **He wrote to a friend**: "**I never wanted to do something like this** —
  **I just wanted to oppose indulgences — but the storm has gone past me**".

— **The first hammer-stroke of the Reformation fell almost by accident** —
**Luther was not a revolutionary** — he was an **accidental revolutionary** —
**He was being pushed forward by his own theses** —
**He spent years afterward trying to explain to himself + to the world what he actually meant** —
**This pattern of "the movement runs ahead of its leader" is a classic shape in history** —
**Lenin 1917 + Mao 1927 + Mandela 1962 — all had similar arcs** —
**Luther was the first modern example**.

##### April 18, 1521 — The Diet of Worms

**Luther was 37** — summoned to **the Diet of Worms** chaired by **Holy Roman Emperor Charles V** —
**Worms is a city in western Germany** —
**This was the most dangerous moment of Luther's life**:
- **The Emperor could sentence him to death** —
- **Bohemian reformer Jan Hus had been burned at a similar council in 1415** —
- **Luther's friends begged him not to go** —

**But Luther went** —
**On the afternoon of April 17 — he stood before Charles V for the **first** time** —
**In the hall sat the Emperor + the papal legate + the 7 electors + dozens of German princes + the Latin scribes** —
**He was facing the highest secular authority in Europe** —
**On a table — a stack of 25 books he had written** — the scribe **read out each title in turn** —
**The interrogator asked**: **«Are these your books?»**
**Luther** said yes.
**The interrogator asked again**: **«Will you recant?»**

— **At that moment Luther **did not** answer immediately** — he **hesitated** — he **asked for 24 hours to think** —
**Charles V** agreed — giving him **until the following evening** to return.

**The night of April 17 — Luther went back to his lodgings in Worms** —
**That night he **did not sleep**** — he **prayed + searched Scripture + took notes** —
**His friend Justus Jonas later wrote in a letter**: **«He **knelt at the table all night** — the oil lamp never went out — when dawn came through the window he was still there.»**
**That night — Luther **alone** carried the weight of «is the entire 1500-year authority structure of Western Christianity wrong?»** —
— **This lens lets you hear what he never said aloud**: **he knew that returning to the hall the next day to say «I do not recant» = a death sentence already written** — **he also knew that saying «I recant» = a theological self-negation + the last four years since 1517 all undone** — **he could escape neither side**.

**On the evening of April 18 — he stood before Charles V the **second** time** —
**This time he **no longer hesitated**** — **he delivered an answer first in Latin, then in German** —
**The core sentence**:

> **«Unless I am convinced by Scripture and clear reason —**
> **I cannot and will not recant anything —**
> **for to act against conscience is neither safe nor right —**
> **Here I stand — I can do no other —**
> **God help me. Amen.»**

— That sentence — **"Here I stand. I can do no other"** —
**became the most famous slogan of the Reformation** —
**It turned "individual conscience vs. collective authority" into a new political philosophy** —
**That spirit — "I will stand against every authority"** — became the modern tradition of the **conscientious objector** —
**Mahatma Gandhi + Martin Luther King 1963 + Edward Snowden 2013 — all are **spiritual descendants** of that moment**.

**Charles V listened** — he could not execute Luther on the spot (he had given a safe-conduct) —
**Luther left Worms** —
**On May 26 — Charles V issued the Edict of Worms**: **Luther is an outlaw — anyone may kill him** —
**But Luther was already kidnapped to Wartburg Castle and hidden** (the kidnappers were actually his own supporters, sent by Frederick the Wise).

##### 1521-1522 — Hidden in Wartburg Castle

**Luther at 38** — hidden in Wartburg Castle — under the alias "**Junker Jörg (Knight George)**" —
**He stayed there 10 months** — and did one thing **that changed the history of Germany**:

**He translated the New Testament into German** —
**He used common Saxon dialect** — not high Latin — **the German that ordinary people actually spoke** —
**His pace was astounding**: **11 weeks to finish the entire New Testament**.

**This "Luther German New Testament" was published in 1522** —
**It sold 200,000 copies within 5 years** —
**This was**:
- **The first Bible ordinary Germans could read** —
- **The foundation of standard modern German** —
- **The source of German national identity**

— **Luther gave Germany two gifts**: **Protestantism + a national language** —
**The German Germans speak today** is Luther's German —
**Luther is the "father" of German** — even Goethe and Schiller said so.

##### 1525 — The Disaster of the Peasants' War

**1524-1525 — a great peasant uprising swept Germany** —
**100,000 peasants rose against feudal lords** —
**They claimed Luther had inspired them**:
- **Luther taught "the priesthood of all believers"** — they said "then we are equals too"
- **Luther opposed church authority** — they said "then we oppose landlord authority too"

**They wrote the *Twelve Articles* demanding**:
- **abolish serfdom**
- **lower taxes**
- **villages elect their own pastors**
- **commons own land in common** —

**Luther in early 1524 wrote *Admonition to Peace*** — sympathizing with them — but **warning** them **not to revolt** —
**The peasants did not listen** — they kept revolting + burning manors + killing nobles —

**By May 1525, Luther's stance flipped** —
**He wrote *Against the Murderous, Thieving Hordes of the Peasants*** —
**In that pamphlet he cursed them**:
> "**Kill them + whip them** —
> **as you would beat a mad dog** —
> **any noble who slays a peasant rebel is doing God's work** —
> **God will reward him** —
> **and if you can die for this cause — you die holy**".

— **That pamphlet doubled the confidence of the noble armies** —
**On May 15, the Battle of Frankenhausen — the peasant army was massacred** —
**100,000 peasants died across the war** —
**Luther personally drove his own movement away from the lower classes** —
**Lutheranism from this point on became a religion of nobles + middle class — and almost no peasants**.

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Luther's 1525 stance has put him on trial again and again throughout history —
> **20th-century Marxist historians** (especially Friedrich Engels) **flatly call Luther "a lackey of the nobility"** —
> They say:
> "**Luther rose to power on the peasants' shoulders** —
> **then turned on them the moment they revolted** —
> **He was always the spokesman for noble class interest**".
>
> **20th-century Lutheran theologians** defend him —
> They say he genuinely believed "**resistance to worldly authority is wrong**" —
> He didn't switch sides for gain — he was **theologically consistent**.
>
> **The truth is probably somewhere in between**:
> Luther had **both** real theological logic **and** a political instinct that feared the movement spinning out of control —
> **When you study history**, **don't simplify into "hero or not"** —
> **see complexity**: he gave Germany the Bible + a national language — **and** he abandoned the peasants.

##### June 13, 1525 — Married at 41

**Luther was 41 — and did something **that shocked everyone**** —
**He got married** — to **Katharina von Bora** (a 26-year-old **former nun** — escaped from her convent two years earlier).

**This was an earthquake in 1525**:
- **Luther was a monk** — by Catholic rule **never permitted to marry** —
- **Katharina was a nun** — even more forbidden to marry —
- **The two of them marrying = a double violation of Catholic law**

**But Luther married on purpose** —
**He wanted his own marriage to be a demonstration**:
> "**A monk and a nun marrying is normal** —
> **Not a fall — but a return to the order of creation** —
> **God never required monks to be celibate** — that's a **human-made rule**".

**Luther + Katharina were married 21 years** — had 6 children (3 surviving to adulthood) —
**Their household became a cultural center in Wittenberg** —
**Katharina ran the household + brewed beer + took in escaped nuns + advised Luther on theology** —
**She is the first fully formed "reformer's wife" in Reformation history** —
**Paving the way for Lutheran / Calvinist / Anglican pastors to marry** —
**The fact that Protestant pastors have families today began with Luther + Katharina in 1525**.

##### February 18, 1546 — Death at 63

**Luther's body had always been wrecked (monastic asceticism + later obesity + high blood pressure)** —
**In February 1546 — he traveled back to his birthplace Eisleben to mediate a family dispute** —
**He had a heart attack in Eisleben** —
**At 3 a.m. on February 18, he died** —

**Just before he died, he wrote to his wife**:
> "**Käthe — we will meet again in this life — and again in the next** —
> **and when we meet there we will laugh at all of this** —
> **I am going into God's arms — and praying for you still**".

— He was **buried in the Wittenberg Castle Church** —
**right next to the door where he had nailed the 95 Theses in 1517** —
**buried beneath the very hammer-stroke that changed history**.

##### His Contested Legacy

**Luther was not a simple "hero"**:

**Positive**:
- **The archetype of individual conscience vs. authority**
- **Spiritual equality** (priesthood of all believers)
- **Vernacular Bible** (the source of German national identity)
- **The foundation of modern German**

**Negative**:

- **The Lutheran church in Germany has always been close to the state** — eventually becoming a "state church" —

> ⚠️ **AP thinking**: Luther is a **complex hero** — not a simple one —
> **Studying history** cannot mean **whitewashing** — and cannot mean **total dismissal** —
> **Acknowledge that the right of individual conscience he gave the world is genuinely great** —
> **At the same time acknowledge his stance toward peasants + Jews was genuinely violent** —

#### Johann Tetzel (the indulgence salesman, 1465-1519)

##### Origins: A Saxon Dominican Friar

**Born 1465 in Pirna** (eastern Saxony) —
**Studied theology at Leipzig University** —
**Joined the Dominican order at 24 in 1489** —
**The Dominicans were the medieval church's strongest "preaching + heresy-prosecution" order** —
**Tetzel's branch** — he later became an Inquisitor (heresy judge).

**From 1503 on Tetzel was an "indulgence ambassador"** —
**He was a master of dramatic preaching** —
**He toured Germany preaching + selling indulgences** —
**Tetzel was **the most successful salesman**** — he brought **enormous cash** to the church.

##### 1517 — The Peak of the Indulgence Sale

**In 1515 Pope Leo X authorized Albrecht of Brandenburg (a German archbishop)** —
**to sell a special "plenary indulgence" across Germany** —
**50% of revenue to Albrecht** (to pay down his debt — **Albrecht had borrowed roughly 21,000 ducats from the Augsburg Fugger bank** — some sources push the figure to 24,000 — this is the standard figure used by the modern Reformation school (Lyndal Roper / Heinz Schilling and others); **the older textbook number «30,000 ducats» was an early estimate that has since been revised downward** — the loan covered **the archbishopric of Mainz + the bishopric of Magdeburg + the diocese of Halberstadt all at once** — holding three sees simultaneously already violated canon law, and Albrecht had to pay Rome an **additional fee** for the dispensation) + **50% to St. Peter's Basilica** (which Pope Leo X wanted finished).

— **This is why Tetzel's indulgence sale ran so hot**: **Albrecht couldn't repay the bank + the Pope was burning through money** — **two layers of debt sitting on the backs of ordinary German believers** — **the chain of money that Luther's 1517 *95 Theses* directly attacked**.

**Albrecht hired Tetzel as the salesman** —
**1516-1517 — Tetzel toured the towns of Germany** — using a famous sales pitch:

> "**As soon as the coin in the coffer rings** —
> **the soul from purgatory springs!**"
>
> (Sobald der Gulden im Becken klingt, im huy die Seel im Himmel springt)

**This line is still famous in Germany today** —
**It represents the most extreme commercialization of the indulgence** —
**Ordinary Germans heard it** — some were **moved enough to buy** — others were **enraged** —
**Luther was one of the enraged ones**.

##### How the Sales Model Actually Worked

**Tetzel rolled into a town with full theatrics**:
1. **Enter the city** — local clergy would ring bells in welcome
2. **Build a high stage in front of the cathedral** — **red carpet + papal flag + Tetzel sitting on top**
3. **Preach for hours** — about **how terrible hell is + how long purgatory lasts + how the indulgence guarantees salvation**
4. **Open the chest on the table** — people lined up to drop in coins
5. **Tetzel handed out **a receipt + a verbal pardon**** —
6. **Three to seven days later he moved on to the next town**

**A single town visit could net 1,000-10,000 ducats** —
**Albrecht + St. Peter's together earned hundreds of thousands of ducats over a few years** —
**This was **one of the largest fundraising drives of the entire 16th century**.

##### After 1517 — Luther Made Him the Target

**Luther's 95 Theses attacked Tetzel's sales pitch directly** —
**Especially Thesis 27**:
> "**Tetzel teaches**: '**when the coin in the coffer rings — the soul from purgatory springs**' —
> **This is **a human-made fairy tale** — not biblical teaching**".

**Tetzel read the 95 Theses by late 1517** — and **fought back** —
**He wrote a few anti-Luther counter-theses in 1517-1518** — but **he was no match for Luther** —
**He wasn't a scholar — he was a salesman** —
**Nobody read what he wrote** — while all of Germany read Luther.

**In 1519 Tetzel died at 54** —
**In his last months — he had a breakdown** —
**He hated himself for being dragged into this storm** —
**By the time he died, Lutheranism had **sunk roots in Germany**** —
**He became the most awkward figure in the entire Reformation story**:
**Was he a villain? He believed in indulgences — he didn't deliberately do evil** —
**Was he a hero? He pushed indulgence sales to such an extreme that he detonated the Reformation** —
**He was history's **accidental catalyst**** —
**Without Tetzel's extremes — perhaps no Luther reaction**.

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Tetzel is a **complex character** —
> Not a simple "greedy bad guy" — he was the **endpoint of the entire indulgence chain** —
> **What was actually "bad" was the whole system** (Pope + bishop + bank + salesman, all interlocked) —
> **Tetzel was simply the most visible link**.
>
> **This pattern of "the system's problem turning into one individual's problem" = the classic scapegoat mechanism in history** —
> **2008 financial crisis → Lehman Brothers gets pushed forward to take the fall for the entire financial system** —
> **Same logic** — one person represents the whole error — everyone else exits the stage with the system intact.

### The French-Swiss Reform Faction

#### John Calvin (1509-1564)

##### Origins: A Picardy Middle-Class Family

**Born July 10, 1509 in Noyon, a small city in northern French Picardy** —
**His father, Gérard Cauvin, was the secretary of the Noyon diocese + a lawyer** —
**The family had money for the **best education**** —
**Calvin went to the University of Paris at 14** — to study Latin and literature —
**At 18 he went to Orléans to study law** —

**Two traits showed up early**:
1. **Brilliantly intelligent + systematic mind** — he was **debating adults at 5**
2. **Extremely serious + zero sense of humor** — classmates called him "**the Accusative Case**" — he was **always criticizing**

**1532 — Calvin at 23 finished his law degree at Orléans** — but he **didn't want to be a lawyer** —
**What he really wanted to do was study literature** —
**He wrote his first book**, *Commentary on Seneca's De Clementia* —
**This was a French humanist work — with no anti-Catholic content yet** —
**Calvin was **still Catholic** at this point**.

##### 1533 — A Sudden Conversion

**Calvin at 24 — somewhere in Paris** —
**He wrote**: "**God, by a sudden conversion, pulled me out of my devotion to Catholicism**" —
**He never wrote down the details** —
**But during 1533-1534 — he gradually became Protestant** —
**In 1534 King Francis I of France began arresting Protestants** —
**Calvin at 25 fled France** —
**First to Basel in Switzerland** — **1535-1536 in Basel he wrote *Institutes of the Christian Religion*** —
**That book changed the Reformation**.

##### *Institutes of the Christian Religion* (1536) — Reformation Systematic Theology

**Calvin at 27 — book published** —
**It was the first **complete systematic theology** of Protestantism** —
**Luther had been the explosion** — but Luther had not written a complete system —
**Calvin was the systematizer** — he gave Protestantism a **complete theological framework**:

**Five core points** (later called TULIP):
1. **Total Depravity**: humans are **completely fallen** — no "works" can save you
2. **Unconditional Election**: God **chooses in advance** who is saved — your behavior doesn't change it
3. **Limited Atonement**: Christ **died only for the elect** — not for all humanity
4. **Irresistible Grace**: those who are predestined **must believe** — they cannot refuse
5. **Perseverance of the Saints**: those predestined **must hold to the end** — they cannot fall away

— **This is "predestination"** —
**More radical than Luther's Sola Fide** —
**Luther says "believe and be saved" — Calvin says "God decides in advance who is saved"** —
**Even "faith" is a gift from God — you cannot choose to believe** —
**This theology has a **serious + inescapable** force**.

##### 1541 — Calvin Returns to Geneva to Take Charge

**1536 — Calvin at 27 was passing through Geneva** —
**Geneva had just thrown off its Catholic bishop** — the city council was looking for reformers —
**The local reformer William Farel pressured Calvin to stay** —
**Calvin didn't want to — but Farel told him**: "**If you don't stay — God will curse you**" —
**Calvin was **frightened** — and stayed**.

**1538 — Calvin was kicked out of Geneva for being too strict** —
**He fled to Strasbourg for three years** —
**1541 — Geneva called him back** — and **this time he stayed 23 years** — until his death.

##### Calvin's Geneva — A "Protestant Theocracy"

**In Geneva for 23 years Calvin built **the strictest city-state in Protestant history**** —
**Officially he was just **the leader of the pastors' council**** — but **in reality he ran the Geneva government**:
1. **Set up the Consistory** (church court) — meeting weekly — judging **adultery / drunkenness / dancing / singing "frivolous songs" / not attending church** —
2. **Public flogging + fines + exile + execution** —
3. **Mandatory Sunday attendance** for the entire city —
4. **Banned dancing + makeup + theater + gambling + brothels** —
5. **Church oversight of every household's family life** —

**1541-1564 — these 23 years in Geneva**:
- **89 people executed** (including Servetus 1553)
- **76 people exiled**
- **Hundreds publicly flogged + shamed**
- **Thousands fined**

— **This kind of "Protestant theocracy" was an extreme later groups (Lutheran / Anglican / Methodist) didn't dare match** —
**But it **succeeded** in shaping a Geneva citizenry of "strict + hardworking + frugal" character** —
**Two hundred years later Max Weber in 1905 described this character as "the Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism"** —
**Calvin, without knowing it, **shaped the psychological foundation of modern capitalism****.

##### 1553 — Servetus Burned at the Stake

**Michael Servetus** was a Spanish doctor + theologian — he **rejected the Trinity** —
**He had written a book against the Trinity** — a capital crime even in Catholic territory — and was on the run from the Inquisition —
**On August 13, 1553, somehow he wandered into Geneva** — **he was recognized in church** —
**Calvin himself was the prosecutor** — **the Geneva council sentenced him to be burned** —

⚠️ **A note on historiography** — there is a **key nuance often flattened out of the textbook version**:
**After the verdict — but **before the execution itself** — Calvin wrote a letter to the Geneva council** —
he **asked**: **«The fire is too cruel — change the sentence to beheading.»**
— This isn't a whitewash of Calvin — it's drawn from the documented archive of his October 1553 petition to the council.

**The Geneva council refused** — their reasoning: **a heretic must be publicly burned — the act has to function as a **symbolic deterrent** for the whole city**.
— So Servetus was burned anyway.

**Both facts hold at once — and both have to be told together**:
1. **Calvin was the official prosecutor** — **he drove the whole trial** — he wrote the heresy memorandum to the council — **that responsibility is his**
2. **Calvin also petitioned for a more humane execution** — **the council overruled him** — **he had reservations about the **method** of execution**

**Holding both at once** —
**means Calvin is not the same as the Inquisition** —
**but he also did not, in principle, oppose **executing** a heretic** —
**His tension was about the method — not about the act itself**.
**On October 27, Servetus was burned at the stake outside Geneva** (**slow-burning green wood** — to prolong the agony).

— **This was **the darkest single moment in Protestant history**** —
**A Protestant reformer burning a heretic** — doing **exactly what the Reformation was supposed to oppose** —
**Calvin left a permanent stain on his legacy** —
**A 21st-century Servetus monument in Geneva reads**: "**We acknowledge the wrong — we ask forgiveness**" —
**But Calvin himself never thought it was wrong** —
**He genuinely believed Servetus was a heretic — and had to be burned** —
**He used **Catholic tools to fight Catholic content**** —
**This is the Reformation's deepest internal contradiction expressed in its rawest form**.

##### May 27, 1564 — Death at 55

**Calvin's body was wrecked his whole life** —
**Headaches + ulcers + lung disease + gout** —
**In April 1564 he gave **his last sermon**** —
**He died on May 27** — **buried by his own request with no name + no marker** —
**No one today knows exactly where his grave is**.

**He told his followers at the end**:
> "**I have done what I could** —
> **You will continue from here** —
> **I have never bragged** — only **done my duty**".

**His influence is **more international than Luther's****:
- **Scotland**: John Knox brought Calvinism home → the Presbyterian Church
- **Netherlands**: Calvinism became dominant
- **France's Huguenots** (2 million people + 100 years of religious wars)
- **English Puritans** — who later founded **American colonies (Plymouth 1620 / Massachusetts 1630)** —
  **This is the root of America's Protestant tradition** —
- **South African Afrikaner Calvinism** — the spiritual basis of apartheid —

— **Calvin alone, through one book (the Institutes)** + **one model city (Geneva)** —
**shaped one-third of global Protestantism** —
**Wider in reach + deeper in structure + more systematic than Luther**.

> ⚠️ **AP thinking**: Calvin is **even more complex than Luther** —
> **Luther was the explosion** — **Calvin was the system** —
> **Luther gave the Reformation its spark** — **Calvin gave it its frame** —
> **Without Calvin — the Reformation might have collapsed** —
> **But Calvin's Geneva makes modern people deeply uncomfortable** —
> **He is the Reformation's **two-faced god**** — necessary + frightening.

### The English Royal Faction

#### Henry VIII (1491-1547)

##### Origins: Tudor Second Son — King by Accident

**Born June 28, 1491 at Greenwich Palace** —
**Father: Henry VII, founder of the Tudor dynasty** —
**Mother: Elizabeth of York, Yorkist princess** —
**Henry was the **second son** — his older brother Arthur was the heir**.

**Henry's childhood received a **first-rate humanist education****:
- **Latin + French + Spanish + Italian** —
- **Music** — he **composed his own** — some are still performed today ("**Pastime with Good Company**") —
- **Hunting + fencing + archery** —
- **Theology** — he read **Aquinas** — and in 1521 wrote a book against Luther —
  **Pope Leo X gave him the title "Defender of the Faith"** —
  **English monarchs still use it today** — irony at its richest
- **6 feet 2 inches tall + red hair + powerful build** — at 20 one of the most handsome princes in Europe

**1502 — His brother Arthur died suddenly at 15** (likely the sweating sickness epidemic) —
**Henry at 11 became heir overnight** —
**1509 — at 17 — Henry VII died — and he was crowned Henry VIII**.

##### 1509 — Marrying Catherine of Aragon

**Henry at 17 took the throne — and immediately **did something unexpected**** —
**He married his brother's widow, **Catherine of Aragon**** (daughter of Ferdinand and Isabella of Spain — 24 years old) —
**She had been a 6-year widow** since Arthur's death 18 months earlier — **everyone assumed she was going back to Spain** —
**Henry married her right after his coronation** — **his first impulsive decision**.

**This marriage lasted 24 years** — **Catherine was pregnant 6 times** — but **only one child survived: their daughter Mary, born 1516** —
**Henry wanted a male heir** —
**He grew more and more anxious** —
**By 1525 Henry was 34** — and **starting to think about divorce** —
**He had **fallen in love with Anne Boleyn**** —
**This is the trigger of the English Reformation**.

##### 1527-1533 — "The King's Great Matter"

**From 1527 Henry **formally petitioned Pope Clement VII for an annulment**** —
**His argument**: **Leviticus 20:21 says "you shall not take your brother's wife"** —
**Henry said**: "**I married my brother's widow** — that violates the Bible — so our marriage was **invalid from the start**" —

**The problem**:
- **Catherine was the aunt of King Charles V of Spain**
- **Charles V was militarily controlling Rome** (1527 Sack of Rome)
- **Pope Clement VII was effectively Charles V's prisoner** —
- **Clement could not grant Henry an annulment** without offending Charles V

**1527-1533 — six years of stalemate** —
**Henry sent envoy after envoy to Rome** — **Clement stalled** — **Henry grew more furious**.

**January 25, 1533 — Henry at 41 secretly married Anne Boleyn** (Anne was already pregnant) —
**May 23 — newly appointed Archbishop of Canterbury Cranmer unilaterally declared Henry's marriage to Catherine void** —
**June 1 — Anne crowned queen** —
**September 7 — Anne gave birth to Elizabeth (later Elizabeth I)** —

**Pope Clement VII excommunicated Henry in July 1533** —
**Henry passed the **Act of Supremacy** in 1534** — formally declaring the King of England the **only supreme head** of the Church of England —
**England's 480-year Catholic relationship with Rome was over** —

— **This is the start of the English Reformation** —
**But note**: this is **not a real theological reform** — it is **a divorce dispute leading to political separation** —
**Henry himself was **still theologically Catholic**** — he **did not want** to become Lutheran or Calvinist —
**He just wanted **the English church under his own control****.

##### 1535 — Thomas More Executed

**Thomas More** was Henry's **Lord Chancellor + friend + humanist** —
**He had written *Utopia* (1516)** —
**He had been Henry's close friend for 17 years** —

**1534 Henry's Act of Supremacy required all officials to swear an oath** —
**recognizing Henry as supreme head of the Church of England** —
**Thomas More refused to swear** —
**He said**: "**I cannot betray my conscience + I cannot betray papal authority**" —

**On July 6, 1535, Thomas More was beheaded** —
**Just before the axe fell, he said to the executioner**:
> "**I am the King's good servant** —
> **but God's first** —
> **please go about your work with a clear mind**" —

— **Thomas More is the most famous "conscientious objector" of the Reformation** —
**He chose **"Here I stand"** just like Luther** — but **on the Catholic side** —
**The Catholic Church canonized him as a saint in 1935** —
**Robert Bolt wrote the play *A Man for All Seasons* in 1966** —
**The film of the same name won the Oscar that year** —

— **Thomas More gives the Reformation a **bitter irony****:
**Luther followed his conscience against the Pope = hero** —
**More followed his conscience against the King = dead** —
**Both followed conscience — but **the outcomes were completely different**** —
**This is the **eternal injustice** of conscience vs. authority** —
**Some live, some die** — depending on **who's on the other side**.

##### May 19, 1536 — Anne Boleyn Beheaded

**Henry and Anne had been married three years** —
**Anne too had only produced a daughter (Elizabeth)** — and **miscarried several times** —
**Henry wanted **a new queen — this time Jane Seymour**** —

**April-May 1536 — Henry had men fabricate evidence of Anne's adultery + treason** —
**Anne was arrested — she **denied** every charge** —
**It didn't matter** — **on May 17 she was sentenced to death** —
**On May 19 she was beheaded at the Tower of London** —
**Her last words**: "**God preserve my king and husband — he is a good man — I wish him better**" —

— **Anne died at 35** —
**Her daughter Elizabeth I would in 1558 become queen + rule for 45 years** —
**The real "success" of the English Reformation was Elizabeth's work** —
**But **without Anne's death, no Elizabeth**** —
**Anne's death indirectly shaped the future of English Protestantism**.

##### January 28, 1547 — Death at 55

**When Henry VIII died at 55**:
- **Married 6 times** (2 beheaded, 2 divorced, 1 dead in childbirth, 1 outlived him)
- **3 children**: Mary (Catherine's), Elizabeth (Anne's), Edward (Jane Seymour's)
- **Massive debts** — from war + palace-building + dissolved-monastery wealth gone fast —
- **England broken from Rome** — but **English church theology still strongly Catholic**
- **Thousands of monasteries dissolved** + huge amounts of art + manuscripts destroyed

**At the end** — he **gripped Archbishop Cranmer's hand** —
**and said**: "**Mercy** — God have mercy on me" —
**And died like that**.

##### His Complicated Legacy in the English Reformation

**Henry VIII was not really Protestant** —
**The real English Reformation was completed by his three children in relay**:
1. **Edward VI** (1547-1553) — strict Protestant + Cranmer's Book of Common Prayer
2. **Mary I** (1553-1558) — "Bloody Mary" returned to Catholicism + burned 300 Protestants
3. **Elizabeth I** (1558-1603) — "Elizabethan Settlement" 1559 — a middle-way compromise — neither too Catholic nor too Calvinist — the foundation of **Anglicanism**

— **Henry VIII only opened the door** —
**Elizabeth built the house** —
**Henry was personally a chaotic figure who **broke from Rome to get a divorce**** —
**But he **accidentally** gave England a national church + a national identity** —
**The English of Cranmer's 1549 Book of Common Prayer is still read today** — **the same generation as Shakespeare's English** —
**It shaped the tradition of English as a "poetic + ceremonial" language**.

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Henry VIII is the **least "spiritual" reformer** in the entire Reformation —
> **He acted not for faith** — but **for divorce + a male heir** —
> **But his secular motives accidentally produced spiritual consequences** —
> **This pattern is common in history**: **secular motives can produce spiritual results** —
> **Studying history** is not just looking for "noble motives" — also looking for **unintended consequences** —
> **Henry was not a hero — but he accidentally changed the English-speaking world**.

### The Counter-Reformation Faction

#### Ignatius of Loyola (1491-1556) — Founder of the Society of Jesus

##### Origins: A Basque Noble Knight

**Born 1491 at Loyola Castle in the Basque country** (today's northern Spain) —
**The youngest son** — by noble custom no inheritance — must **become a soldier** —
**He grew up dreaming of being a knight** — chivalric novels + sword practice + hunting + heavy gambling + a string of women —
**He later said himself**: "**Until I was 26 I was a completely worldly knight** — in love with **vanity + weapons + reputation**".

##### 1521 — A Battlefield Wound Changes Everything

**Ignatius at 30 — defending Pamplona for Charles V of Spain** —
**On May 20, a French cannonball shattered his right leg** — he **lay in bed for 9 months** —
**Leg broken + reset + reset wrong + had to be re-broken and reset again** — **he had 3 operations** —
**He nearly died — but his vanity wouldn't let him quit**:
"**I cannot be a cripple** — I **must reset the leg again** — I **must keep being a knight**" —
**He survived — but the leg was lame**.

##### Nine Months in Bed — The "Conversion"

**Lying in bed with **nothing to do**** — he wanted **chivalric novels** for distraction —
**The household had no chivalric novels** — only **two religious books**:
- ***Life of Christ*** by Ludolph of Saxony
- ***Lives of the Saints***

**Reluctantly** Ignatius read these two — and over **9 months read them again and again** —
**He gradually went through an inner shift**:
> "**When I read chivalric novels** — I felt **excited at the moment** — but **after the excitement** my heart felt **empty**" —
> "**When I read saints' lives** — **excited at the moment** — but **after** my heart felt **full**" —
>
> "**I realized** — these two excitements **are not the same** —
> **One is worldly vanity** — one is **real joy**".

— This is what was later called "**Ignatian Discernment**" —
**Judging the truth of a feeling by its aftermath** — and **the foundation of Jesuit spirituality**.

##### 1522 — Cave Retreat at Manresa

**1522 — at 31 — he left Loyola — went to **Manresa** (a small town in northern Spain)** —
**He lived in a cave on the bank of the Cardoner river for 11 months** —
**Doing the strictest discipline**:
- **Praying 7 hours a day**
- **Whipping himself 3 times a day**
- **Vegetarian + no bathing + no haircuts**
- **Visiting the sick at the hospital every day**

**He had several "mystical experiences"** — visions of **the Trinity / Mary / the structure of creation** —
**He took notes** — these notes later became **the *Spiritual Exercises*** —
**The Jesuit core spiritual text** — **still in use 500 years later**.

##### 1534 — The Society of Jesus Founded

**August 15, 1534 — Ignatius at 43** —
**He had been studying theology at the University of Paris for 7 years** —
**On that day he and 6 friends** (including Francis Xavier and Peter Faber) **at the chapel on Montmartre hill** —
**took vows together**:
- **Poverty**
- **Chastity**
- **Obedience** — **especially obedience to the Pope** —
- **Mission to Jerusalem** + (if impossible) **service wherever the Pope sent them**

— This is the founding of the Society of Jesus.

##### 1540 — Papal Approval

**Pope Paul III formally approved the **Society of Jesus** in 1540** —
**Ignatius at 49 — became the first Superior General** —
**For the next 16 years he directed Jesuit expansion worldwide from Rome**:

**The Jesuit specialty**:
- **No monk's robe** — they wore **plain priestly dress** — to blend in with society
- **No fixed monastery** — they could be deployed anywhere in the world
- **Education** — they founded **elite schools + universities** (the first one in Italy in 1548 — later hundreds across Europe + Latin America + Asia)
- **Mission** — **Francis Xavier reached India 1542 + Japan 1549** + Matteo Ricci reached China 1583

**Jesuits gave their entire lives to the Pope** — they became the **elite force of the Counter-Reformation** —
**They kept Catholicism from collapsing in the wake of the Reformation** — and instead drove **a reverse expansion** —
**Catholic growth in Latin America + India + China + Japan from 1500-1700 **far outpaced Protestant expansion**** —
**This is the Counter-Reformation's greatest achievement** —
**Ignatius alone set it in motion**.

##### July 31, 1556 — Death at 65

**When Ignatius died, the Jesuits had **1,000 members + 35 schools + a global mission network**** —
**He is buried in the Gesù Church in Rome** —
**The Jesuit order is still **the largest single order in the Catholic Church**** —
**Over 16,000 Jesuits worldwide today**.

**His biggest legacies**:
- **The Jesuit school + university network** — touching hundreds of millions of graduates worldwide — including **Pope Francis 2013- (the first Jesuit pope)**
- **The Spiritual Exercises** — still in use 500 years later —
- **The core engine of the Catholic Counter-Reformation** —
- **Scientific contributions** — Jesuits contributed enormously to **astronomy + mathematics + linguistics** —
  especially **Matteo Ricci** arriving in China in 1583 — **the single largest bridge in Sino-European cultural exchange**

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Ignatius is **the most under-told character in the Reformation story** —
> **Textbooks usually spend 5 paragraphs on Luther + 2 paragraphs on Calvin + 1 paragraph on Henry VIII** —
> **And nearly nothing on Ignatius** —
> **But Ignatius is the **real engine of the Counter-Reformation**** —
> **Without him — Catholicism might have collapsed by the 1700s** —
> **The actual outcome of the Reformation is **not** a Protestant solo victory** —
> **It is **a balance** between Protestants + a reformed Catholic Church** —
> **Studying history can't be one-sided** — it must **see the force of the Counter-Reformation**.

### Ordinary Women's Perspective

#### Marie Dentière (former-nun reformer, c. 1495-1561)

##### Origins: From a Tournai Noble Family into an Augustinian Convent

**Born around 1495 in Tournai** (today Belgium) —
**Noble family** — **entered an Augustinian convent** —
**At 26 in 1521 — she became prioress** —

**1524 — at 29 — she was influenced by the Reformation** —
**She **left the convent**** — at the time this was a **crime + heresy** —
**She fled to Strasbourg** — and married **a former monk, Simon Robert** —
**The two of them did Protestant missionary work together** —
**Simon died in 1533** — Marie was widowed at 38 with 5 children —
**1535 — she remarried Antoine Froment** (another reformer) —

##### 1535-1539 — A Geneva Reformer

**Marie and Antoine moved to Geneva** —
**1535 — Geneva had not yet fully turned Protestant** —
**Marie preached on the streets of Geneva** — at the time this was an **absolute violation** of the rule (in both Catholicism and Protestantism) that **women may not preach** —
**She publicly opposed monasticism** — she went **personally into the Geneva convent to urge nuns to come out** —
**The nuns were furious and chased her away** —
**But Marie did not give up**.

##### 1539 — She Wrote the First "Public Letter of Theology by a Woman"

**1539 — Marie at 44 — published *A Most Beneficial Letter, Prepared and Written by a Christian Woman of Tournai*** —
**This is the **first publicly published theological work by a woman** in Reformation history** —
**In the book she**:
- **Publicly argued that women may read the Bible + interpret the Bible**
- **Criticized **Calvin** and the Geneva reformers** — for **excluding women** from the reform
- **Publicly argued that women may preach + teach**

**The book was banned **immediately**** —
**Calvin himself wrote against her** — saying: "**Women preaching violates Paul's 'Let women keep silent in the church'**" —
**The Geneva council banned the book** — **the printed copies were burned** —
**Marie was officially shamed** —

##### 1561 — Death at 65

**Her last 22 years were relatively quiet** —
**She **continued to educate her children + help her husband do reform work**** —
**But she **never published again**** —
**She died in 1561** —
**She was almost forgotten by history** —
**Until the 1980s women's history movement rediscovered her** —
**Today the International Monument to the Reformation in Geneva (added in 2002)** carries **her name** —
**She is the **only woman** on this monument** (the others are Luther / Calvin / Knox / Zwingli — all men).

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Marie Dentière is **a completely forgotten figure** in the Reformation story —
> **She wasn't restored to history until the 21st century** —
> **She represents **the women's movement inside the Reformation**** —
> **Hundreds of "former-nun reformers" like her** — left convents + married + helped spread Protestantism —
> **But once the Reformation was done** — **male leaders still excluded women** —
> **Calvin would not let Marie preach** — this is **a gender problem the Reformation did not solve** —
> **A women's Reformation** had to wait for the 19th-20th centuries —
> **Studying history** has to **see this "forgotten half"** — male narratives by default cover up women's contributions.

**Hans — 34 — a peasant on a Saxony manor** —
**1521 he heard a sermon — about Luther's 95 Theses** —
**He had **never heard language like this before**** —

**1524 — peasants in Schwabach revolted** —
**Hans joined** —
**They wrote the *Twelve Articles*** — demanding **abolish serfdom + villages elect their own pastors + commons own land in common** —
**They claimed Luther had inspired them** —

**May 1525 — Battle of Frankenhausen** — 6,000 peasants vs. a noble army —
**Before the battle, someone read aloud Luther's pamphlet to the peasants** —
**That pamphlet said**: "**Killing peasant rebels is God's work**" —
**Hans was **furious + confused**** —
**He thought**: "**Luther himself in 1521 said we were all equal — and now he says God is happy when we die**" —
**Is this 'liberty' just a trick to use us?**

**On May 15, the Battle of Frankenhausen began** —
**Hans was scattered with the others by cannon fire** —
**On the run — he was cut down by a noble knight's sword** —

— No one collected his body —
**His wife stayed on the manor with 4 children, still serfs** —
**No one remembers his name** —
**He is one of the 100,000 dead peasants** —
**The Reformation gave him **brief hope** — and then **personally extinguished it**** —
**This is one of the Reformation's **deepest wounds**.

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Every "movement of ideas" has its **ground-level believers** —
> **They listened to elite reformers' speeches — they "heard it clearly" — they acted** —
> **But once the elite reformers saw the lower classes "heard it too literally"** —
> **the reformers immediately pulled back + drew clear lines** —
> **This pattern of "ideological elites abandoning their ground-level believers" repeats**:
> - **Luther abandoning the peasants in 1525**
> - **Robespierre abandoning the sans-culottes 1791-1794 in the French Revolution**
> - **Stalin abandoning the early liberal Bolsheviks in the 20th century**
> - **2010 Arab Spring elite abandoning street protesters in Tunisia / Egypt**
>
> **Hans dying in 1525** is the first large-scale instance of this **eternal abandonment**.

#### Sister Agnes, an English Nun, 1539

**Sister Agnes — 28 — a nun at a Lincolnshire convent** —
**Her father was a country gentleman** — **second daughter** — **the family hoped she would enter the convent** —
**The convent gave the family **social status + no dowry needed**** —

**Agnes entered the convent at 18** —

**She wrote poetry + studied Latin + composed music** —

**1535-1540 — Henry VIII dissolved 800+ monasteries across England** —
**September 1539 — her convent was closed** —
**Henry gave nuns a **pension** — but **a small one**** —
**Agnes had to **return home**** —

**Going home meant facing**:
- **No suitable husband** — at 28 she was past the marriage age — and her **"former nun" status** made noble families **afraid** to marry her
- **No professions + no jobs available to women**
- **No way to keep copying manuscripts + studying Latin + gardening** — she had **lost her space**

**She had 5 children + spent most of her time on housework + never wrote another poem** —

**No one remembers her**.

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: The English Reformation's dissolution of the monasteries was a **double blow for nuns** —
> **Convents had limits** — but they gave medieval women **the only "knowledge space" they could enter** —
> **They could pray + copy manuscripts + read Latin + compose music** —
> **After the Reformation dissolved the monasteries** —
> **Women lost this space** — and went back to **pure housework + marriage** —
> **For 200 years, Protestantism kept no parallel "knowledge space" for women** —
> **Not until Mary Wollstonecraft and the 19th-century women's education movement** —
> **did women regain this space** —
> **This is the Reformation's gender shadow** — and textbooks barely mention it.

## 4. The Story: Those 130 Years (1517-1648)

### October 31, 1517 — The Hammer at Wittenberg

> Martin Luther at 33 — posting the 95 Theses on the Wittenberg Castle Church door —
> He thought it was a university debate — he had no idea he had just changed history.

### 1518 — The Printing Press Relays the Spark

> Luther's 95 Theses spread across Germany on the printing press in 6 weeks —
> **By 1500, Europe already had 1,000+ print shops** —
> **Any idea could travel 1,000 km in 6 weeks** —
> **Without the printing press — no Reformation** —
> **The Renaissance's technology (printing) was the detonator**.

### 1521 — Diet of Worms

> Luther stood before Emperor Charles V — and said "Here I stand" on April 18 —
> May 26 the Edict of Worms made him an outlaw —
> He was kidnapped and hidden in Wartburg Castle.

### 1522 — Luther Translates the German Bible

> 11 weeks to finish the New Testament — 200,000 copies printed within 5 years —
> This book is the **source of German national identity** —
> **Goethe + Schiller said later**: "**The German language is the language of Luther's Bible**".

### 1524-1525 — The Peasants' War

> 100,000 peasants revolted — claiming Luther had inspired them —
> Luther turned on them — May 15 the Battle of Frankenhausen — peasants massacred —
> Lutheranism lost its rural base — became a **religion of nobles + middle class** —
> **This is the darkest turning point in Reformation history**.

### 1525 — Luther Marries at 41

> He married former nun Katharina von Bora — a double violation of Catholic law —
> A deliberate demonstration that **Protestant pastors can marry + have families** —
> The foundation of Protestant family sanctification —
> 80% of American Protestant churches today have married pastors with families — directly traceable to Luther in 1525.

### 1530 — The Augsburg Confession

> Lutheran doctrine systematized — written by Luther's assistant Philip Melanchthon —
> The official doctrinal document of the Lutheran church —
> **Five core points**: faith alone + Scripture alone + grace alone + priesthood of all believers + clarity of the Gospel.

### 1534 — Henry VIII's Act of Supremacy

> England breaks from Rome — but Henry himself is **still theologically Catholic** —
> **He just wants the divorce + a male heir** —
> The real English Reformation was completed by Elizabeth I in 1559.

### 1536 — Calvin's Institutes Published + Tyndale Dies

**Two events the same year**:
- **Calvin at 27 — publishes *Institutes of the Christian Religion*** — the foundation of Reformation systematic theology
- **William Tyndale** (the English Bible translator) **was executed on October 6, 1536, at Vilvoorde near Antwerp** —

  ⚠️ **A note on historiography**: Tyndale was not simply «burned alive». He was **first strangled with a rope, and only then was his body burned**.
  In 16th-century Habsburg law, this «strangle-then-burn» sequence was the «merciful» version of the death penalty, reserved for those who had repented or accepted some measure of church reconciliation —
  the full live-burning was the heaviest, most shameful form.
  Tyndale's last recorded words on the scaffold: **«Lord, open the King of England's eyes.»**
  — **80% of the 1611 King James Bible was lifted directly from his translation** —
  **He died — but his English Bible lived for 500 years**.

— **Tyndale is the **quiet hero** of the English Reformation** —
**He had to die for the English Bible to exist** —
**Calvin systematized Protestant theology** — **the two events landed in the same year**.

### 1545-1563 — Council of Trent

> The Catholic **Counter-Reformation** council — 25 sessions over 18 years —
> **Reforms**:
> - **Banned the abuse of indulgences**
> - **Built a seminary system to train priests**
> - **Redefined Catholic theology**
> - **Strengthened papal authority**
>
> **Catholicism after Trent** is more disciplined + more intellectual + more global —
> **Roughly 80% of the modern Catholic Church's shape** comes from Trent —
> **Scholarly consensus**: Trent is the **most important council** in Catholic history —
> **More far-reaching than Vatican II (1962-1965)**.

### 1546 — Luther Dies

> At 63 — back in his birthplace Eisleben to mediate a family dispute — heart attack —
> Buried near the Castle Church door where he had nailed the 95 Theses in 1517 —
> **Buried under the very hammer-stroke that changed history**.

### 1553 — Servetus Burned in Geneva

> Calvin himself burned a heretic — the **darkest single moment in Protestant history** —
> Catholic tools used against Catholic content —
> **The starkest expression of the Reformation's internal contradiction**.

### 1555 — Peace of Augsburg

> 30 years of religious wars inside the Holy Roman Empire end (briefly) —
> The agreement: "**Cuius regio, eius religio**" ("the prince picks the religion of his subjects") —
> The prince chose Lutheran or Catholic — subjects had to follow —
> **No third option allowed** — Calvinism was not yet recognized —
> The Reformation's first official "tolerance" — but it **still restricted individual choice**.

### August 24, 1572 — Saint Bartholomew's Day Massacre

> France — Paris — Catholic forces **massacred Huguenots (French Calvinists)** —
> **5,000-30,000 dead in 3 days** (estimates vary widely) —
> **The most famous religious massacre in 17th-century Europe** —
> **Eight rounds of French Catholic vs. Huguenot wars from 1562-1598** —
> **Saint Bartholomew was the most violent**.

### 1588 — Spanish Armada

> Catholic Philip II of Spain sent **130 ships + 30,000 men to invade England** —
> Hoping to overthrow Elizabeth I and restore a Catholic England —
> **Failed** — storms + the English navy + fire ships —
> **Armada's defeat = Protestantism secure in England** —
> **Already disconnected from Reformation theology** — by now this is **a nation-state war + a contest of sea power**.

### 1598 — Edict of Nantes

> Issued by King Henry IV of France — giving Huguenots legal protection:
> - **Protestants could hold public office**
> - **Allowed self-governing towns**
> - **Could maintain self-defense forces**
>
> **The first legally recognized religious pluralism after the Reformation** —
> **But it did not last** — Louis XIV revoked it in 1685 — 200,000 Huguenots fled —
> **France lost its most enterprising middle class** — this loss **shaped France's different developmental path from England / Holland in the 18th-19th centuries**.

### 1618-1648 — The Thirty Years' War

> **30 years of war** — on the surface, **Protestant vs. Catholic** — actually, **nation-state vs. nation-state**:
> - **Bohemian revolt 1618** (Protestants against the Habsburgs)
> - **Denmark + Sweden join the Protestant side**
> - **France (Catholic) joins the Protestant side** — because it **feared Habsburg dominance** —
>   **Political calculation > theology**
> - **Death toll**: 8,000,000 Europeans dead — parts of Germany lost 50% of population —
> - **More brutal than the Black Death** (plague is a disease — this was **deliberate human killing**)
>
> **The result**: everyone **was exhausted** — **and realized religious war has no winners** —
> **Some new political framework was needed** — and that's **Westphalia**.

### 1648 — Peace of Westphalia

> **The starting point of modern international law** —
> **Five principles**:
> 1. **State sovereignty** — every state has **supreme authority within its borders**
> 2. **Non-intervention** — one state cannot force its religion on another
> 3. **International law** — states settle disputes through **treaty + arbitration**, not war
> 4. **Ambassadors + permanent diplomacy**
> 5. **Balance of power** — European states **check each other** to prevent any single power from dominating
>
> **Westphalia is the Reformation's final outcome** —
> **Not a Protestant victory** — and **not a Catholic victory** —
> **It is the **victory of the nation-state**** —
> **From this point on, secular politics in Western Europe outranks religious politics** —
> **The modern global political system** (the UN / WTO / NATO) **all sits on top of Westphalia's 5 principles**.

### 1685 — Edict of Nantes Revoked

> Louis XIV revoked it — 200,000 Huguenots fled France —
> Heading to **England / Holland / Prussia / the American colonies** —
> France lost its **most enterprising** middle class —
> This is the **last major event of the Reformation period** — and a kind of **reversal**:
> **France chose religious uniformity** = **France missed some of the engines of the Enlightenment + industrialization** —
> **Holland / England / Prussia chose religious pluralism** = **they became the fastest-developing 18th-century states**.

### 1791 — The U.S. First Amendment

> "**Congress shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof**"
>
> **This is the final lesson of the 130 years of Reformation bloodshed** —
> **The Founding Fathers learned directly**: "**The state forcing religion = 130 years of war + millions dead**" —
> **Solution = separation of church and state + freedom of religion** —
> **The First Amendment is the Reformation's ultimate gift to the world**.

### Document 1: Luther's *95 Theses* (October 31, 1517)

#### What Makes This Document Special

**The 95 Theses are the **starting point** of the Reformation** — but **the document itself is not a radical revolutionary manifesto** —
**It is **an invitation to academic debate**** —
**Luther wrote it in Latin** — and only intended to **discuss it with other university teachers** —
**He did not intend to **mobilize the public****.

**But it became a revolution** —
**Because**:
1. **The printing press carried it across Germany in 6 weeks** (Luther didn't predict this)
2. **A student translated it into German** — letting ordinary people read it
3. **The church's over-reaction** pushed Luther into an anti-Catholic position — **and he refused to recant** —
4. **Luther's wording**, though academic, **was radical** — it directly challenged the basis of papal authority

#### The Structure of the 95

**The 95 Theses are not 95 independent points** — they are **3-4 core arguments + a lot of supporting material**:

**Core argument 1**: **Repentance is not a ritual — it is a lifelong attitude of turning** (Theses 1-7)
> "**When the Lord Jesus said 'repent'** — he meant the **whole life of a Christian** should be **repentance** —
> **not just **a single external rite** (such as confession)**".

**Core argument 2**: **Indulgences cannot remit future sins + cannot release souls from purgatory** (Theses 8-22)
> "**Indulgences only remit the punishments **the church itself** has imposed** —
> **The Pope has **no power to release souls from purgatory** — only God does**".

**Core argument 3**: **The true treasure is the Bible + the Gospel — not indulgences** (Theses 32-43)
> "**Any **truly repentant Christian** can be saved **without an indulgence**** —
> **The greatest thing the Pope can do is **not selling indulgences — but preaching the Gospel clearly****".

**Core argument 4**: **The Pope too should be held to the standard of Scripture** (Theses 86-95)
> "**Why does the Pope — richer than Croesus — **build St. Peter's with the money of the poor** —
> **rather than with **his own**?**"

— **Thesis 86 directly challenges the Pope's power and wealth** —
**This radical language enraged the Pope** + **accelerated Luther's excommunication**.

#### Selected Passages (edited and simplified to roughly Lexile-1000)

> "**1.** When our Lord Jesus Christ said '**repent**', he meant that the **whole life of a Christian** should be repentance.
>
> **27.** Those who teach '**when the coin in the coffer rings, the soul from purgatory springs**' are telling **a human story** — not **biblical teaching**.
>
> **32.** Those who think they have **secured their salvation through indulgences** **will be eternally damned together with their teachers**.
>
> **42.** Christians should be told that the Pope has **no intention or wish** that buying an indulgence **could be compared in any way** to a work of mercy.
>
> **43.** Christians should be told that **giving to the poor + helping those in need** is **better than buying indulgences**.
>
> **45.** Christians should be told that **anyone who sees a poor neighbor and ignores him while buying an indulgence** is not earning the church's pardon — he is **earning God's wrath**.
>
> **86.** Since the **Pope is richer than Croesus**, why does he **not build St. Peter's with his own money** — instead of with **the money of the poor**?
>
> **94.** Christians should be encouraged — to **follow Christ through pain + death + hell** — this is **true faith**.
>
> **95.** Thus they enter heaven **through suffering** — not **through false comfort**."

#### Five Layers of Meaning in This Document

**Layer 1: Academic Debate vs. Revolutionary Mobilization**

**Luther wrote this as **an academic invitation to debate** — but it became a revolution** —
**This is a classic pattern in history**:
**A scholar's **academic essay** turns into a **social movement**** —
**Luther himself was **surprised** + **reluctant**** —
**But he refused to recant** — so he **became an accidental movement leader**.

**Layer 2: The Printing Press as Accelerator**

**Luther wrote in 1517 — Gutenberg's press was already 67 years old (1450)** —
**But the 95 Theses are **the first time** all of Europe **felt the social-mobilization power of print**** —
**Earlier, print mostly produced **Bibles + scholarly books + literature**** —
**The 95 Theses showed people that **printed text can mobilize society in weeks**** —
**Six weeks turned a forgotten monk's **academic debate** into **a national German conversation**** —
**This is **the first large-scale demonstration of an information revolution**** —
**Like 21st-century social media — where a single tweet can travel the world in hours** —
**Print in 1517 = the Twitter of the 16th century**.

**Layer 3: The Politics of Thesis 86**

> "**The Pope is richer than Croesus — why does he build St. Peter's with the money of the poor?**"

**This is the most radical thesis** —
**Because it directly questions **the Pope's wealth + morality + power**** —
**Not just challenging theology — challenging **the institutional economic base**** —
**The Pope could not tolerate this** — this is exactly the line that pushed Luther toward excommunication.

**Layer 4: The Theology of Christian Suffering**

> "**Theses 94-95**: Christians follow Christ **through pain + death** — **not through false comfort**"

**This **suffering-theology** is the **opposite** of the indulgence**:
- **Indulgence**: pay money + no pain + go to heaven
- **Luther**: a Christian **should** suffer — that's true faith

— **This **anti-comfort** theology separates Luther from **Erasmus / Calvin** types** —
**Luther emphasizes **suffering + faith**** —
**Erasmus emphasizes **reason + education**** —
**Calvin emphasizes **predestination + system**** —
**Three internal Protestant tensions** are already visible in the 95 Theses**.

**Layer 5: Did He "Really Nail It"?**

**Scholarly debate**: **Did Luther actually nail the 95 Theses to the Wittenberg Castle Church door?**
- **Early Lutheran biographies** say he did
- **In 1961 Erwin Iserloh raised a doubt** — **Luther himself never mentioned a nailing**
- **He may simply have **mailed it to the bishop + the university**** — and the legend grew up later

**But whether or not he nailed it doesn't matter** — what matters is **he wrote it + it spread** —
**This skill of separating "legend vs. fact" is essential historical thinking** —
**The impact of the 95 Theses is real** — the nail is a literary tradition.

#### Modern Parallel: What Does the 95 Theses Look Like Today?

> **Parallel scenario**: **An ordinary person writes an academic critique** of **the corruption of some institution** —
> **It spreads via new media technology** and **accidentally becomes a global movement** —
> **The author is reluctant — but cannot turn back** —
>
> Modern parallels:
> - **2010 — Mohamed Bouazizi self-immolates in Tunisia** — an ordinary fruit vendor — video spreads on Twitter — Arab Spring detonates
> - **2017 — #MeToo Twitter hashtag** — a hashtag becomes a global movement
> - **2013 — Snowden documents** — an NSA contractor passes internal documents to the press — accidentally becomes a global surveillance debate
>
> **Luther's 95 Theses** and these events are **spiritually related**:
> An individual **accidentally** triggers **a social movement** —
> The printing press in 1517 is the **grandfather** of 21st-century Twitter.

### Document 2: The Decrees of the Council of Trent (1545-1563)

#### Author Profile

**The Council of Trent is the Catholic Church's **response to the Reformation**** —
**1545-1563 — 25 sessions over 18 years** —
**Held at Trent** (in northern Italy) —
**Because it was on **the border of northern Italy + the Holy Roman Empire**** —
**Both northern and southern bishops could attend**.

**Three popes + three phases**:
- **1545-1547 under Paul III** — mainly handled **doctrine**
- **1551-1552 under Julius III** — mainly handled **the sacraments**
- **1562-1563 under Pius IV** — mainly handled **reform + clergy training**

**A total of 270+ bishops attended** (different ones in different phases) —
**Mostly Italian + Spanish bishops** — **German Protestants did not attend** —
**Trent is **a one-sided Catholic reform** — not a reconciliation council**.

#### Trent's Five Core Decisions

**Decision 1 — Doctrine**:
- **Reaffirmed**: **Bible + church tradition** are **dual authorities** — rejecting Luther's "Bible alone"
- **Reaffirmed**: **faith + works** **both** save — rejecting Luther's "faith alone"
- **Reaffirmed**: all **7 sacraments** are valid — rejecting the Protestant claim of only 2 (baptism + Eucharist)
- **Reaffirmed**: **Mass is a sacrifice** — rejecting the Protestant view of Mass as memorial
- **Reaffirmed**: **purgatory + the intercession of saints** — rejecting Protestant rejection of these

**Decision 2 — Against corruption**:
- **Banned **bishops holding multiple posts** (pluralism) — previously a single bishop might collect income from many dioceses**
- **Banned **untrained priests** — bishops must **certify their priests have been trained****
- **Built the **seminary system** — every diocese **must build a priest-training school**** —
  **This is Trent's biggest practical reform**

**Decision 3 — Bible + liturgy**:
- **Kept the Latin Mass** — rejecting the Protestant move to vernacular
- **Confirmed the Latin Vulgate** as the authoritative translation
- **But **encouraged bishops to explain the Mass in the vernacular** to the people**

**Decision 4 — Papal authority**:
- **Reaffirmed** the Pope as **supreme head of the church**
- **Rejected any view that "there is an authority equal to the Pope"**

**Decision 5 — Against the abuse of indulgences**:
- **Affirmed the concept of indulgences as valid** — but **forbade commercial sale**
- **Banned the "coin rings — soul springs" style of marketing**

#### Selected Decree Passages (edited for accessibility)

> "**On Bible + church tradition**:
>
> This sacred council recognizes: **the truths of faith and morality are preserved in two places**:
> **the Bible's written portion** — and **the unwritten apostolic tradition that has been preserved by oral handing-down to today** —
>
> Anyone who recognizes only one and not both **shall be excommunicated** —
>
> **On indulgences**:
>
> This council **reaffirms** the Catholic faith: **indulgences truly have force** —
> **However**: **any abuse of indulgences** — **especially those for commercial purposes** — **must cease** —
> **Any bishop must forbid those who exploit the practice** to make money by selling indulgences —
>
> **On priest training**:
>
> Every **diocese must establish a seminary** — **training young men starting at age 12** —
> They shall study **Bible + theology + history + Latin + chant** —
> They must complete **8 years of training before being ordained** —
>
> **Any bishop who fails to establish a seminary** — **the bishop himself shall be punished**."

#### Four Layers of Meaning

**Layer 1: Catholicism Doesn't Just Hold the Line — It Actually Reforms**

**Trent is the **Counter-Reformation**** —
**Note the keyword: "**Reformation**"** —
**Catholicism really did reform internally**:
- Banned the abuse of indulgences (**which is what Luther initially opposed**)
- Built the seminary (addressing Luther's "uneducated priests" concern)
- Raised bishops' accountability (addressing the "pluralism" corruption)

— **After Trent, Catholic clergy quality rose dramatically** —
**The Counter-Reformation is **not pure backlash** — it is **substantive reform**.

**Layer 2: No Doctrinal Compromise**

**Catholicism reformed internally** — but **made no doctrinal concessions**:
- **Bible + tradition as dual authorities** (rejecting sola scriptura)
- **Faith + works** save **together** (rejecting sola fide)
- **7 sacraments** (rejecting the Protestant two)
- **Papal authority** (rejecting the priesthood of all believers)

— **This pattern of "internal reform + external rejection" made the Reformation a permanent split** —
**No "compromise" could let the two sides recombine** —
**This is why Europe had 130 years of religious war until Westphalia**.

**Layer 3: The Revolution of the Seminary System**

**Trent's most practical impact is the seminary system** —
**Before Trent, priest training **had no system**** — many priests were **illiterate + ignorant of theology** —
**After Trent, every diocese had to build a priest-training school** —
**This is the **foundation of the Catholic education system**** —
**Today there are 450+ Catholic universities globally** —
**Notre Dame / Georgetown / Fordham / Catholic universities in Beijing — all of them** —
**all rooted in Trent 1545 decrees**.

**Layer 4: A Strange Mirror of the 95 Theses**

**Luther's 95 Theses (1517)** — treated **abuse of indulgences** as Catholicism's main problem —
**Trent (1545) agreed** — and also banned the abuse of indulgences —
**This could have been a basis for reconciliation** —

**But**:
- **Trent rejected sola fide + sola scriptura**
- **Luther could not abandon those two**
- **30 years of mistrust + mutual excommunications + hardening of camps**

— **From 1517 to 1545 — 28 years — Luther's position **radicalized**** —
**Becoming **"impossible to reconcile"**** —
**By the time Trent met in 1545, it was **too late**** — it could only **deepen the split**.

> ⚠️ **AP thinking**: **If the Pope had banned the abuse of indulgences immediately in 1517** —
> **Luther might not have radicalized** — **maybe no Reformation** —
> **History is **the accumulation of multiple moments**** —
> **Not a **single decision****—
> **Trent's late reform in 1545** could no longer stop the Reformation — but it **shaped Catholicism's next 400 years**.

### Document 3: The Treaty of Westphalia (October 24, 1648)

#### Author Profile

**The Westphalia treaty is actually **two treaties**** —
- **The Treaty of Münster** (May 15) — signed by Spain + the Netherlands + France + the Holy Roman Empire
- **The Treaty of Osnabrück** (October 24) — signed by Sweden + the Holy Roman Empire

**Together they are called **the Peace of Westphalia**** — ending the Thirty Years' War —
**178 sovereign delegates negotiated** — **the largest diplomatic conference in human history at the time** —
**Four years of negotiation** — **60,000 pages of documents** —
**This is the **prototype of modern diplomacy**** —
**including treaty structure + ambassador systems + arbitration mechanisms + the irreversibility of treaties** —
**Today's UN / WTO negotiation styles trace directly to Westphalia**.

#### The Five Westphalian Principles

**These five principles are now called **Westphalian Sovereignty**** — and they are **the core concept of international relations**:

**Principle 1 — Sovereignty**

> "**Each state has supreme authority** over **the law + religion + currency + military** within its borders —
> **No foreign power may interfere**".

— **This is the **first formal recognition** that **states are the final authority**** —
**Before Westphalia, there were "supranational" authorities like the **Pope + the Holy Roman Emperor**** —
**After Westphalia, those supranational authorities **were no longer recognized**** —
**The state becomes the **final unit**.

**Principle 2 — Non-Intervention**

> "**A state has **autonomy over its internal affairs**** —
> **including **what religion its people follow**** —
> **No other state may **invade in the name of religion****".

— **This is the **final lesson** of 130 years of Reformation bloodshed** —
**"Cuius regio, eius religio" was already set at Augsburg in 1555** —
**Westphalia 1648 went further**: **foreign states cannot use religion as a pretext for invasion** —
**This is the **core starting point of modern international law**.

**Principle 3 — International Law**

> "**States resolve disputes through **treaty + arbitration**, not war**".

— **This is **the foundation of international law**** —
**Hugo Grotius's 1625 *On the Law of War and Peace*** laid the basis for this legal framework —
**Westphalia is the implementation of Grotius's thought** —
**Today's UN + ICJ + WTO all sit on top of Westphalia + Grotius**.

**Principle 4 — Ambassadors + Permanent Diplomacy**

> "**Each state stations a **permanent ambassador** in another state's capital** —
> **The ambassador enjoys **diplomatic immunity**** — **local law cannot arrest him**".

— **Before, diplomats were **temporary envoys**** — they returned home when their mission was done —
**After Westphalia, every state **had ambassadors in each other's capitals**** —
**This is the **foundation of modern diplomatic practice**** —
**Every embassy in Washington / Beijing / Moscow today** —
**traces directly to Westphalia 1648**.

**Principle 5 — Balance of Power**

> "**No single state should be **strong enough to threaten all the others**** —
> **A few major powers should **balance each other**".

— **This is the **core political wisdom of 18th-19th century Europe**** —
**The Concert of Europe 1815-1914 was built directly on this** —
**Today's G7 / G20 + NATO + ASEAN + EU are all modern forms of balance of power**.

#### Selected Passages

> "**Under the guidance of the most merciful and almighty God**, this treaty has been signed by **Holy Roman Emperor Ferdinand III + King Louis XIV of France + Queen Christina of Sweden + 178 imperial princes** — to **end the Thirty Years' War** —
>
> **Article 64**:
>
> Each prince **retains sovereignty over his territory** — no **foreign power** has **right of interference in any prince's internal affairs** — each **prince may freely determine the religion of his subjects** — but **minorities** must have **rights of emigration + protection of property** —
>
> **Article 65**:
>
> **Calvinist believers are formally recognized** — equal in legal status to Lutherans + Catholics — **before, at Augsburg 1555, Calvinism was not recognized — now, corrected** —
>
> **Article 67**:
>
> Any **dispute concerning religion** must be **settled by majority arbitration in the imperial diet** — **never by military force** —
>
> **Article 121**:
>
> States **place permanent ambassadors** in each other's capitals — **ambassadors enjoy diplomatic privileges + immunity from local jurisdiction** — all states **acknowledge** this **diplomatic system**".

#### Five Layers of Meaning

**Layer 1: Religion Drops from the Center to the Edge**

**For the 130 years 1517-1648 — every European political question was tied to "which kind of Christianity"** —
**Westphalia **moved religion from the center of international relations**** — **sovereignty + national interest** became the **core** —
**This is the **biggest single step toward secularization**** —
**Today's politics **scarcely depends on "religious legitimacy"** — the root is Westphalia**.

**Layer 2: The State Becomes the Final Unit**

**Before, Europe had **multiple layers of authority****:
- **Pope** (transnational religious)
- **Holy Roman Emperor** (transnational secular)
- **King** (national)
- **Lord** (regional)

**After Westphalia — only the **state** counted** —
**The Pope became simply the head of **the small state of the Vatican**** —
**The Holy Roman Emperor became simply a title held by the **Habsburg family**** —
**The state became **the only final unit**** —
**This is the **starting point of the modern political system**** — it still holds today.

**Layer 3: The Birth of Modern Diplomacy**

**178 delegates negotiating Westphalia for 4 years** —
**This **multilateral negotiation** model** —
**Today's UN General Assembly + climate change negotiations + trade negotiations** —
**all trace to Westphalia** —
**Westphalia is the **mother template of modern multilateral diplomacy****.

**Layer 4: "Westphalian" as a Term**

**International relations today calls this "**the Westphalian system**"** —
**The term is **still active in the 21st century**** —
**Debate**: **Should the Westphalian principles of sovereignty + non-intervention still apply?**
- **Pro**: "**Without Westphalia — there would be more wars**" —
- **Con**: "**Westphalia lets dictators hide behind 'sovereignty' to slaughter their own people** — Rwanda 1994 / Syria 2011-" —
  **R2P (Responsibility to Protect)** is a **partial revision** of Westphalia —
- **Centrist**: "**Westphalia is imperfect** — but **better than the chaos before** + we should **improve, not abolish**".

— **This 21st-century debate **is still living inside the 1648 framework**** —
**Studying history isn't studying the past** — it's **learning the mother tongue of modern politics**.

**Layer 5: Does Westphalia Still Work in the Globalized Era?**

**21st-century forces challenging Westphalia**:
- **Multinational corporations** (Apple / Amazon / bigger than some countries)
- **Cross-border pandemics** (COVID-19 doesn't respect borders)
- **Climate change** (no borders)
- **The internet** (no borders)

**These all put **Westphalian sovereignty under pressure**** —
**Many 21st-century theorists say**: "**We are entering a post-Westphalian era**" —
**But this is **prediction** — not yet **fact**** —
**Westphalia is still **the default framework of international relations today**.

#### Modern Parallel: What Does Westphalia Look Like Today?

> **Parallel scenario**: A few states that have been **killing each other for decades** — **exhausted + helpless** —
> **Decide to settle their disputes through **multilateral negotiation rather than continued war**** —

>
> **Westphalia 1648 left the modern world **two gifts****:
> 1. **Sovereignty + non-intervention** — letting states exist + not attack each other
> 2. **Multilateral negotiation** — letting states cooperate

> ⚠️ **AP thinking**: Westphalia is **the real winner of the Reformation** —

### Matteo Ricci (利玛窦, 1552-1610) — The Single Largest Bridge in Sino-European Cultural Exchange

**Born in Macerata, Italy in 1552** —
**Joined the Jesuits in 1571** — **a second-generation Jesuit, after Ignatius** —
**Sent to the Far East mission in 1577** —
**Arrived in Macau in 1582** — **entered mainland China (Zhaoqing, Guangdong) in 1583** —
**Spent the next 27 years in China** — until his death in 1610.

##### What He Did in China

**He didn't preach directly** (Chinese were wary) — he did **adaptation to Chinese culture first**:
1. **Learned Chinese + wore scholar's robes + took the Chinese name "Li Madou" (利玛窦)**
2. **Drew the *Map of the Myriad Countries of the World* (1602)** — astonishing Chinese elites — they saw for the first time **that China was part of the world, not the entirety of it**
3. **Translated the first 6 books of Euclid's *Elements*** — together with Xu Guangqi (1606-1607) —
   **the first systematic Western mathematics into China**
4. **Made a **mechanical clock for the Wanli emperor** (1601) — the emperor **liked** this "self-striking clock"**
5. **Debated + wrote essays with literati** — entering scholar-gentry circles
6. **Slowly some literati came to accept Catholic teaching** — famous converts include **Xu Guangqi + Li Zhizao + Yang Tingyun** —
   called "**the Three Pillars of the Late Ming**" — they wrote Catholic theology in Chinese

##### His Irony with the Reformation

**Note**: when Ricci arrived in China in 1583 —
- **Europe had been split between Protestant + Catholic for 60+ years**
- **The Thirty Years' War was still 35 years away**
- **Westphalia was still 65 years away**

**Ricci as a **Catholic Jesuit**** — what he carried into China was **Counter-Reformation Catholicism** —
**The more disciplined + more intellectual + more international Catholicism after Trent (1545-1563)** —
**He did **not bring** the corrupt Catholicism of 1517** — he brought **the reformed Catholicism after Trent**.

— **Without the Counter-Reformation — perhaps no global Jesuit mission** —
**Without the Jesuits — perhaps no Ricci — perhaps no early Sino-European cultural exchange**.

##### 1610 — After His Death, the "Rites Controversy"

**After Ricci's death** — the "**Chinese accommodation**" strategy he pioneered (allowing Chinese Catholics to continue ancestor worship + Confucian rites) **was disputed in Rome** —
**The Vatican debated 1701-1742** — and finally **in 1742 Pope Benedict XIV banned the Jesuit "accommodation"** —
**All Chinese Catholics had to **abandon ancestor worship + Confucian rites** — or stop being Catholic**.

**Result**:
- **The Kangxi emperor expelled Catholic missionaries in 1721** —
- **Catholicism in China was severely set back through the 18th century** — until the 19th century Opium Wars reopened the door
- **Sino-European cultural exchange was nearly broken for 100 years**

— **This is the **global afterlife** of the Reformation**:
**The Catholic "papal supremacy" model made "adapting to local cultures" hard** —
**Protestants arriving in China 200 years later (in the 19th century) actually adapted better than Catholics** —
**This ties to the **core difference** between Catholic and Protestant**:
- **Catholic emphasizes **Pope + Roman authority**** — centralized decisions — local adaptation difficult
- **Protestant emphasizes **personal Bible**** — local decisions — local adaptation easier

> ⚠️ **AP thinking**: Ricci's story shows the **global reach of the Reformation**:
> **Decisions in 1517 still echo 600 years later in the actual operation of the Chinese Catholic church** —
> **There are no "local" events in history** — every event is **a node in the global network** —
> **Studying history requires the **long arc + the global view**** — not just Europe.

### Wang Yangming vs. Luther — Same Era, Different Paths

**1517 Luther 95 Theses** —
**1517 Wang Yangming at 45 — has just put down Prince Ning's rebellion in Jiangxi (1519)** —
**1518-1521 Wang Yangming completes his "unity of knowledge and action" philosophy** —
**1529 Wang Yangming dies at 57**.

**The two men are **almost exactly contemporaries** — but their paths are **completely different**:

| Dimension | Luther (1483-1546) | Wang Yangming (王阳明, 1472-1529) |
|---|---|---|
| **Core question** | How can I make the "angry God" accept me? | Is principle outside the heart, or is the heart itself principle? |
| **Method** | Faith alone + Bible alone | Heart is principle + unity of knowledge and action + extending innate moral knowing (zhi liangzhi 致良知) |
| **Stance toward authority** | Against papal authority — back to the Bible | Against Cheng-Zhu Neo-Confucian orthodoxy — back to the heart |
| **Political result** | Reformation + 130 years of bloodshed | No political revolution — but seeded later Japanese Meiji thought |
| **Individual subjectivity** | Conscience vs. authority | Innate conscience vs. external rules |
| **Modern impact** | Western individualism + separation of church and state | Eastern conscience philosophy + driving force of the Japanese Meiji Restoration |

— **Two men answered **the same question**: "**individual vs. authority**"** —
**Both gave individual subjectivity** — but **walked different paths**:
- **Luther**: "**Here I stand** — I oppose the Pope" — **outward + breaking + revolution**
- **Wang Yangming**: "**The heart is principle** — I find the answer **inside**" — **inward + integration + self-cultivation**

— **Both paths are great** —
**But in 1517 — Europe walked the outward-breaking path → Reformation + modern political revolutions** —
**China walked the inward-integration path → School of Mind + no political revolution**.

> ⚠️ **AP thinking**: **The other side of the Joseph Needham question** —
> **Not just "why did the scientific revolution not happen in China"** — also **"why did the Reformation not happen in China"** —
> **Luther 1517 vs. Wang Yangming 1517 — same era** —
> **The same question, different answers** —
> **Shaping the next 500 years of divergent Sino-European trajectories** —
> **Studying history is not ranking better/worse** — it is **seeing both paths have their own wisdom** —
> **As Rule 0 says**: "**As a Chinese person — I acknowledge that Western political revolutions shaped the modern world — but I also acknowledge that Eastern philosophy of the heart has its own depth — neither can substitute for the other**".

## 9. What Historians Argue About (Historiography)

### Four Major Schools

#### A. "Reformation = Origin of Modernity" School (Classic Whig)

**Representative**: **Leopold von Ranke**, *Deutsche Geschichte im Zeitalter der Reformation* (1839-1847).

**Core thesis**:
- **Reformation = the origin of individualism + nation-state + capitalism**
- **Luther = hero of the modern world**
- **Without the Reformation — no modernity**

**Strength**: clear narrative — Luther as hero.

**Weakness**: **excessive romanticism** — **ignores Luther's 1525 abandonment of peasants + 1543 anti-Semitism** —
**also ignores the **reform dimension** of Catholic Counter-Reformation**.

#### B. "Reformation = Continuation of the Middle Ages" School (Revisionist)

**Representative**: **Heiko Oberman**, *The Dawn of the Reformation* (1986) + **Steven Ozment**.

**Core thesis**:
- **Reformation was not **a sudden revolution**** —
- **The 15th century already had **anti-clerical corruption + biblical scholarship + personal piety + nationalist** seeds** —
- **Luther is **one node** in this gradual change** — not the only one —
- **John Wycliffe in 1380s England + Jan Hus in 1410s Bohemia** — already pre-Reformation reformers

**Strength**: corrects "Luther alone" narrative + makes history more continuous.

**Weakness**: it "downgrades" the Reformation's drama.

#### C. "Reformation = Print-Press Effect" School (Media Technology)

**Representative**: **Elizabeth Eisenstein**, *The Printing Press as an Agent of Change* (1979).

**Core thesis**:
- **The Reformation is **a byproduct of the print revolution**** —
- **Without 1450 print — no 1517 Reformation** —
- **Luther was the **first** to grasp the mobilizing power of print** —
- **Print + mass literacy + vernacular Bible** = the **real engine** of the Reformation —
  **not Luther's individual genius**

**Strength**: shifts from "individual hero" to "technological structure" — deeper.

**Weakness**: may **reduce Luther's actual theological contribution** — risk of technological determinism.

#### D. "Multiple Reformations" School (Modern Pluralist)

**Representative**: **Diarmaid MacCulloch**, *The Reformation: A History* (2003) + **Carlos Eire**.

**Core thesis**:
- **There is no **single** Reformation** — there are **multiple Reformations**:
  - **Lutheran Reformation** (Germany + northern Europe)
  - **Calvinist Reformation** (Switzerland + France + Holland + Scotland)
  - **English Reformation** (England)
  - **Anabaptist Reformation** (radical fringes)
  - **Catholic Reformation** (not just Counter-Reformation — **also a real reform**)
  - **Radical Reformation** (Quakers / Mennonites / etc.)
- **Each has **its own theology + politics + outcomes**** —
- **Cannot be reduced to Luther alone** —

**Strength**: most accurate + most complete + the 21st-century scholarly consensus.

**Weakness**: complex — **hard for a 13-year-old to hold all of it at once**.

### Why This Topic Is Especially Hard to Stay Neutral On

**The Reformation falls easily into two extremes**:

**Extreme A**: **Protestant progress narrative** ("Luther = hero who liberated Europe") —
**Ignores Luther's 1525 peasants + 1543 anti-Semitism + Calvin's Servetus** —
**This is the bias of **Protestant Whig history**.

**Extreme B**: **Catholic counter-narrative** ("Reformation = heresy that triggered bloodshed") —
**Ignores 1500s Catholic corruption + Trent's real reforms** —
**This is the bias of **Catholic anti-Protestant history**.

**The right middle path**:
- **Acknowledge Luther's real spiritual contribution** (individual conscience + vernacular Bible + against indulgences)
- **At the same time acknowledge Luther's shadow** (peasants + Jews)
- **Acknowledge 1500s Catholic corruption** (indulgences + Borgia popes + ignorant clergy)
- **At the same time acknowledge Catholic Counter-Reformation **real reform**** (Trent + Jesuits + education systems)
- **Acknowledge the Reformation drove **Westphalia + the First Amendment**** — but **through 130 years of bloodshed**

> ⚠️ **Teaching hard constraint** (Rule 0):
> "**As a Chinese person — I acknowledge that the Reformation drove Western individualism + nation-state + separation of church and state** —
> **But I also acknowledge that 130 years of religious war killed 8 million people — and this cost cannot be erased by a progress narrative** —
> **At the same time, the Ming Dynasty walked a different path — Wang Yangming's School of Mind + Zhang Juzheng's reforms + Matteo Ricci's mission** —
> **Studying history is not ranking better/worse — it is seeing the complexity on both sides**".

## 10. Eight Common Misconceptions (Cleanup Zone)

### Misconception 1: "Luther wanted to split the church"

**Wrong** — **Luther's whole life he called himself a Catholic monk** —
**He wanted to **fix** the church — not split it** —
**He was excommunicated in 1521 — the church **actively** kicked him out** —
**Luther's reaction was**: "**Fine — I don't want Rome either**" —
**But the starting point wasn't a "split" — the church **first** refused to reform**.

### Misconception 2: "The Reformation immediately split into Protestant vs. Catholic"

**Wrong** —
**1517-1530 — Lutherans were still **groping for theology**** —
**Calvin didn't publish the Institutes until 1536** —
**1530-1555 Europe had **multiple Protestant factions** fighting + excommunicating each other**:
- **Lutheran vs. Anabaptist**
- **Lutheran vs. Calvinist**
- **Calvinist vs. Anglican**
- **Anglican vs. Puritan**

— "Protestant" is a **collective term** — **internally as split as Catholic vs. Protestant** —
**1648 Westphalia officially recognized only 3 (Lutheran / Calvinist / Catholic)**.

### Misconception 3: "Henry VIII was really Protestant"

**Wrong** —
**Henry VIII wrote a book against Luther in 1521** — the Pope gave him "Defender of the Faith" for it —
**In 1534 he broke from Rome** — but **his theology was still 60-70% Catholic** —
**He **rejected Luther's "faith alone"** — he **still believed in Mass + the 7 sacraments** —
**Real English Protestantism is Edward VI (briefly) + Elizabeth I (45 years)** —
**Henry only broke from Rome **for the divorce** — not for theology**.

### Misconception 4: "Calvin's Geneva was Protestant democracy"

**Wrong** — **Calvin's Geneva was a **Protestant theocracy**** —
**89 executed + 76 exiled + hundreds flogged** —
**Stricter than Catholic Rome** —
**Calvin tolerated no heresy** (Servetus burned 1553) —
**The concept of "tolerance" only formed 100+ years after Calvin**.

### Misconception 5: "The Reformation immediately produced the Scientific Revolution"

**Wrong** —
**Reformation 1517 onward** — **the Scientific Revolution (Galileo / Newton) is 100-150 years later** —
**The direct connection is unclear** —
**Many Protestant countries' science went hand in hand with Catholic countries** —
**Galileo + Descartes were both in Catholic countries** —
**Newton was in Anglican England** —
**The Reformation **indirectly** shaped a spirit of **questioning authority + individual inquiry**** —
**But the **direct causal link is debated by scholars**.

### Misconception 6: "Counter-Reformation = Catholic backlash + no reform"

**Wrong** —
**The Counter-Reformation = **substantive reform**** :
- **Trent 1545-1563 banned the abuse of indulgences + built priest training**
- **Established the seminary system**
- **Jesuit global education network**
- **Catholic theology became **more systematic + more intellectual**** —
**Roughly 80% of modern Catholic shape comes from Trent — not the Middle Ages** —
**The "backlash" label **ignores real reform** — a simplifying error**.

### Misconception 7: "Westphalia 1648 immediately ended religious wars"

**Wrong** —
**Westphalia 1648 dramatically reduced international religious wars** — but **internal religious tension continued**:
- **France revoked the Edict of Nantes 1685** — 200,000 Huguenots fled
- **17th-century English Civil War + 18th-century Test Acts** — Catholics + Protestants kept fighting
- **18th-century Spanish Inquisition was still active** —
- **Until the **secularization** of the 19th-20th centuries** — religious tension truly weakened

— **Westphalia is an **important node**** — but **not the endpoint** —
**Studying history can't assume any treaty **immediately** solves a problem** —
**Treaties are nodes in a long process**.

### Misconception 8: «The Reformation was a Protestant-vs-Catholic theological dispute — no gender dimension, no violence against ordinary women»

**Wrong — and **this is the dark side most middle-school textbooks systematically avoid****.

**Across the 1450-1750 European witch-hunt peak** — an estimated **40,000 to 60,000 people** (a conservative scholarly estimate) were executed as «witches» —
**75-85% of them were women** — mostly **widows + elderly women + marginal / poor women + midwives + folk-herb healers**.

**The Reformation was not a bystander to this violence — it was a **direct accelerant****:
- **The printing press** mass-distributed the witch-hunt manuals — the *Malleus Maleficarum* (*Hammer of the Witches*, written in 1487 by the Dominican friar Heinrich Kramer) ran multiple printed editions within 5 years and went through 30+ editions over the next 200 years; it effectively became the «field SOP» of inquisitors
- **Protestants and Catholics accused each other of «consorting with the devil»** — both sides used «finding witches» to prove they were the side of God
- **A society of war + famine + plague needed scapegoats** — and marginal women were the easiest to fingerpoint
- **Protestant regions (parts of Germany + Scotland + parts of Switzerland) burned **more** witches than Catholic regions** — a counter-intuitive fact that modern historiography keeps confirming

**A few numbers to show the scale**:
- **1626 in the Würzburg prince-bishopric (Catholic) in Germany**: 219 «witches» burned in a single year —
- **1645 in eastern England, Matthew Hopkins (self-styled «Witchfinder General»)**: roughly 100 women killed in 14 months —
- **Scotland 1563-1736**: about 4,000 trials, about 1,500 executions — under a Calvinist-theocratic backdrop

**Why is this Reformation-era and not «medieval»?** —
**The medieval period (13th-14th centuries) was actually **relatively tolerant** about «witches»** — official church teaching was **«witches do not exist; believing in witches is superstition»** (Canon Episcopi, 906 CE) —
**The real witch-hunt explosion landed in 1450-1750** — **exactly covering the Reformation era** —
**The religious anxiety, social upheaval, and concrete-fear of «the devil» that the Reformation generated** — were the seedbed of this violence.

— **Teaching the Reformation without teaching the witch-hunts is a pedagogical gender blind spot** —
**The deaths of these 40,000-60,000 women — happened in the **same era** as Luther nailing the *95 Theses*, Calvin burning Servetus, Trent reforming the church** —
**Studying history cannot be only theology debates + kings divorcing + noble wars** —
**It also has to include how nameless marginal women died across these 300 years**.

## 11. Open Questions (No Final Answers)

### Q1. Luther abandoning the peasants in 1525 — how do we judge it?

**Luther in 1517 said**: "**priesthood of all believers — everyone is equal**" —
**In 1525 the peasants rose up saying**: "**we want equality too**" —
**In 1525 Luther said**: "**killing peasant rebels is God's work**" —

**Question**: Is this the "necessary compromise" of a spiritual leader, or a real betrayal?
- If he had not written *Against the Murderous Hordes* — the nobility might have **abandoned** him — and the Reformation might have **failed** —
- But he wrote it — 100,000 peasants died — he is a **partial accomplice**

— **There's no standard answer** — but **how you think about this question matters more than the answer**.

### Q2. Calvin burning Servetus — how is this different from the Catholic Inquisition?

**Calvin burning Servetus in 1553 looks **structurally** very similar to the Catholic Inquisition burning Bruno in 1600**:
- **Both based on theological disagreement**
- **Both **public burnings** (Servetus with green wood for slow burn)**
- **Both **state + church cooperation****

**Question**: Is Calvin's Geneva just "another kind of Inquisition"?
- If yes — the Reformation didn't actually "reform" religious violence
- If not — what exactly is the difference?

— **The capacity to **reflect on the dark history of your own camp**** is one of the most important goals of historical education.

### Q3. Westphalia 1648 vs. R2P in the 21st Century

**Westphalian sovereignty**: a state has **final authority over internal affairs — others may not interfere** —
**R2P (Responsibility to Protect)**: when a state **massacres its own people, the international community has a duty to intervene** —

**These two principles **conflict****:
- **Pro-Westphalia**: "**Root of international law — cannot be moved**"
- **Pro-R2P**: "**Rwanda 1994 — 1 million dead — Westphalia let dictators hide behind 'sovereignty'**"

**Question**: Should the 21st century use Westphalia or R2P?
- This question keeps appearing in **Syria / Myanmar / Venezuela / North Korea**
- **No standard answer** —
- **But thinking about it lets you see the core tension of modern international politics**

### Q4. Translating the Bible into the vernacular — was this a good thing?

**Luther's 1522 German Bible** = ordinary Germans can read —
**This looks **obviously good**** —
**But the Catholic position**: ordinary people can't correctly interpret the Bible without professional training —
**Result**: **free interpretation → hundreds of Protestant sects excommunicating each other + 100+ years of war** —

**Question**: Specialization vs. democratization — which is more "true"?
- Modern parallel: **medical specialists vs. online self-diagnosis** — good or bad?
- **Legal specialists vs. self-representation** — good or bad?
- **Reformation Bible-for-the-people** — democracy or chaos?

### Q5. Does your family have a "Reformation experience"?

**This is the closest question to a kid's life**:
- Did your ancestors get **forcibly converted** (Christianity / Islam / Buddhism)?
- Did your parents **leave their hometown** because of religion or political belief?
- Do you know anyone whose faith differences **broke their family ties**?

— These are all **modern versions of the Reformation template** —
**Not necessarily religion — could be politics / values / lifestyle** —
**Pull the Reformation into a child's actual life** —
**"History isn't the past — it lives in your family's stories"**.

### Q6. 1517 Luther vs. 1517 Wang Yangming

**Two contemporary geniuses — one nailing theses in Wittenberg — one completing the unity of knowledge and action in Jiangxi** —
**Two different paths** —
**Shaping 500 years of divergent Sino-European development**.

**Question**: Is this difference:
- **Cultural determination?**
- **Individual choice?**
- **Geographic + economic determination?**

— **The other side of the Joseph Needham question** — there's no standard answer.
