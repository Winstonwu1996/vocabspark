## CN 版

### §3 主角群 — 9 个关键人物

#### 3.1 唐太宗 李世民 (599-649)

**出身**
李渊次子，关陇贵族世家，母窦氏。少年随父征战，17 岁起兵随李渊从太原出发，参与建立唐朝。

**关键事件**
- 626 年 7 月 2 日玄武门之变：李世民在长安宫城北门玄武门设伏，射杀长兄太子李建成、四弟齐王李元吉。同年 9 月 4 日继位，27 岁。
- 627-649 年贞观之治：在位 23 年，重用房玄龄、杜如晦、魏徵。魏徵直谏 200 余次，太宗多次采纳。
- 641 年文成公主入藏：嫁吐蕃赞普松赞干布，带工匠、佛经、医书，开启唐蕃通婚交流。
- 645-649 年三征高句丽：晚年战事消耗国力，未能克东北。
- 649 年崩于含风殿，51 岁，葬昭陵。

**对 Tang-Song 影响**
贞观之治成为后世帝王模板，《贞观政要》传至日本朝鲜。「水能载舟，亦能覆舟」的政治哲学被纳入儒家教科书，影响中国政治伦理 1200 年。

**21 世纪 cross-cultural anchor**
始于政变的合法性焦虑——靠后续治理实绩来重写历史评价，这种「founder violence → 长期善治」的模式，在很多国家的开国史叙事里都能看到对照。

---

#### 3.2 唐玄宗 李隆基 (685-762)

**出身**
唐睿宗第三子，太宗曾孙，武则天孙。710 年与姑姑太平公主联手发动唐隆政变诛韦后，27 岁助父登基。712 年继位，28 岁。

**关键事件**
- 712-741 年开元盛世：在位 29 年，用姚崇、宋璟、张九龄三任贤相。长安人口约 100 万，外国使节、商人、留学生云集。
- 745 年纳杨玉环为贵妃，时玄宗 60 岁，杨贵妃 26 岁。
- 752 年起用杨国忠（杨贵妃堂兄）为右相。
- 755 年 11 月 9 日安禄山在范阳（今北京）起兵，史称安史之乱。
- 756 年 6 月 18 日长安陷落，玄宗 71 岁带杨贵妃西逃蜀地。
- 756 年 7 月 13 日马嵬坡兵变，禁军将领陈玄礼率众杀杨国忠，逼玄宗缢杀杨贵妃，时年 38 岁。
- 762 年崩于太极宫，77 岁。

**对 Tang-Song 影响**
开元盛世是中国古代经济文化的双高峰之一。安史之乱是中国中古史的分水岭——人口减半，藩镇割据从此持续 150 年，唐由盛转衰只用了一个夏天。

**21 世纪 cross-cultural anchor**
盛世顶点同时埋藏崩溃种子的范式——前期 30 年治理成就被晚期 8 年用人失误几乎全部抵消，是国家治理研究里反复被引用的案例。

#### 3.3 杜甫 (712-770)

**出身**
河南巩县（今巩义）人，祖父杜审言为初唐诗人。开元年间科举不第，长期漫游齐鲁吴越。

**关键事件**
- 746 年 35 岁到长安求仕，困顿 10 年。
- 755 年安史之乱爆发，正在长安郊外，被叛军俘往长安城内。
- 757 年 4 月逃出长安投奔肃宗，授左拾遗。
- 757-759 年因直谏房琯事件被贬华州司功参军，后弃官西行。
- 759 年到成都，760 年在浣花溪畔建草堂。
- 761 年秋《茅屋为秋风所破歌》写于成都草堂——「安得广厦千万间，大庇天下寒士俱欢颜」。
- 765 年离蜀漂泊，766-768 年在夔州（今重庆奉节）写《秋兴八首》《登高》。
- 770 年冬死于湘江舟中，59 岁，贫病交加。一生写诗约 1500 首，存世 1400 余首。

**对 Tang-Song 影响**
杜甫是安史之乱最重要的诗史见证者。「国破山河在，城春草木深」（《春望》写于 757 年长安）成为后世亡国诗的母题。北宋苏轼、黄庭坚都奉杜甫为宗，定型「诗圣」称号。

**21 世纪 cross-cultural anchor**
个人命运被卷入历史大事件的写作者——颠沛流离不是文学题材而是日常生活，这种第一人称的战乱书写，跨越 1300 年仍然击中读者。

#### 3.4 王安石 (1021-1086)

**出身**
江西临川人，进士出身。1042 年 21 岁中进士第四名，任地方官 16 年，以基层经验闻名。

**关键事件**
- 1058 年 37 岁上《万言书》给仁宗，提出系统改革主张，未被采纳。
- 1069 年 48 岁被宋神宗任命参知政事，开始变法。
- 1069-1076 年新法陆续推出：青苗法（春放秋收，年息 20-30%）、市易法（官府收购囤积货物平抑物价）、保甲法（农村军事化保甲互保）、募役法（以钱代役）、方田均税法（重新丈量土地）。
- 1074 年因旱灾和反对派攻击第一次罢相。
- 1075-1076 年再度拜相，1076 年因长子王雱去世彻底辞官，回江宁（今南京）。
- 1085 年神宗崩，哲宗继位，太皇太后高氏听政，司马光为相，新法基本被废。
- 1086 年 5 月王安石死，65 岁。同年 9 月司马光也死。

**对 Tang-Song 影响**
新旧党争从此撕裂北宋政坛 60 年——元祐党争（1086-1094）、绍圣党争（1094-1100）、靖康前夜的蔡京党争。变法本身成败争议至今未息：钱穆视之为亡国根源，邓广铭视之为先进改革。

**21 世纪 cross-cultural anchor**
顶层设计型改革者的两难——理论自洽但执行扭曲，在地方层面催生新型官僚寻租，是经济政策史里反复出现的结构性问题。

#### 3.5 苏轼 (1037-1101) — lens 2 候选

**出身**
四川眉山人，苏洵长子，苏辙之兄。1057 年 21 岁中进士，主考官欧阳修惊为天人。父子三人「三苏」皆名列唐宋八大家。

**关键事件**
- 1071 年因反对王安石新法自请外放，任杭州、密州、徐州、湖州地方官。
- 1079 年 7 月「乌台诗案」，因诗中讥讽新法被捕入御史台狱 130 天，险被处死。
- 1080 年贬黄州（今湖北黄冈）任团练副使，无实权。在黄州 4 年写《赤壁赋》《后赤壁赋》《念奴娇·赤壁怀古》，自号东坡居士。
- 1086 年元祐党争，旧党司马光得势，召苏轼回京任翰林学士。但苏轼又反对全废新法，再度被排挤。
- 1094 年新党再起，蔡京等人重列「元祐党人碑」，苏轼贬惠州（今广东惠州），59 岁。
- 1097 年再贬儋州（今海南儋州），62 岁。儋州当时是流放最远之地，瘴疠之乡。
- 1100 年徽宗即位大赦北还。
- 1101 年 7 月 28 日死于常州，66 岁。

**对 Tang-Song 影响**
三度流放成就北宋文化最高峰——黄州 4 年、惠州 3 年、儋州 3 年。诗、词、文、书、画、美食（东坡肉）、园林皆开宗立派。被誉为「中国 11 世纪文艺复兴的化身」。

**21 世纪 cross-cultural anchor**
政治失意和文化创造的悖论——三度流放反而催生最高产的创作期，跨文化的「困境美学」原型，从但丁流放到曼德尔施塔姆都能找到回声。

#### 3.6 朱熹 (1130-1200)

**出身**
徽州婺源（今属江西）人，南宋初期生于福建尤溪。父朱松为太学博士。19 岁中进士。

**关键事件**
- 1148-1163 年师从李侗，承二程（程颢、程颐）洛学。
- 1175 年与陆九渊在江西鹅湖寺辩论，「鹅湖之会」开理学心学分野。
- 1190 年集注《四书章句集注》——《大学》《中庸》《论语》《孟子》——为元明清三代科举标准教材。
- 1196 年「庆元党禁」，韩侂胄打击道学，朱熹被诬「伪学」，门生李闳祖等被流放。
- 1200 年 4 月死于建阳考亭，70 岁。
- 1209 年朝廷为朱熹平反追谥「文」。1241 年从祀孔庙。

**对 Tang-Song 影响**
程朱理学经元代统治者采纳后成为官方意识形态，影响中国 700 年（1313 元仁宗指定《四书》为科举范本到 1905 年废科举）。东亚文化圈：朝鲜李朝（1392-1910）、日本江户幕府（1603-1868）、越南后黎朝（1428-1789）皆奉朱子学为官学。

**21 世纪 cross-cultural anchor**
注释家的影响力——朱熹本人没有写过新书，但通过重新注释 4 部古典文本，重塑了东亚 700 年的思想框架，是「解释权决定影响力」的典型案例。

#### 3.7 宋徽宗 赵佶 (1082-1135) — lens 1 候选

**出身**
神宗第十一子，哲宗异母弟。1100 年哲宗 25 岁无嗣崩，徽宗以端王身份继位，时年 19 岁。

**关键事件**
- 1100-1126 年在位 26 年。重用蔡京、童贯、高俅。
- 创「瘦金体」书法，存世真迹《草书千字文》《秾芳诗帖》。
- 1112 年作《瑞鹤图》：画 20 只仙鹤盘旋宣德门顶，自题瘦金体记。
- 约 1110 年代《听琴图》：自画抚琴，蔡京题诗——皇帝与权臣的合像。
- 主持编《宣和书谱》《宣和画谱》，皇家画院（翰林图画院）培养画家如王希孟（《千里江山图》作者，18 岁完成此卷）。
- 1118-1125 年与金联合灭辽（海上之盟），辽 1125 年亡。
- 1126 年 1 月，金军南下围开封，徽宗禅位长子钦宗赵桓，自号太上皇。
- 1127 年 1 月（靖康元年闰十一月）开封陷，徽宗、钦宗、后妃、宗室、工匠约 3000 人被俘北上。
- 1135 年 6 月死于五国城（今黑龙江依兰），54 岁。

**对 Tang-Song 影响**
中国艺术史最高峰皇帝之一，但同时是亡国之君。「靖康之耻」成为后世士大夫的精神创伤——岳飞《满江红》「靖康耻，犹未雪」即指此。北宋亡，南宋偏安 152 年。

**21 世纪 cross-cultural anchor**
艺术天才与政治灾难同体——艺术成就和军事崩溃并存的悖论，让后人难以做单一道德评价，类似法国路易十六（钟表匠+断头台）和俄国尼古拉二世（家庭画家+革命受害者）。

#### 3.8 李清照 (1084-1155) — lens 3 候选

**出身**
山东济南人，父李格非为苏轼门生「苏门后四学士」之一，母王氏为状元王拱辰孙女。书香门第。

**关键事件**
- 1101 年 18 岁嫁太学生赵明诚 21 岁，赵父赵挺之时任尚书右丞。新婚共同搜集金石书画，开始撰写《金石录》（赵明诚主笔，李清照协助）。
- 1107 年赵挺之死，蔡京迫害，赵明诚夫妇返青州（今山东青州）老家，闲居 10 年。这 10 年是李清照创作高峰，词作如《醉花阴·薄雾浓云愁永昼》。
- 1117 年起赵明诚陆续出仕莱州、淄州知州。
- 1126-1127 年靖康之变，开封陷。李清照 43 岁、赵明诚 46 岁，开始大规模南运文物。
- 1128 年到江宁（今南京），文物存于金陵。
- 1129 年 8 月赵明诚赴湖州任途中染疫病死建康，49 岁。李清照 46 岁。
- 1129-1132 年南奔流离，文物大半散失或被盗。
- 1132 年 5 月在杭州 49 岁再婚张汝舟。婚后发现张觊觎文物且有诈骗罪，李清照 100 天内离婚——按宋律妻告夫即使胜诉也要坐牢两年。她坐牢 9 天后被赦。
- 1134 年完成《金石录后序》，记录与赵明诚的金石收藏始末，成千古名文。
- 约 1155 年死于杭州，71 岁。

**对 Tang-Song 影响**
中国古典词坛公认第一女词人，「易安体」开创婉约派新境。《金石录》是中国金石学奠基著作之一。她的人生轨迹——从靖康前的金石收藏家到靖康后的颠沛流离——是北宋南宋之际士大夫家庭命运的缩影。

**21 世纪 cross-cultural anchor**
战乱中文物保护者的个人代价——文化承担和身体流离同时发生，跨越性别和时代，让人想到二战期间的犹太收藏家或叙利亚博物馆员。

#### 3.9 文天祥 (1236-1283)

**出身**
江西吉州（今吉安）人。1256 年 20 岁中进士第一名（状元），殿试时理宗亲擢。

**关键事件**
- 1259-1275 年任地方官，多次因抨击权臣贾似道被罢官。
- 1275 年元军南下，文天祥 39 岁散尽家财在赣州募兵勤王。
- 1276 年 1 月被任右丞相赴元营谈判被扣留，2 月临安陷恭帝降。
- 1276 年 3 月文天祥从镇江脱险，辗转福建、广东组织抗元。
- 1278 年 12 月 20 日在五坡岭（今广东海丰）被元军张弘范俘获。文天祥服冰片自杀未遂。
- 1279 年 3 月被押至崖山海战现场，目睹宋军覆灭，写《过零丁洋》「人生自古谁无死，留取丹心照汗青」。
- 1279-1283 年囚于大都（今北京）兵马司土牢 4 年，元世祖忽必烈亲自劝降许以丞相，文天祥拒。
- 1281 年在狱中作《正气歌》「天地有正气，杂然赋流形」共 60 句。
- 1283 年 1 月 9 日（至元 19 年腊月初九）斩于柴市，47 岁。

**对 Tang-Song 影响**
文天祥与陆秀夫、张世杰并称「宋末三杰」。《正气歌》《过零丁洋》成为中国忠义气节的精神原型。明清之际、抗战时期都被反复援引。

**21 世纪 cross-cultural anchor**
个人对集体命运的最后承担——明知不可为而为之，结局已定但仍按内在原则行动，是道德哲学里「义务论 vs 后果论」的经典实例。

### §4 4 关键时刻详写

#### 4.1 626 年 7 月 2 日 玄武门之变 + 627-649 贞观之治

**当天细节**

626 年 7 月 2 日清晨（武德九年六月初四），唐高祖李渊三个儿子的内斗到达顶点。太子李建成 38 岁，秦王李世民 27 岁，齐王李元吉 24 岁。李世民事先已被告知建成、元吉计划在出征突厥的饯行宴上加害自己。

按《资治通鉴》卷 191 记载：李世民率长孙无忌、尉迟敬德、房玄龄、杜如晦、侯君集等 10 余人，在长安宫城北门玄武门内伏兵。建成、元吉骑马入朝至临湖殿，察觉变化欲返。世民呼，元吉张弓三发不中（因紧张未拉满弦），世民射建成毙之。元吉逃，被尉迟敬德追射坠马杀之。

随后尉迟敬德全副甲胄入太极宫，见高祖李渊于船上湖中：「秦王以太子、齐王作乱，举兵诛之。」高祖被迫下诏。

**贞观之治 23 年**

626 年 9 月 4 日李世民继位，年号贞观。次年起开始的 23 年治理（627-649）成为后世帝王模板：
- **房玄龄**（579-648）：宰相 22 年，以「精于吏治」闻名。
- **杜如晦**（585-630）：与房玄龄并称「房谋杜断」，可惜 46 岁早逝。
- **魏徵**（580-643）：原为太子建成幕僚，玄武门后被太宗收用。在位 17 年直谏 200 余次。最著名一次：643 年魏徵死，太宗叹「以铜为镜可正衣冠，以人为镜可知得失，今魏徵殁，朕亡一镜矣」。
- **641 年文成公主入藏**：嫁吐蕃赞普松赞干布，带工匠、医书、佛经、释迦牟尼 12 岁等身像（即后世大昭寺主供）。开启唐蕃 200 年通婚。
- **贞观律**：650 年由房玄龄等编定的《永徽律疏》（即《唐律疏议》）的前身，影响日本《大宝律令》（701）、朝鲜半岛新罗律令、越南李朝律令。

**「水能载舟亦能覆舟」**

太宗与魏徵对话见于《贞观政要》卷一：「君者舟也，人者水也。水能载舟，亦能覆舟。」此句虽出自《荀子·王制》，但因贞观君臣的政治实践被赋予新生命，成为儒家政治哲学的核心命题之一，影响中国政治伦理 1200 年。

**careful framing**

玄武门之变的伦理评价历来分歧：传统史学倾向于太宗「不得已」叙事，宋代司马光《资治通鉴》已开始批评太宗「篡改实录」。现代史学大多承认两点同时为真——政变的暴力是真实的，贞观之治的成就也是真实的。这两者之间的张力，本身就是制度史研究的重要对象。

#### 4.2 755-763 安史之乱

**755 年 11 月 9 日 范阳起兵**

755 年 11 月 9 日（天宝十四年十一月初九），范阳节度使安禄山在范阳（今北京一带）起兵反唐。安禄山时年约 52 岁，粟特血统，身兼范阳、平卢、河东三镇节度使，掌兵 15 万。起兵借口是「奉密诏讨杨国忠」。

玄宗时年 71 岁，得报后初不信。叛军 12 月攻陷洛阳，756 年 1 月安禄山在洛阳称帝，国号大燕。

**756 年 6 月 18 日 长安陷落 + 7 月 13 日 马嵬坡**

756 年 6 月 9 日潼关失守，守将哥舒翰被俘。6 月 13 日清晨玄宗仓促西逃，带杨贵妃、杨国忠、皇子皇孙、宫人 1000 余人。6 月 18 日叛军入长安。

7 月 13 日（六月十四日）至马嵬驿（今陕西兴平），禁军将领陈玄礼鼓动士兵哗变。先杀杨国忠及其子杨暄，再围驿站要求处死杨贵妃。高力士从内劝玄宗：「贵妃诚无罪，然将士已杀国忠，而贵妃在陛下左右，岂敢自安？」玄宗下令缢杀杨贵妃于驿前佛堂梨树下，时年 38 岁。

玄宗继续西行入蜀，太子李亨北上灵武。7 月 12 日（与马嵬坡几乎同时）李亨在灵武即位，是为肃宗，玄宗被尊太上皇。

**757 年 长安收复 + 763 年 平定**

757 年 9 月唐军在郭子仪、李光弼率领下，借回纥兵收复长安。10 月收复洛阳。

但叛军未灭。757 年正月安庆绪杀父安禄山，759 年史思明又杀安庆绪，761 年史朝义又杀史思明。直到 763 年 1 月史朝义在莫州（今河北任丘）自杀，安史之乱才告结束，前后 7 年 2 个月。

**人口减半的数字**

天宝十四年（755）唐朝在籍户数 891 万，约 5290 万人（一说 8000 万含未登记人口）。
广德二年（764）在籍户数 293 万，约 1690 万人（一说 2000 万）。

减半数字包含战死、瘟疫、流亡未户籍化、北方士族大规模南迁等多重因素。这是中国中古史最严重的人口断崖之一。

**杜甫《春望》同代见证**

757 年 3 月，杜甫困居长安城内（叛军占领期），写下《春望》：「国破山河在，城春草木深。」此诗成为后世亡国诗的母题。

**careful framing**

安史之乱是唐由盛转衰的瞬间，但「转衰」是结构性的而非戏剧性的——藩镇割据从此持续 150 年（直到 907 年朱温灭唐），中央集权在唐后半期实际上从未真正恢复。开元盛世和安史之乱之间，相隔不到 15 年。

---

#### 4.3 1126-1127 靖康之变

**1125 年 金灭辽 + 海上之盟的反噬**

1118 年起，宋徽宗、童贯主导「海上之盟」联金灭辽。1125 年 2 月金军俘辽天祚帝，辽亡。但金军发现宋军在收复燕云十六州过程中表现极弱，遂转兵南下。

**1126 年 1 月 第一次开封之围**

1125 年 12 月金军兵分两路南下：东路完颜宗望、西路完颜宗翰。1126 年 1 月东路金军逼近开封。徽宗惊惶，1 月 18 日（宣和七年十二月二十三）禅位长子赵桓（钦宗），时钦宗 25 岁。徽宗带亲信近臣南逃镇江。

1126 年 1 月底金军围开封，李纲组织防御。2 月金军要求割地、岁币、人质，宋朝同意。3 月金军撤围。

**1126 年 11 月 第二次开封之围 + 1127 年 1 月 城陷**

1126 年 8 月金军再次南下，11 月围开封。这次开封守备不足。

1127 年 1 月 9 日（靖康元年闰十一月二十五）开封外城陷。钦宗赴金营谈判被扣。
1127 年 2 月 6 日（靖康二年正月十日）金废徽宗、钦宗为庶人。
1127 年 4 月 20 日（靖康二年三月二十七）金军押徽宗、钦宗、后妃、宗室、工匠、技艺人约 3000 人北上，包括徽宗 9 子、钦宗皇后朱氏、徽宗皇后郑氏。北宋 167 年（960-1127）亡。

**1127 年 5 月 高宗南逃 + 南宋建立**

1127 年 5 月 1 日（靖康二年五月初一）徽宗第九子康王赵构在应天府（今河南商丘）即位，时年 21 岁，是为高宗，年号建炎。

1127-1138 年高宗辗转扬州、临安（今杭州）、越州、明州（今宁波）、海上、临安，被金军追击。
1129 年「苗刘兵变」，护卫将领苗傅、刘正彦在杭州逼高宗禅位 3 岁太子，月余被韩世忠等平定。
1138 年定都临安。
1141 年 11 月「绍兴和议」，宋向金称臣纳贡，年贡银 25 万两、绢 25 万匹，划淮河-大散关为界。
1142 年 1 月岳飞被赐死风波亭，39 岁。
1135 年 6 月徽宗死于五国城（今黑龙江依兰），54 岁。1156 年 6 月钦宗死于金，57 岁。

**careful framing**

靖康之变的悖论：北宋是中国古代经济文化最繁荣的朝代之一（1100 年开封人口 100 万，年财政收入约 1.6 亿贯），却在军事上几乎没有抵抗就崩溃。这种「文明顶点 + 武力崩溃」的同时性，是宋史研究的核心命题。岳飞《满江红》「靖康耻，犹未雪」一句的情感力量，源自对这种文明断裂的反复回望。

---

#### 4.4 1276-1279 宋元之交

**1276 年 2 月 临安降**

1271 年忽必烈建国号大元。1273 年元军破襄阳。1275 年元军主帅伯颜大举南下。

1276 年 2 月 4 日（德祐二年正月十八）元军兵临临安。谢太后（理宗皇后）以 5 岁恭帝赵显的名义递交降表。临安陷，南宋实质亡国（仅福州、广东、崖山等地小朝廷继续抗元 3 年）。

**1278 年 12 月 文天祥被俘 + 1283 年 1 月 大都殉国**

1276 年 5 月益王赵昰在福州即位，是为端宗，时 7 岁。文天祥任右丞相奉端宗辗转福建、江西、广东抗元。

1278 年 5 月端宗病死，弟赵昺 7 岁继位。

1278 年 12 月 20 日文天祥在五坡岭（今广东海丰）被张弘范元军俘获。文天祥服冰片自杀未遂。

1279 年 3 月文天祥被押至崖山海战现场，目睹宋军覆灭，作《过零丁洋》：
「辛苦遭逢起一经，干戈寥落四周星。」「人生自古谁无死，留取丹心照汗青。」

1279 年文天祥被押至大都（今北京），囚于兵马司土牢 4 年。元世祖忽必烈亲自劝降，许以宰相位。文天祥答：「天祥受宋恩为宰相，安事二姓？」

1281 年在狱中作《正气歌》：「天地有正气，杂然赋流形。」共 60 句。

1283 年 1 月 9 日（至元十九年腊月初九）文天祥被斩于大都柴市口，47 岁。临刑面南而拜：「吾事毕矣。」

**1279 年 3 月 19 日 崖山海战**

1279 年 3 月 19 日（祥兴二年二月初六）崖山海战结束。此前一个月宋元水师对峙于今广东江门崖门一带，宋军主帅张世杰、陆秀夫，宋元宋军船只 1000 余艘，元军主帅张弘范率 500 余艘进攻。

3 月 19 日清晨元军总攻，宋军大败。陆秀夫见大势已去，先驱妻子入海，再背 8 岁少帝赵昺投海。其后宋军及随从军民跳海者据《宋史·陆秀夫传》记载约 10 余万人，「越七日，浮尸出于海十余万人」。

南宋 152 年（1127-1279）亡。中国第一次完全被北方游牧民族征服。

**「崖山之后无中华」的争议**

「崖山之后无中华」一语近年在网络流传，但学界对此命题有较大争议：
- 此说被认为始于明末清初遗民语境，但清代实际文献中「中华」概念依然延续。
- 1942 年钱穆在西南联大讲史时明确反对此种本质化命题：「中华文明从未中断」。
- 1962 年美籍华裔学者刘子健（James T.C. Liu）在 *China Turning Inward* 中提出南宋是中国「内向化」转折点，但未支持「中华灭亡」说。
- 21 世纪学界主流不接受这种本质化命题——朝代更替不等于文明终结，元代继承宋代财政、官制、儒学传统，明代继续制度化朱子学。

**careful framing**

崖山之战的悲剧性是真实的——8 岁皇帝投海、10 万军民殉国都是史实。但「崖山之后无中华」是后世建构的政治叙事，需要与具体史实区分。元代之后中国并未消失，但宋代那种以士大夫为主体的政治文化形态确实发生了深刻转变，这是宋元之际研究的核心命题之一。

### §3 Key Figures — 9 Protagonists

#### 3.1 Tang Taizong (Li Shimin, 599-649)

**Origin**
Second son of Li Yuan, from the aristocratic Guanlong clan. Joined his father's military campaigns at 17 when Li Yuan rebelled against Sui from Taiyuan and helped found the Tang dynasty.

**Key events**
- July 2, 626 Xuanwu Gate Incident: Li Shimin ambushed and killed his elder brother Crown Prince Li Jiancheng and younger brother Li Yuanji at the northern gate of Chang'an's palace. Took the throne September 4 at age 27.
- 627-649 Zhenguan era: 23-year reign with chief ministers Fang Xuanling, Du Ruhui, and the famous remonstrating official Wei Zheng (who memorialized over 200 direct criticisms of the emperor).
- 641 Princess Wencheng's marriage to Tibetan king Songtsen Gampo, bringing artisans, Buddhist scriptures, and medical texts.
- 645-649 Three failed campaigns against Goguryeo drained the treasury in his late years.
- Died 649 at age 51, buried at Zhao Mausoleum.

**Impact on Tang-Song**
The Zhenguan era became the template for later emperors. *Zhenguan Zhengyao* (Essentials of Zhenguan Governance) was transmitted to Japan and Korea. The political philosophy "water can carry the boat or capsize it" entered Confucian textbooks and shaped Chinese political ethics for 1,200 years.

**21st century cross-cultural anchor**
A founder whose legitimacy began with violence but was rewritten by sustained good governance — this "founder violence to long-term competent rule" pattern recurs across many national founding narratives.

---

#### 3.2 Tang Xuanzong (Li Longji, 685-762)

**Origin**
Third son of Tang Ruizong, great-grandson of Taizong, grandson of Wu Zetian. In 710 at age 27, he and his aunt Princess Taiping launched the Tanglong coup against Empress Wei. Took the throne in 712 at age 28.

**Key events**
- 712-741 Kaiyuan era: 29-year reign with three successive worthy ministers (Yao Chong, Song Jing, Zhang Jiuling). Chang'an's population reached approximately one million; foreign envoys, merchants, and students gathered there.
- 745 took Yang Yuhuan as imperial consort (Yang Guifei). Xuanzong was 60, Yang Guifei 26.
- 752 promoted Yang Guozhong (Yang Guifei's cousin) as right chancellor.
- November 9, 755: An Lushan rebelled at Fanyang (today's Beijing area), launching the An-Shi Rebellion.
- June 18, 756: Chang'an fell. Xuanzong, age 71, fled west to Sichuan with Yang Guifei.
- July 13, 756: Mawei Slope mutiny. Imperial guards killed Yang Guozhong and forced Xuanzong to order Yang Guifei strangled in front of a Buddhist altar; she was 38.
- Died 762 at age 77.

**Impact on Tang-Song**
The Kaiyuan era stands among ancient China's economic and cultural peaks. The An-Shi Rebellion was the watershed of medieval Chinese history — population halved, regional military governors gained autonomy that would last 150 years. The Tang shifted from peak to decline in a single summer.

**21st century cross-cultural anchor**
The paradigm of a peak that simultaneously seeds collapse — 30 years of governance achievement nearly canceled by 8 years of personnel failures, a case repeatedly cited in governance studies.

---

#### 3.3 Du Fu (712-770)

**Origin**
Born in Gongxian, Henan (today's Gongyi). His grandfather Du Shenyan was an early Tang poet. Failed the imperial examinations during the Kaiyuan era and spent years wandering through Qi, Lu, Wu, and Yue.

**Key events**
- 746: Arrived in Chang'an at age 35 seeking office; struggled there for 10 years.
- 755: Caught in the suburbs of Chang'an when the An-Shi Rebellion erupted; captured and brought into the city by rebels.
- April 757: Escaped Chang'an, joined Emperor Suzong, appointed left reminder.
- 757-759: Demoted after defending the disgraced Fang Guan; later resigned and traveled west.
- 759: Reached Chengdu. 760: Built a thatched cottage by Huanhua Stream.
- Autumn 761: Wrote "Song of My Cottage Wrecked by Autumn Wind" at the Chengdu cottage.
- 765: Left Sichuan. 766-768 in Kuizhou (today's Fengjie, Chongqing) wrote "Eight Autumn Meditations" and "Climbing High."
- Winter 770: Died on a boat on the Xiang River, age 59, in poverty and illness. Wrote about 1,500 poems in his lifetime, with 1,400+ surviving.

**Impact on Tang-Song**
Du Fu was the most important poetic witness to the An-Shi Rebellion. His "Spring View" (written in occupied Chang'an in 757) — "The state is broken, mountains and rivers remain; spring comes to the city, grass and trees grow deep" — became the archetype for later poems on national fall. Northern Song masters Su Shi and Huang Tingjian both treated Du Fu as patriarch, cementing his title "Sage of Poetry."

**21st century cross-cultural anchor**
A writer whose personal fate became entangled with major historical events — displacement was not literary subject matter but daily life. This first-person witness writing of war still strikes readers across 1,300 years.

---

#### 3.4 Wang Anshi (1021-1086)

**Origin**
From Linchuan, Jiangxi. Passed the *jinshi* examination in 1042 at age 21, ranking fourth. Served as a local official for 16 years, becoming known for grassroots experience.

**Key events**
- 1058: At age 37 submitted his "Ten Thousand Word Memorial" to Emperor Renzong proposing systematic reform; not adopted.
- 1069: At age 48 appointed by Emperor Shenzong as participant in deliberating affairs of state, beginning the New Policies.
- 1069-1076: New Policies rolled out, including the Green Sprouts Law (state loans to peasants at 20-30% annual interest), the State Trade Law (government purchase and resale to stabilize prices), the Baojia Law (rural mutual-responsibility militia organization), the Hired Service Law (commutation of corvée to cash), and the Square Field Equal Tax Law (land resurveying).
- 1074: First dismissed as chancellor due to drought and opposition attacks.
- 1075-1076: Restored to chancellorship; resigned permanently in 1076 after his eldest son Wang Pang died, retiring to Jiangning (today's Nanjing).
- 1085: Emperor Shenzong died. Emperor Zhezong succeeded; Grand Empress Dowager Gao governed; Sima Guang became chancellor; the New Policies were largely abolished.
- May 1086: Wang Anshi died at 65. Sima Guang died that September.

**Impact on Tang-Song**
The factional struggle between New and Old parties tore Northern Song politics for 60 years — the Yuanyou faction war (1086-1094), the Shaosheng faction war (1094-1100), and the Cai Jing faction politics on the eve of Jingkang. The reform's success or failure remains debated: Qian Mu saw it as the root cause of dynastic collapse, while Deng Guangming saw it as progressive reform.

**21st century cross-cultural anchor**
The dilemma of top-down reformers — theoretically coherent but distorted in execution, generating new bureaucratic rent-seeking at the local level. A recurring structural problem in economic policy history.

---

#### 3.5 Su Shi (1037-1101) — lens 2 candidate

**Origin**
From Meishan, Sichuan. Eldest son of Su Xun, elder brother of Su Zhe. Passed the *jinshi* in 1057 at age 21; chief examiner Ouyang Xiu was astonished by his talent. The "Three Sus" all numbered among the Eight Masters of Tang-Song prose.

**Key events**
- 1071: Requested provincial assignment in opposition to Wang Anshi's New Policies; served as local official in Hangzhou, Mizhou, Xuzhou, and Huzhou.
- July 1079: "Crow Terrace Poetry Case" — arrested for poems read as satirical criticism of the New Policies; held 130 days in the Censorate prison; nearly executed.
- 1080: Demoted to Huangzhou (today's Huanggang, Hubei) as deputy military training commissioner without real authority. During his four years in Huangzhou wrote "Red Cliff Rhapsody," "Latter Red Cliff Rhapsody," and "Charm of Niannu: Recalling the Past at the Red Cliff." Adopted the name Layman of Eastern Slope (*Dongpo*).
- 1086: Yuanyou faction war. Old Party's Sima Guang in power; Su Shi recalled as Hanlin academician. But Su Shi opposed wholesale abolition of the New Policies and was sidelined again.
- 1094: New Party returned. Cai Jing and others compiled the "Yuanyou Faction Stele." Su Shi demoted to Huizhou (today's Huizhou, Guangdong) at 59.
- 1097: Demoted again to Danzhou (today's Danzhou, Hainan) at 62. Danzhou was then the most distant exile, a malarial frontier.
- 1100: Emperor Huizong's amnesty allowed return north.
- July 28, 1101: Died in Changzhou at 66.

**Impact on Tang-Song**
Three exiles produced Northern Song's cultural peak — four years in Huangzhou, three in Huizhou, three in Danzhou. He founded schools in poetry, lyric verse, prose, calligraphy, painting, cuisine (*Dongpo* pork), and garden design. Often called "the embodiment of an 11th-century Chinese renaissance."

**21st century cross-cultural anchor**
The paradox of political failure and cultural creation — three exiles instead generated the most productive creative period. A cross-cultural archetype of "aesthetics of adversity" with echoes from Dante's exile to Mandelstam.

---

#### 3.6 Zhu Xi (1130-1200)

**Origin**
From Wuyuan, Huizhou (today in Jiangxi), born in Youxi, Fujian early in the Southern Song. Father Zhu Song was an academician at the imperial university. Passed the *jinshi* at 19.

**Key events**
- 1148-1163: Studied under Li Tong, inheriting the Luo school of the Cheng brothers (Cheng Hao, Cheng Yi).
- 1175: Debated Lu Jiuyuan at Goose Lake Temple in Jiangxi. The "Goose Lake Meeting" marked the divide between the school of principle (*li*) and the school of mind (*xin*).
- 1190: Compiled *Collected Commentaries on the Four Books* — *Great Learning*, *Doctrine of the Mean*, *Analects*, *Mencius* — which became the standard examination text for the Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties.
- 1196: "Qingyuan ban on the Way." Han Tuozhou suppressed the Learning of the Way; Zhu Xi was accused of "false learning"; disciples like Li Hongzu were exiled.
- April 1200: Died at Kaoting, Jianyang, age 70.
- 1209: Court rehabilitated Zhu Xi with the posthumous title "Wen." 1241: Enshrined in the Confucian temple.

**Impact on Tang-Song**
Cheng-Zhu Neo-Confucianism, adopted by the Yuan rulers, became official ideology and shaped China for 700 years (from Yuan Renzong's 1313 designation of the *Four Books* as examination canon to the abolition of the imperial examinations in 1905). Across the East Asian cultural sphere: Joseon Korea (1392-1910), Edo-period Japan (1603-1868), and Later Le Vietnam (1428-1789) all treated Zhu Xi's learning as state orthodoxy.

**21st century cross-cultural anchor**
The reach of a commentator — Zhu Xi himself wrote no entirely new book, yet by re-annotating four classical texts he reshaped East Asia's intellectual framework for 700 years. A textbook case of "interpretive authority equals influence."

---

#### 3.7 Song Huizong (Zhao Ji, 1082-1135) — lens 1 candidate

**Origin**
Eleventh son of Emperor Shenzong, half-brother of Emperor Zhezong. In 1100 when the 25-year-old Zhezong died without an heir, Huizong, then known as Prince Duan, succeeded at age 19.

**Key events**
- 1100-1126: Reigned 26 years. Relied on Cai Jing, Tong Guan, and Gao Qiu.
- Created the "slender gold" (*shoujin*) calligraphic style; surviving works include *Cursive Thousand Character Classic* and *Nongfang Poem*.
- 1112: Painted *Auspicious Cranes* — twenty cranes circling the Xuande Gate, with a self-inscription in slender gold.
- Around 1110s: *Listening to the Qin* — a self-portrait playing the *qin*, with an inscription by Cai Jing — emperor and chief minister combined in one image.
- Sponsored the compilation of *Xuanhe Calligraphy Catalog* and *Xuanhe Painting Catalog*. The imperial painting academy (Hanlin Painting Academy) trained painters such as Wang Ximeng (creator of *A Thousand Li of Rivers and Mountains*, completed at age 18).
- 1118-1125: Allied with the Jin to destroy the Liao (Maritime Alliance). The Liao fell in 1125.
- January 1126: Jin armies pushed south and besieged Kaifeng. Huizong abdicated to his eldest son Emperor Qinzong (Zhao Huan) and titled himself Retired Emperor.
- January 1127: Kaifeng fell. Huizong, Qinzong, consorts, imperial clan, and craftsmen — about 3,000 people — were taken north as captives.
- June 1135: Died at Wuguocheng (today's Yilan, Heilongjiang) at age 54.

**Impact on Tang-Song**
One of the highest peaks in Chinese art history was reached under an emperor who also lost his dynasty. The "Humiliation of Jingkang" became a lasting trauma for the literati class — Yue Fei's "Manjianghong" line "the Jingkang shame is not yet washed clean" refers to this event. Northern Song fell, and Southern Song held only the south for 152 years.

**21st century cross-cultural anchor**
Artistic genius and political catastrophe in one body — the coexistence of artistic peak and military collapse makes simple moral judgment difficult. Comparable to France's Louis XVI (clockmaker on the way to the guillotine) or Russia's Nicholas II (family painter and revolutionary victim).

---

#### 3.8 Li Qingzhao (1084-1155) — lens 3 candidate

**Origin**
From Jinan, Shandong. Father Li Gefei was a disciple of Su Shi and one of the "Four Late Disciples of Su." Mother née Wang was the granddaughter of *zhuangyuan* Wang Gongchen. A scholarly household.

**Key events**
- 1101: Married the imperial university student Zhao Mingcheng at age 18; he was 21. Zhao's father Zhao Tingzhi was then right vice-minister. The newly married couple began collecting epigraphy and paintings together and started compiling *Records of Inscriptions on Bronze and Stone* (Zhao as principal author, Li Qingzhao assisting).
- 1107: Zhao Tingzhi died. Cai Jing's persecution drove the couple back to their ancestral home in Qingzhou (today's Qingzhou, Shandong) for ten years of retirement. These ten years were Li Qingzhao's creative peak, including the lyric "Drunk in the Shade of Flowers."
- From 1117: Zhao Mingcheng successively served as prefect of Laizhou and Zizhou.
- 1126-1127 Jingkang Incident: Kaifeng fell. Li Qingzhao (43) and Zhao Mingcheng (46) began the large-scale southward transport of their collection.
- 1128: Reached Jiangning (today's Nanjing); the collection was stored there.
- August 1129: Zhao Mingcheng died of illness in Jiankang at 49 while traveling to take up a Huzhou post. Li Qingzhao was 46.
- 1129-1132: Fleeing south through the chaos, most of the collection was lost or stolen.
- May 1132: At age 49 in Hangzhou, remarried Zhang Ruzhou. Soon discovered Zhang coveted the remaining collection and had a fraud record. Within 100 days she sued for divorce — under Song law, even a wife's successful suit against her husband meant two years' imprisonment. She was jailed for 9 days before pardon.
- 1134: Completed the *Postface to Records of Inscriptions on Bronze and Stone*, recording her life of collecting with Zhao Mingcheng — one of the great essays in Chinese literature.
- Around 1155: Died in Hangzhou at 71.

**Impact on Tang-Song**
Considered the foremost female lyricist in classical Chinese literature; her "Yi'an style" opened a new dimension within the *wanyue* (delicate-restrained) school. *Records of Inscriptions on Bronze and Stone* is a foundational text in Chinese epigraphic studies. Her trajectory — from pre-Jingkang collector to post-Jingkang refugee — epitomizes the fate of literati households at the Northern-to-Southern Song transition.

**21st century cross-cultural anchor**
The personal cost of cultural preservation in wartime — cultural responsibility and physical displacement happening at once. Across gender and era, evokes Jewish collectors in WWII or museum staff in Syria.

#### 3.9 Wen Tianxiang (1236-1283)

**Origin**
From Jizhou, Jiangxi (today's Ji'an). Took first place (*zhuangyuan*) in the *jinshi* examination in 1256 at age 20; Emperor Lizong personally elevated him during the palace examination.

**Key events**
- 1259-1275: Served as local official; repeatedly dismissed for criticizing chief minister Jia Sidao.
- 1275: Mongol forces pushed south. Wen Tianxiang at 39 sold his family property in Ganzhou to raise troops in defense of the throne.
- January 1276: Appointed right chancellor; sent to negotiate at the Mongol camp and was detained. February: Lin'an fell, the boy emperor Gongdi surrendered.
- March 1276: Wen Tianxiang escaped from Zhenjiang and made his way through Fujian and Guangdong organizing resistance.
- December 20, 1278: Captured at Wupoling (today's Haifeng, Guangdong) by Zhang Hongfan's Mongol forces. Wen Tianxiang attempted suicide by ingesting bingpian (borneol) but failed.
- March 1279: Brought to the site of the Yamen sea battle; witnessed the destruction of the Song fleet. Wrote "Crossing Lonely Ocean": "Since ancient times who has not died? Let me leave a loyal heart to shine in the historical record."
- 1279-1283: Imprisoned for four years in the Bingmasi earth dungeon in Dadu (today's Beijing). Khubilai Khan personally tried to persuade him to surrender and offered the chancellorship; Wen refused.
- 1281: Wrote "Song of Righteous Spirit" in prison: "Heaven and earth have righteous spirit, mingled in all forms." Sixty lines total.
- January 9, 1283: Beheaded at Chaishi market in Dadu at age 47. Before execution he bowed south and said, "My task is done."

**Impact on Tang-Song**
Wen Tianxiang, Lu Xiufu, and Zhang Shijie are remembered together as the "Three Heroes of the Song's End." "Song of Righteous Spirit" and "Crossing Lonely Ocean" became prototypes for Chinese ideals of loyalty and integrity, repeatedly invoked at the Ming-Qing transition and during the Anti-Japanese War.

**21st century cross-cultural anchor**
An individual's final commitment to a collective fate — knowing it cannot succeed yet acting on internal principle. A classic case in moral philosophy of "deontology versus consequentialism."

### §4 Four Pivotal Moments

#### 4.1 July 2, 626 Xuanwu Gate Incident + 627-649 Zhenguan Era

**The day**

On the morning of July 2, 626 (4th day of the 6th month, 9th year of Wude), the rivalry among Emperor Gaozu Li Yuan's three sons reached its climax. Crown Prince Li Jiancheng was 38, Prince of Qin Li Shimin 27, Prince of Qi Li Yuanji 24. Li Shimin had been informed in advance that Jiancheng and Yuanji planned to harm him at a farewell banquet for a campaign against the Turks.

According to *Zizhi Tongjian* volume 191: Li Shimin led Zhangsun Wuji, Yuchi Jingde, Fang Xuanling, Du Ruhui, Hou Junji, and others, more than ten men, in setting an ambush inside the northern gate (Xuanwu Gate) of the Chang'an palace city. Jiancheng and Yuanji rode in to court and reached the Linhu Hall, but sensing trouble turned to leave. Shimin called out; Yuanji drew his bow three times but missed (under stress, unable to fully draw). Shimin shot Jiancheng dead. Yuanji fled; Yuchi Jingde rode him down and killed him.

Yuchi Jingde then entered the Taiji Palace in full armor and found Emperor Gaozu on a boat in the lake: "The Prince of Qin, because the Crown Prince and Prince of Qi rebelled, has raised troops and executed them." Gaozu was forced to issue an edict.

**Twenty-three years of Zhenguan governance**

On September 4, 626, Li Shimin took the throne under the era name Zhenguan. The 23-year reign that began the next year (627-649) became the template for later emperors:
- **Fang Xuanling** (579-648): Chancellor for 22 years, known as a master of administration.
- **Du Ruhui** (585-630): Paired with Fang Xuanling as "Fang the planner, Du the decider"; died early at 46.
- **Wei Zheng** (580-643): Originally an aide to Crown Prince Jiancheng, taken into Taizong's service after the Xuanwu Gate Incident. Memorialized over 200 direct criticisms during 17 years in office. Most famous: when Wei Zheng died in 643, Taizong sighed, "With bronze as a mirror one straightens one's clothes; with people as a mirror one knows gain and loss. Now Wei Zheng is gone, and I have lost a mirror."
- **641 Princess Wencheng to Tibet**: Married Tibetan king Songtsen Gampo, bringing artisans, medical books, Buddhist scriptures, and the Sakyamuni statue (depicting the Buddha at age 12), which became the central icon at the Jokhang Temple. Opened 200 years of Tang-Tibet matrimonial alliance.
- **Zhenguan Code**: The predecessor of the Yonghui Code (650), which became the Tang Code (*Tang Lü Shu Yi*). It influenced Japan's Taiho Code (701), Silla codes on the Korean Peninsula, and the codes of Vietnam's Ly dynasty.

**"Water can carry the boat or capsize it"**

The exchange between Taizong and Wei Zheng appears in *Zhenguan Zhengyao* volume one: "The ruler is the boat, the people the water. Water can carry the boat, and water can capsize it." Although the line originates in *Xunzi*'s "Wangzhi" chapter, the political practice of the Zhenguan ruler and ministers gave it new life as one of the central propositions of Confucian political philosophy, shaping Chinese political ethics for 1,200 years.

**careful framing**

The ethical assessment of the Xuanwu Gate Incident has long been disputed. Traditional historiography tended toward a "Taizong had no choice" narrative; Sima Guang's *Zizhi Tongjian* in the Song already began to criticize Taizong for "altering the veritable records." Modern historiography largely accepts that two things are simultaneously true — the violence of the coup was real, and the achievements of the Zhenguan era were real. The tension between them is itself an important object of institutional history.

#### 4.2 755-763 An-Shi Rebellion

**November 9, 755: Rebellion at Fanyang**

On November 9, 755 (9th day of the 11th month, 14th year of Tianbao), An Lushan, Military Commissioner of Fanyang, rebelled at Fanyang (around today's Beijing). An Lushan was about 52, of Sogdian origin, simultaneously holding Fanyang, Pinglu, and Hedong commissions and commanding 150,000 troops. The rebellion's pretext was "to carry out a secret edict and punish Yang Guozhong."

Emperor Xuanzong was 71 and at first refused to believe the report. Rebel forces took Luoyang in December; in January 756 An Lushan declared himself emperor of the Yan dynasty in Luoyang.

**June 18, 756 Chang'an Falls + July 13 Mawei Slope**

On June 9, 756 the Tongguan pass fell; defending general Geshu Han was captured. Early on June 13, Xuanzong fled west in haste with Yang Guifei, Yang Guozhong, princes and grandsons, and over a thousand palace attendants. Rebel forces entered Chang'an on June 18.

On July 13 (14th day of the 6th month) the imperial party reached Mawei Station (today's Xingping, Shaanxi). Imperial guard commander Chen Xuanli incited the soldiers to mutiny. They first killed Yang Guozhong and his son Yang Xuan, then surrounded the station demanding Yang Guifei's death. Chief eunuch Gao Lishi advised Xuanzong from inside: "The Honored Consort indeed has no crime, but the soldiers have already killed Guozhong, and as long as the Consort remains by Your Majesty's side, how can they feel safe?" Xuanzong ordered Yang Guifei strangled in front of a Buddhist altar beneath a pear tree; she was 38.

Xuanzong continued west into Sichuan; Crown Prince Li Heng went north to Lingwu. On July 12 (almost simultaneous with Mawei), Li Heng ascended the throne in Lingwu as Emperor Suzong; Xuanzong was honored as Retired Emperor.

**757 Chang'an Recovered + 763 Pacification**

In September 757 Tang armies under Guo Ziyi and Li Guangbi, with Uyghur cavalry, recovered Chang'an. Luoyang followed in October.

But the rebellion did not end. In the first month of 757 An Qingxu killed his father An Lushan; in 759 Shi Siming killed An Qingxu; in 761 Shi Chaoyi killed Shi Siming. Only when Shi Chaoyi committed suicide in Mozhou (today's Renqiu, Hebei) in January 763 did the An-Shi Rebellion end, after seven years and two months.

**The numbers behind a halved population**

In the 14th year of Tianbao (755), registered Tang households numbered 8.91 million, about 52.9 million people (some estimates including unregistered population reach 80 million).
In the 2nd year of Guangde (764), registered households were 2.93 million, about 16.9 million people (some estimates around 20 million).

The halving reflects multiple factors: war deaths, plague, refugees who fell off the registers, and large-scale southward migration of northern aristocratic clans. This was one of the most severe population cliffs in medieval Chinese history.

**Du Fu's "Spring View" as contemporary witness**

In March 757 Du Fu was trapped within Chang'an under rebel occupation. He wrote "Spring View": "The state is broken, mountains and rivers remain; spring comes to the city, grass and trees grow deep." The poem became the archetype for later poetry on national fall.

**careful framing**

The An-Shi Rebellion was the moment the Tang shifted from peak to decline, but the "decline" was structural rather than dramatic. Regional military governors retained autonomy for 150 years (until Zhu Wen ended the Tang in 907); central authority in the Tang's second half was never truly restored. The Kaiyuan era's peak and the rebellion were less than 15 years apart.

#### 4.3 1126-1127 Jingkang Incident

**1125 Jin Destroys Liao + the Maritime Alliance Backfires**

From 1118, Emperor Huizong and Tong Guan led the "Maritime Alliance" with the Jurchen Jin to destroy the Khitan Liao. In February 1125 Jin forces captured the Liao Tianzuo Emperor; the Liao fell. But the Jin also observed how poorly Song armies performed in the campaign to recover the Sixteen Prefectures of Yan and Yun, and turned south.

**January 1126: First Siege of Kaifeng**

In December 1125 Jin armies advanced south on two routes: eastern under Wanyan Zongwang, western under Wanyan Zonghan. By January 1126 the eastern army neared Kaifeng. Huizong panicked and on January 18 (23rd of the 12th month, 7th year of Xuanhe) abdicated to his eldest son Zhao Huan (Emperor Qinzong), who was 25. Huizong fled south to Zhenjiang with close attendants.

In late January Jin forces besieged Kaifeng. Li Gang organized defense. In February the Jin demanded territorial cessions, indemnities, and hostages, and the Song agreed. The Jin lifted the siege in March.

**November 1126: Second Siege + January 1127: City Falls**

In August 1126 Jin armies pushed south again; by November Kaifeng was again besieged. This time defensive preparations were inadequate.

On January 9, 1127 (25th day of the intercalary 11th month, 1st year of Jingkang) Kaifeng's outer city fell. Qinzong went to the Jin camp to negotiate and was detained.
On February 6, 1127 (10th day of the 1st month, 2nd year of Jingkang) the Jin demoted Huizong and Qinzong to commoners.
On April 20, 1127 (27th day of the 3rd month) Jin forces took Huizong, Qinzong, consorts, imperial clan, craftsmen, and skilled artisans — about 3,000 people — north as captives, including nine of Huizong's sons, Qinzong's empress née Zhu, and Huizong's empress née Zheng. The Northern Song's 167 years (960-1127) ended.

**May 1127: Gaozong Flees South + Southern Song Founded**

On May 1, 1127 (1st day of the 5th month, 2nd year of Jingkang) Huizong's ninth son, Prince Kang Zhao Gou, took the throne in Yingtian (today's Shangqiu, Henan) at age 21 as Emperor Gaozong, with the era name Jianyan.

From 1127 to 1138 Gaozong fled through Yangzhou, Lin'an (today's Hangzhou), Yuezhou, Mingzhou (today's Ningbo), the open sea, and back to Lin'an, pursued by Jin forces.
1129: "Miao-Liu Mutiny" — guard commanders Miao Fu and Liu Zhengyan in Hangzhou forced Gaozong to abdicate to his three-year-old crown prince. Han Shizhong and others suppressed the mutiny within a month.
1138: Capital fixed at Lin'an.
November 1141: Treaty of Shaoxing. Song acknowledged itself as Jin's vassal, paid annual tribute of 250,000 ounces of silver and 250,000 bolts of silk, and accepted the Huai River-Dasanguan line as the border.
January 1142: Yue Fei was given a death sentence at Fengbo Pavilion at 39.
June 1135: Huizong died at Wuguocheng (today's Yilan, Heilongjiang) at 54. June 1156: Qinzong died in Jin captivity at 57.

**careful framing**

The paradox of Jingkang: Northern Song was among the most economically and culturally prosperous dynasties in Chinese antiquity (Kaifeng had a population of one million in 1100; annual fiscal revenue was about 160 million strings of cash), yet it collapsed militarily with almost no resistance. This simultaneity of "civilizational peak and military collapse" is a central question of Song historiography. The emotional force of Yue Fei's "Manjianghong" line "the Jingkang shame is not yet washed clean" comes from repeatedly looking back at this civilizational rupture.

#### 4.4 1276-1279 Song-Yuan Transition

**February 1276: Lin'an Surrenders**

In 1271 Khubilai Khan declared the Yuan dynasty. In 1273 Yuan armies took Xiangyang. In 1275 the Yuan general Bayan launched a major southern campaign.

On February 4, 1276 (18th day of the 1st month, 2nd year of Deyou) Yuan forces reached Lin'an. Empress Dowager Xie (consort of Lizong) submitted a letter of surrender in the name of the five-year-old Emperor Gongdi Zhao Xian. Lin'an fell; Southern Song effectively ended (only small courts in Fuzhou, Guangdong, and Yamen continued resistance for three more years).

**December 1278: Wen Tianxiang Captured + January 1283: Martyred in Dadu**

In May 1276 Prince of Yi Zhao Shi was enthroned in Fuzhou as Emperor Duanzong at age 7. Wen Tianxiang served as right chancellor and accompanied the boy emperor through Fujian, Jiangxi, and Guangdong organizing resistance.

In May 1278 Duanzong died of illness; his seven-year-old brother Zhao Bing succeeded.

On December 20, 1278 Wen Tianxiang was captured at Wupoling (today's Haifeng, Guangdong) by Zhang Hongfan's Yuan forces. Wen attempted suicide with bingpian (borneol) but failed.

In March 1279 Wen Tianxiang was brought to the Yamen sea battle and witnessed the destruction of the Song fleet. He wrote "Crossing Lonely Ocean":
"Bitter toil from a single classic raised me up; for four wandering years amid sparse battle stars."
"Since ancient times who has not died? Let me leave a loyal heart to shine in the historical record."

In 1279 Wen Tianxiang was taken to Dadu (today's Beijing) and imprisoned for four years in the Bingmasi earth dungeon. Khubilai Khan personally tried to persuade him to surrender, offering the chancellorship. Wen replied, "Tianxiang received Song favor as chancellor — how could I serve a second house?"

In 1281 he wrote "Song of Righteous Spirit" in prison: "Heaven and earth have righteous spirit, mingled in all forms." Sixty lines total.

On January 9, 1283 (9th day of the 12th month, 19th year of Zhiyuan) Wen Tianxiang was beheaded at Chaishi market in Dadu at age 47. Before execution he bowed south: "My task is done."

**March 19, 1279: The Yamen Sea Battle**

The Yamen sea battle ended on March 19, 1279 (6th day of the 2nd month, 2nd year of Xiangxing). For the previous month Song and Yuan fleets had faced off in the waters of today's Yamen Pass area in Jiangmen, Guangdong. Song commanders Zhang Shijie and Lu Xiufu held over 1,000 vessels; Yuan commander Zhang Hongfan attacked with about 500.

In the early hours of March 19 the Yuan launched a general assault and the Song was crushed. Lu Xiufu, seeing all was lost, first drove his wife into the sea, then carried the eight-year-old boy emperor Zhao Bing on his back and leapt into the sea. According to *Songshi*'s biography of Lu Xiufu, soldiers and commoners who followed numbered over 100,000: "After seven days, more than 100,000 corpses floated to the surface of the sea."

The Southern Song's 152 years (1127-1279) ended. China was for the first time conquered in full by a northern steppe people.

**The "After Yamen, no more China" controversy**

The phrase "After Yamen, no more China" has circulated online in recent years, but it is much disputed in academic circles:
- The phrase is generally said to have arisen in the late Ming-early Qing loyalist context, but actual Qing-period writings continued to use the concept of *Zhonghua* without rupture.
- In 1942 Qian Mu, lecturing at Southwest Associated University, explicitly rejected such an essentialist proposition: "Chinese civilization has never been broken."
- In 1962 the Chinese-American scholar James T. C. Liu, in *China Turning Inward*, proposed that the Southern Song marked China's "inward turn," but did not endorse a "death of China" thesis.
- Mainstream 21st-century scholarship rejects the essentialist version — dynastic change is not the end of a civilization. The Yuan inherited Song fiscal, bureaucratic, and Confucian traditions; the Ming continued to institutionalize Zhu Xi's learning.

**careful framing**

The tragedy of Yamen is real — an 8-year-old emperor's plunge into the sea and 100,000 dead are historical facts. But "After Yamen, no more China" is a later political narrative, and needs to be distinguished from the events themselves. China did not vanish after the Yuan, but the literati-centered political-cultural form that defined the Song did undergo a deep transformation. That transformation is one of the central subjects of Song-Yuan transition studies.
