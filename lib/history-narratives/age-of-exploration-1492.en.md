## 0. Open Your Textbook First (Textbook Anchor)

> Your 7th-grade Houghton Mifflin *Medieval & Early Modern Times* —
> **Ch.12 The Age of Exploration** —
> **Lesson 1 Europeans Explore the World** — **p.338-343**.
>
> Your textbook will tell you:
> - **1492 Columbus arrived in the Americas**
> - **1498 da Gama reached India**
> - **1519-1522 Magellan's circumnavigation**
> - **Europe "discovers" the New World + wealth pours in**
>
> **What your textbook will NOT tell you** (this is why we need this lesson):
> - **The word "discovery" itself is a problem** —
>   **The Americas had been inhabited for 15,000+ years** — **Columbus did not "discover" — he**arrived** —
>   **This one-word difference** reflects 500 years of Eurocentric narrative bias —
>   **By 2024 academia has almost completely abandoned the word "discovery"**
> - **Over the next 200 years — 100 million Indigenous Americans died** —
>   **Causes were mainly smallpox + measles + flu** (diseases brought by Europeans — Indigenous peoples had no immunity) —
>   **Plus** massacre + enslavement + forced labor —
>   **This is one of the largest population collapses in human history** — a higher proportion than the Black Death (90%+ vs 30-50%)
> - **1500-1888 trans-Atlantic slave trade** — **12 million Africans** were loaded onto ships and brought to the Americas —
>   **2-3 million died on the ships** (the Middle Passage) —
>   **The remaining 9 million** were enslaved in the Americas for generations —
>   **This is the largest forced human migration in history** — laying the foundation of today's Caribbean + US + Brazilian population structure
> - **Contemporary China**: **Ming Dynasty admiral Zheng He sailed to the Western Ocean seven times, 1405-1433** —
>   **87 years before Columbus** — **fleet of 300 ships + 30,000 men** — **3,000 times larger** than Columbus's three ships and 90 men —
>   **But** after the Yongle Emperor died in 1424, the Ming Dynasty **suddenly stopped its voyages** in 1433 — **burned the ships + destroyed the records + imposed a sea ban (haijin)** —
>   **Why did China choose to "stop" while Europe chose to "continue"** — this is the sharpest version of Joseph Needham's Question
> - **Columbus is not a "hero" or a "monster"** — he is **a complex person** —
>   **He** truly believed **he was preaching the gospel + saving Indigenous souls** —
>   **He also** directly ordered **killing + enslavement + forced labor** —
>   **Studying history** can't be a simple judgment — must **see both sides of a person**
> - **Bartolomé de las Casas 1484-1566** — a **Catholic friar** —
>   **Was an enslaver in his early years** — but after **witnessing with his own eyes** the massacres on Hispaniola **completely transformed** —
>   **Wrote *A Brief Account of the Destruction of the Indies* (1542)** — exposing Spanish atrocities —
>   **He is** a pioneer of 16th-century human rights advocacy — but textbooks barely mention him
> - **Doña Marina / Malinche (c. 1500-1551)** — Cortés's translator + companion —
>   **The Aztec conquest would have been almost impossible without her** —
>   **She is** a complex female figure: **survivor + translator + collaborator + mother** —
>   **Today Mexico** has **deep ambivalence** about her — calling her both "traitor" and "mother of the nation" —
>   **This is one of the deepest tensions in Mexican identity**
> - **The "success" of the Age of Exploration** — **made Europe the global hegemon for 500 years** —
>   **But it also** laid the foundation **for global inequality + colonialism + racism** —
>   **21st-century decolonization movements** are still **digesting the consequences of 1492** —
>   **Studying history** is not to celebrate colonialism — it is to see clearly the **modern roots of global wealth + violence**
>
> **Open your textbook to p.338 and read together** — this lesson uses **Ming Zheng He, whom you already know**, as the foil
> (Why didn't China colonize? — the sharpest version of Joseph Needham's Question).

## 1. The One-Line Hook

**October 12, 1492, 2 a.m. — high in the lookout of the *Pinta* on the Atlantic** —
**Sailor Rodrigo de Triana shouted**: **"Tierra! Tierra!" (Land! Land!)** —
**He didn't know**: what he was shouting at was **the Americas** —
**He didn't know even more**: that over the next 500 years, that one shout would mean:
- **100 million Indigenous Americans dead**
- **12 million Africans forced across the ocean to be enslaved in the Americas**
- **Spain and Portugal suddenly transformed into global hegemons**
- **The Aztec + Inca + Maya — three great civilizations — destroyed**
- **Europe becomes the center of the modern world for 500 years**

**Rodrigo received no reward** — the **"first to sight land" prize he was promised** was **taken by Columbus** —
**Four years later Rodrigo angrily quit sailing** — went back to be a fisherman in Morocco — **died unknown** —

**This is the first second of the Age of Exploration**: **the starting point of the largest reorganization in human history** —
**also one of the most romanticized atrocities**.

## 2. The Stage: The Globe in the 1490s

### Geography (L0)

**In the 1490s the planet had 4 large "ocean spheres"** — and **they did not connect to each other**:

| Ocean sphere | Main participants | State in the 1490s |
|---|---|---|
| **Indian Ocean** | Arab + Indian + Chinese (formerly) | **Most prosperous** — spice trade + gold + silk — but **China withdrew in 1433** |
| **Mediterranean** | Italian city-states + Ottomans + North Africa | **Bottleneck for East-West trade** — after the Ottomans took Constantinople in 1453, they **taxed Europe heavily** |
| **Atlantic** | Almost empty — Europeans only reached Madeira / Azores | **Unknown** — European legend held it was a "Sea of Devils" — ships went and never came back |
| **Pacific** | Almost completely empty — only Indigenous coastal voyages | **Unknown** — Indigenous peoples ran their own ocean activities — Europeans didn't enter until the 1520s |

**Key**: **In the 1490s Europe was on the "geographical edge"** —
- **East Asia + the Indian Ocean**: **the center of the Asian civilizational world** — Europe was **only a faraway customer** here
- **The Mediterranean**: **a European peninsula** — but **the Ottomans were strangling** trade
- **The Atlantic + Pacific**: **completely undeveloped** — this was **the only geography Europe could** actively **change**

— **Why did the Age of Exploration originate in Europe?**
**Simple answer**: **Europe was forced** to the Atlantic —
**Complex answer**: **European technology (cannon + sailing ships + compass) + finance (nation-states) + ideology (Renaissance "we can change the world")** stacked together —
**all matured in the 1490s**.

### Climate (L1)

**The years 1450-1850 were the middle of the Little Ice Age** —
**The North Atlantic cold currents were strong** — **the northern sailing route was hard** —
**So** Portugal + Spain **took the** southern **route around Africa + across the Atlantic** — this was **a route forced by the cold currents**.

**This is different from the 13th-century Vikings** sailing the **North Atlantic to Greenland + Newfoundland** —
**The 13th century was warmer — the northern route was passable** —
**By the 1490s the north was iced over** — **forcing Europe south** —
**Columbus rode the Canary Current + the trade winds** — this is **a textbook example of climate shaping a route**.

### Society (L2)

**1490s European society** had several new layers **reshaping its social fabric**:

**The Iberian Peninsula was special**:
- **Spain had just completed the Reconquista in 1492** — 700 years of fighting was over — **a huge body of trained soldiers had nothing to do** —
  **The role of the** Conquistador (conqueror) **was born here** —
- **Portuguese Prince Henry the Navigator (1394-1460)** had already invested **50 years in nautical technology** —
- **Spanish king Ferdinand and queen Isabella**, after unifying Spain, **looked for new sources of revenue** —
- **A wave of Genoese / Venetian merchants** moved to the Iberian Peninsula **looking for new opportunities**

**This was Iberia's unique 1490s ecosystem**:
- **Soldiers** (Reconquista ended)
- **Navigators** (Portugal had been investing for 50 years)
- **Merchants** (Italian immigrants)
- **The royal court** (looking for money)
- **The Church** (after the Reconquista, eager to "spread the gospel to a new place")

— **5 roles combined** — exactly what was needed **to ignite** the Age of Exploration.

### Institutions (L3) — Several Key Technologies + Institutions

**3 technologies**:
1. **Caravel** — Portuguese improvement in the 1440s — **could sail against the wind** — this was a revolution
2. **Astrolabe + Magnetic Compass** — Arab + Chinese inventions — improved by Europeans — **made open-ocean navigation possible**
3. **Cannon on ships** — matured in 1500s Europe — **let small ships defeat large fleets** —
   **Completely changed naval warfare + colonization**

**3 institutions**:
1. **Royal patronage** — **the Portuguese + Spanish royal courts** directly invested in voyages —
   **Not private adventures — state projects** —
   **This was the same as China** — but **the result was completely different** (see §8)
2. **Joint stock company** (early form) — **starting in the 16th century** —
   **Dutch East India Company 1602 / English East India Company 1600** —
   **Risk dispersion + capital accumulation = the colonial economic model**
3. **Treaty of Tordesillas (1494)** — **Pope Alexander VI 1493 + Spain and Portugal 1494** —
   **The Pope drew a line dividing the world between two countries** —
   **Spain west / Portugal east** —
   **This is one of the most absurd treaties in human history** —
   **Two European countries plus an Italian Pope** decided **who owned the globe** —
   **Everyone else at the time** was **not consulted** —
   **Including Indigenous Americans + Africans + Asians** —
   **This** Eurocentric **mindset** — **is the spiritual foundation of 500 years of colonialism**

## 3. The Main Characters (Multi-Perspective + Multi-Continent + Multi-Class)

### The Spanish Conquistador Side

#### Christopher Columbus (1451-1506)

##### Origins: A Genoese Weaver's Son

**Born in 1451 in Genoa** (a port city in northern Italy) —
**His father was** a wool weaver + small tavern keeper —
**The family was not wealthy** — but **Genoa was a maritime center** — Columbus from childhood **watched ships + learned navigation** —
**From age 14** he was riding merchant ships across the Mediterranean —

**He had no formal university education** — but he **taught himself**:
- **Latin** (enough to read Marco Polo + classical authors)
- **Math + astronomy** (for navigation)
- **Geography** (read every book he could find)

**He was especially obsessed with Marco Polo's *Travels*** —
**This book made him believe** "the East has gold + spices + the Great Khan's palace" —
**He thought**: **what if I sail** west **— could I reach the East**?

##### His Mistaken Geography

**Columbus got it wrong**:
- **He thought** the Earth's circumference was **25% smaller** than it really is
- **He thought** the east coast of Asia was **much further east** than it really is
- **He** estimated **the Canary Islands to Japan = 4,200 km**
- **The actual distance is 19,500 km** — **5 times further**
- **If Columbus had not** accidentally **bumped into the Americas — he and his crew** would have all died on the Atlantic

**This is one of the most ironic things in history**:
**Columbus was wrong by a factor of 4** — but **the Americas were sitting right in the way** —
**Because of his miscalculation** — he **accidentally** changed history —
**He is the** reluctant **type of "hero"** — he is **not** a man whose math was right — he is **lucky + stubborn**.

##### 1485-1492 — Seven Years Looking for a Sponsor

**From 1485 Columbus** sought royal sponsorship — and was **rejected over and over**:
- **Portugal's King João II**: **a panel of experts** said his math was wrong — rejected
- **Spain's Ferdinand and Isabella**: **first** rejected (the panel agreed his math was wrong) — but **Isabella quietly remembered him**
- **England's Henry VII**: **didn't reply**
- **France's Charles VIII**: **didn't reply**

**Early 1492 — the Reconquista was complete + the Spanish royal court had money on hand** —
**Isabella in April 1492** reconsidered — **approved** —
**Conditions**:
- Columbus would be "Admiral of the Ocean"
- **Governor** of any lands found
- **10% of profits**

**Columbus, age 38 — set out from Palos on August 3** —
**3 ships**: Santa María (flagship, about 19 m) + Pinta + Niña — **a total of 90 men** —
**Much smaller than the Yuan Dadu palace Marco Polo had described** — but **this was all Europe could put in**.

##### August 3 to October 12, 1492 — 70 Days on the Atlantic

**August 3 — left Palos** —
**August 12 — arrived at the Canary Islands** —
**September 6 — left the Canaries and entered the unknown Atlantic** —
**For the next 36 days — they saw no land** —
**The sailors were** afraid + ready to mutiny —

**Columbus gave the sailors** falsified ship logs — he **wrote "today we sailed 50 km" while actually sailing 60 km** —
**That way** the sailors thought they weren't too far from Spain —
**This is** classic Columbus "management" — **the trade-off between truth and reassurance**.

**On October 10 the sailors were** about to mutiny —
**Columbus made them an** agreement: **if we don't see land in 3 days we turn back** —
**On October 12 at 2 a.m. — Rodrigo de Triana on the Pinta shouted "land"** —
**Three days into the deadline — at the very last moment** —

**Columbus stepped ashore — this was a small island in the Bahamas** (today possibly San Salvador / Watling Island, scholars debate) —
**He named it "San Salvador" (Holy Savior)** —
**He** met **Indigenous people for the first time** —
**The Taíno people — peacefully came out to greet him** —
**They** brought gifts: **parrots + cotton cloth + spears** —
**Columbus noticed**: **they had no iron weapons — they had no military training** —
**He wrote in his log**: "**With 50 of our men** we could conquer them all — **they would do whatever we want**" —
**This is** the earliest "colonist's plan" in human history — and the **Taíno still didn't know what was coming** —
**Within 60 years almost all the Taíno on Hispaniola were dead**.

##### 1493 Second Voyage — Colonization of Hispaniola Begins

**Columbus returned to Spain in 1493** — **he was greeted as a hero** —
**On September 25, 1493 he set out again — this time** with **17 ships and 1,500 men** — the goal was **colonization** —
**He built the first colony on Hispaniola** — **La Isabela** —
**Starting in 1494 Columbus imposed**:
- **Forced gold quotas on the Taíno** (every person 14 and older had to pay a fixed quantity every 3 months)
- **Anyone who didn't pay had a hand cut off** — many **bled to death**
- **Enslavement + forced labor to build the colony** —
- **Sent 500+ Taíno enslaved people back to Spain** (many died on the ships)

**In 1495 Columbus wrote home**: "**There must be much gold here, I will find it — the people are gentle, they make perfect slaves**" —
**These are** Columbus's own words — he **did not deny** enslavement — he **did not commit crimes "unconsciously"** — he **actively designed** this system.

##### 1500 — Columbus Himself Arrested

**He was** cruel + incompetent **as a colonial administrator** —
- **The Spanish settlers** rebelled against him
- **The local Taíno rebelled**
- **The gold was not as plentiful as he had promised**

**In 1500 Isabella sent Bobadilla to investigate** —
**Bobadilla arrived in September — by October he** arrested Columbus —
**Columbus was sent home to Spain in chains** —
**Isabella saw him** wearing irons — **softened** — **released him** —
**But** stripped him of the governorship — **kept only** the empty title of "Admiral".

##### 1502-1504 — His Fourth and Final Voyage

**Columbus, age 51 — desperate to win back Isabella's favor** —
**In 1502 the fourth voyage — he hoped to find** the "real" passage to Asia (he **still didn't believe the Americas were not Asia**) —
**He reached the Central American coast at** Panama — but **could not find a passage** —
**His ships broke down** — he **and 116 men were stranded in Jamaica for a year** — **bluffed local people by predicting a lunar eclipse** to get food — **finally rescued**.

**In November 1504 he returned to Spain** — **Isabella died that very month** —
**Columbus lost his** last protector —
**On May 20, 1506, at age 54, Columbus died** —
**To his death he believed he had reached Asia** — **to his death he never knew** he had reached a "New World".

##### His Disputed Legacy

**Columbus is** one of the most complex figures in history:

**The positive**:
- **He** accidentally changed human history — opened the global ocean age
- **As a sailor** he was **brave + stubborn**
- **He** truly believed he was preaching for God — he was not a cold-blooded calculator

**The negative**:
- **He** actively designed the Taíno enslavement system
- **He** ordered the cutting off of hands + killings + forced labor — the chain of command is clear
- **To his death he refused to admit** the Americas were a new continent — his miscalculation almost killed 90 men
- **As a colonial administrator he was incompetent** — the colony rebelled against him

**21st-century controversy**:
- **Columbus Day (October 12)** vs **Indigenous Peoples' Day** —
  **Same day — completely different commemorations**
- **2020 anti-Columbus statue movement in the US + Canada** — many statues pulled down
- **2021 the US federal holiday is renamed + the two names co-exist** — meaning **two narratives are simultaneously recognized**

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Columbus is not a "hero" — and not a "monster" —
> **He is** a complex person — **with real ability + real faith + real violence** —
> **Studying history** can't be reduced to labels —
> **You must** acknowledge "he changed the world" + "he started the chain that killed 100 million Indigenous people" — both at once —
> **Simplification is educational failure**.

#### Hernán Cortés (1485-1547)

##### Origins: Minor Extremaduran Nobility

**Born 1485 in Extremadura, western Spain** —
**The family was** declining nobility — his father wanted him to **study law** —
**At 14 Cortés went to Salamanca to study law for 2 years** — but **dropped out** —
**He wanted** adventure + wealth —

**At age 19 in 1504 — he went to Hispaniola** —
**By then Columbus had already been on Hispaniola for 12 years** — the colony was basically built —
**For the next 14 years Cortés was in the Caribbean**:
- **Fighting** (Cuba was conquered in 1511)
- **Running plantations**
- **Building social networks**
- **Learning some Aztec / Maya local Indigenous languages** (partially)

**At 33 in 1518 — Cuba's governor Diego Velázquez chose him to lead an expedition** to Mexico —
**But** Velázquez changed his mind — wanted to replace him —
**Cortés found out — and** in February 1519, without authorization, **took 11 ships and 500 men** out of Cuba —
**This was** disobeying his superior — a **desperate gamble** — **if he failed he would be hanged** — **if he succeeded he would be a hero** —

**He** won the gamble.

##### 1519 — Arrival in Mexico + Burning the Ships

**February 1519 — Cortés arrived at Yucatán** —
**In March he fought local people in Tabasco** — won — and was given **20 enslaved local women** —
**One of them was an 18-year-old girl — her name was "Malinalli" or "Malinche" — later the Spanish called her "Doña Marina"** —
**She** spoke Maya + Náhuatl (Aztec language) + later learned Spanish —
**She became Cortés's translator + companion** —
**Without her — Cortés almost certainly could not have conquered the Aztecs** (see her section below).

**In July 1519 — Cortés founded the colony of Vera Cruz on the Gulf coast** —
**He did something** crazy: **he burned his own ships** —
**All 11 ships** — **burned** —
**To leave his men** with no way back —
**They could only **go inland into Mexico + conquer + die** —
**This is** one of the most famous "burn the boats" moves in military history —
**Similar to** Xiang Yu at the Battle of Julu in 207 BCE — break the cooking pots and burn the boats —
**Did Cortés know that story? No evidence — but the effect is the same** —
**Make sure no one can turn back**.

##### August-November 1519 — Marching on Tenochtitlán

**Cortés took 500 men + Doña Marina + several thousand Tlaxcalan allies** (mortal enemies of the Aztecs) **inland** —
**Along the way he** built alliances: **every tribe that resented the Aztecs joined him** —
**This is** Cortés's true "invention":
- **Not military genius — but** alliance diplomacy
- **The Aztec Empire in 1519 ruled 6 million people** — but **many subjugated tribes** wanted to revolt
- **Cortés exploited this** internal split: **by November when he reached Tenochtitlán — he had 500 Spanish + thousands of Indigenous allies**

##### November 8, 1519 — Meeting Moctezuma

**On November 8 Cortés reached Tenochtitlán** —
**The Aztec emperor Moctezuma II (c. 1466-1520)** personally **came out to greet him** —
**Why didn't Moctezuma just** fight back?

**Scholars have 4 hypotheses**:
1. **The Quetzalcoatl myth**: Aztec legend said "the light-skinned god Quetzalcoatl will return from the east across the sea" — Moctezuma suspected Cortés was Quetzalcoatl
2. **Political calculation**: Moctezuma wanted to **understand Cortés first** before deciding whether to kill him
3. **Military assessment**: the Aztecs had never seen **horses + guns + steel armor** — **psychological shock**
4. **Religion**: before contact with Cortés — a series of **bad omens** (comet + volcano + earthquake) had **shaken Moctezuma psychologically**

**The truth probably involves all 4** — but **Moctezuma's letting Cortés into Tenochtitlán was** a fatal mistake —
**For the next week** Cortés received **state-guest treatment** —
**Then Cortés suddenly** arrested Moctezuma — turned the emperor into **his hostage** —
**This is** one of the boldest "kidnappings" in human history —
**Cortés used 500 Spaniards** to **softly control** the Aztec Empire **for 6 months**.

##### 1520 — First Defeat + Retreat: Noche Triste

**In May 1520 Cortés learned that Velázquez had sent troops** to arrest him (the price of insubordination) —
**Cortés left** Pedro de Alvarado to manage Tenochtitlán + took his own troops back to **Vera Cruz** to defeat the pursuers —
**In June — Alvarado, while Cortés was away,** massacred 600 Aztec nobles at a festival — **he panicked + was scared + struck first** —
**The Aztecs rose up** —
**Cortés rushed back to Tenochtitlán** —
**On June 25 Moctezuma was killed** (scholars debate: by his own people or by the Spanish)
**On June 30 — the Noche Triste (Sad Night) — Cortés retreated from Tenochtitlán** —
**That night**: **400 Spaniards + thousands of Tlaxcalan allies died** —
**Cortés himself** almost died — **on the causeway he heard** the Aztec warriors chasing —

**He fled to Tlaxcala to regroup** —

##### May-August 1521 — Siege of Tenochtitlán

**Fall 1520 to spring 1521 — Cortés rebuilt his army** —
**He brought 80,000 Tlaxcalan + several thousand Spanish reinforcements** —
**He used 13 brigantines** to enter Lake Texcoco **and blockade Tenochtitlán** —
**Siege began in May** — **80 days** —

**Tenochtitlán's population was 200,000** — **one of the largest cities in the world in 1521** (bigger than contemporary Paris) —
**Cortés cut off** fresh water + food —
**The city saw** famine + smallpox —
**Smallpox had been brought to the Aztecs by the Spanish in 1520 — the Aztecs had no immunity — 50% of the city's population died of smallpox** —
**On August 13 — the city fell** —
**The last emperor Cuauhtémoc was captured** —

**When Cortés entered conquered Tenochtitlán**, he wrote to Charles V:
> "**This city is as beautiful as Venice — great canals + great squares + temples + palaces** —
> **But we must** completely **destroy it** — **and rebuild on its ruins**" —

— **Cortés ordered** the entire city torn down —
**All Aztec** temples + palaces + public buildings — **smashed** —
**Mexico City was built starting in 1521 on top of the ruins of Tenochtitlán** —
**Today Mexico City's central plaza, the Zócalo,** sits **on the original site of the Aztec Templo Mayor** —
**In 1978 a subway construction** accidentally **uncovered Aztec temple stones** — re-revealing **a civilization buried under the city for 450 years**.

##### 1521-1547 — Cortés's Later Life

**Cortés conquered the Aztecs at age 36 — but** the rest of his life was **anticlimax**:
- **In 1521** he was **governor of Mexico for 3 years**
- **In 1525** King Charles V sent **a new governor** — Cortés was **demoted**
- **In 1528** Cortés went back to Spain — was made **Marquis del Valle de Oaxaca** — but with **no real power**
- **1530-1547** he kept **going back and forth between Mexico and Spain** — **no more major achievements** —
- **December 2, 1547 — at age 62 he died in Seville** — **bitter at the end** — feeling **he was never paid what he was owed**

**His children in Mexico**:
- **With Doña Marina he had** Martín Cortés ("the first Mexican mestizo") —
  **Martín born 1522 — later went to Spain to be a knight — but society** always saw him as "illegitimate + half-Indigenous" —
  **He represents the eternal tension of mestizo identity**

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Cortés was not a "military genius" — he was **an alliance diplomat + master of psychological warfare + extreme gambler** —
> **Without Doña Marina + Tlaxcalan allies — he could not have conquered the Aztecs** —
> **The Western traditional narrative** exaggerates Cortés as an individual — simplifying it into "a few hundred Spaniards conquered millions of Aztecs" —
> **The truth**: **thousands of Indigenous allies + Doña Marina + smallpox + Aztec internal divisions** —
> **Cortés was** a catalyst — **not** a single cause.

#### Doña Marina / Malinche (c. 1500-1551)

##### Origins: Aztec Noble Family — But Sold into Slavery

**Malinalli** (her Náhuatl name, possibly meaning "grass" or "penitent") —
**Born around 1500 in what is now southern Mexico** —
**Her father was** a small Aztec chieftain — **a noble family** —
**From childhood she learned both Náhuatl and Maya** —

**But**: **her father died early** — her mother remarried —
**The stepfather and mother wanted the younger brother to inherit** —
**At age 6 or 7 — her mother** sold her to Maya merchants —
**Said she was "dead"** —
**Malinche from then on became** an enslaved person —

**For the next 12 years** — she was **enslaved in Maya territory** — **and learned Maya well** —
**In 1519 at age 19 — after Cortés fought the Maya in Tabasco** —
**The Maya gave Cortés 20 enslaved women** — Malinche was **one of them** —

##### 1519 — She Becomes Cortés's Translator

**Cortés at first didn't know what use she could be** —
**But** a few days later — he discovered **she spoke both Maya and Náhuatl** —
**Cortés at the time only had one Spaniard who spoke Maya** — **Jerónimo de Aguilar** (a sailor who had been captive among the Maya for 8 years) —
**The translation chain**:
**Aztec → Náhuatl → Malinche → Maya → Aguilar → Spanish → Cortés**

**Malinche learned fast** — **within months she had learned Spanish** —
**The translation chain became**: **Aztec → Náhuatl → Malinche → Spanish → Cortés** —
**She became Cortés's only translator + intelligence officer + strategic advisor**.

##### Her Real Role

**Scholars debate**: **was Malinche a "traitor" or a "survivor"**?

**Both sides**:
- **She** actively **helped Cortés conquer**: she gave Cortés **intelligence** about the Aztecs + local tribes + Moctezuma's thinking
- **She had a son with Cortés** — Martín Cortés ("the first mestizo")
- **In Cholula in 1519 she warned Cortés** that an Aztec ambush was waiting — **Cortés in turn massacred 6,000 in Cholula** — **she was the trigger of that massacre**
- **She did not** resist Cortés — she **cooperated as fully as possible**

**But**:
- **She was sold into slavery by her own mother at 6 or 7** — she had **no "loyalty" to Aztec culture**
- **At 19 she had** no choice — it was cooperate or die
- **She used intelligence** to survive — this is the most rational strategy for any **enslaved person**
- **At the end she married a Spanish noble Juan Jaramillo** — became "Doña Marina" —
  **She** actually **changed her own fate** — from enslaved person to noblewoman

##### Modern Mexico's Ambivalence

**Malinche died in 1551** — for 200 years afterward **few people mentioned her** —
**Until after Mexican independence in the 19th century** — she has been **interpreted and reinterpreted**:

- **19th century**: Mexican nationalists called her "traitor + national mother + Eve" — "**malinchismo**" became **a slur** meaning "**fawning over outsiders**"
- **Early 20th century**: Octavio Paz's *The Labyrinth of Solitude* (1950) cast her as "the raped mother" — **a metaphor for Mexican pain + humiliation** — but **also** opened **a path to sympathy**
- **Late 20th century**: **the Chicana feminist movement** reinterpreted Malinche — **acknowledging her as** the earliest multilingual Mexican woman + cross-cultural intermediary — **not a traitor — a survivor**
- **21st century**: Mexico has **statues of Malinche + complex narratives** — both criticism + admiration — classic ambivalence

**The son she had with Cortés, Martín, is** the first mestizo —
**He represents the core of** Mexican identity:
- **Both Spanish and Aztec**
- **Both conqueror and conquered**
- **Both paternal and maternal**
- **Both Christian and pagan**

— **The Mexican nation** has not **resolved this identity tension in 400 years** —
**Malinche is the deepest "mother / traitor" double image** —
**She shaped the Mexican soul**.

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Malinche is **the most complex female figure of the Age of Exploration** —
> **Western textbooks barely write about her** —
> **But** without her — the Aztecs would not have been conquered —
> **She is not a hero — and not a traitor — she is** a survivor + cultural intermediary + mother —
> **She represents all colonial-era women caught between two civilizations** —
> **From Pocahontas to Sacagawea to modern cross-cultural women** —
> **The "marginal figures" of history** often **carry the heaviest cultural weight** —
> **Studying history** can't only look at "main characters" — must **look at the women who get simplified out**.

#### Bartolomé de las Casas (1484-1566) — The Anti-Colonial Friar

##### Origins: A Seville Merchant's Son

**Born in 1484 in Seville** — his father Pedro de las Casas was **a merchant** —
**In 1492 when Columbus returned from his first voyage — Bartolomé was 8 — his father was a friend of Columbus** —
**Pedro later sailed with Columbus on the second voyage to Hispaniola in 1493** —
**At 17 in 1502 Bartolomé himself went to Hispaniola** —

##### 1502-1514 — His Early Years as an Enslaver

**Bartolomé from 18 to 30 — was an encomendero himself** —
**The encomienda was a Spanish colonial institution**: **the king "assigned" Indigenous populations to Spaniards** — the colonist **had the right to force Indigenous people to work for him** — nominally **in exchange for catechism + protection** — **in practice it was slavery** —
**Between 1502 and 1514 Bartolomé** had his own encomienda —
**He himself** enslaved Indigenous people —
**In his early years he didn't see the problem** — **this was the** normal practice **of Spanish colonists at the time**.

##### 1514 — The Key Conversion

**May 1514 — Bartolomé was 30 — he was in Cuba** —
**He was preparing a Pentecost sermon** —
**He** read Sirach 34:21-23 (a passage about oppressing the poor):
> "**Whoever stains himself with the blood of the poor** —
> **is like a murderer** —
> **the gift from oppression** — **God** does not accept**"

**Bartolomé** suddenly **broke down** — he **realized**: **the enslaved people he owned + the labor he used** = **the gift of murder** —

**Within months he made 3 decisions**:
1. **Free all his enslaved people**
2. **Enter the Dominican order as a friar**
3. **Spend the rest of his 50+ years advocating against colonial violence**

**This is** one of the most profound "conscience conversions" of the 16th century —
**3 years before Luther in 1517** —
**7 years before Ignatius in 1521** —
**Bartolomé going from "enslaver" to "liberator of the enslaved" is another inner revolution of the Reformation era**.

##### 1515-1542 — 27 Years of Advocacy

**For the next 27 years — Bartolomé** went back and forth to Spain to talk to the king —
**What he did**:
- **In 1515 he saw King Ferdinand for the first time** — urged him to stop the encomienda — failed
- **In 1517 he saw the second king Charles V (newly crowned)** — he succeeded **in convincing temporary reforms** — but **implementation failed**
- **1519-1521 he tried** a utopian colony in Cuba **("peaceful evangelization + no enslavement")** — failed — he could not protect Indigenous people
- **1531-1538 he ran a "peaceful conquest" experiment** in Verapaz, Central America — succeeded **in converting Maya** without **enslavement** — this is **the first successful nonviolent missionary effort in history** — but later the **Spanish governor** ended the experiment
- **In 1542 he wrote** *Brevísima Relación de la Destrucción de las Indias* (*A Brief Account of the Destruction of the Indies*) — this book **changed history**

##### 1542 — *A Brief Account of the Destruction of the Indies*

**The book is only 60+ pages — but** with **explosive force** —
**Bartolomé methodically + systematically** documents Spanish atrocities in the Caribbean + Central America:
- **Hispaniola 1492 had 3 million Taíno → in 1542 only 200 left** — he **saw with his own eyes** —
- **Spaniards** burning whole villages + cutting off hands + roasting in tar + feeding to dogs + raping + working people to death —
- **Specific names + specific places + specific years** — **not vague summaries**

**His book was officially published in 1552** — and immediately **translated all over Europe**:
- **Latin** (1578)
- **French** (1579)
- **English** (1583, 5 years before the Spanish Armada)
- **Dutch** (1578)
- **German** (1597)

**This book** shaped the **"Black Legend (the dark legend of Spain)"** — **the way Europeans saw** Spanish colonization **for the next 400 years** —
**Blacker than the truth** (Bartolomé exaggerated numbers — but **the core atrocities were real**) —
**Protestant nations** (England / Netherlands / Germany) especially **loved** this book — they used it **against Spain** as a colonial competitor.

##### The **Ironic** Consequence of This Book

**Bartolomé wrote the book to** protect Indigenous people —
**He** demanded the Spanish king stop the encomienda —
**But**: **in 1542 the Spanish king issued the New Laws** banning the inheritance of encomiendas — colonists **revolted** —
**By 1545 the New Laws were** partly repealed —
**Bartolomé failed**: **the encomienda continued** —

**Even more ironic**: **In 1516 Bartolomé** had recommended **using African enslaved people instead of Indigenous people** —
**Because Indigenous people** were dying too fast — Africans **were more durable** —
**He** in 1552 **realized this was an error** — **he later** opposed all slavery — but **the 1516 recommendation** was already out there —
**Some historians have therefore called him "the father of the African slave trade"** —
**This** simplification **is unfair** — he was **only** one of many at the time recommending African enslavement — but **he did write it** —
**Bartolomé in his late years** suffered painful self-reflection:
> "**I thought switching to African enslaved people** would **lighten the burden on Indigenous people** —
> **I did not see clearly** — **slavery itself is wrong** — **regardless of who is enslaved** —
> **I beg God's forgiveness**".

##### July 18, 1566 — Dies at 82

**Bartolomé continued his advocacy for his last 24 years** —
**He** almost succeeded in getting the Spanish king to **completely abolish the encomienda** — but **failed** —
**On July 18, 1566 — at age 82 he died** —
**He was buried in a monastery in Madrid** — but **his influence kept growing**:

**Long-term influence**:
- **18-19th century European abolitionist movement** drew direct inspiration from Bartolomé
- **The roots of 20th-century international human rights law** — Bartolomé was **one of the pioneers of human rights theory**
- **In 1965 Vatican II the Pope formally** praised **Bartolomé as a "voice for the oppressed"**
- **In 2002 Pope John Paul II** opened the beatification process for Bartolomé (the step before sainthood) —
  **If completed — he will be** the first saint canonized for "opposing colonial violence"

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Bartolomé is **the deepest "internal opponent" of the Age of Exploration** —
> **Textbooks barely cover him** — but **he represents the possibility of conscience + reflection in the 16th century** —
> **Not all Spaniards were Cortés** — there were also people like Bartolomé —
> **Studying history** can't only look at "mainstream power" — must **look at the voices of opposition** —
> **The Age of Exploration had Cortés / Pizarro / Columbus's violence** — and also Bartolomé's opposition —
> **Both** are history — **simplifying to one side = educational failure**.

### The Aztec / Inca Side

#### Moctezuma II (c. 1466-1520)

##### Origins: Aztec Royalty

**Born around 1466 in Tenochtitlán** —
**His grandfather was Moctezuma I** — his father was a high priest — he **grew up at the very top of Aztec nobility** —
**From childhood he received a complete Aztec elite education**:
- **Náhuatl literature + poetry** (the Aztecs had a rich oral + pictographic writing tradition)
- **Astronomy + the calendar** (the Aztec calendar was **more accurate than contemporary European calendars** — 365.2422 days per year)
- **Military training** — Aztec men entered the calmecac school at age 12 to learn warfare
- **Religion** — Moctezuma was **deeply religious all his life** — he served as high priest several times

##### 1502 — Crowned at Age 36

**In 1502 Moctezuma at 36 ascended the throne** —
**The Aztec Empire he inherited was at its** peak:
- **Territory** covered today's central Mexico + parts of Central America — **6 million people**
- **Capital Tenochtitlán** had 200,000 — bigger than contemporary Paris
- **Military hegemony** — the Aztecs **had almost no rival capable of challenging them**
- **Economy** — gold + corn + cacao + cotton + jade — **a tribute system from conquered tribes**

**Moctezuma reigned for 17 years** (1502-1519) — **what he did mainly was**:
- **Strengthen the tribute system** — increase tribute from conquered tribes
- **Expand** the elite military (Eagle Warriors + Jaguar Warriors)
- **Build the great temple** — restore the Templo Mayor
- **Expand the empire's borders**

— **The Aztec Empire in 1519** looked strong on the outside — but **internal contradictions had been building**:
- **Conquered tribes** were furious **(tribute burden + human sacrifice)**
- **Especially the Tlaxcalan and other independent tribes** hated the Aztecs —
- **This** internal split **was exactly what Cortés later** exploited as the key

##### November 1519 — His Critical Mistake

**In November 1519 Cortés reached Tenochtitlán** —
**Moctezuma** personally **came out to greet him** —
**This was something that had not happened in 17 years of the Aztec Empire** — the emperor personally welcoming a foreigner —

**Why did he do this**?
- **Quetzalcoatl myth** + **bad omens** + **wanting to understand before deciding** + **military shock** — already mentioned —

**But** — **this decision let Cortés into Tenochtitlán** —
**Over the next week** — **Cortés received state-guest treatment** — but **Cortés suddenly** arrested Moctezuma —
**Used 500 Spaniards + several thousand Tlaxcalan to softly control Tenochtitlán for 6 months** —

**During Moctezuma's captivity**:
- **Imprisoned in his own palace** — but **pretending to be** free — he was still **receiving envoys** — but **all his orders were monitored by Cortés**
- **He was forced to wear chains** several times
- **Forced** to publicly acknowledge **Charles V as his lord** — which **enraged the Aztec nobility** —

##### June 25, 1520 — His Death

**In June 1520, after the Aztec uprising — Cortés pushed Moctezuma onto the palace roof** —
**He tried to talk the Aztec people into stopping the fighting** — but **the people were** furious:
- **They believed Moctezuma was a traitor** — he had **failed to protect them**
- **They** threw stones **at Moctezuma** —
- **Moctezuma was severely wounded** — **and died a few days later**

**Disputed cause of death**:
- **Aztec sources**: he was stoned to death by Aztec people
- **Spanish sources**: **his own people** killed him
- **Modern scholars**: he **may have been killed directly by Cortés** — because Moctezuma **was no longer useful**

— **Moctezuma died at age 51** —
**He left** a complex legacy:
- **Early on he was** a powerful emperor
- **Later he became** an imprisoned puppet
- **At the time of his death** the Aztec people **despised him**

**How is he remembered by later Aztec descendants**?
- **Aztec descendants** generally **think of him as** weak — he **failed to protect** the people
- **Modern Mexican historiography** is **more complex**: he faced **a completely unfamiliar enemy + internal divisions + smallpox + psychological shock** — his **choices** were limited — but **he could not find the right path**

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Moctezuma is **the tragic figure of "unable to decide"** —
> **He had time** to fight Cortés — but **he** kept hesitating —
> **As a result** he **lost** the Aztec Empire + his own life —
> **In history** hesitation at a critical moment **often** leads to **the greatest disasters** —
> **Moctezuma is not incompetent — he is** unable **to judge in time** when facing a completely unfamiliar situation —
> **This pattern of "unfamiliar situation → paralysis"**:
> - 17th-century Ming Emperor Chongzhen facing Li Zicheng + the Manchus also hesitated
> - 19th-century Qing facing the Opium War, the same
> - Any era facing **an opponent not seen before** carries this risk —
> **Studying history** lets us **be aware** of this "unfamiliar → paralysis" pattern — **to prepare for modern decisions**.

#### Atahualpa (c. 1502-1533) — The Last Inca Emperor

##### Origins: Inca Royalty — Winner of a Civil War

**Born around 1502** — **his father was the Inca emperor Huayna Capac** —
**The Inca Empire by 1500** already covered **today's Peru + Ecuador + Bolivia + parts of Colombia + Chile + Argentina** —
**6,000 km long — the largest empire in the world** at the time —
**Much bigger than the Aztec Empire** —

**1525-1527 — smallpox spread from northern America to the Incas** —
**Inca emperor Huayna Capac died of smallpox** (1527) — **his heir Ninan Cuyochi also died** —
**Two candidates fought for the throne**:
- **Huáscar** (Huayna's son with his wife) — **the traditional heir**
- **Atahualpa** (Huayna's son with a Quito woman) — **had support in the Quito region**

**1529-1532 civil war** — **Atahualpa won in early 1532** —
**At age 30 — Atahualpa became** the new Inca emperor —

**But he didn't know**: **in January 1532** Pizarro had already landed at Tumbes —

##### November 1532 — Meeting Pizarro

**Atahualpa was at the city of Cajamarca in November 1532** —
**Pizarro arrived in Cajamarca on November 15, 1532** — only **168 Spaniards + 60 horses** —
**Atahualpa had 80,000 troops around Cajamarca** —
**Pizarro was at an** absolute disadvantage —

**How did he win**?

**On November 16 Atahualpa entered Cajamarca to meet Pizarro** — he **brought 6,000 unarmed attendants** (a ceremonial reception) —
**Pizarro hid** the 168 Spaniards **in the buildings around the Cajamarca square** —
**Atahualpa entered the square + met the Spanish friar Vicente de Valverde** —
**Valverde gave Atahualpa** a Bible + **said**: "**Accept Christianity + submit to Charles V**" —
**Through the translator** Felipillo — but **Felipillo's translation may not have been accurate** —
**Atahualpa took the Bible** — he **didn't know what this book was** — he **threw it on the ground** (because he could hear no voice — there was no Quechua translation of the Bible) —
**Valverde shouted**: "**He blasphemes the Bible!** **Kill!**" —

**The 168 Spaniards rushed out** — **using guns + steel armor + horses + steel swords to massacre 6,000 unarmed Inca** —
**Atahualpa was captured** —
**This is** the Cajamarca massacre — **even more absurd than Tenochtitlán in 1519 meeting Cortés**:
**The world's largest empire in the 1500s was kidnapped in one hour by 168 foreign adventurers**.

##### 1532-1533 — Gold Ransom + Execution

**Atahualpa was held for 8 months** —
**He** offered Pizarro a huge ransom:
- **A room** filled with gold
- **Two rooms** filled with silver
- **Total value about** $150 million in today's money

**In July 1533 — the gold arrived in Cajamarca** —
**But Pizarro** went back on his word —
**He was afraid that if Atahualpa was released he would** organize Inca resistance —
**Pizarro found pretexts —** charging Atahualpa with:
- **Killing his own brother Huáscar** (a fact)
- **Worshipping pagan gods** (a fact, but **had not been illegal before**)
- **Plotting against Charles V** (fabricated)

**On July 26, 1533 — Atahualpa was** garroted (not burned as originally planned — because at the last moment he **accepted baptism**) —
**He died at 31** —
**The Inca Empire formally fell** —
**Pizarro then** marched into Cuzco — **the Inca Empire's 6,000 km from then on** became **a Spanish colony** —

##### Aftermath

**Pizarro was assassinated by his own men in 1541** — **he didn't live to enjoy his wealth** —
**Inca resistance** continued:
- **1536-1572 — Inca remnants in the Vilcabamba mountains** —
- **In 1572** the last Inca king Túpac Amaru was executed — **Inca resistance formally ended**

##### Modern Peru's Memory of Atahualpa

**21st-century Peru** sees Atahualpa as **a national symbol**:
- **Cajamarca city every year** commemorates **the day Atahualpa was killed**
- **The Inca Quechua language** is still spoken by 8 million people — **a living fossil of Inca civilization**
- **In 2008 the Peruvian government** demanded **a formal apology from the Holy See** — **the Vatican has not responded**

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Atahualpa died at 31 in 1533 —
> **6 years after his father Huayna Capac died of smallpox in 1527** —
> **If smallpox had not** killed the Inca emperor + caused civil war —
> **Atahualpa would not have just ascended the throne when Pizarro arrived** —
> **The Incas might still have been** unified in 1532 — **and might have been able to fight off the Spanish** —
>
> **The conquests of the Age of Exploration** depended almost entirely on **smallpox + flu + measles** —
> **If Indigenous people had had immunity** — Spain's few hundred soldiers **could not possibly have conquered millions** —
> **Studying history** — **biological factors often determine outcomes more than "military genius"**.

### The China Comparison Side

#### Zheng He (1371-1433) — The Contemporary "Anti-Columbus"

##### Origins: A Yunnan Muslim Family — Then a Eunuch

**Born in 1371 in Kunyang, Yunnan** (today's Jinning, Yunnan) —
**A Hui Muslim family** — his father and grandfather **had both made the pilgrimage to Mecca** — **the family name was Ma (the transliteration of the Arabic "Mahmud")** —
**His original name was Ma He** —

**In 1382 the Ming army invaded Yunnan** — **Ma He was 11 — he was captured** —
**Boys were** castrated to be eunuchs — **and sent to Prince Zhu Di** (Prince of Yan) —
**Ma He grew up at Zhu Di's side + was educated + learned military arts + learned navigation** —

##### 1402 — Zhu Di's Coup Succeeds + Ma He Is Renamed

**In 1402 Zhu Di usurped the throne and became the Yongle Emperor** — **Ma He at age 31 was given the surname "Zheng" — given name "He"** —
**This was** one of the highest honors — for a eunuch to be granted a surname is very rare.

**The Yongle Emperor trusted Zheng He** — gave him **the entire Ming Dynasty maritime expedition program** —

##### 1405-1433 — Seven Voyages to the Western Ocean

**In 1405 the Yongle Emperor ordered the first voyage** —
**Zheng He** took 317 ships and 27,800 men —
**This was** the largest fleet on the planet in 1405:
- **Each "treasure ship" was 130 meters long — 50 meters wide — 5,000 tons displacement**
- **33 times bigger than Columbus's 1492 Santa María (19 m — 150 tons)**
- **Total personnel was 300 times Columbus's 90 men**

**Over the next 28 years — seven voyages**:

| # | Year | Route | Main events |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 1405-1407 | South China Sea → Calicut, India | First time to South India |
| **2** | 1407-1409 | South China Sea → India → Ceylon | Diplomatic exchanges |
| **3** | 1409-1411 | South China Sea → India → Ceylon → Arabian Sea | Helped a Ceylonese prince to the throne |
| **4** | 1413-1415 | India → Persian Gulf + Mecca + Medina | Pilgrimage + diplomacy |
| **5** | 1417-1419 | Arabian Sea + East Africa (Mogadishu / Mombasa) | Reached the East African coast |
| **6** | 1421-1422 | Same as 5 — further | Brought a giraffe back to Beijing — astonished the court |
| **7** | 1431-1433 | Same as 5 + Persian Gulf + Mecca | Zheng He's last voyage — he died on the return in 1433 |

— **A total of 28 years** — **Zheng He's fleet visited**:
- **Southeast Asia** (Java / Sumatra / Malaya / Siam / Vietnam)
- **South Asia** (India / Ceylon / Bengal)
- **Middle East** (Hormuz / Yemen / Mecca)
- **East Africa** (Somalia / Kenya)

**87 years before Columbus's 1492 first voyage** —
**93 years before da Gama reached India in 1498** —
**116 years before Magellan's 1519-1522 circumnavigation** —
**— China** should **have become the global maritime hegemon first**.

##### But in 1433 — Everything Stopped

**In 1433 Zheng He died on the return leg of his seventh voyage — at age 62** —
**The Yongle Emperor had died 9 years earlier (1424)** —
**The new emperors Hongxi + Xuande didn't have** the Yongle Emperor's maritime ambition —
**Especially the** Confucian scholar-officials **strongly opposed the voyages**:
- **Massive cost** to the treasury
- **Confucian "agriculture over commerce"** — overseas trade **had no point**
- **Confucian "China-as-center"** — other countries **don't matter — tribute is enough**

**After Zheng He's death in 1433**:
- **The Ming Dynasty stopped** all official maritime expeditions
- **Starting 30 years later — the "sea ban (haijin)" was strictly enforced** — private overseas trade was **illegal**
- **In 1474** "not a plank may go to sea" — all ocean activity **forbidden**
- **In 1525 — some sources show** that Zheng He's voyage records were destroyed — to **prevent the possibility** of resuming voyages

— **This is** one of the largest "voluntary withdrawals" in human history:
**While China was leading the Indian Ocean + East Africa + Southeast Asian maritime economy** —
**China voluntarily** stopped —
**100 years later, Europe** filled **the vacuum**.

##### Zheng He vs Columbus Comparison

| Dimension | Zheng He (1405-1433) | Columbus (1492-1506) |
|---|---|---|
| **Fleet size** | 317 ships + 27,800 men | 3 ships + 90 men |
| **Each ship** | 130 m — 5,000 tons | 19 m — 150 tons |
| **Purpose** | Tribute diplomacy + display Ming power | Find gold + spices + personal fortune |
| **Means** | Ceremony + trade + occasional force | Colonization + enslavement + massacre |
| **Political consequences** | Tribute states grew (30+ states paying tribute to Ming) + Southeast Asian Chinese migration begins | 100 million Indigenous deaths + trans-Atlantic slave trade + global colonialism |
| **Aftermath** | After 1433** voluntarily stopped** — 1474 sea ban | After 1492 **kept expanding** — global colonialism from 1500 |

— **Two** almost opposite **paths** —
**Why this difference**?

**5 hypotheses**:

1. **Political system**: China was **a unified central state** — one emperor decides to stop and the entire country stops. Europe was **fragmented** — one king stops + another king continues
2. **Economic motivation**: China was **tributary + not short of food** — overseas voyages had **no economic necessity**. Europe was **strangled in the Mediterranean by the Ottomans** — had to find new sea routes
3. **Ideology**: China's "China-as-center" — other countries **only deserved to pay tribute**. Europe's Renaissance "we can change the world" — finding new lands was **heroic**
4. **Military vs commercial**: the Ming fleet was **military + ceremonial** — not designed to recoup cost through trade. Europe's **merchants + crowns cooperated** — colonies had to **make money**
5. **Confucianism vs Christianity**: Confucianism's "barbarians are far people — moral influence is enough". Christianity's "must spread the gospel + save souls" — conquest had **a holy reason**

**All 5 hypotheses are right** — together they **explain** the fundamental difference between China in 1433 and Europe in 1492 —
**This is the sharpest version of Joseph Needham's Question** —
**Why did China** voluntarily **give up the chance to be world hegemon**?

> ⚠️ **AP thinking**: **China vs Europe in the 1490s is not "who was more advanced"** —
> **China's tech + finance + experience were stronger than Europe's** —
> **It was** ideology + politics + economic motive **that made China** voluntarily not colonize —
> **Europe didn't "win"** — it **had no choice** — it was **forced to the Atlantic** before **accidentally** finding the Americas —
> **Studying history** is not ranking — it is seeing clearly **why two paths** went different directions —
> **If the Ming had not** stopped in 1433 — **the 16th century could have seen China colonizing the Americas** —
> **The contingency of history** is **bigger than necessity** —
> **The Age of Exploration was not "inevitable" — it was** the accumulation of several contingent decisions.

#### Mansa Musa (1280-1337) — The Golden Pilgrimage of Mali (cross-Topic hint to Mali Empire)

##### 1324 — The Most Lavish Pilgrimage in History

**Mansa Musa was the emperor of the West African Mali Empire from 1312-1337** —
**The Mali Empire 1240-1645 — was the largest empire in West African history** —
**Capital Timbuktu** — **a world knowledge center in the 14th century** —
**Mali Empire** controlled **50% of the world's gold supply** —

**In 1324 Mansa Musa made the pilgrimage to Mecca** —
**He brought**:
- **A 60,000-person retinue**
- **12,000 enslaved attendants** — each **carrying 4 pounds of gold**
- **80 camels** — each **carrying 300 pounds of gold**
- **Total** about 18 tons of gold — **today's value: $400 million to $1 billion**

**He stopped in Cairo for 3 months — he** generously gave gold to ordinary Cairenes —
**Result**:
- **Cairo's gold price** crashed —
- **For the next 12 years** Cairo's economy suffered —
- **This is the** first **recorded "inflation" event in history — one person wrecked** an entire city's economy.

##### How This Hooks Into the Age of Exploration

**Mansa Musa's pilgrimage news** spread to Europe in the 14th century —
**In 1375 the Catalan Atlas first depicted Mansa Musa — holding a gold ball — sitting on the Mali throne** —
**For the next 100+ years Europeans were** obsessed with **"West African gold"** —
**This** obsession **directly drove**:
- **Portuguese Henry the Navigator from the 1430s** sailed down the West African coast — **looking for gold + a route to Mali**
- **Portugal in 1471 took São Jorge da Mina** — the first **permanent colony in West Africa — directly for gold**
- **Columbus 1492** was also **indirectly influenced by the Mali gold story** — he wanted to find **Asian gold**

— **Mansa Musa's 1324 pilgrimage** —
**A butterfly flapping its wings** — **168 years later helped trigger** Columbus and the Age of Exploration —
**This is** the global cascade — history is not "Europe suddenly explodes" — it is **multiple nodes in a 14-16th century global network** influencing each other.

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: Mansa Musa is **covered in detail in the Mali Empire Topic (a later Topic)** —
> **Here only as a hint**: **The Age of Exploration is not Europe's solo story** — it is a **global 14-16th century** network —
> **Mali gold → Portugal goes south → West African colonization → the start of the trans-Atlantic slave trade** —
> **Mansa Musa indirectly** shaped the **starting point** of the Atlantic triangular trade.

#### A Hispaniola Taíno Girl, Anacaona Jr. 1495

**Anacaona Jr. saw Spaniards for the first time** — they **wore iron armor + carried swords + rode horses** —
**She** wasn't afraid — she and the other children **were curious** —
**Hojeda came to Queen Anacaona** **and demanded**: hand over all gold + cotton + food —

**Queen Anacaona gave** everything she had —
**Hojeda left** — but **in 1496** came **back asking for more** —

- **Her father** worked to death in the mines

- **Her brother** died of smallpox
- **She** was **raped by one of Hojeda's friends + got pregnant** — at 15 she gave birth to **a mestizo daughter** — the daughter **died at age 1**

**When all the chiefs were gathered — Ovando ordered** the hall burned — **all 80 chiefs died** — **including Queen Anacaona** —
**Anacaona Jr. was 20 at the time** — **was not in the hall** — she **fled to the mountains** —
**In 1504 she starved to death in the mountains** —

**Her entire family** was wiped out within 5 years —
**1492 Hispaniola had an estimated 3 million Taíno** —
**By 1542 only 200 were left** —
**Anacaona Jr. is** one of the 5-10 million Taíno who died in genocide — **a nameless one** —
**She** has no grave — **no monument** —
**Bartolomé de las Casas's 1542 book made the world know** Queen Anacaona — but **a niece like Anacaona Jr.** is unknown.

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: The scale of "genocide" in the Caribbean from 1492-1542 —

**Adisa — age 24 — wife of a fisherman from the Bight of Benin in West Africa** —
**In June 1620 — she and her 4-year-old daughter were captured in their village by Portuguese traders + local African slavers** —
**Her husband fought back when they were taken — was killed** —
**She and her daughter were taken to Elmina Castle** —

**Adisa waited in the castle's** underground **for 2 months** — **a 6 m × 9 m underground cell** held **200 Africans** —
**No windows + one bucket for waste — one bucket for water — bread twice a day** —
**Many died of disease + suicide + were beaten to death over small things** —
**Adisa's 4-year-old daughter** died of **dysentery** —

**In September 1620 — Adisa was loaded onto the** *São Cristóvão* — this ship had 350 Africans —
**She was on the lower deck**:
- **Each person had** 60 cm × 1.5 m space — **could not stand up**
- **Chained to wooden boards** —
- **2 hours a day** on the upper deck **to see the sun** — the other 22 hours below
- **Food**: **corn meal mush** — often **spoiled**
- **Water**: **1 cup** a day

**The Middle Passage — 70 days** —
**What Adisa saw**:
- **30% of the people on the ship died** — bodies thrown to the sea
- **Several crew members raped** her and the other women — she was already **pregnant with a second child**
- **Several disease outbreaks** — the crew **drowned some of the sick** — to prevent spread
- **2 Africans committed suicide** — they **jumped into the sea — choosing death over slavery**

**In November 1620 — the *São Cristóvão* reached Veracruz, Mexico** —
**Adisa was put ashore — she was** 6 months pregnant —
**At the Veracruz slave market she was sold to** sugar mill owner Diego Hernández —
**In January 1621 — she gave birth to** a son, Pedro —
**She was enslaved on the sugar mill** for the next 30 years —
**She died in her 50s** — **in 1652** —

**Her son Pedro continued to be enslaved** —
**Pedro's grandson Pedro III born in 1750** — they were **still enslaved** —
**Adisa's whole** family was enslaved in Veracruz for 200+ years —
**Until Mexico abolished slavery in 1829** —

> ⚠️ **Teaching point**: The trans-Atlantic slave trade **is not abstract history** —
> **It is 12 million Adisas** —
> **Each with a name + a family + a dream** —
> **Forced across the Atlantic — 70 days + 30% mortality + rape + humiliation** —
> **Their descendants** were enslaved in the Americas for generations — until the 19th century —
> **Today the African-descended populations of the Caribbean + US + Brazil** are direct grandchildren of women like Adisa —
> **The "success" of the Age of Exploration** is built directly on this **human pain** —
> **Studying history** can't be "Caesar's perspective" ignoring the bottom — must **acknowledge Adisa** —
> **Without this acknowledgment — history education is** a tool for the powerful.

## 4. The Story: That 100 Years (1487-1620)

### 1487-1488 — Bartolomeu Dias Rounds the Southern Tip of Africa

> Portugal's João II sent Dias to find a sea route to India —
> In 1488 Dias was the first to round Africa's southern tip, the Cape of Good Hope —
> He wanted to keep going — but the crew was **scared + refused** — he was **forced to turn back** —
> This is **the fruit of 50 years of Portuguese nautical investment** —
> But Dias himself **didn't reach India** — 10 years later da Gama did.

### January 2, 1492 — Reconquista Complete

> Spain's Granada falls — 700 years of Catholic vs Muslim war ends —
> A huge body of **trained soldiers with nothing to do + the crown with money on hand + Catholic desire to "spread the gospel to a new place"** —
> **3 months later Isabella approves Columbus's plan** —
> **A perfect convergence** — this is the contingent power of 1492.

### October 12, 1492 — Columbus Reaches the Bahamas

> Rodrigo de Triana shouts "land" —
> **The starting point of the largest reorganization in human history** —
> **The beginning of 100 million Indigenous deaths + the beginning of global colonialism**.

### 1493 — Columbus's Second Voyage + Colonization Begins

> Columbus 17 ships + 1,500 men — colonization of Hispaniola begins —
> Forced gold tribute on Taíno + cutting off hands + enslavement begins —
> **This is the "operating template" of European colonialism** —
> **For the next 400 years** — Spain / Portugal / England / France / Netherlands **repeat** this template.

### 1494 — Treaty of Tordesillas

> Pope Alexander VI 1493 + Spain and Portugal 1494 sign —
> **The Pope draws a line dividing the world between two countries** —
> Spain west / Portugal east —
> **The supreme expression of European centrism** —
> **The entire globe** was not consulted — the most absurd treaty in human history.

### 1497 — John Cabot Reaches Newfoundland

> Genoese in English service — finds the Newfoundland fishery —
> For the next 200 years — Newfoundland fish became **a main protein source for Europe** —

> Portugal's Vasco da Gama **rounded Africa to India** —
> This is **the fruit of 80 years of Portuguese nautical technology** —

> **Until 1600s when the Dutch East India Company took over** —
> **The Indian Ocean** from then on **entered the European network** —
> **Reshaped global trade** for 500 years — until the 21st century.

> Portugal's Pedro Álvares Cabral wanted to go to India — accidentally **drifted to Brazil** —

> **Accidentally** changed the language of the largest country in South America.

### 1502-1504 — Columbus's Fourth Voyage

> 51-year-old Columbus desperately searched for "an Asian passage" — failed —
> **He died never knowing the Americas were a new continent** —
> Died in 1506.

> Vasco Núñez de Balboa in Panama crossed the mountains and **was the first European** to see the Pacific —

### 1519-1521 — Cortés Conquers the Aztecs (see §3)

> 11 ships + 500 men + Doña Marina + 80,000 Tlaxcalan allies + smallpox —
> **An Aztec Empire of 6 million people conquered in 2 years**.

### 1519-1522 — Magellan's Circumnavigation

> Ferdinand Magellan was Portuguese **serving Spain** (the Portuguese king ignored him) —
> **5 ships + 270 men** set out from Seville —
> November 1520 he **found the passage at the southern tip of South America** (the Strait of Magellan) —
> April 27, 1521 **Magellan himself was killed in the Philippines** (fighting the Lapu-Lapu people) —
> September 1522 — only **1 ship + 18 men** returned to Seville —
> **Completed the first circumnavigation** — 3 years —
> **Proved the Earth is a sphere + the oceans are connected** —
> **In ironic contrast to Columbus's miscalculation**: **Columbus got the Earth wrong** and accidentally found the Americas — **Magellan got the Earth right** and proved it.

> **More absurd than Cortés conquering the Aztecs**: **the Inca Empire was bigger than the Aztec** — but **168 people kidnapped the emperor in 1 hour**.

### Document 1: Columbus's 1493 Letter to Isabella (Conqueror's Perspective)

#### The Special Position of This Document

**February 1493 — on Columbus's return from his first voyage — he wrote a letter** to the Spanish royal court from the Azores —
**This is** the earliest "first-hand record of the Americas" in human history —
**Within 3 months it had spread across Europe through the printing press** —
**Between April and May of 1493** there were 9 different **printed editions across Europe** —
**This is** 25 years before the Reformation — the first major demonstration of the printing press as an information accelerator —
**This "letter" is the manifesto of the Age of Exploration's beginning** — shaping **European understanding of the Americas for 100+ years**.

#### Full Passage (edited to Lexile-1000)

> "**Most High and Royal Majesties** Ferdinand and Isabella —
>
> **I** write to report we have arrived in the Indies 35 days ago (Columbus believed his whole life that he had reached Asia — he called this place the Indies) —
> **I have already discovered** many islands + a mainland —
>
> **The peoples of these islands** — they are **very gentle** —
> **They have no** iron weapons — their **spears are sharpened bamboo** —
> **They do not have full clothing** — they use **palm leaves to cover themselves** —
> **They are** very **generous — they** give us **whatever we want** — only **for** a few glass beads + a copper bell —
>
> **I** have sent some men to **learn their language** + learn their **customs** —
>
> **About** gold **— this land has** much gold **— they** wear gold balls on thin strings around their necks **— they** told me **— there are** mountains of gold **inland** —
>
> **About conversion** —
> I **believe** — these people **easily convert** to Christianity — they **have no religion of their own** —
> **If Your Majesties send a few** friars — within a few years **they will all be converted** —
>
> **About work** —
> **They** are suited to **any work — they** have no **resistance** —
> **50 of our men** could **make all 50,000 of them work for us** —
>
> **I** left **39 men** at La Navidad (a colony on Hispaniola) — they will **continue to look for gold + prepare for trade** —
>
> **I beg Your Majesties** to be generous — **I** have undertaken **this great voyage** — I am **willing to** continue —
> **If Your Majesties need** any **enslaved people** — there is **an inexhaustible supply** here —
>
> Your loyal servant Christóforo Colombo"

#### The 5 Layers of Meaning in This Document

**Layer 1: Total "othering" of Indigenous people**

**Columbus describes the Taíno**:
- "**No** full clothing"
- "**No** iron weapons"
- "**No** religion of their own"
- "**No** resistance"

— **All described in terms of "no"** —
**This "diminished other" language** shaped European descriptions of Indigenous people for the next 500 years —
**In reality the Taíno had** their own language / complex agriculture / weaving technology / religion / politics — but Columbus **didn't see it** —
**This is** the psychological root of colonialism: **first** deny **the complexity of the other's culture** — then **enslavement + conquest** look reasonable.

**Layer 2: The "convertible + workable + enslaveable" three-pack**

**Columbus simultaneously emphasizes the Taíno are**:
- **"Easily converted"** = useful to the Church
- **"Suited to any work"** = useful to the economy
- **"Inexhaustible enslaved people"** = useful to colonists

— **This "3-pack" logic** dominated colonialism for the next 400 years:
**Any** colony **was reduced** to **these 3 uses** —
**English / French / Dutch / American / German / Belgian colonists** used the same template —
**Until 19-20th century decolonization** reversed this logic.

**Layer 3: Glass beads + copper bells vs gold "trade"**

**Columbus describes this exchange as** "generous trade" —
**Taíno gave gold / corn / cotton — got glass beads / copper bells** —
**Columbus thought** the Taíno were generous —
**In reality** in Taíno culture: **glass beads** were **exotic treasures** — **copper bells** were **new musical instruments** —
**For the Taíno** these had **real value** —
**For Europeans** the **gold** the Taíno gave had **real value** —
**The two sides valued the "trade"** differently —
**Columbus exploited this** asymmetry **— starting** centuries **of unequal trade** —
**This is** the embryonic form of today's global wealth gap: **trading low-cost manufactured goods for developing-country natural resources**.

**Layer 4: The impact of the printing press**

**This "letter" was** printed across Europe within 3 months —
**Between 1493-1500** at least 17 editions — Latin + Spanish + Italian + German —
**24 years before Luther's 1517 95 Theses** — the first major demonstration of the printing press as an **accelerator** —
**Columbus wrote**: "**This letter** became **16th-century Europe's "first impression of the Americas"** —
**Generations of European elites** formed their psychological image of the Americas based on this letter —
**Even in the 21st century** many **European museums + textbooks still carry Columbus's perspective** —
**Because he was** first — and **his words** first **shaped the narrative**.

**Layer 5: The "loyal servant" self-naming**

**At the end Columbus signs himself**: "**Your loyal servant Christóforo Colombo**" —
**This** self-deprecation **before the crown — but** in reality **he kidnaps Indigenous people** to his own purposes —
**This** "servant vs master" **cunning**:
**Columbus uses** humility to the crown + violence to the Indigenous **— this** double posture **made him an** ideal colonial agent —
**For the next 500 years colonial officers used the same pattern** —
**Humble before** the king — violent toward **the conquered**.

#### Modernized Reading: What is the Columbus 1493 Letter Like Today?

> **Parallel scene**: **An "explorer" first encountering a new culture** —
> **Writes a report** to an employer —
> **Simplifies + romanticizes** the other culture —
> **Emphasizes the other is "improvable + workable + exploitable"** —
> **The report spreads through** new media technology **— shaping the next generation's understanding**.
>
> Modern parallels:
> - **18-19th century European colonial officials' reports** — straight Columbus template
> - **Early 20th century** anthropologists' **"primitive" vs "civilized" dichotomy** — straight Columbus template
> - **21st century** foreign companies' **new market analysis reports** — to some degree still Columbus template ("potential market — educable — serviceable")
>
> **Columbus's 1493 letter is not** a strange document of the past — **it is** the template **of 500 years of colonial language** —
> **Studying history** lets us recognize **when this template** is reactivated — this is **important judgment for modern decisions**.

### Document 2: Bartolomé's *A Brief Account of the Destruction of the Indies* (1542)

**Author profile in §3** — here we focus on **the full text structure**.

#### The Special Position of This Book

**Bartolomé wrote it in 1542 — formally published 1552** —
**60+ pages — but with the explosive force of a 600-page book** —
**For the next 250 years** it shaped European understanding of Spanish colonization —
**Today in the 21st century** it is still the most important first-hand source on **16th-century colonial violence** —
**The UN Human Rights Court + international legal scholars** often **cite Bartolomé** as **a reflective source**.

#### The 4 **Parts** of This Book

**Part 1 — Hispaniola** (the first Spanish colony — colonized starting with Columbus's second voyage in 1493):
- **1492 had 3 million Taíno** —
- **1542 only 200 left** —
- **Detailed causes**: massacre + forced labor + smallpox + famine + suicide (**many Taíno mothers killed their own infants** — to **prevent them becoming enslaved**)

**Part 2 — Cuba** (colonized starting 1511):
- **Similar pattern to Hispaniola** — more **concentrated**
- **Cuban Taíno almost** completely wiped out

**Part 3 — Mexico** (Cortés starting 1519):
- **Cortés directly ordering** massacres — recorded
- **Cholula 1519 massacre 6,000**
- **Tenochtitlán siege killed 100,000+**
- **Mexico's Indigenous population in 1542 had dropped 80% from 1519**

**Part 4 — Peru + the rest of Central America**:
- **Massacre after Pizarro's conquest of the Inca**
- **Forcing Quechua / Aymara to work in Potosí silver mines — extreme mortality**
- **Massacres in El Salvador / Guatemala / Nicaragua**

#### Full Passage (edited to Lexile-1000)

> "**Dear Royal Majesty** —
>
> **I as a friar** — have **witnessed with my own eyes** 50 years of Spanish colonization —
> **I must write it down** — **otherwise** these **crimes** will be **buried forever** —
>
> **In Hispaniola — Spain's first colony** — when **I arrived** (1502) —
> **The island had** about **1 million Taíno Indians** —
> **When I left (1547)** — only **a few hundred remained** —
>
> **The ways of dying include**:
>
> **1. Direct massacre** —
> **Spaniards** entered villages — killed all the men + raped the women + killed the **infants** —
> **They** cut off hands **as a "warning" — many Taíno died of blood loss** —
>
> **2. Forced labor** —
> **Spaniards forced all Taíno** to work in the gold mines + in the fields —
> **No food + no rest** —
> **Within months they died** —
>
> **3. Smallpox + flu** —
> **Diseases the Spanish brought** — the Taíno **had no immunity** —
> **Whole villages died** —
>
> **4. Suicide** —
> **The Taíno saw no hope** — **many** committed suicide —
> **Mothers** killed their own children — to **prevent them becoming enslaved** —
> **Whole families** collectively **jumped off cliffs** —
>
> **I** saw with my own eyes:
>
> **Spaniards burned a whole village** — to make other villages **fear and hand over gold** —
>
> **Spaniards snatched babies from their mothers' arms** — **smashed them on the rocks** —

> **Spaniards used hungry dogs** to chase Taíno women — **fed the women to the dogs** —
> **As "entertainment"** —
>
> **Spaniards tied 13 Taíno together** — and with one axe stroke chopped through 13 people —
> **To see whether the axe could pass through 13 in one swing** —
> **As an "experiment"** —
>
> **Royal Majesty** —
> **If** Your Majesty does not intervene immediately —

>
> **I** beg Your Majesty: **stop the encomienda** — **ban** enslavement — **protect Indigenous people's legal rights** —
>
> Your loyal friar Bartolomé de las Casas"

#### The 5 Layers of Meaning in This Document

**Layer 1: First-hand witness + 50-year time span**

**Bartolomé didn't** hear about it — he **saw it for over 40 years** —
**Reached Hispaniola in 1502** —
**Wrote this book in 1542** —
**40 years of witness** make this book **more reliable than any second-hand record**.

**Layer 2: Confronts the "Are Indians Human?" debate**

**1500s Europe was fiercely debating**: "**Are Indians human**?"
- **Some Spaniards**: "**Indians have no soul — they are lower animals — can be killed + enslaved**"
- **Bartolomé**: "**Indians are real humans — have souls — have rights**"
- **In 1550 the Valladolid debate** — Bartolomé vs Sepúlveda — **the first formal debate in Europe** about **"what is a human being"** —
  **Bartolomé partly** won — but **actual change was limited**

**This is** one of the starting points of human history's "universal human rights" concept —
**Directly leads to** 18-19th century abolition — 20th century UN 1948 Universal Declaration of Human Rights —
**Bartolomé is** the first link **in this chain**.

**Layer 3: "Feed to dogs + chop 13 + smash babies" specific details**

**Bartolomé** uses **specific details** —
**Not** abstract numbers — **specific scenes** —
**This** imagery **shocks European readers psychologically** —
**This is** the template of anti-colonial advocacy —
**Today** human rights reports **still use the same template**: **specific names + specific places + specific scenes** —
**Make readers unable to pretend "this is just numbers"** —
**Bartolomé is** the grandfather of modern human rights reports.

**Layer 4: The double burden of being a colonizer**

**Bartolomé himself was an enslaver from 1502-1514** —
**When he wrote this book he carried** 13 years of complicity —
**He is not** "an outside truth-teller" — he is **a repentant internal opponent** —
**This** self-reflection **gives the book special force**:
**He is not** an outside critic — **he** quietly **acknowledges he was once part of the problem** —
**This "internal opposition"** sometimes is **more powerful for change** than "external critique".

**Layer 5: The birth of the "Black Legend"**

**Bartolomé wanted** to change Spanish colonial policy — but **he accidentally** did something else:
**He gave Protestant nations (England / Netherlands / Germany) the perfect anti-Spanish weapon** —
**For the next 250 years — European understanding of Spanish colonization was based on this book — called the "Black Legend"** —
**Protestant nations**: "**Look — Spain is so cruel — our colonization is** gentler**"** —
**In reality**: **English / Dutch / French colonization was equally cruel** (see later African + Indian + North American Indigenous history) — but **without a Bartolomé-type internal opponent to expose it** —
**So Protestant colonization** appeared **"gentler" than Catholic colonization** — this is **completely false** —
**But it shaped 500 years of** the English-speaking world's **bias** about colonialism —
**To this day** some **Americans / English people** still feel **"we colonized better than Spain"** — this is **Bartolomé's accidental side effect**.

#### Modernized Reading: What is Bartolomé Like Today?

> **Parallel scene**: **An internal participant** — **50 years later writes a report exposing atrocities** —
> **The report shapes how the next generations understand that history** —
> **The report accidentally** lets other factions weaponize **it politically**.
>
> Modern parallels:
> - **1968 Vietnam War veterans wrote** the Winter Soldier hearing testimony
> - **1990s South African Truth and Reconciliation Commission**
> - **2003 Abu Ghraib internal opponents in the U.S.**
> - **2013 Edward Snowden NSA surveillance**
> - **2017 #MeToo internal opponents** exposing harassment culture
>
> **Bartolomé 1542 is** the grandfather of human rights advocacy —
> **Studying history** lets us recognize: **change often** comes **from** internal opposition —
> **Not "outside truth-tellers"** — **but** people who were once complicit **writing their confession**.

### Document 3: Florentine Codex (1555-1577) — The Aztec Perspective

#### Author Profile

**Bernardino de Sahagún** (1499-1590) —
**A Spanish Franciscan friar** —
**In 1529 went to Mexico** — for **the next 60 years** he **studied Aztec culture** —
**He did something** unprecedented:

**He used** Náhuatl **to interview** dozens of elderly Aztec people —
**They had** lived through **Cortés's conquest of Tenochtitlán 1519-1521** —
**Sahagún let the Aztec elders** describe in their own words — he **translated + edited** —
**The result**: 12 volumes of the *Florentine Codex* — bilingual Náhuatl + Spanish —
**Edited 1555-1577** —
**Today preserved in** Florence (hence "Florentine Codex") —

**This is** the only colonial-era document in human history with a real "voice of the conquered" —
**Closer to true Aztec perspective than any** Western history.

#### How Aztec Elders Describe First Meeting Cortés in 1519

**Florentine Codex Book 12 — the full record of Aztec elders' description of Cortés's 1519 arrival** —
**Edited to Lexile-1000**:

> "**That year** (1518) — **strange things began** —
>
> **First — a comet appeared in the sky** — **it** burned for 10 weeks — **like** an arrow dripping blood —
>
> **Then — the statue of Huitzilopochtli on top of the Templo Mayor** caught fire by itself —
> **No one** lit it — **it** burned on its own —
>
> **Then — lightning struck the Templo Mayor** —
> **No clouds + no rain** — but **lightning** hit the temple —
>
> **Then — a woman walked the streets at night** — she **shouted**: "**My children — my children — we must flee**" —
> **No one** saw her — **only her cries were heard** —
>
> **Then — a bird with a human face was caught** —
> **Brought to Moctezuma** — **Moctezuma saw a 'mirror' on the bird's face** — **in the mirror** he saw **a strange army** riding **strange animals** —
>
> **Moctezuma was very** afraid — he **did not know** the meaning —
>
> **He sent** 4 envoys **to the eastern coast** — **carrying** fine gifts —
> **The envoys reached Cortés** —
> **They** offered **a feather crown + jade carvings + gold** —
> **Cortés accepted** — but **he** put iron chains on the envoys —
> **Made them** walk on the sand — **they almost** died —
> **Then** released them — to **bring back the report**:
>
> '**These** strangers **have** iron clothes + long animals (horses) + fire tubes (guns)** —
> **Their** breath **smells like dogs** (Spaniards rarely bathed + ate meat) —
> **They** are not like men — **they are Quetzalcoatl** or **something else**?'
>
> **Moctezuma heard the report** — **he** sat coldly —
> **He** could not **eat** — **he** could not **sleep** —
>
> **For months** — **he** kept **hesitating** — **should he** kill **them? Or** receive **them**?
>
> **He** finally **chose to** receive **them** — **because he wanted to understand** — but **this was** a fatal mistake"

#### The 5 Layers of Meaning in This Document

**Layer 1: Aztec perspective — a completely different cognitive framework**

**The Aztec elders explain** 1519 using **a completely different framework**:
- **Comet + self-igniting statue + lightning** = **supernatural omens**
- **Human-faced bird + army in the mirror** = **a "supernatural warning"** received by Moctezuma
- **Cortés's "stranger" features** = **like the god Quetzalcoatl or something else**

**This** religious + supernatural **framework — is completely different** from Western history —
**Western history**: "**Cortés used military + diplomacy + smallpox to conquer the Aztecs**" —
**Aztec history**: "**The Aztecs received** supernatural warnings — but Moctezuma **hesitated** — and the result was disaster**" —

— **Both narratives are right** — but **with different emphasis** —
**Without the Aztec perspective** — we **only see** Cortés the individual — and **lose** the psychological + cultural pain of Aztec decision-making —
**With the Aztec perspective** — we see that **Moctezuma is not incompetent** — he is **facing a completely unfamiliar** event — **his cognitive framework** is collapsing —
**This pattern of "unfamiliar → cognitive collapse"** still applies **today** — for example **with the rise of AI in the 21st century** — many people are **like Moctezuma facing the unknown — not knowing how to** respond.

**Layer 2: The "shackling of the envoys" humiliation**

**In Aztec sources**: **Cortés put chains on the envoys** and **made them walk on the sand** —
**In Western history** — this event is **barely mentioned** —
**Because** Cortés thought it was **normal military intimidation** —
**The Aztecs felt it was** an insult to the nation —
**The two sides** read the same event **completely differently** —
**Studying history** — must **hear both sides** to be complete.

**Layer 3: Moctezuma's psychological pain**

**Aztec sources** — Moctezuma **sat coldly + couldn't eat + couldn't sleep** —
**This** description of psychological pain — is **rare** in 16th-century documents —
**Brings us close to** Moctezuma the **real human** —
**Western sources** — Moctezuma is **a weak emperor + a puppet who missed his chance** —
**Aztec sources** — he is **a complex subject torn between religion and politics** —
**Both are right** — but **the human psyche** is only visible from the Aztec perspective.

**Layer 4: The role of Sahagún the friar**

**Sahagún was a** Franciscan friar — he **should have been a "representative" of Spanish conquest** —
**But he** went the other way — preserved Aztec culture —
**This is** another colonial mode opposite to Cortés:
- **Cortés**: **destroy the Aztecs** — built Mexico City on Tenochtitlán's ruins —
- **Sahagún**: **preserve the Aztecs** — 12 volumes of bilingual books — for posterity

**Sahagún was contemporary with Bartolomé** — both **Catholic friars reflecting on colonization** —
**They didn't** directly **oppose conquest** — but **they preserved the conquered's voice** — this is **another form of resistance** —
**Today without Sahagún's *Florentine Codex* — our understanding of Aztec culture would be 90% poorer** —
**Sahagún is** the "accidental protector" of Aztec culture.

**Layer 5: The rarity of "the conquered writing history"**

**Throughout human history — the** vast majority **of history is written by the victors** —
**The voices of the conquered** are rare:
- **70 BCE Roman conquest of Carthage** — **Carthaginian perspective is almost completely lost**
- **17th-century English conquest of Massasoit Wampanoag** — **Wampanoag perspective is almost completely lost**
- **20th century Belgian conquest of Congo** — **Congolese perspective is only partially recovered after 1960**

— **The Florentine Codex is** a rare "complete conquered perspective" —
**It preserves** not the victor's myth — but **Moctezuma's hesitation + the envoys' humiliation + the self-igniting statue** —
**This** completeness **makes the Aztec 1519 story** more completely preserved than most conquered civilizations —
**This is one of the** luckiest accidents **of history**.

#### Three Documents Read Together: Columbus + Bartolomé + Florentine Codex

| Document | Stance | Subject | Core information | Modern impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Columbus 1493 letter** | Conqueror | Individual | "New land + convertible + workable + enslaveable" | 500-year template of colonial language |
| **Bartolomé 1542 book** | Opponent (internal) | Catholic friar | "Indigenous people are real humans + 50-year genocide record" | Modern human rights + anti-colonialism + Black Legend |
| **Florentine Codex** | Conquered (preserved) | Aztec elders + Sahagún | "Moctezuma's psyche + cultural collapse + detailed 1519 experience" | Aztec cultural preservation + cross-cultural history methodology |

**3 documents** that don't directly converse — but **complement each other**:
- **Columbus** = **how the conqueror sees himself**
- **Bartolomé** = **conscience opposition** within the conqueror camp
- **Florentine Codex** = **how the conquered see the conquest**

**No single one** is complete —
**3 together** get close to the truth —
**This is** the highest expression of the SOAPS method (Source / Occasion / Audience / Purpose / Subject):
**Always look at different** sources + different purposes + different audiences —
**Any single side** is **incomplete**.

## 6. Impact: 500 Years of Echoes

### Short-Term (1500-1700)

#### Global Trade System Forms for the First Time

- **1571 Acapulco-Manila galleon route** for 250 years — **silver + silk + porcelain global trade**
- **Trans-Atlantic triangular trade** — English / French / Dutch colonies → Americas enslavement → Europe sugar coffee cotton
- **Indian Ocean spice trade** — from Vasco da Gama 1498 — Europe controls for 250 years
- **The first** real global trade **in human history** — before, **only** regional + slow

#### Genocide in the Americas + Enslavement of Africans

- **1492-1700**: 100 million Indigenous Americans dead (90%+ proportion)
- **1500-1888**: 12 million Africans forced across the Atlantic to be enslaved
- **The Caribbean almost entirely becomes African-descended**, Indigenous peoples nearly wiped out
- **Brazil + North America** — population structure **permanently reorganized**

#### Europe Becomes Global Hegemon

- **In 1500 Europe was on the economic edge of the world**
- **By 1700 Europe controlled 50% of the global economy**
- **For the next 250 years Europe expanded** — until the two World Wars in the 1900s when it declined

### Medium-Term (1700-1900)

#### Material Foundation of the Industrial Revolution

- **The Age of Exploration brought** American silver + Caribbean sugar + Indian cotton — laying the foundation for **British capital accumulation + industrial capital**
- **Without the Age of Exploration's triangular trade** — **the British Industrial Revolution would have been almost impossible**
- **Modern capitalism** is built directly **on the Age of Exploration**

#### European Nations Expand Globally

- **18-19th centuries**: England / France / Netherlands / Russia / later Germany + Belgium **colonize globally**
- **By 1900** — European nations + USA + Japan **directly or indirectly controlled 84% of the world's land**
- **The "European century"** 1492-1945 — 450 years — **traceable directly to Columbus**

#### Globalization of Science + Scholarship

- **Western European universities** + **European science** spread globally
- **This has** two sides: **giving the world modern science** — and also **using science** to rationalize colonialism

### Long-Term (1900- )

#### Decolonization + Postcolonial Studies

- **1900-1947**: India / Latin America independence movements rise
- **1945-1990**: Africa + Asia + Caribbean colonies become independent in waves — 65 new nations
- **Postcolonial studies**: Edward Said's 1978 *Orientalism* — analyzes how European narratives shaped colonization
- **From the 2010s onward**: **Black Lives Matter + Indigenous Peoples' Day + Reparations debates** —
  **The aftermath of the Age of Exploration** is still **at the center of 21st-century politics**

#### Global Wealth Gap

- **Around 1500 the world's regions had relatively similar levels of economic development**
- **In 2024**: Europe + North America + East Asia = high GDP; Caribbean + Africa + Latin America = low GDP
- **This** global wealth gap **traces directly to the Age of Exploration + colonial economic structures**
- **"Globalization" is actually the continuation of the Columbus system — only more refined**

#### The 21st-Century Columbus Statue Movement

- **After 2017 Charlottesville in the US** — anti-**colonial statue** movement rises
- **After 2020 BLM** — Columbus statues are pulled down in many places
- **California + Maine + New Mexico + several cities** — **Columbus Day renamed Indigenous Peoples' Day**
- **By 2023, 12 U.S. states officially observe Indigenous Peoples' Day** — **the U.S. narrative is being rewritten**
- **This narrative rewriting** is **the contemporary digestion of 500 years of colonial history**

> - **The roots of Caribbean + Latin American poverty** are still in the 1492 colonial structure

## 8. Contemporary China: Ming Dynasty + Zheng He + Joseph Needham's Question

### What Was Ming China Doing in 1492?

**1492 Columbus reached the Bahamas** —
**Contemporary China in 1492**:
- **Ming Hongzhi reign year 5 — Zhu Youtang at age 22 had been emperor for 5 years** —
- **The Ming Dynasty was in the "Hongzhi Restoration"** — economy stable + politics relatively clean
- **In 1492 China was most concerned with**: Yellow River floods + the Mongol border —
- **No Chinese official cared** about a faraway "Americas" —
- **If someone in 1492 had told a Chinese official "there is a continent on the other side of the planet"** —
  **the official might have answered**: "**What's the use — we already have** 30 tributary states — **the world is already big enough**"

— **This** "mindset" **is the deepest answer to Joseph Needham's Question**:
**China was not** unable **to expand globally — it was** unwilling —
**Because there was no** economic + political + ideological need —
**While in 1492 Europe** — **all three needs existed** — so **it actively went out**.

### Zheng He vs Columbus Detailed Comparison (already in §3 — strengthened here)

**The first Zheng He voyage in 1405 — 87 years before Columbus** —
**The last Zheng He voyage in 1433 — 59 years before Columbus** —
**The two eras almost** completely overlap — but **the results are completely opposite**.

**Why**? 5 hypotheses listed in §3 — **regrouped here**:

**1. Political system**:
- China unified — **one emperor decides = whole country obeys**
- After Yongle died, **the next emperor disliked sailing** — **the whole country stopped**
- Europe fragmented — **one country stops + another keeps going** — **no single ruler** could stop everyone

**2. Economic motivation**:
- China was **self-sufficient** — **not short of food + not short of people + tribute brings money** — overseas had no economic necessity
- Europe was **strangled in the Mediterranean by the Ottomans** — had to **find a new spice route = economic necessity**

**3. Ideology**:
- China's "China-as-center + tribute" — **moral influence on far peoples is enough**
- Europe's Renaissance "we can change the world" + Reformation "spread the gospel" — **must expand**

**4. Financial model**:
- China's voyages **ran on the treasury** — **stop the court and the money stops**
- Europe's **Joint stock company + Royal patronage** — **diverse capital** — **not easy to stop**

**5. Contingency**:
- Yongle died in 1424 — that year decided China's maritime future
- Reconquista completed in 1492 — that year decided Europe's maritime future
- **If the two contingencies were reversed** — **China might have led the Age of Exploration**

### Wanli + Late Ming — Saw the Consequences but Too Late

**1572-1620 Wanli reign** — **roughly contemporary with the Reformation** —
**During his reign in China**:
- **1573-1582 Zhang Juzheng's reforms** (contemporary with mid-Reformation Europe)
- **In 1583 Matteo Ricci arrived in China** — the first major China-Europe cultural contact —
  **Ricci brought** the Renaissance + post-Trent reformed Catholicism
- **1592-1598 Imjin War — Ming + Korea vs Hideyoshi's Japan** —
  **This war drained generations of Ming financial reserves** —
- **1593-1600 Wanli's "Three Great Campaigns"** — **Ming finances suffered fundamental damage** —

**After 1600 the late Ming + early Qing** — began **to realize**: **the world had changed** —
**But too late**:
- **1644 Ming falls — Li Zicheng + the Manchus enter the pass**
- **1683 Qing fully unifies China — but** by then **the world** had already been dominated by the European maritime system
- **1757 Qianlong's "single-port trade"** — **only Guangzhou one port** open to foreign trade — this was **the last attempt at isolation**
- **1840 Opium War** — **Western maritime powers found the Qing** —
  **For the next 100 years** China was "lost" globally — **until 1949** when it **really** rebuilt and re-engaged with the world

— **This is** the complete story of Joseph Needham's Question:
**1433 China voluntarily stopped** — **1840 China was forcibly opened** — **400 years of "voluntary isolation + forced opening"** —
**21st-century China** is still **digesting these 400 years of consequences**:
- **From the 1990s reform and opening up** — **actively** rejoining the world
- **2010s Belt and Road** — **trying again** to lead global trade
- **2020s "Made in China 2025"** — **trying to lead** in technology

> ⚠️ **AP thinking**: **Joseph Needham's Question is not a question of the past** —
> **It is a 21st-century question**: **Can China** redo **the role it gave up in 1433**?
> - **Infrastructure** (Belt and Road / high-speed rail / 5G) — already done
> - **Technology** (semiconductors + AI) — being done
> - **Ideology** (the great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation) — already formed
> - **Political system** (unified central authority) — still distinctively Chinese
>
> **Today's question**: **Will 21st-century China** repeat **the 1433 "voluntary stop"** — or **for the first time** become **a sustained global leader**?
> **Studying the Age of Exploration history** is not nostalgia — it is **providing historical perspective** for **21st-century decisions**.

## 9. What Historians Are Arguing About (Historiography)

### 4 Main Schools of Reading

#### A. "Age of Exploration = Progress" School (19th-century classic)

**Representative**: **Samuel Eliot Morison** *Admiral of the Ocean Sea* (1942).

**Core thesis**:
- **Columbus + Cortés + Magellan = heroic explorers**
- **Age of Exploration = human progress** — the modern Western world begins
- **"Atrocities" are individual cases** — they don't affect "great achievement" valuation

**Strengths**: Clear narrative — easy for kids to remember the people.

**Weaknesses**: **Almost completely ignores** Indigenous people + African enslavement + colonial violence —
**21st-century scholarship has nearly completely abandoned** this narrative.

#### B. "Age of Exploration = Disaster" School (21st-century decolonial perspective)

**Representative**: **Walter Mignolo** *The Darker Side of Western Modernity* (2011) + **Eduardo Galeano** *Open Veins of Latin America* (1971).

**Core thesis**:
- **Age of Exploration = the largest colonial violence in human history**
- **100 million Indigenous deaths + 12 million African enslaved people = a crime that cannot be whitewashed**
- **Columbus / Cortés / Pizarro = war criminals** — not **heroes**
- **European "modernity"** is in fact **built on colonial violence**

**Strengths**: Adds the overlooked **bottom-up perspective** — history more **complete**.

**Weaknesses**: **Some versions slide toward** wholesale negation of European contribution **— also a simplification**.

#### C. "Columbian Exchange" School (ecological + global history)

**Representative**: **Alfred Crosby** *The Columbian Exchange* (1972).

**Core thesis**:
- **Age of Exploration = mass ecological + biological** exchange
- **Pathogens** (smallpox / measles / flu → Americas) + **crops** (corn / potato / tomato / chocolate → Europe) + **animals** (horses / cattle / sheep → Americas)
- **Ecological factors > military genius** — smallpox killed more than war
- **Importance of "Columbus the individual"** lower — ecological networks matter more

**Strengths**: **Most accurate** — 21st-century scholarly consensus.

**Weaknesses**: Can **soften human violence** — reducing "genocide" to **"biological accident"**.

#### D. "Multiple Encounters" School (global history + multi-perspective)

**Representative**: **Camilla Townsend** *Fifth Sun: A New History of the Aztecs* (2019) + **John Demos** *Heathen School* (2014).

**Core thesis**:
- **The Age of Exploration has** no single narrative — it is **multiple cultural encounters + violence + adaptation**
- **Each encounter has its own dynamic** — Cortés vs Aztec ≠ Pizarro vs Inca ≠ John Smith vs Powhatan
- **Indigenous people are not** passive victims — they too **actively** adapted + resisted + negotiated
- **See the diversity** — don't **simplify** to "colonizer vs victim" binary

**Strengths**: **Most complete + most respectful of Indigenous agency** — 21st-century scholarly consensus.

**Weaknesses**: **Complexity** — hard for **a 12-year-old** to fully grasp.

### Why This Topic Is Especially Hard to Approach Neutrally

**The Age of Exploration easily falls into two extremes**:

**Extreme A**: **Progress narrative** ("Age of Exploration = modern world begins = great achievement") —
**This is 19th-century Whig history** — almost completely **abandoned in the 21st century** —
But **U.S. textbooks partly still lean this way**.

**Extreme B**: **All-black narrative** ("Age of Exploration = pure violence — Europe entirely wrong") —
**This** anti-colonial narrative — **sometimes** slides into **reverse simplification** —
**Ignoring Bartolomé-type internal opponents** — also ignoring **the genuine positive complexity of global trade + technology exchange**.

**The right middle path**:
- **Acknowledge** the Age of Exploration **as the largest reorganization in human history** — objective + neutral
- **At the same time** acknowledge **100 million Indigenous deaths + 12 million African enslaved people = irreversible atrocities** —
  **Cannot be whitewashed by "progress" or "inevitability" or "biological accident"**
- **Acknowledge the personal complexity of Columbus / Cortés / Pizarro** — both real ability and real crime
- **Acknowledge** the reality of internal opponents like Bartolomé
- **Acknowledge** the real complexity of Aztec / Inca / Maya civilizations —
  **They were not "primitive" — they were different forms of high civilization**
- **Studying history is not picking sides — it is seeing complexity**

> ⚠️ **Teaching hard rule** (Rule 0):
> "**As a Chinese person — I acknowledge the Age of Exploration shaped the modern global system — but I also acknowledge it is built on the atrocity of 100 million Indigenous deaths + 12 million African enslaved people** —
> **At the same time China voluntarily stopped** — this had its own price (the 19th-century Opium Wars) + its own wisdom (not participating in atrocity) —
> **Studying history is not ranking — it is seeing that every civilization in every era faces** complex choices — not simple "right or wrong"**".

## 10. 7 Common Myths (Myth-Busting)

### Myth 1: "Columbus discovered America"

**Wrong** — **the Americas had been inhabited for 15,000+ years** —
**Columbus did not "discover" — he** arrived —
**21st-century scholarship has almost completely abandoned the word "discovery"** —
**Use** "encounter" or **"arrival"**.

### Myth 2: "Columbus proved the Earth is round"

**Wrong** — **In 1492 European scholars 100% knew the Earth was round** —
**Aristotle proved it in 350 BCE** —
**Eratosthenes calculated Earth's circumference in 240 BCE** (margin of error 0.5%) —
**By the 1490s European scholars** generally accepted it — **no one** still believed in a flat Earth —
**What Columbus actually did**: **miscalculated Earth's size** → thought Asia was nearby → set out — he **did not** prove the Earth is round.

### Myth 3: "Cortés conquered millions of Aztecs with a few hundred Spaniards"

**Partly wrong** —
**Truth**: **Cortés had 500 Spaniards + 80,000 Tlaxcalan allies + Doña Marina + smallpox** —
**Without Tlaxcalan + without Doña Marina + without smallpox** — Cortés **almost certainly couldn't have conquered** —
**The simplistic "500 men conquered millions"** makes the Aztecs look **"incompetent"** — this is **false**.

### Myth 4: "Indigenous Americans died in war"

**Wrong** —
**90%+ of Indigenous Americans** died of smallpox + measles + flu —
**They had** no immunity —
**The deaths from war + massacre** are **only the remaining 10%** —
**Biological factors > military factors** — this is **21st-century scholarly consensus**.

### Myth 5: "African slave trade was mainly done by Arabs"

**Wrong** —
**The trans-Atlantic slave trade** — was **primarily done by Europeans** (Portugal + England + France + Netherlands + Spain) —
**1500-1888 trans-Atlantic 12 million Africans** — **all on European ships** —
**The Arab trans-Saharan slave trade** is **a different system** — **1000-1900** about 17 million — **longer than the trans-Atlantic but** slower —
**Both systems are real** — but **don't confuse them**.

### Myth 6: "Spanish colonization was the most violent — English colonization was gentle"

**Wrong** —
**This is** the side effect of Bartolomé's 1542 book — the "Black Legend" **shaped a** false **comparison** —
**English / Dutch / French colonization was equally** violent:
- **Tasmania 1803-1876 British colonization — Tasmanian Indigenous peoples** completely wiped out
- **19th-century Australia** — Indigenous population dropped 80%
- **India 1757-1947 British colonization** — **1943 Bengal famine 3 million dead** — directly caused by British policy
- **Belgian Congo 1885-1908** — **10 million Congolese dead** — **larger in scale than the Holocaust**

— **No European colonial power was "gentle"** —
**It's just that** internal opponents like Bartolomé **caused Spanish atrocities** to be the **most documented**.

### Myth 7: "The Age of Exploration started suddenly in 1492"

**Wrong** —
**Portugal had** Henry the Navigator **investing 50+ years from 1394-1460** —
**In 1488 Dias had already rounded southern Africa** (4 years before Columbus) —
**In 1497 da Gama was already nearing his Indian voyage prep** —
**Columbus 1492 is** the milestone of an entire generation of maritime development —
**Not** the starting point — **and not** an isolated event —
**Studying history**: don't take **"obvious events" as** the starting point — **long-term accumulation matters more**.

## 11. Thinking Questions (Don't Conclude)

### Q1. Columbus Day vs Indigenous Peoples' Day

**The U.S. has had Columbus Day on October 12 since 1937** —
**Since the 2010s** — **many states + cities have changed it to Indigenous Peoples' Day** —
**By 2024** — **12 U.S. states officially observe Indigenous Peoples' Day** —

**Question**: **Do you support Columbus Day or Indigenous Peoples' Day**?
- Support Columbus Day: **commemorate European history + the start of the Age of Exploration + the origin of globalization**?
- Support Indigenous Peoples' Day: **acknowledge 100 million Indigenous deaths + recenter Indigenous history**?
- Both at once: **acknowledge two narratives**?

— **There is no standard answer to this question** — but **the way of thinking matters more than the answer**.

### Q2. 1433 Ming Stops Sailing vs 1492 Europe Begins

**1433 the Ming voluntarily stopped maritime expeditions** — **1492 Europe begins the Age of Exploration** —
**60 years apart** shaped the next 500 years of difference between China and Europe.

**Question**: **If you were a Ming court official in 1433 — how would you have advised the emperor**?
- Continue Zheng He's voyages — keep maritime hegemony
- Agree to stop — focus on internal affairs — avoid atrocities
- A third path?

— **This** "imagine going back to that moment" **thinking lets a 12-year-old feel** the real weight of historical decisions.

### Q3. Doña Marina — Traitor or Survivor?

**She was sold into slavery by her mother at 6 or 7** — **at 19 given to Cortés** — **had no choice** —
**She helped Cortés conquer the Aztecs** — **bore Cortés a son** —

**Question**: **How do you evaluate her**?
- **Traitor** — helped invaders against her own people
- **Survivor** — used cleverness to survive in an impossible situation
- **Complex** — both sides
- **Cannot judge** — she **had no real choice**

— **This is** the classic thought exercise of "moral judgment of the oppressed".

### Q4. Bartolomé's 1516 Recommendation of African Enslavement — Was He Wrong?

**Bartolomé in 1516** recommended **using African enslaved people instead of Indigenous people** — he **in 1552 reflected and saw the error himself** —

**Question**: **How do you evaluate**:
- He started with the wrong recommendation — but later reflected = **forgivable**?
- He **made a great mistake** — later reflection **doesn't make up for it**?
- In **the knowledge background of his time** he made a **constrained choice** — can't be judged by 21st-century standards?

— **This** "intellectual evolution" judgment — is **the hardest and most valuable exercise in the study of history**.

### Q5. Age of Exploration vs 21st-Century AI / Space Age

**Columbus 1492 gave** the unknown "New World" — Europe chose **colonization + violence** —
**In the 21st century we are facing**:
- **AI** (an **unknown** force)
- **Space colonization** (Mars + Moon)
- **Biotech** (gene editing)

**Question**: **Facing unknown forces in the 21st century** — should we **learn from 1492 Europe** or **from 1433 Ming**?
- Bold expansion — could be great but also violent
- Voluntarily stop — avoid atrocities but also miss opportunities
- A third path?

— **This** "historical experience vs modern decision" **thinking — is one of the greatest values of history education**.

### Q6. Are There Age-of-Exploration Consequences in Your Family?

**This is the question closest to a child's life**:
- **If your family** comes from China — **a Ming family in 1492 — the Age of Exploration indirectly shaped the 19th-century Opium Wars + your great-grandparents**
- **If your family comes from Europe** — your **grandparents may have colonized + or resisted** colonization
- **If your family comes from Africa** — you **may be a descendant** of the slave trade
- **If your family comes from Indigenous American peoples** — **your family lived through 90% genocide**

— **Bring the Age of Exploration into the child's own family** —
**"History is not the past — it lives in your bloodline"**.
